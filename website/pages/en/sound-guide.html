Which sounds should guide you?

Twangle is getting a sound guide, for tuning without looking. Before it is built, we would like your ears: which of these sounds would you like to hear while you tune?

What the sound guide does

While the tuner hears a string, a short sound plays between its readings: one kind of sound while the string is too high (sharp), the other while it is too low (flat). The closer the string gets, the quicker the sounds come: one every 0.7 seconds when it is far off, easing to one every 0.3 seconds when it is nearly there. This is the same pace as the vibration guide's taps. Once the string is in tune, one in-tune sound plays, then all is quiet while the string holds.

Each sound is short on purpose. While it plays, the tuner can't hear the string, so the shorter the sound, the more it keeps listening. The sound guide will have its own switch, next to the vibration guide's, so you can use either one or both.

How to listen

Each sound has a play button. After you press Play, there is a short pause of about half a second, so a screen reader can finish speaking, then the sound plays three times: the sharp and flat sounds one every 0.7 seconds, as the guide plays them when the string is far off, the in-tune sounds with a short pause after each. Earbuds let you hear the sounds clearly; the phone's speaker lets you hear them as they would sound while you tune with the phone on a table. Try both if you can. The demos last about 7 seconds each and play once.

Sharp and flat: five pairs

Each pair has a sound for too high (sharp) and one for too low (flat). Each sound lasts a fraction of a second, and its player plays it three times. Pairs B to E also have a demo: a string coming up to pitch from 40 cents flat. You hear the pair's flat sound, quicker and quicker as the string gets closer, then the in-tune bell. The string itself is not in the demo, only the guide.

Pair A: a quick high glide (the first try)

A bright glide, a tenth of a second, between 2,000 and 2,500 Hz. This was the first idea; it is here to compare, as it sounded sharp to us when heard many times.

Pair B: a water drop

A short, round “bloop” that bends quickly up or down, like a drop falling into water. A tenth of a second, between about 720 and 1,240 Hz.

Pair C: two steps

Two short, soft notes a major third apart (F sharp and A sharp): the second is higher when the string is too high, lower when it is too low. About a seventh of a second in all.

Pair D: wooden knocks

Two soft knocks, like a small woodblock, a major third apart (G sharp and C): rising when the string is too high, falling when it is too low. About a fifth of a second in all.

Pair E: a soft glide

Like A, but lower (850 to 1,275 Hz), rounder, quieter and a little longer (0.16 seconds), fading in and out.

In tune

Played once when the string becomes in tune. You will be able to choose how long it lasts: short (0.3 seconds, the default), 1 second or 2 seconds. Each player below plays its sound three times, with a short pause after each.

A soft bell

A C (523 Hz) with a bell's ring, fading away.

A two-note chime

An F, then the C above it (698 and 1,047 Hz), like a soft doorbell.

A plain high C (the first try)

A clear, almost pure high C (2,093 Hz), short. Here to compare.

What we would like to know

Which pair of sharp and flat sounds would you be happy to hear many times while you tune, and still tells you clearly which way to turn the peg? Which in-tune sound do you like best: the bell, the chime or the plain high C? And would a long in-tune note, 1 or 2 seconds, help you, or would it get in the way of hearing the string? Anything else you notice is welcome too.

Please write to twangle.tuner@proton.me, in English or Dutch. A few words are enough, for example “B and the bell, short”.
