# Twangle — Ideas for after v1.0

Not planned yet: ideas the owner wants written down, to pick up after launch only if their condition is met. Each becomes a numbered phase in [ROADMAP.md](ROADMAP.md) once it's chosen, and leaves this file. The music tools became Phases 28–34 on 2026-10-16.

## Custom themes (idea, 2026-10-16)
Let people make their own look on top of the six styles. **Condition:** start only if several closed-test testers or early users ask to change the look. Rules that stay: no internet, no account, readable by the same rule as the six palettes (`StylePaletteTest`), calm by default. The six built-in backgrounds stay drawn in code (they fit every screen, have light, dark and quiet versions, and weigh almost nothing).
1. ☐ **Your own background photo with matching colours:** a "Make your own" card at the end of the style choices; the photo comes from Android's photo picker (no permission), is copied into the app's own storage, shrunk to the screen once, dimmed or softly blurred under a veil of the theme's colour (stronger behind Settings) so the needle and note stay readable; the theme's colours are taken from the photo, like Material You does from the wallpaper. The widget and watch keep their built-in look.
2. ☐ **A small theme builder** around that card's live preview: background (a built-in picture or your photo), main colour, lettering, meter look, light / dark / follow the phone. Twangle works out the other colours and keeps them readable; no editing of every colour one by one.
3. ☐ **On the shelf, only if 1 and 2 prove popular:** a theme code (a short text to save and share themes; a theme with a photo needs a small file instead) and a round trip with the user's own AI app: Twangle shares instructions through Android's share menu, the user chats with any AI, copies the code it gives, and Twangle offers to preview it from the clipboard. Never a live connection (that would need internet and an account).

## After the drone and ear training (idea, 2026-10-26)
What is left of these two ideas now that they are Phases 48 and 55. **Condition:** only once both have landed, and if players use them.
1. ☐ **The drone and the metronome together:** both sounding while you practise, which needs them on one screen (leaving the metronome stops its clicks today)
2. ☐ **A scale or mode by ear:** Twangle plays a scale over the drone, the player names it (major, Dorian, Mixolydian, …). Phase 55 stops at three exercises against bloat, so this would replace or join them only if players and teachers ask, under the same rules: a tool, not a game, no scores kept

## Twangle on the iPhone (idea, 2026-10-26)
One codebase for both phones: Kotlin Multiplatform, with Compose Multiplatform drawing the screens on the iPhone. **Condition:** only after v1.0 is on Play; the restructuring is the wrong thing to do in the weeks before a release. A friend of the owner has offered to build the iPhone side (2026-10-26): he has the Mac, the iPhone and the Apple developer account, which removes the cost, so after the launch this becomes a phase on the owner's say-so rather than waiting for reviews to ask for it (the feasibility look of 2026-10-26).

**What carries over as it is:** the pitch detector and all the music maths (`:core:dsp` has no Android in it), and all 36 languages (the translations keep their file format). **Shared after a mechanical move:** every screen, the six styles, the meter and wave looks, the metronome and the practice tools; their texts and fonts move to Compose's multiplatform resources (`composeResources`, the same `values-<language>/strings.xml` layout). **Needs an iPhone twin:** the microphone and the sound output (`:core:audio`, behind the same interfaces; `AVAudioSession`'s measurement mode is the iPhone's counterpart of `UNPROCESSED`), haptics, keep screen on, the in-app language switch, the app shell and its navigation. **Written in Swift, not shared:** the home-screen widget (WidgetKit) and an Apple Watch app (watchOS has no Compose; it could still use the Kotlin detector as a framework). The UI-test scripts, the full check, the store screenshots and the release scripts speak to Android phones and to Play; each needs an Apple counterpart, and every visible change is then checked on two platforms.

**The arrangement with the friend** (to settle before he starts):
- **One codebase, not a second app.** He works in this repository, on branches and pull requests like every change here, with access to the private repository (the free plan allows collaborators); the iPhone app is the same Kotlin code with an iPhone shell. Not his own Swift app around the pitch detector: two apps would drift apart, and the iPhone one would live only as long as his spare time.
- **Who does which part.** Step 1, the modules becoming multiplatform, touches every module and collides with the daily Android work, so it is a roadmap phase done here on Windows: the Android app keeps building, and the shared code compiles for the iPhone on Windows, though only a Mac can produce the app. His part starts when it lands: steps 2 and 3, the audio twins, the shell, the Xcode project and the App Store side. He checks the iPhone app on his phone the way the owner checks the Android one; the owner can't. His Mac is the gate for iPhone changes, as the owner's local run is for Android; no iPhone build in CI (GitHub's Mac runners cost ten times the minutes). A change here can break the iPhone build unnoticed: he keeps the iPhone side in step, and a broken iPhone build never holds up an Android fix.
- **Whose name on the App Store.** The developer account's name is the seller: it owns the listing, the reviews and the crash reports, and carries the legal responsibility, privacy policy included. Advice: the owner enrols (about €99 a year, also for a free app; Apple's identity check may need an iPhone, borrow his) and adds him to the account, so he builds and uploads while the app stays in the owner's name and under the free-forever promise. If it is his account instead, the iPhone app is his in Apple's eyes; a transfer later is possible but formal. Either way every release passes Apple's review (usually a day), and the store listing needs a privacy label and a sentence on why the app uses the microphone; both are easy, Twangle records nothing and sends nothing.
- **The licence** (Phase 93). Today the owner is the only author, which lets a GPL 3 app go on the App Store (others could not republish the code there). His contributions are his copyright: a short written note that they are under Twangle's licence and that the owner may publish the app on the App Store keeps Phase 93 simple.

**Without him** it would also need a Mac (Apple only lets iPhone apps be built on one; a Mac mini is the cheapest, a rented cloud Mac does for building but not for the daily phone check), an iPhone for the device checks (the simulator can use the Mac's microphone, but the tuner is tested against a real phone's microphone and audio path) and the developer programme.

**Rejected routes:** redrawing every screen natively in Swift over the shared Kotlin maths (months of work, two user interfaces to keep in step forever), or a full Swift rewrite (two of everything). Compose draws its own pixels, so Twangle looks the same on both phones, which suits a tuner with six custom styles.
1. ☐ **Here: the modules become multiplatform,** one at a time, with the Android app working unchanged at every step (the UI tests prove it): `:core:dsp` first (its tests keep running on the JVM), then `:core:settings` (DataStore is multiplatform), then the design system and the feature modules with their texts and fonts in `composeResources`.
2. ☐ **His, on the Mac: the iPhone twins in `:core:audio`:** the recorder (AVAudioEngine in measurement mode, float frames into the same `Flow<FloatArray>`) and the three players (reference tones, clicks, drone), checked on his iPhone against the laptop's test tones.
3. ☐ **His, on the Mac: the iPhone shell:** the app, navigation bar or rail, microphone permission, keep screen on, haptics, the language switch, opening screen and icon; then the Mac side of the scripts (UI tests on the simulator, screenshots, a release script for App Store Connect) and the App Store listing.
4. ☐ **On the shelf, only if the phone app finds users:** the widget (WidgetKit) and the Apple Watch app (Swift, with the Kotlin detector as a framework).

## From the tuner comparison, left out (the owner's review, 2026-10-26)
The comparison of eight popular Play tuners (2026-10-25, Phase 51) listed features Twangle lacks. The owner's review of 2026-10-26 ("just because other tuners have a feature doesn't make it good or useful; maybe Twangle can offer a better replacement") left these four out. Each stays here with why, and with the condition that brings it back.
1. ☐ **Transposing instruments** (clarinet, trumpet, saxophones, horn: the tuner showing the note as the wind player reads it). A feature of the professional chromatic tuners, not of the tuners compared, and not what someone searching for a guitar tuner needs. **Condition:** wind players ask. Then not as a key in Settings (once set, a guitarist sees the wrong notes with nothing on screen saying why) but as instruments in the instrument panel, "Trumpet in B♭", "Alto sax in E♭", which put the tuner in chromatic mode and say on the button what is going on; the reference tone and strings mode unchanged
2. ☐ **Which way to turn the peg** (a drawn headstock with a curved arrow on the string's peg, after Simply Tune's step-by-step guidance): the needle shows within a second whether the peg went the right way, and the drawing is wrong for half the instruments (three-a-side, six-in-line, classical, bass, ukulele friction pegs and violin pegs all turn differently, and it depends on how the string was wound). Hint clutter, against the owner's rule. **Condition:** beginners say, unprompted, that they turned a peg the wrong way and the needle didn't tell them
3. ☐ **On to the next string by itself** (GuitarTuna: the lock moves to the next string once one is in tune). GuitarTuna needs it because its mode is "pick a string, tune it, next"; Twangle's Auto mode already follows the string plucked, and a player locks a string exactly when Auto picked wrong, so moving the lock by itself undoes the lock, and it is the kind of surprise the calm rule forbids. **Condition:** testers who lock strings ask for it
4. ☐ **Temperaments** (just, Pythagorean, meantone, as gStrings and DaTuner Pro have): frets are built for equal temperament, and the chord charts and capo helper assume it; a just or meantone tuning puts a guitar in tune in one key and out in the others, and those temperaments serve harpsichord and early-music players, who use specialist apps. What guitarists do ask for are **sweetened tunings** (Peterson sells them): each string offset by a few cents so chords sound sweeter, as James Taylor's well-known tuning does. That fits Twangle's tuning model with a cent offset per string in the tuning editor, free where Peterson charges. Weighed again on 2026-11-09 (briefly Phase 91, taken out the same day): the meter already shows exact cents, so a player can sweeten by stopping the needle at, say, −2, and the default in-tune band (Relaxed, ±5 cents) already counts a sweetened string (offsets of 1 to 4 cents) as in tune; the right offsets differ per guitar, and the 12th-fret check fixes the cause, the intonation. **Condition:** players ask for sweetened tunings or James Taylor's; the temperaments themselves only if early-music players ask

## Capo for the banjo (idea, 2026-10-30, from Phase 59)
Phase 59 taught Chords, Scales and Ear training's Chord type the 5-string banjo's short string; the Capo helper still shows its quiet line for the banjo. On a banjo the short string needs a capo of its own, often small spikes in the neck (at the 7th, 9th and 10th frets) or a sliding fifth-string capo, so the helper's sums, one capo raising every string by the same fret, don't hold: with the capo at the 2nd fret, the short string's spike goes at the 7th, and a key its spikes can't reach needs it retuned instead. **Condition:** banjo players ask for it. Then a rough mock-up first (the table with the short string's own fret beside the capo's, and what it says where no spike reaches), and `CapoTableTest` and `CapoScreenTest` with the banjo.

## A fresh start: the app designed from scratch (idea, 2026-11-01)
On 2026-11-01 the owner asked what Twangle would look like if its screens were designed again from nothing around the core (the pitch detector, cents, tunings, reference tones, the metronome, the charts), every earlier design decision set aside. The answer is the interactive mock-up [docs/design/fresh-start-preview.html](docs/design/fresh-start-preview.html) (open it in Firefox from a local server like every mock-up; the tuner phone on it is live, with a demo that tunes all six strings, a pitch slider, the PC's microphone through a small pitch detector in the page, and a pluck per peg) and the design written out below. **The owner's decision (2026-11-01):** nothing is built before the production launch; the code stays as it passed the launch review (Phases 64 and 65). **Became the 2.0 phases on 2026-11-02** (ROADMAP.md, *2.0 — the app designed again*, built on the `v2` branch while `main` stays 1.1.x): the owner chose one look after all (Ink, the mock-up's quiet one, after four candidates in [docs/design/v2-one-look-preview.html](docs/design/v2-one-look-preview.html)), three meter looks in the tuner's lens instead of six (the pitch trail, the Light arc, the strobe; [docs/design/v2-tuner-screens-preview.html](docs/design/v2-tuner-screens-preview.html)), no wave looks, the ruler-and-lens tuner with the screen's soft colour, focus mode as one button, and the string tick-off as a toggle off by default. The text below is the record of 2026-11-01 as it was written; where it differs from the roadmap's *2.0* part, the roadmap wins.

**The design in one sentence:** the tuner is the app, and one screen answers which string, what note and which way to turn in one glance from an arm's length, with words a hand understands and a colour that means done.

**The tuner screen** (the same screen in every state; only the note, the word and the colour change, nothing moves):
- A chip at the top, "🎸 Guitar · Standard ▾", opens the one setup question (below); a gear beside it opens Settings.
- The tuning's strings run faintly down the screen behind everything, thick on the left to thin on the right; the string being played lights up in the tone's colour and buzzes, a string that has been tuned turns green.
- The note is the biggest thing on the screen, the octave small beside it, "Low E · 6th string" above, cents and hertz small below. While nothing is heard it says "Listening…" and the word below says "Play a string".
- A horizontal meter, "♭ low" on the left and "high ♯" on the right, a green zone of ±3 cents in the middle, the needle in the tone's colour. Under it a trail: the last two seconds of pitch as fading dots, so a wobble reads as a band and nobody chases the needle.
- The word, big, in the tone's colour: "↑ Tighten" or "↓ Loosen"; "↑ Tighten a little" or "↓ Loosen a little" within 15 cents, so nobody overshoots; "✓ In tune" within 3. In Any-note mode, where there is no peg to turn: "↑ Too low" / "↓ Too high" and "↑ A little low" / "↓ A little high".
- The colour: the tone (needle, lit string, word, active peg) is blue while far off, amber within 15 cents, green in tune, and in tune the whole screen glows softly green, readable without focusing. No red anywhere: out of tune is not an error.
- A row of round pegs, one per string in the tuning's order, where the strings end: the one being played glows with "♪ hear" under it, a string that held in tune for a moment gets a green fill and a tick, and once all have one the note's place shows a big green ✓ with "In tune. Play.", so a player always knows what is left. Tap a peg: it plays that string's tone and locks the tuner to it, with "Only the A string · Auto" under the row until Auto is tapped.
- A strum of the open strings puts ↑, ↓ or ✓ above every peg and "4 of 6 in tune" in the note's place with "A ↑  B ↓" as the word, by itself, no mode to switch on; a strummed fretted chord shows its name where the string's name is.
- Nothing to learn and no tour: "Listening…", the lit string and the pegs carry a first-time player through; a hint ("Tap a string to hear it") shows once, in place, under the pegs.
- Three tabs at the bottom: Tune · Metronome · Practice. The metronome is the second most used tool, so it is one tap, not two; Settings are set once, so they sit behind the gear.

**The other screens:**
- **What are you tuning?** A sheet from the chip: Guitar · Bass · Ukulele · Any note across the top, the instrument's tunings below with their notes (Standard E A D G B E, Drop D, Half step down, DADGAD, Open G, 12-string, "Make your own…"), the reference pitch named at the foot with a link to Settings. Instrument, tuning and chromatic in one place; the pegs on the tuner follow it.
- **Metronome:** the tempo fills the screen (a big number with − and + beside it, the tempo word under it, a ruler to drag), beat lights, meter (4/4 · 3/4 · 2/4 · 6/8) and sound (Click · Wood · Beep · Vibrate) as segmented choices, "👆 Tap the beat" and one big "▶ Start" side by side, the speed trainer a row with a switch ("+2 BPM every 4 bars"), not a screen.
- **Practice:** four big cards with a picture each, "Chords: every shape, left-handed too", "Scales: with a drone to play along", "Capo: which fret for the key you want", "Ear training: hear a note, play it back"; below them a row for the intonation check ("fret 12 against the open string") and a row with what was used last. Each card opens its tool full screen and comes back here; nothing hides behind a "more" button.
- **Settings:** behind the gear, nine rows in four groups: Tuning (reference pitch as a stepper, note names C D E / Do Re Mi, My tunings), While tuning (keep the screen on, vibrate when in tune, a short ding when in tune), Look (theme: phone / light / dark; the mock-up has no styles here, see the verdict above), Twangle (About: version, free, no ads, nothing leaves the phone).
- **The watch:** the top half of the phone screen and nothing else: the note, a dot along an arc, the word; the arc turns green and the watch taps the wrist when in tune. No pegs, tabs or settings; the watch takes the phone's tuning.
- **One look, dark by default** (stages, bedrooms, OLED), light when the phone is, one accent colour: as proposed, and rejected by the owner (the six styles stay). The screen layout above does not depend on it; every style can draw it.

**What stays as it is:** everything under the hood. YIN and the cents maths, tunings, A4, note names in every language, the reference tones, the drone, the metronome clock, the chord, scale and capo charts, Ear training's questions, no internet, no account, no ads.

**What each idea means in today's code**, estimated on 2026-11-01 (a day is one session with the phone plus the full check that checks it; every visible change also needs a night of `/translate`, new store screenshots where the bar or the tuner shows, and, before a release, a fresh Baseline Profile):
1. ☐ ★ **One "What are you tuning?" picker, Any note included:** the instrument and tuning panel exists (`InstrumentTuningPanel`, shared by the tuner and Practice); fold the tuner's separate mode switch (`ModeSwitch`) into it as the instrument "Any note". About 1 day, low risk. The wind players' instruments from *From the tuner comparison* above would fit the same sheet.
2. ☐ ★ **The navigation phase:** Settings behind a gear on every screen instead of a tab; the metronome as its own tab, so the bar reads Tune · Metronome · Practice (`PracticeTool` loses METRONOME, the `practiceTab` switch its metronome fallback); Practice as four big cards that open a tool full screen, in place of the tool button and "opens on the tool used last". The bar is in every store screenshot and about sixty UI test files navigate by it; the website's pages follow. 3 to 4 days together, medium risk, all UI. Open question for then: the cards cost the tap that "the tool used last" saves; the "last time" row on the cards screen gives it back.
3. ☐ ★ **No tour:** remove `FirstStartTour`, the tuner's `tour` package (1,300 lines, with `TourFlowTest` and `TourStylesTest`), Settings' "Show me around" row and the tour's texts from every language. Half a day, but last: today's first screen still has the tools button, the instrument button, focus mode and the mode switch that the tour explains, so take it out only once 1, 2 and 4 have made the screen explain itself.
4. ☐ ★ **Strum check and chord name by themselves:** today `PitchPipeline.listen` runs either the tuner or a strum tool, never both (`TunerTool`: one at a time, in the meter's place). Automatic means the strum detector running next to the pitch detector on every frame (CPU on older phones, hard rule 2 of CLAUDE.md), telling a strum from a single string without false alarms, the marks above the string row and the chord's name in the string's place, and the "more tools" button keeping only the intonation check and the pitch trail. 4 to 6 days plus the owner's guitar check: the riskiest of these, it touches the audio loop. DSP tests first (`StrumDetector` and `ChordDetector` against the recorded plucks and the synthetic strums), then the UI.
5. ☐ **The rest of the tuner screen,** not among the favourites, each a small phase of its own if the owner picks it: the strings as the backdrop with the played one lit; the word "Tighten / Loosen" with "a little" (and "Too low / Too high" in Any note); the tone's colour over the whole screen (blue, amber, green) and no red; "In tune. Play." once every string has its tick (Phase 65 ticks strings off already); the trail under the needle as part of every meter look (the pitch trail tool then goes); a peg that plays its string and locks to it in one tap.
6. ☐ **The other screens as drawn:** the metronome with the tempo as the screen and the trainer as a switch; Settings in four short groups; the watch's arc with the dot and the word. Each on the owner's pick, against what the screens are today.

## A tick guide (idea, 2026-11-07, from Phase 85)
The tuning guide as sound instead of vibration: short ticks at the vibration guide's rate (`VibrationGuide`), a low tick for too low (tighten), a high one for too high (loosen), a short chime in tune, played through `AudioTrack` like the metronome's click with each tick's frames and a short tail set aside (`ignoredFrames`), as the mock-up played them ([docs/design/tuning-guide-preview.html](docs/design/tuning-guide-preview.html)). Left out of Phase 85 (2026-11-07): what blind players say praises vibration, and noise is their main complaint about phone tuners; a tick through the speaker also puts sound into the very microphone that must hear the string. The model if it comes: the home-made clicking tuner on Hackaday, whose clicks quicken as the string nears pitch. **Condition:** only if players ask for a sound guide, or played only through earbuds or headphones, where the microphone can't hear it.

## Speech waits for the string to fade (idea, 2026-11-07, from Phase 85)
TalkBack says the reading only once the plucked string has died away, as Talking Tuner does, so the voice never talks over the note being tuned and the reading it says is the one the string ended on. Phase 81's pacing (one phrase per note, the voice's own frames set aside) covers most of it, and Phase 85's vibration guide says how far off while the string rings. **Condition:** only if blind players say the speech talks over the string.

## Hausa (idea, 2026-11-08, from Phase 87)
Twangle in Hausa (`ha`), for Nigeria and Niger. Left out of Phase 87 (2026-11-08): Android offers it as a phone language (`ha-GH`, `ha-NE`, `ha-NG`), but Google Play has no store listing language for it (Play Console's 86 listing languages, https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9844778, go from Gujarati to Hebrew), so players would find an app in Hausa only behind an English listing, and `StoreListingTest` wants a listing for every language. **Condition:** when Play offers a Hausa store listing; then one `/add-language` like Swahili's (its Latin letters with ɓ ɗ ƙ: check Nunito has them or that they come from the phone's font).

## Chords by key: the circle of fifths (idea, 2026-11-09)
The circle of fifths as a **Key** side of Chords' picker: a tap on a key shows its six chords in a row above the chart (G major: G C D Em Am Bm), major keys on the outer ring and minor keys on the inner one, no Roman numerals or theory words, and a row on to Scales in that key with the drone. Mock-up with the owner's picks: [docs/design/circle-of-fifths-preview.html](docs/design/circle-of-fifths-preview.html). It was Phase 92 for an hour on 2026-11-09; the owner moved it here the same day: it answers a theory question few players ask (beginners play from chord sheets, experienced players know the common keys by heart, the songwriters in between have free circle-of-fifths apps), every Chords user would see a Key switch to puzzle over, and its upkeep (36 languages, TalkBack, every instrument, left-handed charts) lasts as long as the app. **Condition:** players or teachers ask which chords go together in a key, or for help moving a song to another key. Then:
1. ☐ **The circle in Chords**, as the mock-up drew it and with its picks (inside Chords under Chords' switch, six chords, minor keys from the inner ring, the row on to Scales)
2. ☐ **Hearing a key's chords:** a play button by each chord of the row that strums its chart with the reference tones' string sound; only if a test strum sounds like a guitar to the owner, not six reference tones at once
