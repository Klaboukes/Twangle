Phase 29 · rough mock-up · round 1

Owner's pick, 2026-10-16: option A, with the small icons (strum check, 12th-fret check and later tools) gathered behind one "more options" (^) button, so the tuner doesn't fill up with icons. Millimetres only where their accuracy can be guaranteed.

Intonation check

Play a string open, then the same string pressed at the 12th fret. The 12th fret should sound exactly one octave higher. If it's sharp, that string's saddle moves back (away from the neck); if it's flat, forward (towards the neck). Three ways to fit it into Twangle are below, each as a few phone screens in the default style. Pick A, B or C (or mix parts of them).

What it says (the same in every option)

Short words, calm colours: green only for "fine", the accent colour for a saddle to move, never red.

A · A switch beside the strum check recommended

Works like the strum check: a second small switch on the same row turns it on, a panel takes the meter's place and a line takes the note's place. You choose the string on the string row you already know.

Next to the other modes

  • Strings and Chromatic stay as they are. The row above the strings gets one more small icon: "Guitar · Standard … [12th fret] [strum]".
  • Only one of the two switches can be on. Chromatic turns it off, as it does the strum check.
  • With the switch off, nothing on the tuner changes. Release builds don't show the icon.

Good

  • Nothing new to learn: the same pattern as the strum check.
  • The results stay on the string row (✓, ← or → under each string), so you see what's left.
  • Two icon switches, never a crowded row of buttons.

Watch out

  • An icon without words: found by its long-press name and a tour step, like the strum check.
  • A long tuning name has a little less room on that row.

B · A third mode: Strings · Chromatic · Intonation

A third button in the mode switch at the top. The meter's place shows a short checklist of the steps, then a table of all six strings.

Next to the other modes

  • The mode switch at the top gets a third part; the strum check's switch hides in this mode.
  • Release builds show the two parts they show today.

Good

  • Easy to find: it says "Intonation" in words.
  • The checklist spells out each step; the table gives every string's number.

Watch out

  • Each part of the switch gets a third of the width: Dutch "Chromatisch" and longer languages shrink more.
  • A check you do now and then gets the same weight as tuning, which you do every day.

C · Its own screen, from the Instrument panel

Nothing changes on the tuner. The Instrument panel (bottom bar) gets a last row, "Check intonation", which opens a screen of its own with a drawing of the bridge: the arrows show which way each saddle moves.

Next to the other modes

  • The tuner screen stays exactly as it is; the row sits under the tunings, after "New tuning".
  • Its screen has its own close button (like the tuning editor) and no bottom bar.

Good

  • The bridge drawing shows where each saddle goes, at a glance.
  • The tuner stays the calmest of the three.

Watch out

  • Hardest to find: tucked away in a panel about tunings.
  • One more screen to build and test; a bridge drawing per instrument (4 to 8 strings, ukulele, bass).

Under the hood (every option)

Each step waits until the note is steady, then takes the middle value of about a second of readings, so the pluck and the dying end don't count. The open note must be near the chosen string, or Twangle asks to tune it first. Within 3 cents (about 1 mm of saddle) counts as fine. More than half a semitone off is "not the octave". A light touch at the 12th fret (a harmonic) always sounds perfect, so the tip asks for a pressed note.

The millimetres in the verdict ("about 3 mm") assume a guitar's usual scale length; they could be left out.
