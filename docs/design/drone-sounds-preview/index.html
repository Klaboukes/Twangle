Drone sounds, round 2

Four quite different sounds for the steady low note the Scales tool will play under a scale. Each plays the root (D) alone, with its fifth, with its fifth and octave, a fifth lower, and under a scale on the guitar, then stops. Listen on the phone's speaker and in headphones: which one (or which two) should the app have, and should the fifth and the octave be offered?

Tanpura plucked, buzzing

The Indian drone instrument: four strings plucked one after another, over and over, each with a bright buzz that blooms a moment after the pluck and slowly sinks. Lively and rhythmic, never the same twice. Its fifth is the traditional one, below the root.

Harmonium reedy, breathing

A hand-pumped reed box (a shruti box), as singers use: bright and nasal, two reeds a note that shimmer against each other, and the bellows swelling in and out. Cuts through well on the phone's speaker.

Flute organ pure, still

One soft, hollow organ pipe, almost a pure tone: round and completely still, the calmest of the four. A note played out of tune over it is easy to hear as a slow wobble. Very quiet on the phone's speaker, best in headphones.

Warm pad soft, wide, slowly opening

A soft synthesizer pad: several voices a little apart, so it sounds wide and smooth, swelling in slowly and opening and closing like a slow "wah" every few seconds.

All twenty are about equally loud by ear (in headphones). In the app the drone keeps sounding for as long as you like; these samples are 8 seconds (the scale ones 12.5) and fade out.

  • All four are in tune: on average the tuner's own pitch detector reads the root within 0.4 cents.
  • The fifth is tuned pure (a hair, 2 cents, above a piano's), so the drone itself never wobbles.
  • On the phone's speaker the flute organ almost disappears and the pad is softer: most of their sound is too low for a small speaker. The tanpura and the harmonium carry well.
  • The guitar is synthetic (the app's own reference tones), so judge the drone, not the guitar.
