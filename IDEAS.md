# Twangle — Ideas for after v1.0

Not planned yet: ideas the owner wants written down, to pick up after launch only if their condition is met. Each becomes a numbered phase in [ROADMAP.md](ROADMAP.md) once it's chosen, and leaves this file. The music tools became Phases 28–34 on 2026-10-16.

## Custom themes (idea, 2026-10-16)
Let people make their own look on top of the six styles. **Condition:** start only if several closed-test testers or early users ask to change the look. Rules that stay: no internet, no account, readable by the same rule as the six palettes (`StylePaletteTest`), calm by default. The six built-in backgrounds stay drawn in code (they fit every screen, have light, dark and quiet versions, and weigh almost nothing).
1. ☐ **Your own background photo with matching colours:** a "Make your own" card at the end of the style choices; the photo comes from Android's photo picker (no permission), is copied into the app's own storage, shrunk to the screen once, dimmed or softly blurred under a veil of the theme's colour (stronger behind Settings) so the needle and note stay readable; the theme's colours are taken from the photo, like Material You does from the wallpaper. The widget and watch keep their built-in look.
2. ☐ **A small theme builder** around that card's live preview: background (a built-in picture or your photo), main colour, lettering, meter look, light / dark / follow the phone. Twangle works out the other colours and keeps them readable; no editing of every colour one by one.
3. ☐ **On the shelf, only if 1 and 2 prove popular:** a theme code (a short text to save and share themes; a theme with a photo needs a small file instead) and a round trip with the user's own AI app: Twangle shares instructions through Android's share menu, the user chats with any AI, copies the code it gives, and Twangle offers to preview it from the clipboard. Never a live connection (that would need internet and an account).

## A drone under the scales (idea, 2026-10-26)
A steady low note, the key's root (alone, with its fifth, or with its octave), that keeps sounding while the player plays over it. It lets you hear how each note of a scale sits against the key, which is how players practise intonation (violin, cello, fretless bass, singing) and how a mode's sound is learned: D Dorian over a D drone sounds unlike C major over a C drone, though the notes are the same. It goes with Phase 49's modes. **Claude's view:** not a tool of its own but a play button on the Scales tool that sounds the scale's root, so it follows the root and key picked there; not in the tuner, whose microphone would hear it.
1. ☐ **A sustained voice in `:core:dsp`:** made in the app like the reference tones (no recordings), e.g. the bowed cello voice the tuner already has, or a soft organ or tanpura-like hum, chosen by **the owner** from short samples (each ends by itself, nothing loops). Rendered on and on, not as a looped piece, so it never clicks or drifts; a choice of root, root and fifth, root and octave; allocation-free; tests that it stays on pitch (±1 cent), never clips and has no seam
2. ☐ **The button on Scales:** ▶ starts the drone, ■ stops it; it follows a new root at once (a short fade), stops when the screen goes (as the metronome, no foreground service) and keeps the media volume. A rough mock-up first; TalkBack, every language
3. ☐ **On the shelf:** the drone and the metronome together, which needs both on one screen (leaving the metronome stops its clicks today)

## Ear training (idea, 2026-10-26)
Short rounds of questions that train the ear, as a Practice tool of its own. What only Twangle can do among the free tuners: it already hears, so the player can answer on the instrument, not just with buttons. Rules that stay: no account, scores kept on the phone only (hard rules 7 and 9), calm (no streaks, no nagging). **Claude's view:** worth it, but the largest of the practice ideas: it needs the microphone, which `:feature:practice` doesn't use by design, so a module of its own that holds the mic only while it is shown (hard rule 4), and a way to stop the tuner hearing the app's own question tone from the speaker (listen only after it has faded). A rough mock-up first; each exercise one increment:
1. ☐ **Play it back:** Twangle plays a note in the instrument's voice, the player finds it on the instrument, and the tuner says right (the same note in any octave, or the exact one) or sings it again; later two to four notes in a row
2. ☐ **Intervals:** two notes up, down or together; the player picks the interval (minor second to octave) from buttons
3. ☐ **Chord quality:** a chord strummed in the string voice (major, minor, 7, maj7, sus, dim, …) from `ChordShapeFinder`'s shapes; the player picks its type
4. ☐ **On the shelf:** a scale or mode by ear (after the drone and Phase 49's modes), and a short score per round kept on the phone
