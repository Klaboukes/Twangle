## Phase 46 — Stiff strings: the tuner's last few cents (the accuracy review of 2026-10-24)
Goal: the tuner and the 12th-fret check read a real, stiff string as well as they read a synthetic one. A real string is stiff, so its overtones sit a little sharp of exact multiples of the fundamental (inharmonicity, Fletcher 1964: partial k at `k·f₀·√(1 + B·k²)`), and YIN, which uses the whole waveform, is pulled sharp by them. Modelled on 2026-10-24 with `StrumChords.stiffStrum` and the stiffness the strum tests already assume (B = 1.5·10⁻⁴ for the low E, down to 2·10⁻⁵ for the high E), what the needle shows after a hard pick attack on the low E: **+8.5 cents at 0.25 s, +3.6 at 0.5 s, +1.0 at 1 s, +0.5 at 1.5 s**, sliding down as the overtones die away (the same pluck without stiffness: −0.2 to 0.0; a finger pluck, partials at 1/k², stays under 0.5 throughout). A note at the 12th fret has half the string's length and so four times its B, so the 12th-fret check, which takes 0.25–1.25 s of each note, would report a well set-up guitar played with a pick as **+4.3 cents on the low E** ("move the saddle back 1.5 mm"), +3.2 on the A and +3.1 on the G, all past its 3-cent tolerance; with a finger, under 1 cent on every string. The strum check and the chord name are tested with stiff strings (`StrumAccuracyReport`, `ChordAccuracyReport`); the tuner isn't. The owner's live checks passed, so this matters most for players with a pick and for the Precise setting (2 cents). Everything here is `:core:dsp` (hard rules 1, 2 and 6); nothing changes on screen. In order:
- ☑ **Stiff-string tests first:** `EdgeCaseTest` cases from `StrumChords.stiffStrum`'s model for every string of the standard tuning at the strum tests' B values and at three times them, plucked with a pick (partials at 1/k) and a finger (1/k²): the shown reading 0.5 s and 1 s after the pluck within 1 cent of the fundamental (`f₀·√(1 + B)`, as `StrumDetectorTest` defines it); and `IntonationTest` with the fretted note at 4× the open string's B: `FINE` on every string, the offset within the 3-cent tolerance. They defined done for the next item. Done 2026-10-28, landed with the next item: before it, the low E picked hard read +3.5 at 0.5 s (+7.6 at three times the stiffness), and the 12th-fret check gave it +3.4, "move the saddle back"
- ☑ **The refinement on the lowest partials:** after YIN has chosen the period (its threshold, its whole-period checks and its above-range check stay exactly as they are, so the octave decisions don't change), the sub-sample refinement (step 5) runs again on the frame limited to the lowest three or four partials of that note: a low-pass at about 3.5 × f₀ (a Butterworth's start-up transient is long at such a cutoff, so likely through the frequency domain instead, since `FftDifference` already holds the frame's spectrum: the band-limited cross-correlation's peak near τ, refined with the same cubic fit). Measured in this review: limiting E4 to three partials took its pick-attack bias from +2.5 to +0.5 cents at B = 1.5·10⁻⁴, with the 20 dB SNR precision still under 0.5 cent. Allocation-free; every existing accuracy test still within its limit (sines ≈ 0.1 cent, 20 dB SNR under 1, 10 dB under 5, no octave errors, every sample rate, the low range); the stiff-string tests pass; `YinBenchmark` and `scripts/dsp-phone-benchmark.sh` for the cost per frame against [docs/architecture/performance.md](docs/architecture/performance.md); the YIN section of [docs/architecture/signal-pipeline.md](docs/architecture/signal-pipeline.md) and the Intonation section of [docs/architecture/notes-and-audio.md](docs/architecture/notes-and-audio.md) say how the refinement works and lose their stiff-string known limits. Done 2026-10-28: gains 1 up to 2·f₀, 0 from 3.5·f₀, applied to the spectra `FftDifference` keeps (the window band-limited, then tapered smoothly, so neither the filter nor the window's ends move a partial), at the ~100 lags around the dip. It counts in full once the note rings on, less while the window holds the attack, and not where the band has no clean dip of its own (a chord, mains hum under a low string). A hard pick on the low E now reads +0.2 cents at 0.25, 0.5 and 1 s, at three times the stiffness +0.6, every string within 0.7. The 12th-fret check reads at most +1.0 picked (the low E) and 0.5 with a finger. Sines stay within 0.13 cents and 20 dB SNR within 0.65 (single frames, 62–1500 Hz). Cost: Xiaomi 12X chain 0.33 → 0.37 ms a frame, low range 0.76 → 0.83. The drone tests now measure the drone with the whole waveform (`refineOnLowestPartials = false`), because a few partials of the harmonium's two reeds follow their beating and read about 1 cent flat
- ◐ **Real recordings in the tests:** every accuracy figure so far rests on synthetic sound (`core/dsp/src/test/resources/fixtures/` is empty). First a recorder for the test phone that is not Twangle (an instrumented test in `:core:testing` run by `scripts/record-plucks.sh`, writing 16-bit mono WAV files to the phone's storage and pulling them with adb), so hard rule 7 stands untouched. Then **the owner** (Claude says when the script is ready) plays each open string of a real guitar a few times in front of the Xiaomi, hard with a pick and softly with a finger, and in the same session a few plucks in a noisy room (talk, a TV on) for the next item, and the files go into the fixtures folder (a few MB, in git). A test runs them through `TunerChain`: the right note from the first shown frame, no octave flips, and the reading 0.5 s after the pluck within 2 cents of the reading 2 s in (the settled fundamental); `RealPluckReport` (print-only, with the benchmarks) prints each pluck's trajectory for the docs. The *Accuracy target* line of the signal-pipeline doc gets the measured figure in place of the ±3 cents target, and the pick and finger partial models in the tests are checked against what the recordings show. **Recorder ready (2026-10-28):** `scripts/record-plucks.sh` (`PluckRecorder` in `:core:testing`'s on-device tests, through the tuner's own recorder; [testing.md](docs/architecture/testing.md), *Real recordings*), checked end to end on the Xiaomi with `--test` (48 kHz, unprocessed). Next: **the owner**'s recording session (*Waiting on the owner*), then the `TunerChain` test and `RealPluckReport`
- ☐ **Quick and steady on real strings** (from Phase 51, moved here on 2026-10-26: it uses the same recordings and the same session with the owner, and it answers the most common complaint about the big tuners, a needle slow to catch the note or stuck): from the recordings, measure for each pluck how long until the first note shows and until the needle settles, and that the noisy-room plucks read the right note without a wandering needle. A test holds the measured figures; where a figure is worse than the synthetic one, fix it in `:core:dsp` first (hard rules 1, 2, 6), without undoing the no-flicker rules (the steady hint, the sticky string, the in-tune hold), which are part of why Twangle feels calm. The figures go into the *Accuracy target* part of [docs/architecture/signal-pipeline.md](docs/architecture/signal-pipeline.md)
- ☑ **Mains hum notch for the low range** (verified for real only with the bass from *Owner checks*, Phase 15; until then synthetic): in `PitchRange.LOW` alone, where 50 and 60 Hz are inside the range (today hum 10 dB below a B0 string pulls it up to 8 cents, 2–3 cents from E1 up, and hum alone shows as G1 or B♭1), narrow notches at 50 and 60 Hz before YIN, and at their 2nd and 3rd harmonics only where the measurements show they are needed, each so narrow that the nearest notes (G1 49.0 Hz, B1 61.7, G2 98.0, B2 123.5) stay within 0.1 cent and a semitone-flat string still reads. `LowPitchRangeTest`: hum 6 dB below every low string within 1 cent, hum alone nothing, every existing low-range figure kept; the *Low notes* and *Edge cases* sections of the signal-pipeline doc updated; the guitar's standard range is left as it is (60 Hz already lies below it). Done 2026-10-28: adaptive notches (Widrow's canceller, 1 Hz wide) at 50, 60 Hz and their harmonics up to the 6th (with three, the test hum's 4th–6th harmonics still took strings an octave off), which learn only between notes and hold their estimate while a note sounds, since fixed notches narrow enough for G1 rang on a pluck beside them and moved G1, B1, G2 and B2 by 3 to 20 cents in a quiet room; they follow mains up to ±0.25 Hz off nominal, and a pitch within 0.5 Hz of 50 or 60 Hz is no note. Hum 6 dB under every low string, in tune or a semitone flat: within 1 cent from the first frame (before: up to two octaves off or nothing); hum alone: nothing, from the moment the mic opens; quiet room: G1, B♭1, B1, G2, B2 within 0.011 cents of before; a note already ringing as the low range opens reads as before too (only estimates that turn like hum are taken out). Cost on the Xiaomi 12X: low range 0.83 → 0.91 ms a frame, the standard range untouched. Known limit: it needs to hear the hum for a second or two before the note

## Phase 50 — Getting found, before launch (the growth plan of 2026-10-25)
Goal: guitarists who already have a tuner find Twangle and switch to it. Nearly everyone finds a tuner by searching Play for "guitar tuner", and nobody looks for a new one while the old one works, so the plan is to rank in Play search (by rating, number of reviews, installs and how many keep the app) and to be there when players are fed up with ads, paid tunings, subscriptions and needing the internet (the most common complaints in the big tuners' reviews, comparison of 2026-10-25). The message everywhere: every feature free, no ads, works offline, nothing leaves the phone. No paid advertising (it never pays back, and doesn't fit *free forever*), no buying or swapping reviews and no rewards for a rating (Google removes apps for that), no other app named in the listing or criticised by name in posts. Nothing in the app measures anything (hard rule 9): Play Console's own reports (installs and uninstalls per country, the search terms that brought visitors, store-listing conversion, ratings) and `scripts/release.sh health` and `reviews` are enough. Claude writes the texts, in every language where it helps; **the owner** posts and sends them under their own name, and does the Play Console steps. This phase is the part that must be ready before launch (owner, 2026-10-25: settled by the time v1.0 goes to production); the rest follows launch in Phase 56. In order:

## Phase 58 — Open the code after v1.0
Goal: the Twangle repository goes public as open source, once v1.0 is live on Play. The licence is the **GPL version 3** (owner, 2026-10-17): anyone may copy, change and share the code, but what they build from it stays open under the same licence, so nobody can turn Twangle into a closed app with ads. The bundled fonts keep their own SIL Open Font License, and the AndroidX and Kotlin libraries' Apache 2.0 fits inside GPL 3.
- ☐ `LICENSE` with the full GPL 3 text, and a *Licence* section in README.md naming it
- ☐ README.md says the name "Twangle" and its icon are not covered by the licence, so copies must use their own name and icon
- ☐ Before opening: check the history for anything that shouldn't be public (the owner's personal email in old commits, `local.properties`, keys); the owner decides whether to keep the history or start the public repository fresh
- ☐ The owner makes the repository public; later commits use the GitHub no-reply email
- ☐ While the owner is the only author, the licence can still change; once others contribute, it can't without them, so settle any change before accepting outside code

## Phase 59 — Chord charts and scales for the banjo (owner, 2026-10-27)
Goal: the 5-string banjo from Phase 57 gets the Practice tools the guitar has, so a beginner can tune and then learn the first chords (G, C, D7) without leaving Twangle. Today Chords, Scales and the Capo helper show a quiet line in their place for it (`ShortStringLine`, gated on `Tuning.hasShortString`), because the charts and the shape finder treat every string as starting at the nut, and on a guitar a string outside the chord is muted. The banjo's short fifth string starts at the 5th fret, and players let it ring as a drone. Visible changes land with their UI tests, every language where words change, both hands (Phase 57's left-handed switch), and the Practice doc. In order, Capo last:
- ☐ **Rough mock-up first** (Contemporary, one row per idea), for **the owner** to pick: a banjo chord chart (G, C, D7) with the short string drawn starting at the 5th fret, open as a drone or fretted above it; whether the open short string always rings, or only where its note belongs to the chord (and is otherwise fretted or marked unplayed); and the Scales neck with the short string
- ☐ **The shape finder knows the short string:** in `:core:dsp`, a tuning's strings can start at a fret (the banjo's fifth at 5), so no shape frets that string below its start; the drone rule the owner picked decides when it rings open. `ChordShapeFinderTest`'s every-tuning checks stop leaving out `hasShortString` tunings (every shape sounds the chord on open G, double C and double D, within the hand's reach), plus banjo cases: the common first shapes come out first (G open, C, D7)
- ☐ **Chord charts for the banjo:** `ChordChart` draws the short string from its own start fret (mirrored with the left-handed switch), Chords offers the banjo and its line goes; the chord's sound plays its five strings in the banjo voice. `ChordsScreenTest` with the banjo, both hands, TalkBack naming the short string; every language at 2× text
- ☐ **Scales for the banjo:** `ScaleNeck` (upright and on its side) draws the short string from the 5th fret, with no notes below it; Scales offers the banjo and its line goes. `ScalesScreenTest` with the banjo, both hands
- ☐ **Capo, only if players ask:** on a banjo the short string needs its own capo (often small spikes in the neck), so the helper's sums differ; until then Capo keeps its quiet line for the banjo, and this item moves to [IDEAS.md](IDEAS.md) with that condition if Phase 59 is otherwise done
