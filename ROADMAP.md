## Phase 46 — Stiff strings: the tuner's last few cents (the accuracy review of 2026-10-24)
Goal: the tuner and the 12th-fret check read a real, stiff string as well as they read a synthetic one. A real string is stiff, so its overtones sit a little sharp of exact multiples of the fundamental (inharmonicity, Fletcher 1964: partial k at `k·f₀·√(1 + B·k²)`), and YIN, which uses the whole waveform, is pulled sharp by them. Modelled on 2026-10-24 with `StrumChords.stiffStrum` and the stiffness the strum tests already assume (B = 1.5·10⁻⁴ for the low E, down to 2·10⁻⁵ for the high E), what the needle shows after a hard pick attack on the low E: **+8.5 cents at 0.25 s, +3.6 at 0.5 s, +1.0 at 1 s, +0.5 at 1.5 s**, sliding down as the overtones die away (the same pluck without stiffness: −0.2 to 0.0; a finger pluck, partials at 1/k², stays under 0.5 throughout). A note at the 12th fret has half the string's length and so four times its B, so the 12th-fret check, which takes 0.25–1.25 s of each note, would report a well set-up guitar played with a pick as **+4.3 cents on the low E** ("move the saddle back 1.5 mm"), +3.2 on the A and +3.1 on the G, all past its 3-cent tolerance; with a finger, under 1 cent on every string. The strum check and the chord name are tested with stiff strings (`StrumAccuracyReport`, `ChordAccuracyReport`); the tuner isn't. The owner's live checks passed, so this matters most for players with a pick and for the Precise setting (2 cents). Everything here is `:core:dsp` (hard rules 1, 2 and 6); nothing changes on screen. In order:
- ☐ **Stiff-string tests first:** `EdgeCaseTest` cases from `StrumChords.stiffStrum`'s model for every string of the standard tuning at the strum tests' B values and at three times them, plucked with a pick (partials at 1/k) and a finger (1/k²): the shown reading 0.5 s and 1 s after the pluck within 1 cent of the fundamental (`f₀·√(1 + B)`, as `StrumDetectorTest` defines it); and `IntonationTest` with the fretted note at 4× the open string's B: `FINE` on every string, the offset within the 3-cent tolerance. They fail today (the low E reads +3.6 at 0.5 s); they define done for the next item
- ☐ **The refinement on the lowest partials:** after YIN has chosen the period (its threshold, its whole-period checks and its above-range check stay exactly as they are, so the octave decisions don't change), the sub-sample refinement (step 5) runs again on the frame limited to the lowest three or four partials of that note: a low-pass at about 3.5 × f₀ (a Butterworth's start-up transient is long at such a cutoff, so likely through the frequency domain instead, since `FftDifference` already holds the frame's spectrum: the band-limited cross-correlation's peak near τ, refined with the same cubic fit). Measured in this review: limiting E4 to three partials took its pick-attack bias from +2.5 to +0.5 cents at B = 1.5·10⁻⁴, with the 20 dB SNR precision still under 0.5 cent. Allocation-free; every existing accuracy test still within its limit (sines ≈ 0.1 cent, 20 dB SNR under 1, 10 dB under 5, no octave errors, every sample rate, the low range); the stiff-string tests pass; `YinBenchmark` and `scripts/dsp-phone-benchmark.sh` for the cost per frame against [docs/architecture/performance.md](docs/architecture/performance.md); the YIN section of [docs/architecture/signal-pipeline.md](docs/architecture/signal-pipeline.md) and the Intonation section of [docs/architecture/notes-and-audio.md](docs/architecture/notes-and-audio.md) say how the refinement works and lose their stiff-string known limits
- ☐ **Real recordings in the tests:** every accuracy figure so far rests on synthetic sound (`core/dsp/src/test/resources/fixtures/` is empty). First a recorder for the test phone that is not Twangle (an instrumented test in `:core:testing` run by `scripts/record-plucks.sh`, writing 16-bit mono WAV files to the phone's storage and pulling them with adb), so hard rule 7 stands untouched. Then **the owner** (Claude says when the script is ready) plays each open string of a real guitar a few times in front of the Xiaomi, hard with a pick and softly with a finger, and in the same session a few plucks in a noisy room (talk, a TV on) for the next item, and the files go into the fixtures folder (a few MB, in git). A test runs them through `TunerChain`: the right note from the first shown frame, no octave flips, and the reading 0.5 s after the pluck within 2 cents of the reading 2 s in (the settled fundamental); `RealPluckReport` (print-only, with the benchmarks) prints each pluck's trajectory for the docs. The *Accuracy target* line of the signal-pipeline doc gets the measured figure in place of the ±3 cents target, and the pick and finger partial models in the tests are checked against what the recordings show
- ☐ **Quick and steady on real strings** (from Phase 51, moved here on 2026-10-26: it uses the same recordings and the same session with the owner, and it answers the most common complaint about the big tuners, a needle slow to catch the note or stuck): from the recordings, measure for each pluck how long until the first note shows and until the needle settles, and that the noisy-room plucks read the right note without a wandering needle. A test holds the measured figures; where a figure is worse than the synthetic one, fix it in `:core:dsp` first (hard rules 1, 2, 6), without undoing the no-flicker rules (the steady hint, the sticky string, the in-tune hold), which are part of why Twangle feels calm. The figures go into the *Accuracy target* part of [docs/architecture/signal-pipeline.md](docs/architecture/signal-pipeline.md)
- ☐ **Mains hum notch for the low range** (verified for real only with the bass from *Owner checks*, Phase 15; until then synthetic): in `PitchRange.LOW` alone, where 50 and 60 Hz are inside the range (today hum 10 dB below a B0 string pulls it up to 8 cents, 2–3 cents from E1 up, and hum alone shows as G1 or B♭1), narrow notches at 50 and 60 Hz before YIN, and at their 2nd and 3rd harmonics only where the measurements show they are needed, each so narrow that the nearest notes (G1 49.0 Hz, B1 61.7, G2 98.0, B2 123.5) stay within 0.1 cent and a semitone-flat string still reads. `LowPitchRangeTest`: hum 6 dB below every low string within 1 cent, hum alone nothing, every existing low-range figure kept; the *Low notes* and *Edge cases* sections of the signal-pipeline doc updated; the guitar's standard range is left as it is (60 Hz already lies below it)
- ☐ **Owner check added** under *Owner checks*: with a real guitar and a pick, play each string hard: the needle lands within a cent of where it settles within half a second, instead of sliding down from several cents sharp; run the 12th-fret check on a guitar a shop has set up: every string `FINE`

## Phase 50 — Getting found, before launch (the growth plan of 2026-10-25)
Goal: guitarists who already have a tuner find Twangle and switch to it. Nearly everyone finds a tuner by searching Play for "guitar tuner", and nobody looks for a new one while the old one works, so the plan is to rank in Play search (by rating, number of reviews, installs and how many keep the app) and to be there when players are fed up with ads, paid tunings, subscriptions and needing the internet (the most common complaints in the big tuners' reviews, comparison of 2026-10-25). The message everywhere: every feature free, no ads, works offline, nothing leaves the phone. No paid advertising (it never pays back, and doesn't fit *free forever*), no buying or swapping reviews and no rewards for a rating (Google removes apps for that), no other app named in the listing or criticised by name in posts. Nothing in the app measures anything (hard rule 9): Play Console's own reports (installs and uninstalls per country, the search terms that brought visitors, store-listing conversion, ratings) and `scripts/release.sh health` and `reviews` are enough. Claude writes the texts, in every language where it helps; **the owner** posts and sends them under their own name, and does the Play Console steps. This phase is the part that must be ready before launch (owner, 2026-10-25: ahead of Phase 45 and Phase 51, so it is settled by the time v1.0 goes to production); the rest follows launch in Phase 55. In order:
- ☐ **Christmas in mind:** many guitars are gifts, so late December into January is very likely the busiest time for tuner searches (Claude checks it in Google Trends for a few languages and notes the figures in [docs/store-listing.md](docs/store-listing.md)). Aim: Twangle in production by early December, with a few hundred ratings by Christmas; if Phases 46 to 49 and 51 put that date at risk, Claude says so, so the owner can send v1.0 to production first
- ☐ **Ratings at a good moment, the owner's choice:** today a calm "Rate Twangle" row in About opens the Play page, and the store-listing plan (Phase 38) said "no pop-up asking for a rating". Ratings weigh most in Play search, so Claude makes a rough mock-up of the alternative for the owner: Google's own rating window (Play's In-App Review), shown at most once, right after every string of the tuning has been in tune in one go (never while tuning, never on the first day), with no question before it (Google forbids filtering out the unhappy). Only if the owner picks it: first check that Google's library adds no permission (the merged manifest: no `INTERNET`, hard rule 7) and sends nothing of Twangle's own; the version in `gradle/libs.versions.toml`; behind an interface with a fake for tests (`TunerViewModel` tests for when it may show); [PRIVACY.md](PRIVACY.md) and its Dutch copy say the window is Google's; the Ratings line of [docs/store-listing.md](docs/store-listing.md) updated. Either way, the closed testers are asked to rate Twangle once it is public
- ☐ **The store listing leads with what sets Twangle apart:** the first screenshot shows the tuner alone, calm (one string, in tune, no tools open), and the short description and the first lines of the long one say: free, no ads, no account, no internet permission, so nothing leaves the phone. In all 16 languages ([docs/store-listing.md](docs/store-listing.md), `scripts/store-screenshots.sh`); sent with `scripts/release.sh listing` after **the owner**'s yes
- ☐ **Ask Google to feature it:** **the owner** sends Play Console's featuring nomination at launch (Claude writes the text: free in 16 languages, no internet permission, TalkBack, the listening tools), and again with each bigger update (the watch app, Phase 57's instruments)

## Phase 54 — Ear training (owner, 2026-10-26)
Goal: a Practice tool of its own with short rounds of questions that train the ear. What only Twangle can do among the free tuners: it already hears, so the player can answer on the instrument, not just with buttons. Rules that stay: no account, scores kept on the phone only (hard rules 7 and 9), calm (no streaks, no reminders, no nagging; right and wrong shown as gently as the tuner's in-tune effect). It needs the microphone, which `:feature:practice` doesn't use by design, so it is a module of its own that holds the mic only while it is shown (hard rule 4). Built behind its own switch in `ToolSwitches`, hidden in release builds until the owner's check passes (the music tools' rule). Visible changes land with their UI tests, every language and every style. In order:
- ☐ **Rough mock-up first** (Contemporary), for **the owner** to pick: picking an exercise, one question (the ▶ to hear it again, the answer buttons or "Play it on your instrument" with the note the tuner hears), right and wrong, the end of a round (10 questions) with its score, and where the difficulty is set (e.g. which intervals or chord types are in the round)
- ☐ **The questions in `:core:dsp`:** pure and seeded, so tests can repeat a round: a note within the instrument's range for its tuning, an interval (minor second to octave, up, down or together), a chord type from the Chords tool's sixteen; and the answer checks (the same note in any octave or the exact one, by the tuner's own note maths). `EarTrainingTest`
- ☐ **The tool and its module:** `:feature:eartraining` (the CLAUDE.md modules table and ARCHITECTURE.md updated), `PracticeTool.EAR_TRAINING` in Phase 47's tool list, the microphone asked for as the tuner does and held only while the tool is shown (`WhileSubscribed`, the tuner's pipeline reused, `UNPROCESSED` as hard rule 5 says). The question's sound plays in the instrument's voice through `TonePlayer`, and listening waits until it has faded, so the speaker's sound is never taken for the answer. Tests with a fake recorder and a fake player
- ☐ **Play it back:** Twangle plays a note, the player finds it on the instrument and holds it; the tuner says right once the note is steady (the strings mode's lock rules, no flicker), or plays the question again; then two to four notes in a row
- ☐ **Intervals:** two notes, the player picks the interval from buttons (steady sizes, the names in every language, TalkBack)
- ☐ **Chord type:** a chord strummed in the string voice from `ChordShapeFinder`'s shapes for the tuning picked; the player picks its type, from a few at first (major, minor, 7) up to all sixteen
- ☐ **Scores on the phone:** each exercise's last rounds, kept in `SettingsRepository`; [PRIVACY.md](PRIVACY.md) and its Dutch copy name them among the saved settings
- ☐ **Owner check** added under *Owner checks*: with a real guitar, a few rounds of each exercise from the speaker and in headphones: Play it back hears right and wrong notes reliably and never takes the question for the answer, the sounds are clear, and nothing flickers
- ☐ **Live:** after the owner's check, the switch on in release builds too

## Phase 56 — Open the code after v1.0
Goal: the Twangle repository goes public as open source, once v1.0 is live on Play. The licence is the **GPL version 3** (owner, 2026-10-17): anyone may copy, change and share the code, but what they build from it stays open under the same licence, so nobody can turn Twangle into a closed app with ads. The bundled fonts keep their own SIL Open Font License, and the AndroidX and Kotlin libraries' Apache 2.0 fits inside GPL 3.
- ☐ `LICENSE` with the full GPL 3 text, and a *Licence* section in README.md naming it
- ☐ README.md says the name "Twangle" and its icon are not covered by the licence, so copies must use their own name and icon
- ☐ Before opening: check the history for anything that shouldn't be public (the owner's personal email in old commits, `local.properties`, keys); the owner decides whether to keep the history or start the public repository fresh
- ☐ The owner makes the repository public; later commits use the GitHub no-reply email
- ☐ While the owner is the only author, the licence can still change; once others contribute, it can't without them, so settle any change before accepting outside code

## Phase 57 — The 12-string, the banjo and left-handed charts (after launch)
Goal: the instruments and the mirrored charts the comparison of 2026-10-25 found missing, as the owner's review of 2026-10-26 narrowed them (Phase 51's goal tells the story). After launch (Phase 53), when reviews or the closed testers ask for them, in the order they ask; it doesn't wait for Phases 55 and 56, which are mostly the owner's steps. Visible changes get a rough mock-up (Contemporary) for the owner first, and land with their UI tests, every language where words change, and the architecture doc they touch. Until someone asks, in this order:
- ☐ **12-string guitar, as six courses:** a 12-string can be tuned with the guitar's six strings already, since a locked string accepts its own octave (*Auto* in [docs/architecture/notes-and-audio.md](docs/architecture/notes-and-audio.md)), so both strings of a course read against it. The instrument gets six course buttons like the guitar's, not twelve: in standard tuning the lower four courses carry a string an octave up, the top two are in unison. The octave rule applies to the course from the first pluck (today an octave read from scratch is its own note), the reference tone plays the pair (the plucked voice an octave apart, `StringVoices`), and the chord charts, scales and capo helper are the guitar's. The strum check and the chord name stay off for it (twelve strings' overtones overlap too much) and the picker says so. `Instrument`, `TunerInterpreter` tests for the octave rule, the names in every language, `StringRowStylesTest`, every language at 2× text; the README's feature list and the tuner doc
- ☐ **5-string banjo:** open G (g D G B D, the short fifth string at the row's start) and double C and D tuning if the owner wants them, with a plucked voice of its own (`StringVoices`). For the tuner first: the chord charts and scales don't know a short fifth string that starts at the fifth fret, so Chords, Scales and the capo helper are not offered for it (as the Practice tools already handle an instrument they don't cover) until a later item teaches them that string. `Instrument`, the names in every language, `StringRowStylesTest` and every language at 2× text; the README's feature list
- ☐ **Left-handed charts:** a switch in Settings, off by default, that mirrors the drawn necks, the chord charts, the scale neck and the capo helper's shapes, so they match a guitar held the other way. The tuner's string row stays as it is: it is a list, not a picture of the guitar, and keeps left to right even in Arabic (buttons keep their place). `ChordsScreenTest`, `ScalesScreenTest`, `CapoScreenTest` with it on, TalkBack unchanged; the settings and Practice docs, and [PRIVACY.md](PRIVACY.md) and its Dutch copy where they list the saved settings
