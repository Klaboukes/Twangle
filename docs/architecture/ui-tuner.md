## Tuner screen
`TunerScreen` wires the ViewModel; `TunerContent` is the stateless body used by previews and UI tests. Upright (`SingleColumnLayout`, 2.0's combination in [docs/design/v2-tuner-screens-preview.html](../design/v2-tuner-screens-preview.html)) the column fills the screen's height: the top row (the gear, "Tune", focus mode's button), the picker chip on its own line under it, the ruler, the lens, the word with the cents under it, and the pegs at the bottom; the free height goes 1 : 2 above the lens and above the pegs (`TOP_GAP_WEIGHT`), each gap at least 24 dp (12 dp in a column under 610 dp, the S26's, with the ruler 4 dp over the lens instead of 12: `SHORT_COLUMN_HEIGHT`, Phase 77, so the pegs stay in view under the chip's line). The ruler names the note: since Phase 71's last step there is no big note, no octave beside it and no hertz under it ([history](history.md), *Tuner screen*). The meter takes what the rest leaves (`LensOverWord` in the `FitColumn`'s shrinking part): 0.6 times as high as wide where there is room (since Phase 78; the lens was 0.85 for the trail, 353 × 287 dp on the Xiaomi 12X), its height giving way on a short screen (the Galaxy S26's 570 dp) down to 0.45 of its width (half until Phase 78, when the meter grew 24 dp wider and a 360 dp wide short phone ran out of room), never its width (owner, 2026-10-21: shrink the note, never the meter; the note went, so now the meter gives way in height only). The word keeps its size. With a tool on, its panel in the lens's place shrinks instead (keeping its 2:1 shape, down to 160 dp, `FitGauge`), so the tool's text under it keeps its size. Only with a very large font does it scroll. The meter spans the column less the 8 dp `METER_MARGIN` each side, as a tool's panel does (since Phase 78, with no frame; the lens was 20 dp in, `LENS_INSET`); everything else keeps a 16 dp text margin (`COLUMN_PADDING`). 1.1's column shrank the big note to fit (`FitNoteColumn`, down to 32 dp, the octave never past half the letter) before the meter; it went with the note.
- **Upright tablet** (Phase 52): the column is at most 480 dp wide, so on a Lenovo Tab M11 upright (800 dp) the meter was 464 dp across and about 290 dp of screen lay empty under the note. There the whole column is now laid out as on a phone and drawn bigger (`columnScaleFor` and `ScaledUp` in `:core:designsystem`'s `ScaledUp.kt`, shared with the metronome's upright column, [Metronome, widget and watch](ui-other-screens.md); `columnScaleFor` is JVM-tested in `ColumnScaleTest`; `ScaledUp` gives the column a `LocalDensity` with that many more pixels to the dp): it is laid out in the window divided by the scale, as on a phone of at least 560 × 760 dp, so the meter, the note, the string buttons, every text and gap grow together and keep the phone's proportions, and no rule above needs a tablet case. The scale is the smaller of width / 560 and height / 760, at least 1 and at most 1.5 (720 dp across on a large tablet): 1.43 on the Tab M11 (a 686 dp column, a 663 dp meter), 1 on every phone (at most about 480 dp wide, and on its side far too short), growing smoothly with the window so a resized window doesn't jump; the provider stays at 1, so crossing the point keeps the column's state. Text keeps Android's own non-linear font scaling (sp go to dp through the screen's density, then to pixels at the scaled one). Popups opened from the column (tooltips) inherit it and grow too; the instrument and tuning panel, the two panes on its side and focus mode keep their own sizes. Tests measuring nodes inside the column read their bounds in the window's dp (`windowBounds` in `TunerContentTest`): `getBoundsInRoot()` uses the node's own, scaled, dp. `TunerContentTest.anUprightTabletShowsThePhonesColumnBigger` checks that every part on an 800 × 1240 dp tablet at 1.3× text sits where it sits on the phone-sized window, that much bigger, with no text cut off, and that a phone keeps its own size.

The reading sits at `rulerPosition(targetMidi, cents)`, the target note plus its cents (`TunerUiState.Detected.targetMidi`, the interpreter's target; in guitar mode a string tuned far off shows the note it sounds, three semitones sharp of E2 shows G). The heard note's name (`rulerHeardMidi`, the nearest) is 36 dp ExtraBold in ink, its neighbours 17 dp Medium in muted; each name grows and shrinks with its distance to the pointer (`rulerEmphasis`: full within a quarter semitone, small from 0.9, smoothstep), the bold name fading in over the plain one, so nothing jumps between two notes; names fade out over the last 32 dp at the ends (in focus mode's wide layout none is drawn within 76 dp of either end, where the exit button stands over it: `Ruler(edgeClear)`, Phase 73). The names are the notation setting's (`rulerNames`: a string keeps its tuning's spelling, E♭ in Half step down, Any note the notation's own; Automatic is already resolved in the state), set with `musicText`, in dp, not sp: a drawing, like the meter's scale. The pointer, a small triangle under the line, takes the reading's tone (`readingToneAmount`, amber easing toward green, green while in tune: v2-plan.md, *The lens*); quiet (no note) the scale rests on the last note heard, a locked string, the tuning's first string or A4 (`rulerRestMidi`), every name and the pointer muted (fading over 300 ms). The position is read only while drawing, through a critically damped spring (2.4 Hz), so readings slide the scale without recomposing; with animations off it follows each reading at once; nothing is allocated per frame (the 24 names are measured once). TalkBack skips it (the note's live region speaks the reading until the word does); UI tests read `RulerNote` and `RulerQuiet` from its semantics (`TunerTestTags.RULER`). On its side the ruler spans the readout pane over the lens (since Phase 71's last step on a phone too: without the big note the pane has room for it); in focus mode it runs across the top between the two corners, clear of the exit button (72 dp in from each side, `FOCUS_RULER_CLEARANCE`), until Phase 73 lays focus mode out wide. ## Under the meter
The word with the cents small under it, or with *Cents* picked in Settings the cents big in the word's place (Phase 78; until then the word always; until Phase 71's last step under the big note and the frequency; 1.1's "Below the note" setting, `TunerDisplay`, also offered the wave view in the cents' place, and was the wave by default; Phase 67 took the setting and the wave view out: [history](history.md), *Meter, wave and styles*).
- **The word** (2.0, Phase 71; `TuneWordLine`, `components/TuneWord.kt`; the choice `TuneWord` and `tuneWordFor` in `TunerHint.kt`, JVM-tested in `TuneWordTest`): one word under the lens that says what to do, the mock-up's 26 sp bold (22 compact) in the reading's tone: with strings **Tighten** (flat: raise the pitch) or **Loosen** (sharp), in Any note **Too low** or **Too high** (no string to turn), and **In tune**; "Play a string" ("Play a note", "Play the A2 string", "Playing D3") while nothing is heard, in a quieter 20 sp semibold, muted. Dutch: "Strakker", "Losser", "Te laag", "Te hoog", "Zuiver". Under it, small and muted, the cents ("12 cents flat"; in tune only how many, "2 cents"), or a tip about the sound ("Play a bit louder", in the accent colour) in their place, with a note and without. It follows the tuner's own cue (`TunerUiState.Detected.hint`: `TuningCue`'s margins and `SteadyHint`'s settle and least show times), so it never flickers between Tighten and Loosen near the note; a heard note whose cue hasn't settled (its first 0.2 s) keeps the word it had, so the line goes from "Play a string" straight to "Tighten". Each line keeps its height whatever it says and keeps to one line, set smaller where it doesn't fit (`TunerContentTest.theWordMovesNothing`). The colour is the `LiveTone`, read while drawing (`ColorProducer`), taken toward the ink on a light background just enough for the amber to read at 4.5 : 1 on the tinted screen (`wordInkShare`, 0 on dark), and the cents' muted colour likewise (`mutedInkShare`: 14 % of the green brings Ink light's muted to 3.9 : 1); `WordColourTest`. Tags: `TunerTestTags.WORD` (the spoken block), `WORD_TEXT`, `WORD_CENTS`.
