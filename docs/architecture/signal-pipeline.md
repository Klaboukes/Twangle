## Pipeline
```
 Microphone
    │  AudioRecord.read()  (dedicated thread, 48 kHz mono float, blocking; built-in mic while a
    │                      Bluetooth headset is connected)
    ▼
 AudioRecorder ──Flow<FloatArray>(chunk = one hop ≈ 21 ms, 1024 samples at 48 kHz)──►
    │                  while the strum check's button is on, every chunk also goes to StrumWatcher beside
    │                  the chain below (2.0, Phase 74): a sudden sound read as a strum of the open strings
    │                  → TunerViewModel.heardStrum (strum-and-metronome.md, *The strum check's
    │                  button*); the tuner's readings are the same with it or without
    ▼
 FrameAssembler        ring buffer → overlapping analysis window (≈ 85 ms, 4096 samples at 48 kHz;
    │                  ≈ 171 ms, 8192 samples, when the tuning goes below the guitar: see Low notes;
    │                  there the samples pass through MainsHumCanceller on their way in)
    ▼
 SignalGate            RMS level check (< ~-50 dBFS → "no signal"), DC offset removal
    ▼
 SustainCheck          newest hop vs the window's loudest (energy and peak): a tick that has
    │                  died away (> 12 dB down, or > 4 dB and back at the room) is not analysed
    ▼
 PitchDetector (YIN)   [upsample to ≥ 32 kHz if the rate is lower] → 4 kHz low-pass → difference fn
    │                  → CMNDF → absolute threshold → whole-period checks → above-range check
    │                  → least-squares refinement → PitchResult(frequencyHz, confidence), or NONE
    ▼
 NoiseFloorTracker     note-free frames' levels → median of 5 → minimum over 2 s = room's floor;
 + Clarity             with a pitch: SNR over that floor, clarity 0..1 from YIN's confidence
    ▼
 ReceptionTracker      level, SNR and clarity scored 0..1, each smoothed (fast up, slow down);
                       reception = the lowest of the three (needle and wave crisp ↔ faint)
    ▼
 PitchSmoother         Hz → fractional MIDI (NoteMapper.midiOf, honours A4) → onset: 3 agreeing
    │                  frames → octave-jump rejection → median of 5 → EMA (snaps on a new note,
    │                  smaller steps when unsure) → note with ±15-cent hysteresis; a passed
    │                  tick's frames neither start a note nor count as its dropout
    ▼
 NoteMapper / Tuning   note name + octave; in Auto mode → nearest string in tuning, cents from it
    ▼
 TunerViewModel        combine with settings → StateFlow<TunerUiState>
    ▼
 Compose UI            meter, note name, cents or wave, string row, in-tune indicator + haptic
```

## One frame chain
`PitchChain` (`:core:dsp`, Phase 65) is the part of the pipeline above from the `FrameAssembler` to the noise floor: chunks in, then per hop gate → `SustainCheck` → detector → mains-pitch rule (low range) → `NoiseFloorTracker`, with the low range's longer window and hum canceller sharing one ring buffer, gate and noise floor, so `range` can change at any hop without losing audio. It is the one copy every listener runs: the phone's `PitchPipeline` (which adds the microphone, the strum check and the chord name: since 2.0 as `StrumWatcher` beside the chain on every chunk; 1.1's tools took the microphone while on, until Phase 74), Ear training (through `TunerPitchListener`, the tuner's `PitchPipeline`), the watch's `WatchPitchPipeline` (standard range only; the low range's detector, sustain check and hum canceller are made when first asked for, so the watch never makes them, only its ring buffer is the long window's length) and the tests' `TunerChain`, so `EdgeCaseTest`, `LowPitchRangeTest` and `RealPluckTest` test the app's own chain, not a mirror of it. A fix to the frame chain (the sustain check, the noise floor's rule, a non-finite sample) now lands once. `PitchChainTest` checks it on synthetic signals: within 1 cent at 8–96 kHz, the low range after one long window, a range switch with no lost frame, hum never a note, the floor and SNR, skipped frames unheard, a restart, no allocation per frame. One hop costs what the copies did (`YinBenchmark`, *the shared frame chain*).

**What stayed with each listener, and why (decided 2026-11-01).** Setting aside the app's own sound with its tail was compared too and is *not* shared: the three look alike but differ in every rule that matters, so one class would need an option per difference and be longer than the few lines each has now.
- **The tuner** (`TunerViewModel`): while a reference tone plays and for 0.3 s after (`TONE_TAIL_SECONDS`), the readings still flow through the chain (the gate and noise floor keep following the room) but are shown as the listening state; the strum check and the chord name (since 2.0 the analysis beside the tuner, which then only stops ringing, no new strum) are held off for the same frames; while a strum shown rings, the tuner's own note is set aside too (`HeardShown`, [Tuner screen](ui-tuner.md), *The strum check*); the smoothing starts afresh when the count runs out; the pitch trail gets a gap.
- **Ear training** (`EarTrainingViewModel.hear`): the same 0.3 s after the question, but per `HeardFrame`, outside the pipeline (it can't see `:feature:tuner`), and its smoother and held note are reset on every set-aside frame.
- **The watch** (`WatchPitchPipeline`): its own sound is the wrist tap's buzz, started by its own in-tune moment, not by a player; those frames (the tap plus one window) are skipped *before* the gate (`PitchChain.skipFrame`, so the noise floor never hears the motor) and the reading holds still instead of starting afresh.
The smoothing after the chain stays apart likewise: the phone's `TunerInterpreter` (reception, tips, precision), Ear training's `PitchSmoother` with `HeldNote`, and the watch's `PitchSmoother` with its 0.6 s hold all use `:core:dsp`'s `PitchSmoother`, and what they add on top is each one's own.

**A screen reader's voice** (Phase 81) is set aside by every listener, the metronome's Listen and Chords' Listen too: *when* is shared (`ScreenReaderSpeech.watching`, one 0.4 s tail, `ScreenReaderSpeech.tailFrames`; [Notes, tunings and audio](notes-and-audio.md), *The mic ignores a screen reader's voice*), *how* is each listener's own, as for its own sound: the tuner and the pitch trail as for the tone (the listening state, a gap); Ear training as for the question, but as `Hearing.SPOKEN`, which doesn't count as the question having been set aside (so a voice already speaking as the question starts can't leave the answer unjudged); the metronome's Listen feeds its tempo detector silence in the chunk's place, so the detector's clock and the beat lights stay in step; Chords' Listen passes the chunks by and starts its `StrumWatcher` and `ChordSettler` afresh after; the watch skips those frames before the gate (`PitchChain.skipFrame`) and holds the reading still, as for its tap, and starts its smoothing afresh after.

## Key numbers
- **Sample rate:** 48 000 Hz, the native rate on most phones. `SampleRateSelector` uses `AudioManager.PROPERTY_OUTPUT_SAMPLE_RATE` if it is 44 100–48 000 Hz (higher only costs CPU), then tries 48 000, 44 100, 32 000, 16 000 and 8 000; the first rate `AudioRecord.getMinBufferSize` accepts is expected. If no input opens at it, `AndroidAudioRecorder` tries the other accepted rates in that order (`openFirst`: every input at a rate before the next rate, every rate in float before any in 16-bit; [Notes, tunings and audio](notes-and-audio.md), *Audio source selection*) and its `config` moves to the rate that opened; the pipelines read the rate again with the first chunk. Any rate from 8 to 96 kHz works end to end (see *Other sample rates*).
- **Analysis window:** 4096 samples (≈ 85 ms), about 7 periods of low E (82.41 Hz); YIN needs at least 2. Tunings that go lower (7- and 8-string guitar, bass, cello) use **8192** (≈ 171 ms, see *Low notes*).
- **Hop:** 1024 samples (≈ 21 ms), ~47 estimates per second.
- **Detection range:** 62–1500 Hz for guitar (τ_max = sampleRate / f_min). The floor sits above 60 Hz mains hum and below drop C (65.4 Hz). With the low-note window: **28–1500 Hz**, so B0 (30.9 Hz) reads even a semitone flat.
- **YIN threshold:** 0.15 (the paper suggests 0.10–0.15).
- **Accuracy target:** ±1 cent on synthetic signals. **Measured on a real guitar** (Phase 46, `RealPluckTest` and `RealPluckReport` on the 30 recordings of the owner's steel-string acoustic through the Xiaomi 12X, 2026-10-29; [Testing](testing.md), *Real recordings*): in a quiet room every open string shows its own note from the first reading (0.10–0.20 s after the pluck) and never another; the reading settles over the first two seconds with the string itself. Half a second in, the G and high E strings and the finger-plucked D are within 2 cents of the reading at 2 s; a hard pick on the low E, A or D reads +7 to +9 cents half a second in and +3 to +5 at 2 s (the whole waveform, without the refinement on the lowest partials, +19 → +13 on the low E), and even with a finger the E and A move 3 to 5 cents between 0.5 and 2 s. That is the string, not the detector (*Edge cases*, **A real guitar**): a real string's upper partials start 10–25 cents sharp and glide down 2–4 cents a second, and on this guitar's low strings partial 1 sits 3–6 cents under where partials 3 and up point. The test holds those figures per string ([Testing](testing.md)). **How quick:** the first reading shows 0.10–0.20 s after the pluck (the G, B and high E 0.10–0.16, the low E 0.10–0.15, the A 0.13–0.17, the D 0.16–0.20: a first frame or two in which the detector finds no pitch yet, the pick's noise and the low strings' first periods, then the onset's three agreeing frames; a synthetic pluck shows in 0.08–0.12), and in the noisy room 0.11–0.16, the picked A 0.30 (the TV through its attack). The needle then settles, staying within 2 cents (the Precise window) of where it ends up, from 0.13–0.24 s on a finger-plucked G or high E and 0.2 on a finger-plucked D, 0.4 s on a picked G or high E, 0.4–0.65 on the B, 0.9 on a picked D, 1.0–1.6 s on the A and low E strings, which glide (`RealPlucks.settleSeconds`; the test holds each string's figure with a margin). **In the noisy room** (the six takes with a TV 20–25 dB under the pluck) every take shows its own note from the first reading and never another: as the string sinks into the TV the detector reads a whole number of its periods for a few tenths of a second, which the smoother holds as the string (*Smoothing*, item 1), and the needle goes blank 1.1–1.7 s in when the detector finds nothing more (the A string, whose TV sat near its own pitch, rings out to the end; the picked low E, read as "flickering" when the recordings were first looked at, in fact shows without a break until it is lost at 1.2 s). Measured 2026-10-29, after the smoother's attack rule.
- **"In tune":** |cents| ≤ 5 held for 300 ms (15 hops at 48 kHz), released past 7 cents, so a note hovering at 5 doesn't flicker. That is **Relaxed**, the default; **Precise** is ≤ 2, released past 3 ([Lifecycle](lifecycle.md), *Settings*). The shown "in tune" goes through the steady hint line (see [Notes, tunings and audio](notes-and-audio.md), *Audio capture*), so it also lasts at least 0.5 s.

## Other sample rates
`AnalysisSettings.forSampleRate(rate)` (8–96 kHz) scales window and hop so they always last ≈ 85 ms and ≈ 21 ms and the smoother's frame counts keep their meaning (44.1 kHz: 3763 / 941 samples). `PitchChain` builds its `FrameAssembler` and detectors from it, and `AudioConfig.forSampleRate` makes the recorder's chunks one hop long. A rate outside 8–96 kHz ends the flow with `InitializationFailed`.

Below 32 kHz a period spans too few samples for ±1 cent, so `UpsamplingPitchDetector` first interpolates by an integer factor to ≥ 32 kHz (polyphase windowed-sinc FIR: Kaiser β = 8, 32 taps per phase, cut-off 0.45 × input rate), and YIN's low-pass drops to 0.4 × the input rate so no images get through. Measured: every rate from 8 to 96 kHz within 1 cent on sines, saws, plucks and weak fundamentals; 8 kHz costs 0.5 ms per frame on the JVM.

## Frame assembly
`FrameAssembler` keeps a ring buffer of `capacity` samples (4096 by default). `push()` takes chunks of any size and stops as soon as a frame is ready, so none is skipped; `readFrame(out, length)` copies the latest `length` samples (by default the 4096-sample window) oldest-first into a caller-owned array, and `skipFrame()` passes on a frame. First frame after one full window, then one per hop. `PitchPipeline` gives it a capacity of the low-note window (8192 at 48 kHz) so either window can be read at every hop; `available` says how much audio it holds since the mic opened. While its `filter` is set (a `SampleFilter`: the low range's hum canceller, *Low notes*), every pushed sample passes through it on its way into the ring, the caller's chunk left as it was. A NaN or infinite sample (a broken driver or resampler) goes into the ring as 0 before the filter sees it (Phase 65): one would otherwise stick in the hum canceller's state and every sum over the window and silence the tuner until the mic restarted. Allocated once.

## Low notes
`PitchRange` (`:core:dsp`) says how low the detector listens:
- **`STANDARD`:** 62–1500 Hz, 4096 samples. Every guitar tuning down to D2, ukulele (the baritone's D3 too), violin, viola, mandolin.
- **`LOW`:** 28–1500 Hz, **8192 samples** (`AnalysisSettings.forSampleRate(rate, PitchRange.LOW)`; ≈ 171 ms at any rate). 28 Hz is below B0 at −50 cents (30.0 Hz); τ_max = 1715 lags at 48 kHz, which with the filter's start-up and the fit's margin needs ≈ 8000 samples (W = 6276, ≈ 3.7 periods of the lowest note, like the standard window's 4). The hop stays 1024, so readings still come ~47 times a second and the smoother's frame counts keep their meaning.

`PitchRange.forLowestNote(hz)` picks `LOW` when the tuning's lowest string, **a semitone flat**, is below 62 Hz, at the current A4. So 7- and 8-string guitars, both basses, the cello and the guitar's Open C (C2, 65.4 Hz: a semitone flat is 61.7 Hz) get `LOW`; no other guitar preset does, even at A4 = 415 Hz (drop D's D2 at 69 Hz). `TunerOptions.pitchRange` works it out once per tuning or A4 change; `TunerViewModel` passes `{ options.pitchRange }` to `PitchPipeline.readings(range)`, which reads it every frame. The pipeline keeps a frame buffer and a detector for each range (made once per listening session, ≈ 200 KB for the low one) and one assembler holding the long window, so switching the tuning changes the window on the next hop without reopening the mic or losing audio. After the mic opens, low-range readings start once a whole long window has arrived (171 ms instead of 85). Chromatic mode follows the tuning too, so a bass player's chromatic mode also reads low.

The cost: twice the window, about 2.3× the CPU per frame (bigger FFT), and a reading that reflects audio ≈ 43 ms further back on average. Measured in [Threading and performance](performance.md) (*Low-note window on the Xiaomi 12X*).

**Measured (`LowPitchRangeTest`, 48 kHz):** sines 30–1500 Hz within 0.09 cents; sawtooth, square, pluck and weak fundamental within 0.52; every string of every low instrument within 1 cent from a semitone flat to 50 cents sharp, the right note even with a weak fundamental; 20 dB SNR within 0.33, 10 dB within 0.83; nothing below 28 Hz; 8, 16 and 44.1 kHz also within 1 cent; no allocation.

**Mains hum** (Phase 46, `MainsHumCanceller`). 50 and 60 Hz lie inside the low range. Before this, hum alone showed as G1 or B♭1, hum 10 dB under a B0 string pulled it up to 8 cents (2–3 from E1 up), and hum 6 dB under a string (`TestSignals.hum`: six harmonics at 1/k) put most low strings 35 cents to two octaves off, or hid them. So in `LOW` alone the samples pass through adaptive notches on their way into the frame buffer (`FrameAssembler.filter`, which the pipeline sets at every hop from the range; `AnalysisSettings.newHumCanceller()` gives none for the standard range, whose 62 Hz floor already keeps hum out, so the guitar's tuner is untouched):
- *The notches:* at 50 and 60 Hz and their harmonics up to the 6th (11 frequencies, 300 Hz once). Each is Widrow's adaptive noise canceller with a sinusoidal reference (Widrow et al. 1975): the hum is estimated as `Σ a_k·cos(ω_k n) + b_k·sin(ω_k n)` and subtracted, every weight following the LMS rule `a_k += 2μ·y·cos(ω_k n)` on the output y, which makes a notch `μ·fs/π` = 1 Hz wide at each ω_k (1 Hz followed an off-nominal mains better than 2 or 4). Six harmonics, not three: with three, the test hum's 4th to 6th harmonics took strings from 98 to 220 Hz an octave low and left others up to 9 cents off.
- *Learning only between notes:* a fixed notch narrow enough to pass G1 (49.0 Hz) next to 50 Hz still rings when a note starts beside it (a tone at the notch's own frequency, B/2Δ of the note's level, lasting about 1/(πB) s). Measured with fixed notches (RBJ cookbook biquads, 0.25 to 2 Hz wide) in a quiet room, the first second of a G1, B♭1, B1, G2 or B2 pluck moved by 3 to 20 cents. So the weights only learn while the room is just room: a sample above 5× the room's RMS (a pluck), or the last 10 ms 6 dB above it (a swell), freezes them at once and the hum goes on being subtracted as learned while the note itself passes untouched; back within 3 dB of the room they learn again. While held, the room's level creeps up 1 dB/s, so a lasting change (an amplifier switched on) is learned in the end. In a quiet room the weights only hold the noise's tiny share.
- *Only what turns like hum is taken out:* the notches learn whatever the room holds, a note already ringing as the low range opens (or as the range switches mid-note) too, and taking that out would ring them beside it (B0 read 7.6 cents off). So the output loses only the estimates that move like hum: each block, every estimate's turn is added up in 10 ms steps (once a block, a partial a whole number of 10 Hz from a notch, A2 at 110 Hz from all of them, turns by whole circles and looks still), and an estimate counts as hum after two blocks in a row in which its base's fundamental turned less than 0.25 rad (0.4 Hz) and it turned k times as far as that fundamental (300 Hz, shared, on either base). It then fades in over a block; held while a note sounds. Hum alone is taken out from 0.4 s.
- *Following mains:* mains is never exactly 50 or 60 Hz (±0.05 Hz is common), and a held estimate a few thousandths of a hertz off drifts from the hum: over 2 s that took G2 an octave low. With the references held still, a settled estimate turns at exactly the hum's distance from them, so once the strongest fundamental's estimate has been steady for 1.5 s (5 of the notches' time constants; after only 2.5 the next step went the wrong way), its turn averaged over 0.5 s is what every reference then moves by, in blocks of 100 ms (a whole period of the 10 Hz between 50 and 60 Hz and their harmonics, which ripples the weights). Held for a note, the references run on at that frequency plus the turn averaged so far. 50.1 Hz is locked to within 0.3 mHz after about 3 s of hum; at most ±0.5 % off nominal.
- *The guard:* in the low range a pitch within 0.5 Hz of 50 or 60 Hz is no note (`MainsHumCanceller.isMainsPitch`): hum read before the notches have learned it, e.g. just after the microphone opens, or 0.1 Hz off nominal while the tracking catches up (that read up to 0.35 Hz off). No string is tuned there (G1 + 35 cents, B1 − 47), the band is ±17 cents at 50 Hz and ±14 at 60, and a note held there would lose its fundamental to the notches anyway.
- After the strum check or chord name had the microphone, the canceller learns the hum afresh.

**Measured with hum (`LowPitchRangeTest`, through the whole chain, `TunerChain`):** hum 6 dB under every string of every low instrument's tunings, in tune and a semitone flat, at 50 and 60 Hz after 2 s of hum: shown within 0.25 s of the pluck and within 1 cent from 0.25 s to 2.5 s, the first readings before that within 2 (worst 0.45 cents from the first frame in the exploration that chose the settings; a B0 over 50.05 Hz hum shows −1.5 for one frame since 2026-10-29, when the refinement on the lowest partials began to count from a window whose first half has 80 % of its second's energy, *Pitch detection*, item 3; before, up to two octaves off or nothing). Mains at 49.9, 50.05, 59.95 and 60.1 Hz, after 4 s of hum: the same. Hum alone at 50, 60, 50.1 and 59.9 Hz, with and without noise, from the moment the microphone opens: no note. In a quiet room G1, B♭1, B1, G2 and B2 (sine, pluck, weak fundamental) read within 0.1 cent of how they read without the canceller (measured 0.011), in the same frames; so do B0 to B2, A2 included, sounding from the very first sample, within 0.2 cent (measured 0.15: while the canceller still learns, a G2's lowest partials, beside the 100 and 200 Hz notches, read a little off, and the refinement on them now counts in full; before the gating, B0 7.6 cents off). No allocation; the cost is in [Threading and performance](performance.md). *Known limits:* the hum must be heard before the note: a pluck in the first second or two of hum (just after the tuner opens) or a hum that starts during a ringing note is read as before until it is learned, and while a note holds the estimate a hum that changes isn't followed. Synthetic hum only, until the bass check of *Owner checks* (Phase 15).

## Signal gate
`SignalGate.process(frame)` first measures the **peak** as the mic delivered it (`peakDbfs`, to detect clipping), then removes the DC offset in place and measures the RMS level as `20 · log10(rms)` dBFS (full-scale square 0 dBFS, sine ≈ −3; silence floored at −120). It opens at **−50 dBFS** and closes below **−56 dBFS** (6 dB hysteresis), so a decaying note doesn't flicker. Only frames with the gate open reach the detector.

## Sustain check (ticks and clicks)
A tap on the desk, a clock's tick or a mouse click often rings at a pitch for a few milliseconds. YIN reads that ring, and it stays in the 85 ms window for four hops, so without a check each tick would show a note for ≈ 0.1 s (a 1–1.5 kHz ring sits nearest the high E string, far sharp: "the needle jumps to E4"). A string sustains; a tick dies away. `pitch/SustainCheck` cuts the window from its newest end into blocks of one hop, or one period of the lowest note where that is longer (the low range: 1715 samples, 36 ms, since a bass note's 32 ms cycle doesn't fit in a 21 ms hop and its energy per hop swings by up to 12 dB), and `dropDb` is how far the newest block is below the loudest. It measures both energy and peak and takes the larger drop: energy sees a 30 ms ping fade 12 dB within three hops; the peak sees a 2 ms tick louder than the string by its full height, where in energy it is only ≈ 6 dB. Every block holds a whole cycle, so a steady note reads ≈ 0 dB (up to 2.5 dB of energy on bass notes). The idea is energy-envelope onset detection (Bello et al. 2005) turned around: a pitch is only trusted while the envelope says the sound is still there.
A frame is **not sustained**, so it isn't analysed (`PitchReading.sustained` = false), when its sound had died away by its end:
- **more than 12 dB down** (`SustainCheck.DEFAULT_MAX_DROP_DB`): a loud tick has passed. A note that starts in the newest block is the loudest block, so new notes pass at once; on the phone a played E4 never dropped more than 1.8 dB across a window, a synthetic 24th-fret E fading fast about 7.
- **more than 4 dB down and the newest block within 6 dB of the room's background** (`ROOM_DROP_DB`, `ROOM_MARGIN_DB`, the floor from `NoiseFloorTracker`): a quiet tick has passed. A tick just above the room can't drop further than the room's level (6.6–8 dB on the phone, as little as that fast high note), but a note is still well above the room. Room noise alone varies by 1–2 dB, so it is sustained and ends a reading as usual.

While a note shows, a frame that isn't sustained doesn't count as a dropout (`PitchSmoother.noReading(sustained = false)`): a tick louder than the string hides it until the tick leaves the window. With the smoother's onset (*Smoothing*) a tick can't start a reading: it gives at most 2 analysed frames, and a new reading also needs frames loud enough to open the gate by themselves. On the phone the laptop speaker rang on at 1400 Hz for ≈ 70 ms after a tick, −48 to −55 dBFS: the gate stayed open on its hysteresis after the tick and three sustained frames read 1400 Hz, "E4 +25 semitones".

Measured (`EdgeCaseTest`, `SustainCheckTest`, 48 kHz): ticks (2 ms rings at 330 Hz–5 kHz, a knock, a clock tick, a digital click, a noise burst) and pings (the same rings lasting 8 or 30 ms), at peak −1 dBFS over a −70 or −55 dBFS room: **nothing shown**. Without the check, most rang at a pitch and showed for 5–36 frames per three of them. **On the Xiaomi 12X** (2026-10-15), 45 ticks of seven kinds from the laptop speaker: none shown; a played E4 shows on its third frame, on pitch. Ticks every 0.4 s over a ringing string, 12 dB quieter or 13 dB louder (peak): the reading is never lost and stays **within 0.26 cents**; ticks as loud as the string move it up to 3.5 cents for a frame or two. *Known limits:* a ping ringing 8–30 ms over a string is a pitched sound of its own (like a glass clink) and can pull the reading off, even onto its own note, for a moment; and in the low range a ping ringing ≈ 30 ms fades too little within a 36 ms block, so on its own it may show briefly. Cost: under 0.01 ms per frame.

## Noise floor, SNR and clarity
How well the tuner hears the guitar. Every `PitchReading` carries these besides the level; *Reception* turns them into what the display shows.
- **Noise floor** (`level/NoiseFloorTracker`): the room's background in dBFS. Only frames without a pitch count, gate open or not. Their levels pass a running median of 5 (ignores a glitch), and the floor is the lowest of those over the last **2 s of note-free frames** (scaled with the hop by `AnalysisSettings.newNoiseFloorTracker()`). This is Martin's (2001) minimum statistics on one band, with a median in place of his recursive smoothing (which took ~0.6 s to follow a 20 dB drop). The floor drops within a few frames, rises only once a louder room lasts 2 s, and notes never move it. NaN until the first note-free frame; reads a little low on fluctuating noise.
- **SNR** while a note sounds: `10 · log10(10^((level − floor) / 10) − 1)`, the frame's power minus the background's (as in Boll's spectral subtraction), at least −20 dB; NaN without a pitch or floor. Measured within 1 dB from 0 to 40 dB SNR.
- **Clarity** (`pitch/Clarity`): 0..1 from YIN's confidence `c = 1 − d′(τ)`. `d′(τ)` is the aperiodic share of the power, so `10 · log10(c / (1 − c))` is a periodic-to-aperiodic ratio in dB, like Boersma's (1993) harmonics-to-noise ratio. It depends only on the noise, not pitch or timbre, and sits ≈ 7.8 dB above broadband SNR (YIN's low-pass removes most hiss). Mapped linearly from 7.5 dB (`d′` = 0.15: 0) to 20 dB (`d′` = 0.01: 1): 20 dB SNR or cleaner gives 1, 10 dB ≈ 0.8, 6 dB ≈ 0.5, 0 dB ≈ 0.

All allocation-free (byte-counting tests). **No meters** for these (owner's decision, 2026-10-12): they become one *reception* figure the display shows, tips only when the player can fix something (and, until 2026-11-07, optional signal bars behind a setting: [history](history.md)).

## Reception
`level/ReceptionTracker` (pure, counted in frames) turns each pitched frame's level, SNR and clarity into one steady 0..1 figure: 1 for a strong, clean note, towards 0 when it is quiet, sinks into the room or loses its period.
- **Scores:** level 0 at −50 dBFS to 1 from −35 dBFS; SNR 0 at 0 dB to 1 from 20 dB (unknown room counts as 1); clarity as is. Linear in dB.
- **Smoothing:** a one-pole filter per factor, 0.15 s towards better and 0.5 s towards worse, so a rough frame or two barely dims it. In a note's first 0.1 s a factor rises at once (the window still holds the silence before it). `reset()` (note gone) returns to 1.
- **The figure** is the lowest of the three; `limit` (`ReceptionLimit`: `NONE` from 0.95, else `LEVEL`, `NOISE`, `CLARITY`) names the one holding it down.

`TunerInterpreter` feeds one tracker every pitched frame and resets it when the reading is lost; `Detected.reception` carries it. On screen (`components/ReceptionLook.kt`) it changes **only opacity and sharpness**, never colour, shape or motion, which stay the tuning cue: at worst reception the needle layer and wave fade to 0.62 of their opacity with up to 2 dp blur (`BlurEffect`, 8 steps made once; Android 12+, older phones only fade). The effect scales with how far the picture is from its resting look, so resting looks unchanged, and `HeldReception` keeps the last value while a note fades out. Text never fades.

## Tips
`TipCue` (`ReceptionTip.kt`, pure, counted in frames) picks the `ReceptionTip` the sound asks for, with separate on and off limits so a level hovering at a limit can't flicker:
- **Too loud** ("Too loud, move the phone back"): peak reaches −0.5 dBFS (clipping), until it stays below −3 dBFS. A pluck that clips only in its attack is shorter than the tip's settle time. Comes first, because clipping distorts everything else.
- **Too soft** ("Play a bit louder"): the note's *loudest* level so far is below −44 dBFS, so a well-played note's fading tail never asks.
- **Too noisy** ("Too noisy, move the phone closer"; until Phase 65 "Too noisy here", which didn't say what to do): room background ≥ −38 dBFS, until below −42 dBFS (also between notes); or the note's best SNR stays below 8 dB. Too soft comes first, since playing louder also beats noise.
- A note's verdict holds 2.5 s after it ends, so a tip stays through the gap to the next pluck.

The wanted tip passes through its own `SteadyHint<ReceptionTip>` (not restarted per note): settles in 0.5 s (loud), 0.6 s (soft) or 1 s (noisy), stays at least **3 s**, then the line stays tip-free for 1 s. `Detected.tip` and `Listening.tip` carry it.

On screen (the word under the lens, `TuneWordLine`, since 2.0's Phase 71; `NoteDisplay` before) a tip **replaces something less needed, so nothing moves**: the cents in the small line under the word, with a note and without (so the word itself stays). In 1.1 it took "Play a string" without a note, the direction in the wave's pill, or the frequency under the needle. One line, set smaller (down to 11 sp) when it doesn't fit; its line keeps the height of a full-size line (measured with a `TextMeasurer`), the shrunk text centred in it. Without that it came out a pixel taller at 1.25× text and pushed what was above it up: under Android 14's non-linear font scaling Compose keeps an sp line height in proportion to the font size, and a smaller font is enlarged by a larger factor. TalkBack: "E 2, 12 cents flat, Play a bit louder".

## Pitch detection (YIN)
`YinPitchDetector` follows de Cheveigné & Kawahara (2002), steps 2–5, with `d(τ)` computed by FFT (`FftDifference`, [Threading and performance](performance.md)) and three additions that measurements showed were needed for ±1 cent:
1. **Low-pass pre-filter:** 4th-order Butterworth at 4 kHz (two RBJ biquads), skipping its ~92-sample start-up transient. Without it very bright tones near 1.4 kHz give a V-shaped dip that parabolic interpolation misjudges by ~2.5 cents. Also removes hiss.
2. **Adaptive least-squares refinement:** a cubic fitted to raw `d` over the lags `τ ± k` within 5 % of the way from the dip to the mean (`k` ≤ τ/16). Sharp dips use k = 1 (the classic parabola); broad dips (low pure notes) average dozens of lags. At 20 dB SNR this cut the worst low-E error from ~23 cents to under 1; the cubic term removes a ~0.3-cent bias.
3. **The refinement again on the lowest partials** (Phase 46, 2026-10-28). A real string is stiff, so its partials sit sharp of exact multiples (partial k at `k·f₀·√(1 + B·k²)`, Fletcher 1964), and the dip of `d`, which weighs every partial alike for a pick's 1/k attack, is pulled sharp by them until the upper partials die away: a low E picked hard read +3.5 cents at 0.5 s. So once τ is chosen (threshold, whole-period checks and above-range check exactly as below, so no octave decision changes), the refinement runs again on the frame limited to its lowest partials, and that period counts:
   - *The band:* gains 1 up to 2·f₀, falling as a raised cosine to 0 at 3.5·f₀, so partials 1 and 2 pass whole and the 3rd at a quarter. It is applied to the spectra `FftDifference` already holds, not with a time-domain filter (a Butterworth that low rings for many periods): the window's spectrum A and the span's B are kept from the FFT, the band-limited window `H·A` is then tapered smoothly to 0 at both ends (`g = 3v² − 2v³` over the window, a 7-tap convolution of the spectrum, since g is a cubic in `cos(2πj/N)`), and the tapered, band-limited cross-correlation `r_g(τ)` is summed straight from those bins at the few lags needed (τ ± (τ/16 + τ/50)): about 100 bins × 100 lags, against another 4096- or 8192-point inverse FFT. The order matters. Filtering first keeps each partial's own frequency and decay (a decaying partial is an eigenfunction of any filter). Tapering after keeps the cut-off window's ends, which hold every partial, from ringing into the band. Untapered, or tapered before filtering, the band read harmonic plucks 7 to 13 cents off at the lowest notes.
   - *The fit:* `d̃(τ) = e_g(0) + e_g(τ) − 2 r_g(τ)`, the band's tapered difference function. Its energy `e_g(0)` is `r_g(0)`; how `e_g` changes with the lag (decay, onset) comes from the whole frame's tapered energy at the two ends of those lags, linear in between (`FftDifference.taperedEnergy`, 3·W multiply-adds). The minimum is followed downhill from τ and found with the same cubic fit.
   - *When it counts:* fully while the note rings on: a window whose first half has at least 80 % of its second half's energy (1 dB). Less while the window still holds the attack, where that period strays by up to 20 cents: the weight falls linearly to 0 at half the energy (3 dB), and an attack inside the window leaves the first half a tenth or less. The margin was 10 % until 2026-10-29, when the real recordings showed a ringing string's level wobbling as its two planes of vibration beat (a window's first half up to 15 % quieter than its second, 0.7 dB, a second into the note, on a third of the frames of a finger-plucked low E), which handed those frames to the whole waveform, 2–4 cents sharp; and on a steady low note the halves' alignment to the cycle alone swung the ratio 0.87–1.15. The cost is at the first one or two frames of a note, whose windows start inside the attack: a B0 over mains hum showed −1.5 cents for one frame (`LowPitchRangeTest`, which allows 2 cents before 0.25 s). Not where the band has no clean dip of its own: its floor above 3 % of its mean starts to fade it out, and above 6 % it doesn't count. A note's own band, even at 10 dB SNR, stays well under 1 %. Mains hum 10 dB under a low string leaves 10–20 %, and a tanpura read at its root, whose Pa isn't a partial of it, 30 %. There step 2 is the better reading, and it is the one used.
   - *Measured* (`EdgeCaseTest`, `StrumChords.stiffStrum` at the strum tests' B = 1.5·10⁻⁴ on the low E to 2·10⁻⁵ on the high E, the reading shown by the whole chain): a hard pick (partials at 1/k) on the low E reads +0.2 cents at 0.25, 0.5 and 1 s (before: +8.4, +3.5, +0.9); at three times that stiffness +0.6 (before +14.1, +7.6, +2.6); every string of the standard tuning within 0.7 cents from 0.25 s on, picked or plucked with a finger. The reference tones' steel strings, stiff too, read within 1.3 cents at 0.3 s ([Notes, tunings and audio](notes-and-audio.md), *Reference tone*). On the real guitar (*Edge cases*, **A real guitar**) it halves how far a picked low string settles: the low E +19 → +13 cents from 0.5 to 2 s without it, +9 → +5 with it, the rest being the string's own glide. The cost is in [Threading and performance](performance.md).
   - *Not for detuned voices:* a sustained sound of beating voices (the drone's harmonium reeds or pad chorus) reads its few low partials following the slow beat and leaning towards the louder voice (the harmonium's fifth −1.0 cents averaged over 30 s, against −0.4 from the whole sound), so `DroneSynthTest` measures the drone with `refineOnLowestPartials = false`. A 12-string's unison courses beat the same way, so they wobble a little more than with the whole sound.

**Range edges and whole-period checks.** The first dip is followed to its minimum; if it is still falling at τ_max, the pitch is below 62 Hz and the frame has none (this keeps 60 Hz hum out). Two checks then catch a period off by a whole factor, on raw `d`, and a third drops tones above the range:
- *Too long (noise):* heavy noise inflates `d′` at short lags, so the first accepted dip can be 2–3 periods. If `d` at a local minimum near τ/k is at most 15 % above `d(τ)` (and `d′` < 0.5), τ/k is taken.
- *Too short (harmonics):* a note with a strong 2nd and weak odd harmonics nearly repeats every half period. If the dip floor at 2τ or 3τ is below a quarter of the floor at τ, that lag is the period. *Dip floors* are the minimum of `d` between whole lags, from the quartic through `d` at τ ± 2 (a parabola misjudged a sharp dip by up to 0.3 % of the mean of `d` at a low analysis rate; the quartic is within 0.02 %). A floor under 0.2 % of the mean of `d` is a tone that repeats at τ and is left alone. Comparing raw `d` and skipping every tone with `d′(τ)` < 0.05 is not enough: a G string heard through the phone, its fundamental and odd harmonics faint (0.5–2 % of the mean left at its half period), reads G4 in every frame; a true G4 leaves under 0.2 % even at 20 dB SNR, and its floor at 2τ is as high (in noise) or barely lower.
- *Above the range:* a tone above 1500 Hz has no dip in range at its own period, but one at 2, 3 or 4 periods, so a 2 kHz whistle or a tick ringing at 3 or 5 kHz read as 1000, 1500 or 1250 Hz. After the two checks above, the frame has no pitch if the dip at τ_min is still falling towards shorter lags, or a dip of `d` below τ_min (from lag 4) divides τ a whole number of times and is about as deep. That is the *too long* test, on parabola-interpolated dip floors (a period of 10–30 lags falls well between whole lags, which alone makes `d` there several times `d(τ)` on a clean tone) plus 1 % of the mean of `d`. A note with a strong 2nd harmonic keeps its odd harmonics at the half period, ≥ 4 % of the mean of `d` in every test tone, so it still reads. Measured: sines and bright tones 1.6–5 kHz give nothing, also at 20 dB SNR; 1318–1500 Hz still within 1 cent.

Search range τ = 32…775 (1500…62 Hz) plus 50 lags for the fit; integration window `W = 4096 − 92 − 825 = 3179`. In the low-note range τ = 32…1715 (1500…28 Hz) plus 109 lags, `W = 8192 − 92 − 1824 = 6276`, FFT size 8192. Confidence = 1 − d′(τ). `PitchResult` is a value class packing two floats into a `Long`, with `PitchResult.NONE` instead of null, so it never boxes. Measured (single frames, 61 steps from 62 to 1500 Hz, 2026-10-28): sines within 0.13 cents (0.02 on average), saw, pluck and weak fundamental within 0.5, at 20 dB SNR within 0.65, at 10 dB within 1.8; no octave errors. `detect()` is allocation-free (byte-counting test).

## Smoothing
`PitchSmoother` works in fractional MIDI, so cents are ×100 and A4 is already applied:
0. **Onset:** without a reading, a new one starts only once **3 readings in a row** agree within 50 cents, each from a frame loud enough to open the gate by itself (≥ −50 dBFS; the gate's hysteresis keeps a fading note, it doesn't start one); a frame without a pitch, one too quiet, or one far from the last starts the count over. A tick gives at most 2 such frames (see *Sustain check*). The reading starts at the median of the three, so a new note shows ≈ 43 ms later than without the onset (76 ms after the pluck instead of 33; 119 instead of 76 for the low strings, whose first frames have no pitch yet) and its first value is closer (worst 0.59 cents instead of 1.28 on a C6 pluck).
1. **Octave-jump rejection:** a reading a whole number of periods from the running median, an octave or more or a twelfth (three periods), up or down (±30 cents), is dropped. It is followed, the filters restarting there, once it has held 3 frames (~64 ms) loud enough to start a reading (≥ −50 dBFS, the gate's threshold) **and came with an attack**: one of those frames at least 10 dB above the quietest of the 10 frames (≈ 0.2 s) before it, which a pluck always is (15–40 dB in the recordings, a new string over the last one's tail or the 12th-fret harmonic after the touch that mutes the open string included) and a room's talk is not (a TV rose 6 dB at most); without an attack only once it has lasted 47 frames (≈ 1 s), by when it is a sound of its own (a TV singing it, a bowed string slurring to the octave). In quieter frames it is never followed. The two cases this holds the needle through, both from Phase 46's recordings (2026-10-29): the owner's high E, picked, has its 2nd partial 7 dB above its fundamental, which sinks into the room's noise about 2.2 s in, at −53 dBFS, and the needle then jumped to E5 for half a second until the gate closed; and in a noisy room (a TV 20–25 dB under the pluck) each string, sunk to within about 8 dB of the TV, read a whole number of its periods for 7–16 frames above the gate, with its level flat or falling (G2 and then C2 for the G string, B2 for the B, A2, three periods, for the high E: the string's own frequency divided by 2 or 3, not the TV's pitch, since the string's partials fix the dip while the talk's low partials make the longer period the first under YIN's threshold), and the needle showed them before going blank. Now it holds the string and goes blank, and for a second after a reading is lost the same readings can't start one either, unless an attack came in between (a synthetic high E sinking into a murmur read E2 and E3 for a few frames after the needle had gone blank, and the needle came back with E2). The twelfth is in because the detector's whole-period checks can land on 3τ as well as 2τ; a fifth or a fourth away is another string and is snapped to at once, as before. The cost is a ring of the last ten levels; `PitchSmootherTest` covers each case, `EdgeCaseTest` the whole chain (*Edge cases*, **A pluck sinking into a murmur**).
2. **Median of 5** (~107 ms) removes other outliers.
3. **EMA, α = 0.3,** snapping when the median is > 50 cents away (a new string). Steps are weighted by confidence: 1 from 0.97 up, falling linearly to 0.2 at 0.88 and below. This roughly halves needle wobble in a noisy room and at the end of a decay without slowing clean notes.
4. **Note hysteresis:** the shown note changes only past 65 cents (50 + 15), so displayed cents can reach ±65.

`noReading()` keeps the last reading for up to 4 missed frames (~85 ms), then clears it; frames that aren't sustained (*Sustain check*) don't count. Allocation-free.

## Edge cases (measured, `EdgeCaseTest`)
What the user sees after the whole chain on synthetic signals:
- **White noise:** always the right note; worst error 0.8 cents at 10 dB SNR, 1.7 at 6, 2.5 at 3, ≈ 10 at 0 dB (low notes; high notes stay under 2).
- **Mains hum 10 dB below the string:** within 3 cents; hum alone shows nothing (the standard range, whose 62 Hz floor keeps hum out). *Known limit:* there, hum within ~6 dB of the string can lock YIN onto a shared period or hide low strings; the guitar's range is left as it is. In the low range, where 50 and 60 Hz are notes, the hum is cancelled before YIN: 6 dB below every low string within 1 cent, hum alone nothing (see *Low notes*, *Mains hum*).
- **Ticks, knocks and clicks:** alone, never shown; over a ringing string, within 0.26 cents (see *Sustain check*).
- **High strings with strong 2nd/3rd harmonics** (B3–C6): within 1 cent, no octave flips while decaying.
- **Stiff strings** (Phase 46): every string of the standard tuning, at the strum tests' stiffness and three times it, picked (partials at 1/k) or plucked with a finger (1/k²), and at the strum tests' stiffness also with a weak fundamental (`TestSignals.WEAK_FUNDAMENTAL_HARMONICS`, what the real low strings look like through a phone), within 1 cent of its fundamental `f₀·√(1 + B)` 0.5 s and 1 s after the pluck; measured worst +0.7 (the low E, three times as stiff, picked). The same with the string's level swelling and fading ±25 % at 1.3 Hz, as a real string's does while its two planes of vibration beat: every frame from 0.3 s on within 1 cent. See *Pitch detection*, item 3.
- **A real guitar** (Phase 46, `RealPluckTest` and `RealPluckReport` on the recordings of 2026-10-29, [Testing](testing.md), *Real recordings*): in a quiet room every one of the 24 takes (each open string twice with a pick, twice with a finger) shows its own note from the first reading, 0.10–0.20 s after the pluck, and never another note; from 0.5 s on the reading only settles, by the per-string figures in *Key numbers* (the picked low E: +8.9 at 0.5 s, +8.4 at 1 s, +6.9 at 1.5 s, +5.2 at 2 s; the G: +2.5, +0.6, +1.1, +1.2; the high E: +1.9, +0.7, +0.5, +0.5). What the recordings showed about real strings, against the synthetic models: (1) the partials glide: the stiff-string series through partials 3 and up starts 6–10 cents sharp on the low E and D and settles 2–4 cents in the next second on every string, pick or finger; (2) the lowest partials don't sit on that series: on this guitar's E, D and G strings partial 1 is 3–6 cents *under* where partials 3 and up point, on the B 1–2 cents above (the body's resonances pull the partials near them), so there is no one pitch to be right to within a few cents early in a low note, and the reading, which weighs partials 1 to 3, lands between and settles with the upper partials; (3) the low strings' fundamentals are weak: through the phone the picked low E has partials 2, 3, 6 and 7 at +6 to +11 dB over partial 1 (the 1/k model: −6 to −17), the D its partials 2–4 at +8 to +12, and even with a finger the low E and D have partials 2–7 10–20 dB above 1/k²; the A, G and B fit the models within about ±10 dB, the high E's 2nd partial sits 7 dB above its fundamental; (4) the upper partials decay slower than the model's `τ = 1.5 s / (1 + (k − 1)/2)`: partials 5–8 drop 4–13 dB in 0.85 s against the model's 15–22; (5) inharmonicity, fitted through partials 3 and up: low E 1.0·10⁻⁴ (model 1.5·10⁻⁴), A 3–5·10⁻⁵ (10⁻⁴), D 3–4·10⁻⁵ (6·10⁻⁵), G 2–3·10⁻⁵ (4·10⁻⁵), B 6·10⁻⁵ (3·10⁻⁵, a plain string twice as stiff as the model), high E 2·10⁻⁵ (2·10⁻⁵); every real value lies between the model and three times it, which the tests run, so the models stay as they are. The noisy-room takes (a TV 20–25 dB under the pluck's peak across the room) show the right note from the first reading to the end: as each string sank to within about 8 dB of the TV, the detector read a whole number of its periods for 7–16 frames above the gate (G2 and then C2 for the G string, B2 for the B, A2 for the high E), which until 2026-10-29 the needle showed for a third of a second before going blank; the smoother now holds the string through them, since they come without an attack (*Smoothing*, item 1), and the needle goes blank when the detector finds nothing more. The first noisy takes, with the TV within 3–8 dB of the pluck at the phone, were discarded: they drowned the string after half a second. One thing the recordings show that the app shares: the microphone's first 0.15 s after opening are 5–15 dB quieter than the room (50 ms of digital silence, then a ramp), so the noise floor (*Noise floor, SNR and clarity*, a 2 s minimum) reads 10–20 dB low in a noisy room until two seconds of note-free frames have passed; nothing here depends on the floor, and in a quiet room the gate's −50 dBFS is the lower bound anyway.
- **A pluck sinking into a murmur** (Phase 46, 2026-10-29): speech-band noise (80–600 or 150–400 Hz) at −44 or −48 dBFS, on its own never a pitch, with a G3, B3, E4 or G4 pluck (peak −15 dBFS, decaying 0.6 or 0.8 s) sinking into it: the right note within 0.15 s of the pluck, held within 20 cents until the needle goes blank, and nothing after; on the way the detector reads an octave or a twelfth off, down or up, in 1–8 frames of most takes, which the smoother holds (*Smoothing*, item 1); with the rule before 2026-10-29 the needle showed those on every one of the four strings. *Known limit, found with a synthetic voice:* a steady harmonic sound within a few per cent of the string's subharmonic (a hum, a held vowel at 98 Hz under the G string), even 25–30 dB under the string, flips the detector itself to the subharmonic from the first frame: its *too short* check can't tell that mixture from a string with faint odd partials, and if the needle starts there it stays until the sound moves away. A real voice glides and wobbles too much to do it (the TV in the recordings never did), so the detector is left as it is.
- **Sustain decay:** stays within 1 cent until the gate closes (once); over −60 dBFS room noise the last frames wobble up to 6 cents, never another note.
- **Bluetooth-like audio** (300–3400 Hz or 100–7000 Hz band, native 8 or 16 kHz): within 1 cent.
- **Broken and clipped input** (Phase 65): a NaN, +∞ or −∞ every 0.1 s in a ringing string, in both ranges, never loses the reading and stays within 1 cent; a pluck 4 or 12 times too loud for the input and a steady tone 8 times too loud, cut off flat at full scale, within 1 cent, never another octave.
