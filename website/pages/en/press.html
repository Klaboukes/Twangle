In short

Twangle is a guitar tuner for Android that also tunes bass, ukulele, violin and more, with a metronome and practice tools, and a tuner and metronome for Wear OS watches. Every feature is free: no ads, no purchases, no account and no tracking. Twangle has no permission to use the internet, so the sound from the microphone is analysed live on the phone and never recorded or sent anywhere.

What it does

  • Tunes to a cent. Play a string and a big meter shows how far off it is; the word under it, Tighten or Loosen, says which way to turn the peg. Guitar (6, 7, 8 and 12 strings), bass, ukulele, violin, viola, cello, mandolin and banjo, alternate tunings, your own tunings, and a chromatic mode for anything else.
  • Listens with you. Strum all strings at once to see which are off, check the intonation at the 12th fret, see the name of a chord you strum, or follow a held note's pitch as a line.
  • Helps you practise. A metronome from 30 to 250 beats a minute with a speed trainer, chord charts and scales for your tuning, a capo helper and ear training.
  • On the watch. A tuner that listens with the watch's own microphone, no phone needed, and a metronome that taps the beat on your wrist.
  • For everyone. It speaks with TalkBack without talking over the string, guides you by vibration alone if you like, has a high-contrast look and works with text twice the normal size.

Each tool is explained on its own page.

How it is made

Twangle is made by one person, a software developer who plays guitar and had never made an Android app before. The code is written with Claude, an AI by Anthropic: the developer decides what Twangle does and how it looks and feels, and tests every version on real phones, a tablet and a watch, with a real guitar; Claude writes the code. The code, and the rules it is written by, are public. The longer story is on the home page: Why I made Twangle.
