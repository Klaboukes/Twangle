A tuning guide without words

For a player who can't see the screen. TalkBack needs 1–1.5 s for "12 cents flat" while the string rings 2–4 s, so the guide says only how far off and which way, all the time, without words. Each button plays one string drifting into tune; it stops by itself after 8 s.

What blind players say

  • Vibration is what they praise. A Roadie user tunes by vibration so no sound has to come out, a whole electric guitar in under 25 s (AppleVis). Anthony Ferraro couldn't tune alone until a tuner pedal was modded to vibrate under his foot (Guitar World, March 2026).
  • Speed matters. On stage there are 10–20 s between songs (same AppleVis thread).
  • Noise is the main complaint about phone tuners: "decent as long as you don't have anything else making sound in the room" (AppleVis). A guide that adds sound adds to that; vibration adds none.
  • Where it's felt matters. Roadie buzzes at the peg, Ferraro's pedal under the foot. A phone on a table is heard more than felt.
  • Direction, as others do it. HapTone: a short pulse is too low, a long pulse too high, a brief pulse in tune (Blind Systems). A home-made tuner clicks in an earpiece, faster as the string gets closer (Hackaday).
  • Speech that waits. Talking Tuner speaks only once the note has ended, so it never talks over the string (AppleVis).
  • Left out: StroboPro's steady guiding tone. It would put the very note being tuned into the microphone.

The rate rule, the same for every guide: the gap after each pulse goes from 600 ms at 30 cents off or more down to 120 ms at 3 cents, in a straight line. Within ±3 cents for 0.2 s it is in tune: one long buzz (or the chime), then quiet while the string holds. It starts again once the string drifts more than 6 cents off.

Vibration (heard here as a soft thump)

The phone in a pocket or on the lap: felt, not heard, so it adds no sound to the room.

a · Rate only. Quicker means closer, but which way? Try "from sharp": the overshoot feels the same.
b · Single and double. One pulse: too low, tighten. Two pulses: too high, loosen.
c · Short and long. A short pulse: too low, tighten. A long pulse: too high, loosen (HapTone's way).

Ticks

Short ticks by the same rule: a low tick is too low (tighten), a high tick too high (loosen), a short chime in tune. Through the phone's speaker the tuner sets each tick and a short tail aside, as it does the reference tone, so it never reads its own tick; through earbuds the microphone never hears them (the Hackaday tuner's way).

Where the phone is

In a pocket or on the lap, not on the table, where a vibration is heard more than felt (and the microphone hears it too). Later, the watch on the hand that turns the peg is the best spot of all.
