## Phase 44 — Stiff strings: the tuner's last few cents (the accuracy review of 2026-10-24)
Goal: the tuner and the 12th-fret check read a real, stiff string as well as they read a synthetic one. A real string is stiff, so its overtones sit a little sharp of exact multiples of the fundamental (inharmonicity, Fletcher 1964: partial k at `k·f₀·√(1 + B·k²)`), and YIN, which uses the whole waveform, is pulled sharp by them. Modelled on 2026-10-24 with `StrumChords.stiffStrum` and the stiffness the strum tests already assume (B = 1.5·10⁻⁴ for the low E, down to 2·10⁻⁵ for the high E), what the needle shows after a hard pick attack on the low E: **+8.5 cents at 0.25 s, +3.6 at 0.5 s, +1.0 at 1 s, +0.5 at 1.5 s**, sliding down as the overtones die away (the same pluck without stiffness: −0.2 to 0.0; a finger pluck, partials at 1/k², stays under 0.5 throughout). A note at the 12th fret has half the string's length and so four times its B, so the 12th-fret check, which takes 0.25–1.25 s of each note, would report a well set-up guitar played with a pick as **+4.3 cents on the low E** ("move the saddle back 1.5 mm"), +3.2 on the A and +3.1 on the G, all past its 3-cent tolerance; with a finger, under 1 cent on every string. The strum check and the chord name are tested with stiff strings (`StrumAccuracyReport`, `ChordAccuracyReport`); the tuner isn't. The owner's live checks passed, so this matters most for players with a pick and for the Precise setting (2 cents). Everything here is `:core:dsp` (hard rules 1, 2 and 6); nothing changes on screen. In order:
- ☐ **Stiff-string tests first:** `EdgeCaseTest` cases from `StrumChords.stiffStrum`'s model for every string of the standard tuning at the strum tests' B values and at three times them, plucked with a pick (partials at 1/k) and a finger (1/k²): the shown reading 0.5 s and 1 s after the pluck within 1 cent of the fundamental (`f₀·√(1 + B)`, as `StrumDetectorTest` defines it); and `IntonationTest` with the fretted note at 4× the open string's B: `FINE` on every string, the offset within the 3-cent tolerance. They fail today (the low E reads +3.6 at 0.5 s); they define done for the next item
- ☐ **The refinement on the lowest partials:** after YIN has chosen the period (its threshold, its whole-period checks and its above-range check stay exactly as they are, so the octave decisions don't change), the sub-sample refinement (step 5) runs again on the frame limited to the lowest three or four partials of that note: a low-pass at about 3.5 × f₀ (a Butterworth's start-up transient is long at such a cutoff, so likely through the frequency domain instead, since `FftDifference` already holds the frame's spectrum: the band-limited cross-correlation's peak near τ, refined with the same cubic fit). Measured in this review: limiting E4 to three partials took its pick-attack bias from +2.5 to +0.5 cents at B = 1.5·10⁻⁴, with the 20 dB SNR precision still under 0.5 cent. Allocation-free; every existing accuracy test still within its limit (sines ≈ 0.1 cent, 20 dB SNR under 1, 10 dB under 5, no octave errors, every sample rate, the low range); the stiff-string tests pass; `YinBenchmark` and `scripts/dsp-phone-benchmark.sh` for the cost per frame against [docs/architecture/performance.md](docs/architecture/performance.md); the YIN section of [docs/architecture/signal-pipeline.md](docs/architecture/signal-pipeline.md) and the Intonation section of [docs/architecture/notes-and-audio.md](docs/architecture/notes-and-audio.md) say how the refinement works and lose their stiff-string known limits
- ☐ **Real recordings in the tests:** every accuracy figure so far rests on synthetic sound (`core/dsp/src/test/resources/fixtures/` is empty). First a recorder for the test phone that is not Twangle (an instrumented test in `:core:testing` run by `scripts/record-plucks.sh`, writing 16-bit mono WAV files to the phone's storage and pulling them with adb), so hard rule 7 stands untouched. Then **the owner** (Claude says when the script is ready) plays each open string of a real guitar a few times in front of the Xiaomi, hard with a pick and softly with a finger, and the files go into the fixtures folder (a few MB, in git). A test runs them through `TunerChain`: the right note from the first shown frame, no octave flips, and the reading 0.5 s after the pluck within 2 cents of the reading 2 s in (the settled fundamental); `RealPluckReport` (print-only, with the benchmarks) prints each pluck's trajectory for the docs. The *Accuracy target* line of the signal-pipeline doc gets the measured figure in place of the ±3 cents target, and the pick and finger partial models in the tests are checked against what the recordings show
- ☐ **Mains hum notch for the low range** (verified for real only with the bass from *Owner checks*, Phase 15; until then synthetic): in `PitchRange.LOW` alone, where 50 and 60 Hz are inside the range (today hum 10 dB below a B0 string pulls it up to 8 cents, 2–3 cents from E1 up, and hum alone shows as G1 or B♭1), narrow notches at 50 and 60 Hz before YIN, and at their 2nd and 3rd harmonics only where the measurements show they are needed, each so narrow that the nearest notes (G1 49.0 Hz, B1 61.7, G2 98.0, B2 123.5) stay within 0.1 cent and a semitone-flat string still reads. `LowPitchRangeTest`: hum 6 dB below every low string within 1 cent, hum alone nothing, every existing low-range figure kept; the *Low notes* and *Edge cases* sections of the signal-pipeline doc updated; the guitar's standard range is left as it is (60 Hz already lies below it)

## Phase 47 — Getting found, before launch (the growth plan of 2026-10-25)
Goal: guitarists who already have a tuner find Twangle and switch to it. Nearly everyone finds a tuner by searching Play for "guitar tuner", and nobody looks for a new one while the old one works, so the plan is to rank in Play search (by rating, number of reviews, installs and how many keep the app) and to be there when players are fed up with ads, paid tunings, subscriptions and needing the internet (the most common complaints in the big tuners' reviews, comparison of 2026-10-25). The message everywhere: every feature free, no ads, works offline, nothing leaves the phone. No paid advertising (it never pays back, and doesn't fit *free forever*), no buying or swapping reviews and no rewards for a rating (Google removes apps for that), no other app named in the listing or criticised by name in posts. Nothing in the app measures anything (hard rule 9): Play Console's own reports (installs and uninstalls per country, the search terms that brought visitors, store-listing conversion, ratings) and `scripts/release.sh health` and `reviews` are enough. Claude writes the texts, in every language where it helps; **the owner** posts and sends them under their own name, and does the Play Console steps. This phase is the part that must be ready before launch (owner, 2026-10-25: ahead of Phases 45 and 46, so it is settled by the time v1.0 goes to production); the rest follows launch in Phase 48. In order:
- ☐ **Christmas in mind:** many guitars are gifts, so late December into January is very likely the busiest time for tuner searches (Claude checks it in Google Trends for a few languages and notes the figures in [docs/store-listing.md](docs/store-listing.md)). ## Phase 45 — A choice of metronome clicks
Goal: the metronome can sound in more than the wood block, so it can be heard over a loud amp or a band and stays pleasant through a long practice. The owner (2026-10-25) chose four sounds, wood block, cowbell, claves and rim click, picked in the metronome itself, not in Settings; a rough mock-up, [docs/design/metronome-sound-preview.html](docs/design/metronome-sound-preview.html), decides how. Every click keeps the accents as today (beat 1 higher and louder, the group beats in between) and the peak under 0.8; the clicks are 80–180 ms long, still under a beat at 250 BPM (240 ms), so `ClickTrack` keeps one voice and timing, beat lights and vibration don't change. In order:
- ☑ **Hear them first:** three or four candidates made in `ClickSounds`' way (a few decaying partials, no recordings, so nothing to licence), e.g. a soft electronic beep (a sine with a quick decay), a sharp high click that cuts through an amp, and a low, round knock for players who hear high sounds poorly. Rendered as short WAV files (a bar of 4/4 at 100 BPM each, then silence, so nothing loops) into `docs/design/` with a small page to play them side by side with the wood block. **The owner** listens on the phone's speaker and in headphones and picks the two (or three) that join the wood block *The owner's choice (2026-10-25), after several rounds: four instruments, each clearly its own, picked with a Sound button in the metronome: today's wood block (the default), the cowbell, the claves and the rim click. Round 1 (soft beep, sharp click, low knock) sounded "like a cheap keyboard"; round 2 (real instruments ringing 0.2–0.4 s in a little room) was too much instrument; round 3 made clicks to keep time by (a sharp attack, short, the same every beat, beat 1 hit harder and an octave up), but tuned to nearly the same notes they sounded like one instrument. Now each lives in its own register and keeps its nature: the cowbell a choked, clanging metal bell (tone 560 Hz, beating partials to 3 kHz, 40 dB down at 132 ms), the claves a glassy rosewood tone (2.2 kHz, 100 ms), the rim click a dry, noisy crack with a short drum thump (64 ms), the wood block as it is (880 Hz, 36 ms). Beat 1 is +5.1–5.4 dB at the peak and an octave up (claves: a fifth); the clicks are 80–180 ms long, under a beat at 250 BPM (240 ms), so `ClickTrack` keeps one voice.
