## Owner checks (live tests)
Done by the owner by hand, with a real guitar or headset. They are not roadmap increments: `/roadmap-step` skips them.

- ☐ Phase 3: tested on at least 2 physical devices with a real guitar
- ☐ Phase 5: with a real Bluetooth headset connected, the tuner still listens through the phone's mic and reads a string correctly
- ☑ Phase 11: on the test phone, compare the wave breathing in tune with it holding still, using the temporary switch, and pick one; Claude then removes the other and the switch (owner, 2026-10-15: hold perfectly still; the switch goes in Phase 26)
- ☐ Phase 12: with a real guitar, check the tips feel right: none while playing normally near the phone, "Play a bit louder" when playing very softly from across the room, "Too loud, move the phone back" when strumming hard right next to it
- ☐ Phase 14: try each of the six styles on the phone, light and dark, upright and on its side, and say if any colour, letter or background picture feels wrong
- ☐ Phase 15: with a real bass (or a 7-string guitar or cello), check the lowest strings read correctly (B0, E1, B1, C2), and that the tuner shows no note while the amp only hums
- ☐ Phase 16: make a tuning of your own ("New tuning" in the instrument and tuning panel), tune a real guitar to it, then change it with Edit and delete it
- ☑ Phase 17: hold each string of a real guitar's tuning and tune by ear to the tone; check it sounds like a plucked string, at a comfortable loudness, and fades out by itself (owner, 2026-10-14: good enough for now)
- ☐ Phase 18: with a real guitar in a quiet room, switch on strum check (the strings button beside "Guitar · Standard"), strum all six open strings and check each one reads like the tuner does when you play it alone; then detune one or two strings a little and leave one out (mute it). Try it in drop D too
- ☐ Phase 19: play along with the metronome for a few minutes with a real guitar, at a few tempos and meters: check it feels steady, the accents help you find beat 1, and the clicks are pleasant at a comfortable loudness
- ☐ Phase 20: when you have a Wear OS watch, install Twangle on it and tune a real guitar with it: the note and cents follow the string, the ring glows softly when a string is in tune, and when the watch screen goes dark (or dims to always-on) the microphone icon disappears
- ☐ Phase 22: native speakers look over the new translations where possible
- ☐ Phase 25: tune a real guitar with each of the six meter looks, light and dark, and say if any feels hard to read or too busy
- ☐ Ticks and clicks: with no string ringing, tap the desk, click a pen and let a clock tick next to the phone: the needle stays still. Then tune a real guitar and check a plucked string still shows promptly and holds steady while you tap; the G string stays on G3 (it used to jump to E4)
- ☐ Phase 26: with the metronome playing, unplug wired/Bluetooth headphones (it stops) and take a phone call (it stops)
- ☐ Phase 27: play a few songs from a speaker (slow, medium, fast, and one with a loose feel) and let the metronome listen: the tempo it finds matches the song, and clicking along feels right

## Ideas for after v1.0
Not planned yet: ideas the owner wants written down, to pick up after launch only if their condition is met. Each becomes a numbered phase once it's chosen.

### Custom themes (idea, 2026-10-16)
Let people make their own look on top of the six styles. **Condition:** start only if several closed-test testers or early users ask to change the look. Rules that stay: no internet, no account, readable by the same rule as the six palettes (`StylePaletteTest`), calm by default. The six built-in backgrounds stay drawn in code (they fit every screen, have light, dark and quiet versions, and weigh almost nothing).
1. ☐ **Your own background photo with matching colours:** a "Make your own" card at the end of the style choices; the photo comes from Android's photo picker (no permission), is copied into the app's own storage, shrunk to the screen once, dimmed or softly blurred under a veil of the theme's colour (stronger behind Settings) so the needle and note stay readable; the theme's colours are taken from the photo, like Material You does from the wallpaper. The widget and watch keep their built-in look.
2. ☐ **A small theme builder** around that card's live preview: background (a built-in picture or your photo), main colour, lettering, meter look, light / dark / follow the phone. Twangle works out the other colours and keeps them readable; no editing of every colour one by one.
3. ☐ **On the shelf, only if 1 and 2 prove popular:** a theme code (a short text to save and share themes; a theme with a photo needs a small file instead) and a round trip with the user's own AI app: Twangle shares instructions through Android's share menu, the user chats with any AI, copies the code it gives, and Twangle offers to preview it from the clipboard. Never a live connection (that would need internet and an account).

### More music tools (idea, 2026-10-16)
Tools the owner picked from a brainstorm, beyond the tuner and metronome. **Condition:** none yet; pick them up after v1.0, one at a time, the owner choosing the order. Rules that stay: no internet, no account, audio never saved, every text translated, TalkBack and 2× text from the start, nothing added to the tuner screen's clutter.
1. ☐ **Intonation check:** tells whether a guitar plays in tune up the neck. Play the open string, then the same string at the 12th fret; Twangle compares the two and says to move that string's saddle back (12th fret sharp) or forward (flat), or that it's fine. One string at a time, with the existing pitch detector
2. ☐ **"What chord is this?":** strum a chord and Twangle names it (e.g. "A minor"), building on the strum check's six-strings-at-once detection, pointed at chords instead of tuning; synthetic chord tests in `:core:dsp` like the strum check's
3. ☐ **Pitch trail:** a line that draws the note over the last few seconds, so a held note, a bend or vibrato shows how steady it is; also for singers and violinists. Drawn in the draw phase only, like the strobe, with preallocated history
4. ☐ **Chord and scale charts:** finger diagrams for the chosen instrument and tuning, own tunings included (a DADGAD player sees DADGAD shapes), and scales on a fretboard; no microphone needed
5. ☐ **Capo helper:** "the song is in E♭, where does the capo go to play G shapes?", and which shapes sound which chord with the capo on; pairs with the chord charts

**Where they go in the app** (owner agreed, 2026-10-16; the direction, decided for real when the first of these tools is picked): the bar keeps four items. Five would leave each name about 78 dp on the Xiaomi instead of about 98, too little for long words in 16 languages at 2× text (Dutch "Instellingen" already shrinks with four), and would crowd a calm screen.
- The three tools that listen (1–3) become modes inside the tuner, next to strings, chromatic and strum check; the bar doesn't change for them.
- The Metronome tab becomes **Practice**, holding the Metronome, Chords (4) and the Capo helper (5), with a small switch at the top like the tuner's mode switch. It opens on the tool used last (saved), so someone who mostly uses the metronome still reaches it in one tap. The bar becomes Tuner · Instrument · Practice · Settings. Chords and the Capo helper follow the instrument picked in the Instrument item.
- Later tools that don't listen (ear training, a drone, a practice timer) go into Practice too, so the bar doesn't change again.
