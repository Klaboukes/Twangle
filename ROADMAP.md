## Owner checks (live tests)
Done by the owner by hand, with a real guitar or headset. They are not roadmap increments: `/roadmap-step` skips them.

- ☐ Phase 3: tested on at least 2 physical devices with a real guitar
- ☐ Phase 5: with a real Bluetooth headset connected, the tuner still listens through the phone's mic and reads a string correctly
- ☑ Phase 11: on the test phone, compare the wave breathing in tune with it holding still, using the temporary switch, and pick one; Claude then removes the other and the switch (owner, 2026-10-15: hold perfectly still; the switch goes in Phase 26)
- ☐ Phase 12: with a real guitar, check the tips feel right: none while playing normally near the phone, "Play a bit louder" when playing very softly from across the room, "Too loud, move the phone back" when strumming hard right next to it
- ☐ Phase 14: try each of the six styles on the phone, light and dark, upright and on its side, and say if any colour, letter or background picture feels wrong
- ☐ Phase 15: with a real bass (or a 7-string guitar or cello), check the lowest strings read correctly (B0, E1, B1, C2), and that the tuner shows no note while the amp only hums
- ☐ Phase 16: make a tuning of your own ("New tuning" in the instrument and tuning panel), tune a real guitar to it, then change it with Edit and delete it
- ☑ Phase 17: hold each string of a real guitar's tuning and tune by ear to the tone; check it sounds like a plucked string, at a comfortable loudness, and fades out by itself (owner, 2026-10-14: good enough for now)
- ☐ Phase 18: with a real guitar in a quiet room, switch on strum check (the strings button beside "Guitar · Standard"), strum all six open strings and check each one reads like the tuner does when you play it alone; then detune one or two strings a little and leave one out (mute it). Try it in drop D too
- ☐ Phase 19: play along with the metronome for a few minutes with a real guitar, at a few tempos and meters: check it feels steady, the accents help you find beat 1, and the clicks are pleasant at a comfortable loudness
- ☐ Phase 20: when you have a Wear OS watch, install Twangle on it and tune a real guitar with it: the note and cents follow the string, the ring glows softly when a string is in tune, and when the watch screen goes dark (or dims to always-on) the microphone icon disappears
- ☐ Phase 22: native speakers look over the new translations where possible
- ☐ Phase 25: tune a real guitar with each of the six meter looks, light and dark, and say if any feels hard to read or too busy
- ☐ Ticks and clicks: with no string ringing, tap the desk, click a pen and let a clock tick next to the phone: the needle stays still. Then tune a real guitar and check a plucked string still shows promptly and holds steady while you tap; the G string stays on G3 (it used to jump to E4)
- ☐ Phase 26: with the metronome playing, unplug wired/Bluetooth headphones (it stops) and take a phone call (it stops)
- ☐ Phase 27: play a few songs from a speaker (slow, medium, fast, and one with a loose feel) and let the metronome listen: the tempo it finds matches the song, and clicking along feels right

## Phase 27 — Tempo from music
Goal: the metronome can listen to music playing nearby (from a speaker, a band, or the phone itself) and set its tempo to match, so a player can click along with a song or backing track. Added by the owner on 2026-10-15, before v1.0. Twangle can't hear other apps' audio inside the phone (Android allows that only with a screen-recording prompt, and most music apps block it), so it listens through the microphone, like the tuner. Audio stays in memory only, as everywhere in Twangle.
- ☑ Design first: a mock-up of the metronome screen with a "Listen" button beside Tap tempo, what it shows while listening and when it has found a tempo, and how the player accepts it. Owner approves before building (approved mock-up [docs/design/metronome-listen-preview.html](docs/design/metronome-listen-preview.html), its top comment is the spec; **Owner decided (2026-10-15)** after five rounds: Listen beside Tap tempo as two equal fixed-size buttons, − / + in the dial's bottom corners; a found tempo is suggested in a balloon (½× / 2×, Use, Not now) and nothing changes until Use, a stopped metronome stays stopped; while listening a subtle trace runs round the Listen button's edge and a small ring on the dial marks the tempo it is settling on; a new press effect on every button, lit on touch-down and fading over ¼ s, with a crisp flash for Tap tempo; clicks in step with the song not in scope)
- ☑ Tempo detector in `:core:dsp` (pure, allocation-free): an onset-strength envelope from the microphone frames and a tempo estimate from its periodicity (e.g. autocorrelation as in Scheirer 1998 / Ellis 2007), whole BPM within 30–250, steady enough to show without jumping, and a sensible pick between half and double tempo; synthetic tests (click tracks and drum-like patterns at known tempos, with noise) within ±1 BPM (`OnsetTempoDetector`: click tracks 30–250, drum grooves 70–200, swing, loose timing, a bass line, 8–96 kHz all within ±1 BPM, most within 0.1, after 14 s; first tempo by 3–5 s, steady by 7–10 s; noise and silence never show one; also tells the beat moments, within 6 ms, and the half/double choice; 0.1 ms per mic chunk on the JVM)
- ☐ Listen on the metronome screen: asks for the microphone the same way the tuner does, holds it only while listening (stops on its own once the tempo is steady, after about 15 s, or when the screen is left), pauses the metronome's own clicks while listening, and sets the tempo as the mock-up says; with the mock-up's new layout (− / + at the dial's bottom corners, Tap tempo and Listen as two equal fixed-size buttons) and its new press effect on every metronome button, built as one shared Modifier in `:core:designsystem`, with Tap tempo's crisp flash; translated in every language; JVM tests with a fake recorder, UI tests in the overnight run
- ☐ The same press effect on every other button of the app (tuner, tuning panel and editor, settings, About, tour balloons, dialogs), replacing Material's ripple; each style, light and dark; UI tests in the overnight run (owner asked for this, 2026-10-15)
- ☐ Phone check with real music from a speaker at a few tempos; the owner's live check is under *Owner checks*
