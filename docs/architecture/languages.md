## Languages and registers
**English (default), Dutch, German, French, Spanish, Italian, Portuguese (Brazil), Chinese (Simplified), Japanese, Korean, Hindi, Indonesian, Russian, Turkish, Polish and Arabic.** Register: German, Spanish, Italian and Dutch use the informal you (du, tú, tu, je), French uses vous, Brazilian Portuguese você. French puts a no-break space (U+00A0) before : and ? and inside « ». Brazilian Portuguese calls the guitar "Violão" ("guitarra" is the electric one). Chinese uses 你, Japanese polite です/ます for sentences and short plain labels, Korean 해요체, Hindi आप with the loanwords Indian guitarists use (ट्यूनर, ट्यूनिंग, स्ट्रम), Indonesian Anda and the guitarist's *stem* (tuning), *genjreng* (strum), *kres*/*mol* (♯/♭). Chinese says a sharp before the letter (升C) and reads 2/4 as 四二拍. Chinese, Japanese, Korean and Indonesian have only the plural quantity "other"; Hindi has "one" and "other". Russian and Polish use the informal ты/ty and their guitarist words (тюнер, строй, подтяни/ослабь; stroik, strój, podciągnij/popuść); Turkish uses the short sen imperative (akort, tel, yükselt/düşür). Russian and Polish plurals have one/few/many/other (1 цент, 2 цента, 5 центов, "other" for fractions; 1 cent, 2 centy, 5 centów, 12 centów), Turkish one/other with the noun in the singular (12 sent). Russian writes Гц for Hz. Text set in capitals in code uses the app's locale (`uppercase(LocalConfiguration.current.locales[0])`), so Turkish i becomes İ; Kotlin's plain `uppercase()` is locale-free. Cyrillic is only in Playfair Display, Forum and Alegreya Sans, so in Russian the titles of Retro, Futuristic and Space Age come from the phone's font (whole words, since every letter falls back); Orbitron (Futuristic) lacks ğ İ ş and the Polish ą ć ę ł ń ś ź ż, so in Turkish and Polish Futuristic sets its titles in Michroma, and no word mixes two faces ([Meter, wave and styles](ui-meter-and-styles.md), *Visual styles*, **Lettering**). No style font covers Han, Kana, Hangul or Devanagari, so those glyphs come from the phone's own fonts (Android falls back per glyph), without the styles' extra letter spacing. Indonesian's folder is `values-in` (Android's old code; a phone set to `id` resolves to it). Arabic is Modern Standard Arabic with the masculine imperative of app texts (اعزف وترًا, اختر وترًا) and the words Arab guitarists use: الموالف (tuner), الدوزان (tuning), وتر/أوتار (string/strings), شُدّ/أرخِ (tune up/down), دييز/بيمول (♯/♭ spoken), اضرب (strum); Drop D, Open G and Low G stay in Latin letters, like Russian. Its plurals use all six quantities (zero, one, two, few, many, other: ٠ سنت, بسنت واحد, بسنتين, ٣ سنتات, ١١ سنتًا, ١٠٠ سنت). No style font has Arabic, so it comes from the phone's font. Each module keeps its texts in `res/values/strings.xml` with a copy per language in `res/values-<language>/strings.xml`; brand and symbols (`app_name`, `DADGAD`, ♯/♭, ♪) are `translatable="false"`. Font and library names are proper names and stay in English.

## One language list
`twangle.languages` in the root `gradle.properties` (`en,nl,de,fr,es,it,pt-rBR,zh-rCN,ja,ko,hi,in,ru,tr,pl,ar`), as Android resource qualifiers (`de`, `pt-rBR`, `zh-rCN`, `b+sr+Latn`). The first is the default, whose texts are in `res/values/`.

## In-app language (Settings → Language)
the same choice as phone Settings → Apps → Twangle → Language, made inside the app, on every Android version. `SettingsScreen` reads and sets it with AppCompat's `AppCompatDelegate.getApplicationLocales` / `setApplicationLocales`: on Android 13+ that is the system's per-app language (so both places always agree), on Android 8–12 AppCompat keeps it in a file of its own (`autoStoreLocales` on `AppLocalesMetadataHolderService` in `:app`'s manifest) and sets it on the activity, which is why `MainActivity` is an `AppCompatActivity` (its window theme `Theme.Twangle` derives from `Theme.AppCompat.Light.NoActionBar`, and `Theme.AppCompat.NoActionBar` in dark mode; Compose draws everything, so nothing else of AppCompat shows). The languages come from `BuildConfig.LANGUAGES`, which `:app`'s build file fills from `twangle.languages` like `localeFilters`, so the setting offers exactly the system list (`LocalizationTest` checks the build file). `AppLanguages` (`:feature:settings`, JVM-tested in `AppLanguagesTest`) turns qualifiers into language tags (`pt-rBR` → `pt-BR`), names each language in its own words with a capital ("Polski", "Português (Brasil)"; Chinese by its script, 简体中文, as Android's own list does) and matches a regional variant picked in phone Settings to its language. Names are not translated, and each carries its language (a `SpanStyle` `localeList`, which reaches TalkBack as a `LocaleSpan`), so TalkBack reads "Polski" in Polish and Han characters get their Chinese or Japanese forms. A switch recreates the activity; texts, digits and the note-name default (`appLanguage()`, from the configuration) follow at once, and Settings stays open. The home-screen widget is redrawn in the new language (`onLanguageChange` → `TwangleApplication.redrawWidgets`; Android 13+ also through `onConfigurationChanged`); on Android 8–12 it takes the language from AppCompat (`TuningWidget.inAppLanguage`), so in a process started for the widget alone, before the app has been opened since the phone started, it shows the phone's language until then. The Play Store bundle doesn't split by language (`bundle.language.enableSplit = false`), so every language is installed and one the phone doesn't use works at once. The watch app has no setting and follows the watch's language.

## Per-app language (Android 13+)
`:app` sets `androidResources.generateLocaleConfig = true` with `res/resources.properties` (`unqualifiedResLocale=en`). Both `:app` and `:wear` set `localeFilters` from `twangle.languages`, so the generated locale config (the in-app language list) holds exactly the listed languages, and library texts never appear in an unlisted one. The locale config takes a language from any resource, libraries included (Compose and AndroidX ship most languages), so a listed language shows up in the list even before Twangle's own texts exist; the guard below is what keeps the list honest.

## Formatting
plurals for counts (`tuner_cents_flat/sharp`) and placeholders for numbers (`%1$.1f Hz`), so Dutch shows "440,0 Hz". **Digits** follow the app's language as Android's resources format them (`String.format` in the app locale): Arabic gets Arabic-Indic digits (٤٤٠ هرتز). Numbers the app draws itself (the meter scales, the tempo dial and its BPM, the beat numbers, the time signatures ٣/٤, the watch's "+١٢") go through `localDigits` (`:core:designsystem`; `MeterRoles.digits`), so every number on a screen uses the same digits; literal numbers in an Arabic text are written ٠–٩ too. Note names keep their octave in 0–9 (A4, E♭2): they are Latin symbols, and a Western digit after a letter stays in the letter's left-to-right run (Unicode bidi rule W7), so "E♭2" can't come apart inside Arabic text, where an Arabic-Indic one would move the ♭ in front. Note names are not translated; they follow the notation setting, whose default follows the language ([Notes, tunings and audio](notes-and-audio.md), **Notation by language**). Chinese, Japanese and Korean text may break between any two characters, so a one-word label in a width-sharing row (the mode switch) is measured at its full one-line width, not its narrowest.

## Right to left (Arabic)
both manifests set `android:supportsRtl="true"`, so Compose mirrors the layout: texts, rows, settings, panels, the navigation bar and rail, the mode switch, the meter picker, switches, the tour's buttons; the back arrow and the settings chevrons are `autoMirrored` vector drawables. What stays left to right, after Material's rule not to mirror graphs, gauges and clocks, and because low pitch sits left like a keyboard and a stave: the meter (flat left, sharp right; drawn on a `Canvas`, whose coordinates never mirror), the wave, the strum check, the string row and the style cards' little strings (`CompositionLocalProvider(LocalLayoutDirection provides Ltr)`), the tempo dial (clockwise, like a clock) with − slower left and + faster right below it, the beat lights (counted left to right, as in sheet music), the A4 slider with its − and + (a pitch scale), and the tuning editor's − note + group. A note name with its octave (the big note, the watch's) is also wrapped left to right, so the octave stays right of the letter. Compose sets a paragraph's direction from the layout, not from its text, so a list of note names in a right-to-left screen is given `TextDirection.Ltr` (the panel's open strings, else "E♭ … E♭" puts its last ♭ in front). The play ▶ icon isn't mirrored (Material: media controls keep their direction).

## Guard
`LocalizationTest` (JVM, `:app`) reads `twangle.languages` and fails when:
- a module's English string has no copy in a listed language, or the copy's placeholders differ (for plurals: the "other" item must match, every other item may only use placeholders of "other", and only Android's quantities are allowed);
- a `values-<x>/strings.xml` exists for a language that isn't listed;
- the first language isn't `unqualifiedResLocale`, or an app module doesn't take `localeFilters` from the list;
- Kotlin passes a hard-coded literal to `Text(...)`, `contentDescription`, `stateDescription`, `onClickLabel` and similar.

`StoreListingTest` (JVM, `:app`) holds the Play Store texts in `fastlane/metadata/android/<Play locale>/` to the same list: one folder per language, each with a title, short and full description within Play's limits ([store listing](../store-listing.md), *The texts, ready to paste*).

Every module's `res` and Kotlin sources, the build files, `gradle.properties` and the store texts are declared inputs of `:app`'s unit tests, so changing any of them reruns the test. Lint's `MissingTranslation` and `StringFormat*` back it up.

## Adding a language
append its qualifier to `twangle.languages`, then add `res/values-<language>/strings.xml` to every module that has a `res/values/strings.xml` (today `:app`, `:wear`, the three features and `:core:designsystem`), translating every translatable string and plural. `LocalizationTest` names what is missing. Add its Play Store texts too, `fastlane/metadata/android/<Play locale>/` with `title.txt`, `short_description.txt` and `full_description.txt` translated from `en-US`; `StoreListingTest` names a missing folder. Nothing else changes: the language list on the phone and the watch's texts follow. The `/add-language` skill walks through it, including the note-name default, TalkBack names, fonts and right-to-left checks.
