## Note math
```
midi  = 69 + 12 · log2(f / A4)        (A4 default 440 Hz, configurable 415–466)
note  = ceil(midi − 0.5)               (round half down, so exactly half-way reads +50)
cents = 100 · (midi − note)            range (−50, +50]
```
Scientific pitch notation (MIDI 60 = C4). `Note` is a value class over the MIDI number. `Notation` (`SHARPS`, `FLATS`, `SOLFEGE`, `GERMAN`) names a pitch class with real ♯ and ♭ (C♯ / D♭ / Do♯; fixed-do, B is Si); each entry holds its names in both spellings so `nameOf()` returns the same instances every frame. `GERMAN` calls B "H" and B♭ "B" (Half step down reads E♭ A♭ D♭ G♭ B E♭); its other black keys keep the signs (C♯, E♭), as German players write them even though they say Cis and Es, so only the B/H letters differ from `SHARPS`/`FLATS`.

**Notation by language.** The notation is a default that follows the app's language until the player picks one. `Notation.forLanguage(language)` (`:core:dsp`, JVM-tested) gives `GERMAN` for `de`/`pl`/`ru` (Polish and Russian players write H for B too), `SOLFEGE` for `fr`/`it`/`es`/`pt`/`tr`/`ar` (Turkish and Arab players learn Do Re Mi; Arabic shows the Latin syllables, like every note name, and TalkBack says them in Arabic script), and `SHARPS` for every other language (a new language with its own habit is one line there; owner's rule: what is most common in that country). The saved setting is `AppSettings.notationChoice: Notation?`: null (no saved key) means Automatic, so existing installs that never touched the setting simply get their language's default, and an explicit pick, including the one that equals the default, sticks across language changes. `AppSettings.notationFor(language)` resolves it. The language comes from the screen's configuration (`appLanguage()` in `:core:designsystem`, `LocalConfiguration.locales[0].language`, which follows the per-app language): `TunerViewModel` takes it at creation (so the first frame is right) and the screen passes changes with `setLanguage`; the tuning editor and Settings read it directly. Settings lists Automatic first, labelled with a sample of what it gives ("Automatic: C, C♯, D … B", "Automatique : Do, Do♯, Re … Si", "Automatisch: C, C♯, D … H"; built from `settings_notation_sample`, B's name last because that's where the notations differ); choosing it clears the key. The watch has no notation setting and follows its own locale (`WatchNoteName.letter`), drawing a solfège syllable smaller (`sizeFor`) so "Sol♯4" fits the ring. The home-screen widget shows no note names.

**Tuning spelling.** A preset is written the way players write it, and that wins over the sharps/flats setting: Half step down is E♭ A♭ D♭ G♭ B♭ E♭, Open D keeps F♯. `Tuning.spelling` comes from how the preset is written in `Tunings` (`"Eb2"` flats, `"F#3"` sharps; mixing is refused), and `Notation.nameIn(tuning, …)` applies it in the string row, tuning menu, hint and big note in guitar mode. Chromatic mode follows the setting alone.

**Auto (guitar) mode** targets the closest string of the tuning, in cents; **Chromatic** the nearest equal-tempered note.

## Tunings
`Tuning(id, strings)` lists open-string `Note`s lowest first; `id` is the saved key (never rename). Names live in string resources, so `:core:dsp` has no UI text. `Tunings` holds the presets (Standard, Drop D, Half-step down, Full-step down, DADGAD, Open G, Open D, Open E), `DEFAULT` = Standard, `byId()` to restore.
- `nearestStringIndex(midi)` follows A4 calibration; smallest |Δ| in semitones, ties to the lower string, no allocation.
- `centsFromString(midi, index)` is **not** wrapped to ±50, so a string far off shows the full distance.
- Strings are in instrument order, thickest first. That is lowest pitch first except on re-entrant tunings (standard ukulele G4 C4 E4 A4), so `lowestNote` / `highestNote` scan all strings.

## Instruments
`Instrument(id, stringCount, tunings)` is a stringed instrument with its presets, the first being its standard tuning (`defaultTuning`); every preset must have `stringCount` strings. `Instruments` holds them in picker order, `DEFAULT` = six-string guitar, whose presets are exactly `Tunings.all` with their original ids, so saved settings keep working. Tuning ids are unique across instruments (`<instrument>_standard`, `ukulele_low_g`), so `Instruments.tuningById()` / `ofTuning()` restore a saved tuning and its instrument. A mandolin counts its four pairs as four courses.

| Instrument (`id`) | Strings | Presets | Range |
|---|---|---|---|
| Guitar (`guitar`) | 6 | the eight `Tunings` | D2–E4 |
| 7-string guitar (`guitar_7`) | 7 | B1 E2 A2 D3 G3 B3 E4 | B1–E4 |
| 8-string guitar (`guitar_8`) | 8 | F♯1 B1 E2 A2 D3 G3 B3 E4 | F♯1–E4 |
| Bass (`bass_4`) | 4 | E1 A1 D2 G2 | E1–G2 |
| 5-string bass (`bass_5`) | 5 | B0 E1 A1 D2 G2 | B0 (30.9 Hz)–G2 |
| Ukulele (`ukulele`) | 4 | G4 C4 E4 A4 (re-entrant), low G: G3 C4 E4 A4 | G3–A4 |
| Violin (`violin`) | 4 | G3 D4 A4 E5 | G3–E5 (659 Hz) |
| Viola (`viola`) | 4 | C3 G3 D4 A4 | C3–A4 |
| Cello (`cello`) | 4 | C2 G2 D3 A3 | C2–A3 |
| Mandolin (`mandolin`) | 4 courses | G3 D4 A4 E5 | G3–E5 |

`lowestNote` / `highestNote` span all of an instrument's presets. Tunings whose lowest string goes below the guitar's range (B1, F♯1, E1, B0, and the cello's C2) switch the detector to the low-note window ([Signal pipeline](signal-pipeline.md), *Low notes*). Players pick the instrument and its tuning in one panel ([Tuner screen and tour](ui-tuner.md), **Instrument and tuning**); the instrument is saved with the tuning ([Lifecycle](lifecycle.md), *Saved settings*), and the panel lists only its presets.

**Names** (`PickerNames.kt`, `instrumentNameRes` / `tuningNameRes`, JVM-tested: every instrument and preset has one, none shared within an instrument): English and Dutch — Guitar/Gitaar, 7-string guitar/7-snarige gitaar, 8-string guitar/8-snarige gitaar, Bass/Bas, 5-string bass/5-snarige bas, Ukulele/Ukelele, Violin/Viool, Viola/Altviool, Cello, Mandolin/Mandoline. Every instrument's first preset is "Standard"; the ukulele's other is "Low G"/"Lage G".

## Chord shapes and scales
Pure `:core:dsp` music maths for the Practice tab's charts ([Metronome, widget and watch](ui-other-screens.md), *Practice tab*), worked out for whatever tuning is picked, a preset or the player's own, so a DADGAD player sees DADGAD shapes. Nothing is stored: a chord's shapes take well under a millisecond, so the screen asks again when the chord or tuning changes.

**Chord shapes** (`chord/ChordShapeFinder.shapesOf(chord, tuning, limit = 6)` → `List<ChordShape>`, easiest first). A `ChordShape` holds per string, in instrument order (thickest first), `frets` (`ChordShape.MUTED` = −1, 0 open, else the fret) and `fingers` (1 index … 4 little, 0 for open or muted), and an optional `Barre(fret, firstString, lastString, finger = 1)`. It writes itself as chord books do (`toString()`: "x32010", frets from 10 in brackets), gives `notesIn(tuning)` / `bassIn(tuning)`, and `startFret()` for the chart: 1 (drawn with the nut) when it fits the first four fret rows (`CHART_ROWS`), else its lowest fret ("3fr").
- **Search:** every string muted, open (when its open note is a chord tone) or pressed on a chord tone within `MAX_SPAN` = 3 frets above the lowest fret pressed (four frets in all), up to the 12th. A shape sounds every chord tone and nothing else (7th chords may drop the fifth), mutes strings only on the thick side and plays at least four (all of them on four-string instruments). On five strings or more the bass is the root, except an open tuning's own chord: every string open or under one barre with the fifth in the bass (open G's G is 000000, its C 555555). On four strings an inversion is allowed (ukulele G 0232) but ranks lower.
- **Fingers:** one finger per fret from the 1st fret when open strings ring and that fits exactly (C x32010 = 3 2 1), else from the lowest fret pressed. A barre when more than four strings are pressed (the E and A shapes, F 133211, Bm x24432), or across the last two strings or more pressed side by side at the lowest fret (xx3211, ukulele B♭ 3211). Never more than four fingers, no thumb.
- **Ranking** (`effortOf`, lower is easier): 1 a finger, 0.5 for a barre, 0.5 a fret of stretch, 0.5 a fret up the neck, 0.7 a string left out, 0.25 for a fifth left out, 1 for an inversion, 2 a fret for open strings ringing while a finger is above the 3rd fret, 1 a fret the fingers climb while arching over open strings (`archOf`). Weighed by reading every chord on every preset (`ChordShapeReport`, print-only, `./gradlew :core:dsp:benchmark --tests '*ChordShapeReport*'`) until the first shape is the one chord books show first: standard C x32010, G 320003, D xx0232, A x02220, E 022100, Am x02210, Em 022000, Dm xx0231, F 133211, Bm x24432, E7 020100, C7 x32310, Cmaj7 x32000, Fmaj7 xx3210, Dsus4 xx0233; ukulele C 0003, G 0232, F 2010, Am 2000; mandolin G 0023; DADGAD D 004200.
- **Tests** (`ChordShapeFinderTest`): those first shapes and their fingers, barres and chart start, open tunings and a tuning of the player's own, and for every chord on every preset of every instrument: at least one shape, only chord tones and all the needed ones, within the span, at most four fingers, a finger exactly on each pressed string, the root in the bass on five strings or more.

**Scales** (`scale/Scale`, `scale/ScaleFinder`). `ScaleType`: major, natural minor, major pentatonic, minor pentatonic and blues (the minor pentatonic with the flat fifth). `Scale(root, type)` gives its `notes` from the root up, `degreeOf` (1 = root) and `nameOf(pitchClass, notation)`: the key's sign, from `music/KeySpelling` as chord symbols use (major key for major and major pentatonic, minor key for the rest: G major has F♯, D minor B♭), whatever the notation's own sign; the blues' blue note is always written flat (E blues E G A B♭ B D). `ScaleFinder.notesOf(scale, tuning, highestFret = 12)` → `List<FretNote(string, fret, note, degree)>`, string by string in instrument order, each from the nut up, `isRoot` for the dots filled in the accent: the whole neck from the open strings to the 12th fret, as the owner picked (*Scale*, I). `ScaleTest` checks the spelling of every type, the notation, E minor pentatonic on standard guitar (roots and count), and that on every preset and a tuning of the player's own exactly the scale's notes are found.

## Capo
Pure `:core:dsp` maths for the Practice tab's Capo helper (Phase 33): "the song is in E♭, where does the capo go to play G shapes?", and which chord each shape sounds with the capo on. It reuses the chord model (`Chord`, `ChordSpelling`) and the key signs (`KeySpelling`); nothing is stored.
- **`capo/SongKey(tonic, minor)`**: a key. Its `tonicChord`, its `scale` (major or natural minor), its sign (`accidental`, from `KeySpelling`) and `symbolOf(notation)` ("E♭", "Cm", "Hm", "Mi♭"), so keys read like chord symbols: E♭ not D♯, F♯ and E♭m with six signs.
- **The fret:** a capo raises every string by the same number of frets, one equal-tempered semitone each, so `Capo.fretFor(songKey, shapeTonic) = (song tonic − shape tonic) mod 12`, 0 to `HIGHEST_FRET` = 11 (12 sounds an octave up, the same chords as no capo). Shapes only move up, so a song below the shapes wraps to the next fret round: E♭ is D shapes at 1, C at 3, A at 6, G at 8, E at 11. Minor songs keep minor shapes (Cm with Am shapes at 3).
- **`Capo.optionsFor(songKey)`**: one `CapoPlan` per common shape key, lowest fret first, so the first is the one to suggest (no capo for a song in an open key, else the capo nearest the nut, at most fret 2 for a major song and 4 for a minor one). Major songs get the CAGED keys (`COMMON_MAJOR_SHAPES`: C, A, G, E, D), minor songs the open minor keys (`COMMON_MINOR_SHAPES`: Am, Em, Dm). `Capo.planFor(songKey, shapeTonic)` is the plan for shapes the player picks.
- **`CapoPlan(songKey, fret)`**: `shapeKey` (the song moved down by the fret), `soundingOf(shape)` and `shapeOf(sounding)` (`Chord.transposed(±fret)`; `PitchClass.transposed` wraps round the octave), and the names: `soundingSymbolOf(shape, notation, shapeBass)` in the song's key, `shapeSymbolOf(…)` in the shape key, the bass moving up with the capo (a C/E shape at 3 sounds E♭/G). `Capo.soundingSymbolOf(shape, fret, notation)` names a sounding chord when the key isn't known, in the chord's usual spelling (`ChordSpelling`).
- **Spelling in a key** (`SongKey.symbolOf(chord, bass, notation)`): a root or bass that belongs to the key takes the key's sign, one from outside the chord's usual spelling. So F♯ major's vi is A♯m and D♭ major's IV G♭ (where `ChordSpelling` alone writes B♭m and F♯), while D major's borrowed chord stays B♭. A song in F with the capo at 1 reads shapes E A B C♯m (E's spelling) sounding F B♭ C Dm.
- **Tests** (`CapoTest`): the frets and options for major and minor songs, every key with every shape key, spelling in the key and outside it, the bass, every notation (Mi♭, German B and H), and that `shapeOf` undoes `soundingOf` for every chord at every fret.

## Intonation check
Phase 29 (hidden in release builds until it goes live, Phase 34). The 12th fret halves the string, so it should sound exactly one octave (1200 cents) above the open string. If it is sharp, the sounding length from the fret to the saddle is too short and the saddle moves back, away from the neck; if flat, forward. `intonation/Intonation` in `:core:dsp` is the comparison, pure and JVM-tested:
- `offsetCents(openHz, fretHz) = 1200 · log2(fret / open) − 1200` (positive = sharp).
- `verdictOf(offsetCents, toleranceCents)` gives `FINE`, `MOVE_SADDLE_BACK` (sharp), `MOVE_SADDLE_FORWARD` (flat) or `NOT_THE_OCTAVE`; `check(openHz, fretHz)` returns both as an `IntonationResult`.

**Tolerance: 3 cents** (`DEFAULT_TOLERANCE_CENTS`), its own number, not the tuner's Precise/Relaxed in-tune setting. Setup practice aims for the 12th fret within a few cents of the octave. Each reading is within ±1 cent, so a difference can be 2 cents off, and 3 keeps a well set-up guitar from being told to move. With the open string retuned, moving the saddle back by `d` on a scale of length `L` changes the 12th fret by `1200 · log2((L + d) / (L + 2d)) ≈ −1731 · d / L` cents (frequency ∝ 1 / length): **≈ 2.7 cents per mm** on a 648 mm (25.5″) scale, so 3 cents is about a millimetre of saddle.

**Not the octave:** more than **50 cents** (half a semitone) from the octave, the fretted note is nearer another note: another fret, another string, the open string again, or a misread. Real intonation errors stay far below that (50 cents is a saddle ≈ 20 mm off). A light touch at the 12th fret (the harmonic) sounds the exact octave and reads `FINE`; no reading can tell it from a fretted note, so the mode asks for a pressed note.

**Relative.** Only the two readings are compared, so it works at any A4 and on any instrument's strings, and an open string a little out of tune still gets the right verdict. Players tune first anyway (the open string's tension sets the intonation); the mode checks the open reading is near the chosen string before taking it.

**Readings.** One settled frequency per step, from the tuner's smoothed reading; collecting it is the mode's job: wait until the note is steady, then take the median of what the tuner shows for about a second (`MedianFilter`), so the attack and the dying end don't count.

**Collecting a reading** (`SettledPitch`, `:core:dsp`, JVM-tested): fed the tuner's smoothed pitch each frame (a `PitchSmoother`, as the tuner's), it waits until the note is steady (0.25 s of readings each within 20 cents of the one before: the pluck's glide is over), then keeps 1 s of readings within 20 cents of the first kept one and takes their median; a jump further, or more than 0.15 s without a reading, starts over. Its arrays are allocated once (hard rule 2). `IntonationInterpreter` (`:feature:tuner`) goes through the steps with it:
- **Open:** a reading an octave off the chosen string (a weak fundamental read high) is folded onto it. Taken within **10 cents** of the string (`OPEN_TOLERANCE_CENTS`: 10 cents is 1.2 % of tension, and the comparison is relative anyway); further off, the line asks to tune the string first, with how far off it is, and the next steady note is taken instead.
- **12th fret:** readings within 50 cents of the open note are the open string still ringing (or played again) and are ignored; so is the open string where it was heard an octave up, until a new pluck (a dropout, or a frame 6 dB louder than the one before). A reading two octaves up is folded onto the octave. Not the octave: the line says so and waits for the fret again; otherwise the verdict, kept per string (`IntonationResults`, held by the ViewModel across listening sessions, cleared by a new tuning).

**Millimetres** (`SaddleMove`, `:core:dsp`, JVM-tested): a 12th fret `c` cents sharp needs the saddle back by `d` with `(L + d) / (L + 2d) = 2^(−c / 1200)`, so `d = L · (1 − r) / (2r − 1)` with `r = 2^(−c / 1200)`; for small `c` that is `d ≈ c · L · ln 2 / 1200`, 2.7 cents per mm on 648 mm. Twangle doesn't know the scale length, so millimetres show only for the guitar family (`IntonationInstruments.scaleLengthMm`: 648 mm, 25.5″), whose scales lie within a few percent of it (24″ 610 mm to 27″ 686 mm move a 3 mm answer by under 0.2 mm), rounded to the nearest 0.5 mm, and below 0.5 mm as "less than 0.5 mm". Basses (760–890 mm), ukuleles (330–480 mm) and mandolins (a bridge moved whole) get the direction only. The check is offered only on fretted instruments with a saddle (`IntonationInstruments.offers`: guitars, basses, ukulele, mandolin; not the violin, viola or cello, which have no frets). The tuner's view: [Tuner screen and tour](ui-tuner.md), *12th-fret check view*.

**Measured** (`IntonationTest`, synthetic plucks, 48 kHz): every string of every instrument's standard tuning (B0 to E5, the low window where the instrument needs it), the 12th fret ±4, ±8 and ±25 cents off and within ±1.5: every verdict right, the offset within **0.38 cents** from one frame each (0.19 for the guitar's strings from the median of 1.5 s through the tuner chain). The 11th and 13th frets, the 7th fret, the open string again, a fifth above the octave and an octave plus 55 cents: all `NOT_THE_OCTAVE`.

## Strobe model
`StrobeMotion` (`strobe/`) is the drift of a strobe pattern, one animation frame at a time; every wave look runs one (via `WaveMotion`). A mechanical strobe drifts at the beat rate in Hz, so high strings move faster; here the drift comes from cents, so every string looks the same. `driftSpeed(cents)` in stripe periods per second: **0 within ±0.5 cent** (finer than the ±1 cent target, so an in-tune string stands perfectly still), then **0.4/s per cent beyond**, up to **4/s** from 10.5 cents; positive when sharp. It ignores the in-tune flag (2–5 cents wide) because the strobe exists to show the last cent. `advance(seconds, cents)` eases the shown cents towards the reading (rate 20/s) and moves `phase` (0..1, wrapping); a step is capped at 0.1 s. Without a reading the view stops calling it and the pattern freezes. Tests feed sines 0, ±1, ±3, ±5 and ±30 cents off every string through `TunerChain`.

## Pitch history
The pitch trail (Phase 31, behind its switch `ToolSwitches.pitchTrail`) draws the note over the last few seconds. Its data is `PitchHistory` (`trail/`): a ring of fixed arrays, allocated once, so the tuner's ~47 readings a second make no garbage. Its length is a parameter, **5 s by default** (`DEFAULT_SECONDS`); the look the owner picked (2026-10-17, [Tuner screen](ui-tuner.md), *Pitch trail view*) shows the last 4 s.
- **What an entry holds:** a time stamp (seconds), the note aimed at (a MIDI number), the cents off it and whether the tuner showed it in tune. A gap (silence, the app's own reference tone and its echo, or a tool such as strum check using the microphone) has target `NO_NOTE` and NaN cents, so the line breaks. The pitch is `target + cents / 100` semitones, so a look can draw cents around the note or the pitch on a scale of notes; a change of target shows where the tuner moved to another note or string.
- **Fed on the DSP thread:** `TunerViewModel` passes every tuner reading to `PitchTrailFeed` (in `:feature:tuner`) right after `TunerInterpreter`, before `looksLike` drops readings that look the same, so the trail gets every frame, not only visible changes. Time is the tuner's audio clock, one hop (≈ 21.3 ms) per reading, kept across listening sessions so it never goes back; each session clears the history (`clear` only moves the start, so it allocates nothing either). While the switch is off nothing is written. `targetMidiOf` takes the note from the reading: 12 · log2(f / A4) + 69 less its cents.
- **Read in the draw phase, like the strobe:** the view will walk the history in `drawBehind` on each animation frame, so a new reading never recomposes the screen. One writer, one reader on another thread, no lock: the writer fills a slot and then moves `end` on (a volatile write), so everything below the `end` a reader saw is complete. Entries have lasting numbers (`Long`), and a reader walks `firstWithin(seconds, end) until end` (or the inline `forEachWithin`, which allocates nothing). The ring holds 32 entries (≈ 0.7 s, `SLACK_ENTRIES`) more than a reader may walk, so nothing it walks is overwritten during a frame; a reader stalled longer than that asks `overwritten(first)` and skips the frame.
- **Tests:** `PitchHistoryTest` (entries back in order, only the last seconds walked, wrapping, clear, no allocation per push or walk, a reader on another thread never sees a torn entry) and `PitchTrailFeedTest` (the target note, gaps, the clock, a played A2 filling the history through the ViewModel, nothing while the switch is off).

## Audio source selection
1. `MediaRecorder.AudioSource.UNPROCESSED` if `PROPERTY_SUPPORT_AUDIO_SOURCE_UNPROCESSED` is `"true"`.
2. Otherwise `VOICE_RECOGNITION`, which must have AGC and noise suppression off.
3. Never `MIC` or `VOICE_COMMUNICATION`: their AGC and noise suppression distort pitch and level.

`AudioSourceSelector.candidates()` returns these in order; `AndroidAudioRecorder` opens the first whose `AudioRecord` initializes, so a failed `UNPROCESSED` still falls back.

**Bluetooth headsets.** A headset mic only records over SCO/HFP or LE Audio: 8 or 16 kHz, band-limited, often noise-suppressed. Twangle never asks for that route, and in case something else opened it, `InputDeviceSelector` pins the built-in mic with `setPreferredDevice` whenever a Bluetooth input is present. Wired headsets and USB interfaces keep normal routing. Without a built-in mic routing is left to Android; low-rate audio is still handled. *Not yet verified with a real headset* (owner check).

## Audio capture
`AudioRecorder.chunks()` is a cold `Flow<FloatArray>`: the mic opens when collection starts and is released when it stops. `AndroidAudioRecorder` reads one-hop chunks with `READ_BLOCKING` on its own single-thread dispatcher (buffer ≥ 4 chunks). To avoid ~47 arrays a second it fills a **pool of 4 arrays** behind a channel of capacity 2, so **a chunk is only valid until the collector takes the next one**: downstream code copies samples out inside `collect` and must not `buffer()` the chunks.

`PitchPipeline` (`:feature:tuner`, since it joins audio and DSP) collects the chunks into a `FrameAssembler` and runs gate, detector, `NoiseFloorTracker` and `Clarity` on `Dispatchers.Default`, emitting one `PitchReading(frequencyHz, confidence, levelDbfs, gateOpen, noiseFloorDbfs, snrDb, clarity)` (plus peak) per hop. The window follows the tuning (`readings(range)`, see [Signal pipeline](signal-pipeline.md), *Low notes*). DSP objects are created once per collection; only the small reading is new each frame.

`TunerViewModel` creates a `TunerInterpreter` per listening session, which feeds `PitchSmoother` and turns the smoothed MIDI into a `TunerUiState`, on the same coroutine as the detector. Mode and saved settings are combined into one immutable `TunerOptions` (mode, tuning, A4, locked string, notation, precision), rebuilt only on change. Listening waits until settings have loaded. Changes take effect on the next frame without reopening the mic; only a new A4 restarts the smoother.
- **Chromatic:** the smoother's note and cents.
- **Auto:** the closest string and `centsFromString`. The target is sticky: it changes only once another string is closer by more than 15 cents. A reading within 50 cents of an octave above or below the string being tuned, with no string within 50 cents of it, stays on that string, moved by the octave (same cents). As a G3 fades, its faint odd harmonics sink into the room's noise and no single frame can tell it from G4 any more; nearest to no string, it would jump to high E at +300 cents. A 12th-fret harmonic reads as its string this way too. Only the string already tracked is kept (from scratch an octave reads as its own note), and only one octave away, so in open D plucking D3 after D2 is the D3 string. A locked string takes an octave of itself the same way.
- **Auto, string locked:** tapping a string locks the target to it (`toggleString`, `TunerOptions.lockedString`); tapping again unlocks. Kept in memory only, survives a mode switch, released when the tuning changes.
- The frequency shown is the smoothed one (`NoteMapper.frequencyOf(smoother.midi)`).
- `InTuneDetector` (`:core:dsp`, `smoothing`, shared with the watch) decides "in tune" from smoothed cents and frame duration, and restarts when the note stops or the target changes. The phone's precision setting picks its window through `InTuneDetector.forPrecision` (an extension in `:feature:tuner`, since `:core:dsp` knows no settings).
- **Steady hint line** (the owner saw "Tune up/down" flicker near the note). Two pure layers, counted in frames:
  - `TuningCue` (`TunerHint.kt`) picks the *wanted* hint: `IN_TUNE` while `InTuneDetector` says so, else up or down from the rounded cents. Inside the in-tune window the previous direction is kept, so a wobbling reading doesn't swap; a note starting inside it shows no direction.
  - `SteadyHint<T : SteadyMessage>` adds timing: a message shows once wanted for its `settleSeconds` without a break and stays at least its `minShowSeconds`; an empty line after a message also stays 0.5 s. After a reset the first message shows at once. `IN_TUNE` settles at once (the detector already held 300 ms), `TUNE_UP` / `TUNE_DOWN` after 0.2 s; each stays ≥ 0.5 s.
  - `Detected.inTune = hint == IN_TUNE`, so colour, check mark, haptic tick, glow, TalkBack and hint always agree. Tips use a second `SteadyHint` (see [Signal pipeline](signal-pipeline.md), *Tips*).

Failures end the flow with a typed `AudioRecorderException`, mapped by `toTunerError()` to a `TunerError` that the UI shows with a "Try again" button:
- `PermissionMissing` → `MIC_PERMISSION_MISSING`.
- `InitializationFailed` (no input opens at the rate) → `MIC_UNAVAILABLE`.
- `MicrophoneBusy` → `MIC_BUSY`. Before Android 10 `startRecording()` fails. From 10 the mic is shared and an app with priority (a call, an assistant, another recorder in front) silences us with zeros instead, at the start or mid-session. `ChunkReader` (pure, behind a `MicInput`, so JVM-tested with a fake mic) ends the flow with `MicrophoneBusy` when (a) the recording is silenced: `isClientSilenced` right after `startRecording()`, then an `AudioRecordingCallback` (Android 10+, registered only while recording) that sets a volatile flag the read loop checks per chunk, or (b) **2 s of exact zeros** arrive: a muted or blocked mic (the privacy switch, another app muting it) delivers digital silence, while a working mic always has some noise, even in a quiet room. Without this the tuner said "Play a note" forever.
- `ReadFailed(code)` (e.g. `ERROR_DEAD_OBJECT` when the audio server restarts) → `MIC_STOPPED`; collecting again reopens the mic. Reads that keep returning nothing (50 in a row, 5 ms apart: ≈ 0.25 s) are `ReadFailed(0)` instead of a loop that spins forever. The players' `AudioTrack` writes use the same loop (`transferFully`): 50 empty writes in a row end their session quietly.

**Measured on the Galaxy S26 (Android 16):** no `UNPROCESSED`, so `VOICE_RECOGNITION` at 48 kHz with no effects attached; a 440 Hz tone read 439.98 Hz (−0.1 cents) at −31 dBFS; quiet room noise −52 to −56 dBFS, right at the gate.

## Reference tone
The app can play a string's note so it can be tuned by ear. `TonePlayer` (`:core:audio`) has `play(frequencyHz)`, `stop()` and `isPlaying: StateFlow<Boolean>`; the caller turns the note into Hz with its `NoteMapper`, so the tone follows the A4 calibration.
- **Sound:** `PluckSynth` (`:core:dsp`, pure) adds up harmonics of an ideal plucked string (Fletcher & Rossing, ch. 2): harmonic k starts at 1/k and decays exponentially with time constant 1.5 s / (1 + (k − 1)/2), so the tone starts bright and mellows like a string. Harmonics stop below 6 kHz and 0.45 × the rate (at most 32), so low notes keep enough harmonics to be heard on a phone speaker that can't play their fundamental. Each harmonic is a decaying phasor turned by a fixed complex step per sample: exact pitch, no tables, no allocation.
- **Length and envelope:** 4 s from pluck to silence: a 5 ms raised-cosine attack (harmonics at 1/k starting together would jump like a sawtooth), then a 0.6 s raised-cosine fade that ends on exactly 0. `release()` fades out over 30 ms from the current level; a new note releases the old one first. The level is scaled so the sum of all harmonic amplitudes is 0.8 (about −2 dBFS), so it can never clip.
- **Playback:** `AndroidTonePlayer` streams `ENCODING_PCM_FLOAT` mono at the phone's native output rate through an `AudioTrack` with `USAGE_MEDIA` / `CONTENT_TYPE_MUSIC`, so the media volume sets its loudness. The track exists only while a tone sounds. `AudioTrack.write` blocks, so a session runs on its own single-thread dispatcher and renders 10 ms chunks into one buffer (buffer ≥ 4 chunks); `play` and `stop` only set a request field under a lock and return. At the end the session pads one buffer of silence (a very short tone otherwise might never start), waits for the playback head to pass the last sample, releases the track, and only then `isPlaying` turns false. It turns true on `play`. A track that fails ends the session quietly (logged).
- **Playing a string** (`TunerViewModel.playString`): a long-press on a string button plays that string of the current tuning at the current A4 (`NoteMapper(a4Hz).frequencyOf(note)`); a long-press on another string while one sounds switches to it. `soundingString` is the played string while `isPlaying`, else null. A tap on *any* string while a tone sounds only stops it (`toggleString` → `stopTone`): the lock stays as it was, so stopping the sound never changes what the tuner listens for, and the next tap locks as before. A new tuning, a switch to chromatic mode (no strings to show), the tuner leaving the screen (`ON_STOP`, or another screen taking its place) and `onCleared` all stop the tone too. A long-press gives Android's own long-press haptic (`combinedClickable`).
- **The mic ignores the tone:** the microphone stays open (hard rule 4 is unchanged: no reopening, no privacy-indicator flicker), but while `isPlaying` and for `TONE_TAIL_SECONDS` = 0.3 s after (counted in frames, for the room's echo) `listen()` sets the readings aside and emits the listening state; when the tail ends it starts a fresh `TunerInterpreter`, so no smoothing, hint or reception of the tone carries over.
- **Sounding string:** rings in the style's accent (`primary`) and the button's own shape grow out of it and fade, two at a time like sound waves, every 1.2 s (`soundingRings`, `StringSelector.kt`); drawn outside the clip in the draw phase, so nothing moves (`aSoundingStringMovesNothing`). With Android's animations off: one still ring. TalkBack: the long-press is the named action "Play A 2" (double-tap and hold, or the actions menu); the sounding string's state is "Playing" ("Locked, playing"), and every string's tap action reads "Stop the tone" while one sounds. In Dutch: "a 2 laten horen", "Klinkt", "Toon stoppen" (`dutchTalkBackCanPlayAStringAndHearsItSound`). `SoundingRingsTest` pictures the row on each style's background, light and dark, with and without a sounding string: the rings clearly show round that button in all six styles and change nothing elsewhere.
- **"Playing D3":** while a string's tone sounds, the tuner's hint line (where "Play a string" stands; the state is `Listening` then, since the mic is set aside) says "Playing D3" (`tuner_hint_playing`, NL "D3 klinkt"), the string named in the player's notation like the locked-string hint, and TalkBack hears "Playing D 3" through the note block's polite live region. It takes a reception tip's place for as long as the tone sounds, and goes back to "Play a string" (or "Play the A2 string") when the tone ends or is stopped (a few seconds: no flicker). The tour's strings step says how: "Tap to lock, hold to hear" ([Tuner screen and tour](ui-tuner.md), *Tour*). Tests: `aSoundingToneIsNamedInTheHintUntilItStops`, `aSoundingToneIsNamedInThePlayersNotation`, `aSoundingToneTakesATipsPlace` (`TunerContentTest`).
- **Audio focus:** each session asks for `AUDIOFOCUS_GAIN_TRANSIENT` (not `…_MAY_DUCK`: tuning by ear needs the tone alone, so another app's music pauses, and resumes when the session gives focus back as it ends). A loss (`LOSS`, `LOSS_TRANSIENT`: a call, another app that won't share) stops the tone like `stop()`; a loss that may duck (a notification) is left to Android, which lowers the tone by itself. Refused focus (during a call) means no tone: the session ends at once, quietly.
- **Volume:** the tone plays at the media volume. While the tuner is shown (`MainActivity`, a `DisposableEffect` in the tuner's branch) `volumeControlStream = STREAM_MUSIC`, so the volume keys set the tone's loudness even between tones; on Settings, About and the tuning editor the keys do what they do elsewhere. Only on the tuner, because only it makes sound. At media volume 0 a session shows the phone's own volume panel (`adjustStreamVolume(ADJUST_SAME, FLAG_SHOW_UI)`, the level unchanged), so a silent tone explains itself without a message of ours.
- **Measured on the Xiaomi 12X:** a long-press starts a `USAGE_MEDIA` float `AudioTrack` at 48 kHz (`dumpsys audio`), the tuner showed "Play a string" throughout (a run from before the "Playing A2" line), the track is gone about 4.3 s later, within 0.5 s of a stopping tap, and within a second of pressing Home.
- **Measured on the Xiaomi 12X (focus and volume):** a long-press holds `GAIN_TRANSIENT` focus with `USAGE_MEDIA` (`dumpsys audio`, focus stack) and gives it back when the tone ends; the volume keys reach `STREAM_MUSIC` on the tuner and the phone's default stream in Settings (`adjustSuggestedStreamVolume`); at media volume 0 MIUI's volume panel opens on the long-press. `VolumeKeysTest` checks the tuner's stream on the phone; Settings isn't, since the ever-moving tuner keeps a Compose test rule from going idle.
- **Measured:** every preset string of every instrument, at A4 = 415, 432, 440 and 466 Hz, reads within 1 cent with the app's YIN (`PluckSynthTest`), also 3 s into the tone and at 44.1 kHz.
