## Home-screen widget
`:app` `widget/`. A tile for the home screen, three cells by one (resizable across, down to two): the navigation bar's tuning fork in the accent, the instrument and tuning as the tuner names them ("Guitar · Standard", a custom tuning by its own name, a lowered one as "Guitar · Drop C", "Chromatic" in chromatic mode) in ink, and "Open the tuner" ("Open de stemmer") in the muted colour under it, on a rounded tile in the style's background colour (corners: the launcher's own on Android 12+, 16 dp before). No border, no art: calm beside other widgets. A tap anywhere opens Twangle on the tuner.
- **Tech: plain `RemoteViews`** (`layout/widget_tuning.xml`, an `AppWidgetProvider`), not Jetpack Glance. The widget is one static layout with three values to fill in; Glance would add a library, a second Compose runtime for widgets and a WorkManager-backed session for no gain here. Text sizes come from the layout (the title shrinks from 16 to 11 sp to fit, `autoSizeTextType`), the face is the system's medium sans: `RemoteViews` can't take the styles' bundled faces without a layout per style, and the colours already carry the style.
- **Colours** (`TuningWidget.paint`): the style's scheme as the app ends up with it (`colorSchemeFor` in `:core:designsystem`, wallpaper colours for Contemporary with Material You on, `usesWallpaperColors`): background, `onBackground` (ink), `onSurfaceVariant` (muted) and `primary` (accent), set as colour filters on the tile and the fork and as text colours. With the theme on "System" on Android 12+ both sets go in (`setColorInt` light/night), so the launcher follows the phone's dark mode without a redraw; otherwise the set for the current brightness. The picker's preview (Android 12+, `previewLayout`) is the layout itself in Contemporary light or dark (`values/widget.xml`).
- **Kept up to date by the app, never on a timer** (`updatePeriodMillis` 0): `TwangleApplication` follows the settings (`TuningWidget.follow`) for as long as its process lives and redraws the widgets when what they show changes (`WidgetState`: mode, instrument, tuning, custom name, style, theme, wallpaper colours; A4 or the meter's look change nothing), and when the configuration changes (language, dark mode before Android 12). `TuningWidgetReceiver` draws a widget when it is placed and redraws on the phone's `LOCALE_CHANGED`, reading the settings off the main thread (`goAsync`). With no widget placed, a redraw is one `getAppWidgetIds` call.
- **One tap opens the tuner** (`TuningWidget.openTuner`): an immutable `PendingIntent` to `MainActivity` with `ACTION_OPEN_TUNER` and `NEW_TASK | CLEAR_TOP | SINGLE_TOP`. A cold start opens on the tuner anyway; an open app gets `onNewIntent`, which counts a request, and `TwangleApp` switches to the tuner (an open tuning editor closes as with Back, dropping its draft).
- **Never the microphone:** the widget and its receiver touch only the settings. The mic opens only when the tuner it opens is shown, exactly as from the launcher ([Lifecycle](lifecycle.md)), and the tuner asks for the permission there.
- **TalkBack:** the tile is one button; the title reads as the tuner's button does ("Guitar, Standard"), then "Open the tuner".
- **Tests:** `WidgetStateTest` (JVM: names, custom tuning, chromatic, which changes redraw); `TuningWidgetTest` (device: the views applied as a launcher does show the names in the style's colours and are tappable; drawing them records nothing; the tap's intent brings the open app from Settings back to the tuner).

## Watch app (Wear OS)
`:wear`. A standalone Wear OS app (`com.google.android.wearable.standalone`): it listens with the watch's own microphone and needs nothing from the phone. Compose for Wear OS (`androidx.wear.compose` Material 3, `AppScaffold` with the time at the top), minSdk 30 (Wear OS 3).
- **Same application ID as the phone** (`io.github.klaboukes.twangle`), as Wear OS and Play expect for a phone app and its watch app; namespace `…twangle.wear`. **`versionCode` = the phone's + 1,000,000** (1,010,000 for 1.0.0), because Play needs a different code for each build; both come from `twangle.version` ([Threading and performance](performance.md), *Release build*). The release build is signed with the phone's upload key. The launcher icon is the phone's adaptive icon (copied into `wear/src/main/res`).
- **The pitch engine is the phone's**: `WatchPitchPipeline` runs `:core:dsp`'s `FrameAssembler` → `SignalGate` → `SustainCheck` (with its own noise floor for the room rule) → YIN (`AnalysisSettings.newPitchDetector`) → `PitchSmoother` → `InTuneDetector` (the phone's default window: within 5 cents for 300 ms, released past 7), with frame sizes from the recorder's rate, so a 16 kHz watch mic gets the same window and hop as 48 kHz. On a watch-class core it costs about 13–20 % of the CPU ([Threading and performance](performance.md), *Watch CPU and the native detector*). It leaves out the phone's extras (SNR, reception, tips, strum check, low-note window, precision setting): chromatic, A4 = 440 Hz, the note names in the watch language's usual notation (`Notation.forLanguage`: A♯, La♯ or H; no notation setting). It allocates nothing per frame and sends a new `WatchTunerState` (`Listening`, `Detected(note, cents, inTune)`, `Failed`) only when the note, the whole cents or "in tune" change. A note lost for a moment stays on screen 0.6 s beyond the smoother's own hold (as long as the phone's glow bridges a dropout) and keeps "in tune" if the same note comes back, so the watch never flickers between a reading and "Play a note".
- **The microphone is `:core:audio`'s `AndroidAudioRecorder`**, unchanged: unprocessed source, then voice recognition, the watch's native rate, the built-in mic while a Bluetooth headset is connected.
- **Microphone lifetime** as on the phone: `WatchTunerViewModel.state` is `stateIn(WhileSubscribed(5_000))` and the screen collects it with `collectAsStateWithLifecycle()`, only once `RECORD_AUDIO` is granted. The permission is asked for when the app first opens; afterwards a button asks again, or opens the app's page in the watch's settings once Android stops showing its dialog. `rememberWatchMicPermission` follows the phone's `MicPermissionRules` ([Lifecycle](lifecycle.md)): a first dialog dismissed without an answer keeps "Allow microphone", and only a real "don't ask again" leads to settings. A refusal is remembered in the watch's own small `SharedPreferences` file (`twangle_wear`, `mic_refused`), as the phone keeps it in its settings. `WatchMicPermissionTest` on the JVM. A busy or broken mic shows a message with "Try again". **Coming back on screen** (from the watch face, the tile or the shortcut) after a failure tries again by itself (`WatchTunerViewModel.shown`, on `ON_START`): Android silences a recording the moment its app leaves the screen, which reads as a busy mic, and within the 5 s grace that greeted the player on their return (seen on the emulator, 2026-10-30). **The screen coming on counts only with the tuner on it** (`screenReport` in `ScreenState.kt`, `ScreenStateTest`): waking from always-on lands on the watch face, not the tuner, and within the 5 s grace the screen-on opened the microphone behind the watch face for some 0.4 s until Android silenced it (the Galaxy Watch 5, 2026-10-31); now the tuner catches up on the screen when it comes back (`ON_START`). On the Galaxy Watch 5 the microphone lets go 0.2 s after the screen goes dark or dims to always-on, and comes back as the screen wakes onto the tuner.
- **The screen stays on while the tuner hears playing**, as the phone's Keep screen on does (owner, 2026-10-30: the Galaxy Watch 5 went dark mid-tuning after its own 15 s or so): `WatchScreenAwake` runs the phone's `ScreenAwakeRule` (`:core:dsp`, `awake`) on the watch's clock and holds `View.keepScreenOn`, a window flag, not a wake lock. A note counts once it has sounded 1 s and holds the screen however long it sounds; 3 minutes after the last one, or after the screen came on, the watch's own timeout has the screen again. No warning first, unlike the phone: a raised wrist or a tap wakes the watch and the tuner listens again at once, and the watch has no room for it. No setting either; the watch app has none. It follows the tuner's state only while the tuner is on screen (`repeatOnLifecycle(STARTED)`): until 2026-10-30 it collected it in the background too, so the mic stayed open behind the watch face until Android silenced it, and the tuner came back saying another app had the microphone (`WatchScreenAwakeLifecycleTest` on the emulator). JVM-tested in `WatchScreenAwakeTest`; on the Galaxy Watch 5 the hold came off 3:00 after the tuner opened in a quiet room and the watch went dark on its own timeout, while a TV's music, heard as notes of a second or more, kept it on, as it does on the phone.
- **Screen off and ambient let go of the mic at once.** On Wear OS the activity stays *resumed* when the screen dims to ambient (always-on) or goes off (seen on the emulator: display `DOZE`, Twangle still the top resumed activity), so the lifecycle alone would keep the mic open. `ScreenOnEffect` listens for `ACTION_SCREEN_OFF` / `ACTION_SCREEN_ON` (sent when the display leaves or enters interactive mode, ambient included) and checks `PowerManager.isInteractive` on start; the view model then switches its upstream to a plain `Listening`, without the 5 s grace, and opens the mic again when the screen comes back. The app doesn't declare ambient support, so the watch shows the system's ambient view. Measured on the emulator: the mic is released within 0.9 s of `KEYCODE_SLEEP` (most of it adb) and reopened on wake; Home still keeps the 5 s grace.
- **Screen:** a ring along the edge (inset 6 % of the smaller side, so it sits the same on round and square screens), with the cents scale at its top: ±50 cents over ±60° either side of 12 o'clock, a tick every 5 cents (the tens longer, the fives shorter and fainter) and a longer one in ink at the target, with ♭ and ♯ (the phone's music font, muted) just beyond the scale's flat and sharp ends inside the ring, which stay on their sides in right-to-left languages as the scale does. **A needle** marks the reading (Phase 61, the owner's pick C of [docs/design/watch-look-preview.html](../design/watch-look-preview.html), 2026-10-30; until then a 14 dp dot): a slim taper in the accent pointing in from the ring, 15 % of the dial long (about 30 dp on the Galaxy Watch 5), 1.7 ring lines wide at its head narrowing to a quarter, its head a dot 4.8 lines across in a soft light 14 lines across (a radial gradient, 30 %). It glides there (a low-stiffness spring), drifting back to the centre as it fades when the note stops. The note is large in the middle (30 % of the dial, light weight, the octave small beside it on the same baseline, a ♯ raised and smaller in the phone's cut of Noto Sans Symbols, bundled with its licence; the watch has no About page, so its licence texts, the OFL and Apache 2.0 for AndroidX and Kotlin, ship as assets, `WatchLicencesTest`), the cents under it ("+12 cents", "−3 cents"; `WatchCents`: the number in a slot as wide as the widest from −99 to +99, in tabular digits and set against the unit, the line as wide as its widest reading and starting at the same place, so nothing shifts as the number changes, owner 2026-10-30; the words are `wear_cents` split at the number, so each language keeps its order, and TalkBack hears the line as one text), the **ribbon** under them (`WatchRibbon`, the owner's pick C1 of [docs/design/watch-wave-preview.html](../design/watch-wave-preview.html)), and "Play a note" while nothing is heard; reading and "Play a note" cross-fade (400 ms). Sizes follow the screen, not the font setting, so the reading always fits inside the ring (checked from 170 to 225 dp). The tuner leaves out the time, where the ring runs; the permission and error messages keep it.
- **In tune:** the ring, the dot and the note ease to the in-tune colour over 700 ms (`FastOutSlowInEasing`) with a soft band of light around the ring (a radial gradient, 35 % on the ring, fading to nothing 5 % of the dial either side), hold while the note is in tune and fade the same way. No pulse, no sparks. TalkBack hears the note, the cents and "in tune".
- **The ribbon** (Phase 61): the phone's sound ribbon, small: half the dial wide and 6.5 % tall, its middle 27.5 % below the dial's, a soft band in the accent at 45 %. Its shape is the phone's `ribbonAt` and it moves by the phone's `WaveMotion` (both moved to `:core:dsp` `strobe/` for it, so phone and watch follow one rule): off pitch it pinches into pulses that drift right when sharp and left when flat at the strobe's speed (0 within half a cent, up to 4 pulses a second from 10.5 cents) and breathes slowly; within half a cent it stands still as one even band. It follows the whole cents the watch shows, so "0 cents" never drifts. In tune it eases to the in-tune colour with a faint outline of light, with the ring. It fades in and out with the reading, moves only while it is shown (an infinite animation frame loop; each frame redraws only its own layer) and is hidden from TalkBack.
- **The tile** (Phase 61): `WatchTileService`, a Wear OS tile beside the watch face: "Twangle" at the top, the mark in the accent in the middle (64 dp) and one **Tune** edge button along the bottom that opens `WatchActivity` (a `LaunchAction`; an open tuner is brought to the front, not started twice). Built with `protolayout-material3`'s `primaryLayout` in the watch's colours (`watchTileColors()`, the dynamic theme off). A plain `TileService` hands over the mark as a classic resource (`tileResources()`, version `TILE_RESOURCES_VERSION`): `Material3TileService` sends images along with the layout, which the Wear OS 6 emulator's tile host left blank. The tile never changes, so it asks for no updates; its picture in the tile list is `drawable-nodpi/tile_preview.png`, a screenshot of the tile on the emulator. On Wear OS 6 the system also puts the service's icon (the mark, white) above the title.
- **The watch-face shortcut** (Phase 61): `WatchShortcutService`, a complication data source for the small slots a watch face offers. It fills short-text slots with the mark and **Tune** ("Stem"; at most 5 letters, since faces cut longer words: "Twangle" became "TWAN…" on the emulator's Utility face), icon slots with the mark alone (tinted by the face) and small-image slots with the launcher icon; every one opens `WatchActivity` with one tap (an immutable `PendingIntent`). It shows the same all day, so `UPDATE_PERIOD_SECONDS` is 0. Neither the tile nor the shortcut can listen: they only start the tuner screen, which opens the mic as above (Hard rule 4); checked on the emulator against `dumpsys audio` (no recording while the tile or the face shows, one as soon as the tuner opens). Only the system may bind either service (`BIND_TILE_PROVIDER`, `BIND_COMPLICATION_PROVIDER`), and the app still asks for no permission but the microphone.
- **The tap on the wrist** (Phase 61): the moment the shown note comes in tune, when the ring starts to glow, the motor taps once (`WatchInTuneTapEffect` in `WatchTunerApp`). Once per tuned note by the phone's own rule, `InTuneTickRule` (moved to `:core:dsp` `smoothing/` so phone and watch share it; `WatchInTuneTap` feeds it the shown note): a note wobbling out of tune and back within 1.5 s doesn't tap again, another note taps at once, and the tuner appearing on a note already in tune doesn't tap (the rule's state is saved). **The tap:** one pulse of 150 ms at full strength (`VibrationEffect.createOneShot`, `TAP_MILLIS`), on every watch. Tried on the owner's Galaxy Watch 5 with a guitar (2026-10-31; its motor has amplitude control but no primitives): the system's click (`EFFECT_CLICK`, the phone's tick; it was a `PRIMITIVE_CLICK` at 0.6 where supported) ran on the motor but wasn't felt at all, a 40 ms pulse neither, 80 ms only with the arm still (the first string, not the next ones, though the log shows a tap on every one), and a double tap of 80 ms not while playing either: the guitar's own vibration and the moving arm hide a short pulse, and 150 ms comes through. It drives the motor directly, as the phone does (the normal `VIBRATE` permission, granted at install; the microphone stays the only permission asked for); Android files it as touch feedback (`dumpsys vibrator_manager`: `Step=150ms(amplitude=1.00)`, usage TOUCH), so the watch's own touch-vibration strength applies to it. It taps only while the tuner is on screen, as only then does it listen. **No off switch** (owner, 2026-10-31, after trying it: it works, none needed). **The microphone hears the motor** (the same case): a watch's motor rings at about 150–250 Hz, and mixed in at half the note's level that buzz nudged the reading by up to 3 cents for a few frames (still in tune). So `WatchPitchPipeline` looks away as a note comes in tune: it skips the frames whose window may hold the buzz (`TAP_SECONDS`, 230 ms after the reading: up to 60 ms for the tap to start, its 150 ms and 20 ms to ring out, plus one window: about 320 ms), and the reading, already in tune, holds still meanwhile. `WatchPitchPipelineTest` closes the loop as on a watch: its recorder mixes a motor buzz (150–250 Hz, the tap's 150 ms at full strength, then an 8 ms decay) into the following audio when the tap rule fires, 0, 30 or 60 ms late, on every string held and plucked, at half the note's level and as loud as it or twice: one tap, the same note still in tune, the cents within 1 of the clean reading. The emulator has no motor; on the Galaxy Watch 5 with a guitar the tap came once per tuned string and the needle held still across it (owner, 2026-10-31).
