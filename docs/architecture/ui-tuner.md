## Tuner screen
`TunerScreen` wires the ViewModel; `TunerContent` is the stateless body used by previews and UI tests. Upright (`SingleColumnLayout`, as the approved mock-up `docs/design/theme-styles-preview.html`) the column fills the screen's height: mode switch at the top, the meter, the note, and the guitar controls at the bottom; the free height goes 1 : 2 above the meter and above the strings (`TOP_GAP_WEIGHT`), each gap at least 24 dp. On a short screen (a narrow phone with navigation buttons: the Galaxy S26 overflowed, 2026-10-18) the gaps close and the **note** shrinks, never the meter (owner, 2026-10-21): the meter keeps its full width, and `FitColumn` in `:core:designsystem` gives the note block what the rest leaves (`NoteDisplay`'s `maxHeight`). Only the big note letter gets smaller (`FitNoteColumn`, down to 32 dp), to the size whose line fills the room under the wave or the pill, worked out from the room and the face's line, never from the note's name, so it doesn't change from one note to the next. The octave beside it grows with the text size but never past half the letter's size (set in dp, since large text grows non-linearly in sp): Polish at 2× text on the Xiaomi shrank the letter to 32 dp and left a 37 dp octave beside it (overnight run, 2026-10-24). Space Age's Michroma has the tallest line (1.42 em: 159 dp at the full 112 dp; a single line keeps its face's own height on the phone whatever `lineHeight` or `LineHeightStyle` asks, measured 2026-10-21), so there the note shrinks most. Before, the meter shrank instead and on the S26 it was at its 160 dp minimum in Space Age. With a tool on, its panel in the meter's place still shrinks (keeping its shape, down to 160 dp, `FitGauge`), so the tool's text under it keeps its size. Only with a very large font does it scroll. The meter spans the screen less 8 dp each side (`METER_MARGIN`); everything else keeps a 16 dp text margin (`COLUMN_PADDING`).
- **Upright tablet** (Phase 52): the column is at most 480 dp wide, so on a Lenovo Tab M11 upright (800 dp) the meter was 464 dp across and about 290 dp of screen lay empty under the note. There the whole column is now laid out as on a phone and drawn bigger (`columnScaleFor` and `ScaledUp` in `:core:designsystem`'s `ScaledUp.kt`, shared with the metronome's upright column, [Metronome, widget and watch](ui-other-screens.md); `columnScaleFor` is JVM-tested in `ColumnScaleTest`; `ScaledUp` gives the column a `LocalDensity` with that many more pixels to the dp): it is laid out in the window divided by the scale, as on a phone of at least 560 × 760 dp, so the meter, the note, the string buttons, every text and gap grow together and keep the phone's proportions, and no rule above needs a tablet case. The scale is the smaller of width / 560 and height / 760, at least 1 and at most 1.5 (720 dp across on a large tablet): 1.43 on the Tab M11 (a 686 dp column, a 663 dp meter), 1 on every phone (at most about 480 dp wide, and on its side far too short), growing smoothly with the window so a resized window doesn't jump; the provider stays at 1, so crossing the point keeps the column's state. Text keeps Android's own non-linear font scaling (sp go to dp through the screen's density, then to pixels at the scaled one). Popups opened from the column (the more tools menu, tooltips) inherit it and grow too; the instrument and tuning panel, the two panes on its side and focus mode keep their own sizes. Tests measuring nodes inside the column read their bounds in the window's dp (`windowBounds` in `TunerContentTest`): `getBoundsInRoot()` uses the node's own, scaled, dp. `TunerContentTest.anUprightTabletShowsThePhonesColumnBigger` checks that every part on an 800 × 1240 dp tablet at 1.3× text sits where it sits on the phone-sized window, that much bigger, with no text cut off, and that a phone keeps its own size.

From top to bottom:
- **Top corners:** in the end corner focus mode's button (*Mode switch*, below); the start corner empty, or the signal bars where they are on. Settings and the metronome are reached from the navigation bar ([UI basics](ui.md), *Navigation bar*).
- **Mode switch:** `ModeSwitch`, the app's `SegmentedSwitch` (`:core:designsystem`; until Phase 47 also the Practice tab's tool switch, now only the mode switch), Material 3 segmented buttons "Strings" / "Chromatic" ("Snaren" / "Chromatisch"), the selected segment in `primaryContainer` (Material's secondary put Futuristic's purple beside its cyan). Centred at the top, 8 dp down, in a row of its own (`ModeSwitchRow`) with **focus mode's button** at its end, level with the switch, its 44 dp outline ending at the 16 dp text margin (owner, 2026-10-25, idea A1 of the Phase 43 rough mock-up: Strings and Chromatic look the same, and no button moves to another place on a mode switch; before, it stood above the more tools button, alone above nothing in chromatic mode). The switch stays centred between two equal 52 dp slots (the button's outline and 8 dp), so it never reaches the button; on its side the row heads the controls pane. The signal bars, where they are on, take the other slot, at the row's start (*Signal bars* in [Signal pipeline](signal-pipeline.md)). The button itself: [UI basics](ui.md), *Landscape and wide screens*. No "A4 = … Hz" line. A label that doesn't fit its half of the switch (Dutch "Chromatisch", Russian "Хроматический" at 2× text on a 360 dp phone, beside focus mode's button) shrinks as far as it must, each size tried from the top (`OneLineAutoSize`: Compose's `StepBased` kept French "Chromatique" in Classic at a size where it still wrapped, the overnight run of 2026-10-27) (down to 8 sp in most cases measured; below that, to 5 sp, only where it would otherwise be cut off: Russian "Хроматический" in Classic at 2× text on a phone on its side needs 5.5 sp on the Xiaomi), and one line keeps no line spacing above and below it, which at 2× text was taller than the button. No segment shows Material's check mark: the picked one is told by its fill alone (owner, 2026-10-18: "just highlight"). Every label's whole one-line width is its minimum. With three segments (the Practice tab's tool switch until Phase 47; none today) each keeps 6 dp at its sides instead of Material's 12, so Dutch "Metronoom" fits a 320 dp phone at 2× text; with two, the 12 dp give way down to 6 before a label shrinks (Russian "Хроматический" at 2× text beside focus mode's button fits only so). Each label tells the row its whole one-line size at full size, measured once (`segmentLabel`), and keeps a line 1.2 times its full size high however far it shrinks: asked of the shrinking text itself, the size came out differently before and after a tap, so the switch grew or shrank by up to 10 dp when the mode changed (English at 2× text once it had to shrink beside the button, 2026-10-25).
- **Meter:** `MeterFrame` in the picked look (see [Meter, wave and styles](ui-meter-and-styles.md), *Meter*), with `zIndex(-1)` so its calm field and in-tune glow, which reach past its box, lie behind the mode switch and the note. The scroll areas fill the screen (`heightIn(min = …)`), so the glow fades out on the screen rather than at a scroll area's edge.
- **Note:** `NoteDisplay`: the big note name and octave in the style's display face (shrinking to fit wide faces and solfège), then the cents in a pill ("12 cents flat", whole cents) and the smoothed frequency, or the wave (see *Display*). Pill numbers use `numberFontFor(style)` (Alegreya Sans for Ancient, also its reading face since Phase 65; Bodoni Moda for Classic; otherwise the title face). While listening the meter rests faded and the hint asks for a string (Auto: "Play a string", "Play the A2 string" for a locked one) or a note (Chromatic) in the big note's slot, right under the meter, where the note then comes (`HintInNoteSlot`; Phase 43, review of 2026-10-24: under a faint ♪ in that slot it sat far below, nearer the strings than the meter). It is set in the note's face but at a reading size (32 sp, 24 sp compact, growing with the system's text size), centred in the slot and up to 300 dp wide; a tip about the sound ("Play a bit louder") takes its place there. The slot keeps the height of the ♪'s line, drawn clear under the hint, so nothing moves when the note arrives; the pill and the frequency line (or the wave) rest empty under it, keeping their lines. A hint that doesn't fit wraps to two lines and, where two lines don't fit either (a long language at 2× text, or a note shrunk on a short screen), is set smaller, down to 10 dp, as the note itself shrinks, never cut off (`TunerStylesTest.theIdleHintFitsTheNotesSlot`, Dutch at 2× text upright and on its side). When it shows and goes is unchanged (the tuner's own hold before a note fades back to listening), so it doesn't flicker. With nothing to say (an error) the slot shows the faint ♪. Focus mode's corner note already put the hint in the note's place (`FocusNote`) and is unchanged.
- **Guitar controls** (`GuitarControls`): a row of its own above `StringSelector`, with a button of one kind at each end: at the start the **instrument button** (`InstrumentButton` in `:core:designsystem`, owner 2026-10-19, idea A of the rough mock-up [docs/design/instrument-button-preview.html](../design/instrument-button-preview.html)), at the end the strum check's switch (or the more tools button). The instrument button is the headstock that was the bar's Instrument item (`INSTRUMENT_ICON`, three pegs a side, 1.8 dp lines), no word, on the style's shared button (`StyleButton`, [ui.md](ui.md) *Shared controls*: a 44 dp face, round in Contemporary and Space Age, Ancient's pebble, else the style's `shapes.small`; Futuristic's neon), the same face and shape as the tools button, its outline starting where the first string's face does, as the switch's ends where the last one's does. Between them the instrument and tuning ("Guitar · Drop D", `tuner_tuning_name`; a custom tuning by its own name), quiet text in `titleSmall` and the muted colour, 12 dp from each button; a long name (a large font, a long language, a 40-character own tuning) takes up to two lines and ends in "…", so neither button ever moves or changes size (owner: buttons that look like buttons, and keep one size). A tap on the button or the name opens the instrument and tuning panel; they share one press (`InstrumentButton(interactionSource)`), so a press on either sinks and lights both, as one control, the way a switch's label belongs to its switch (owner, 2026-10-19: the name stays tappable, as best practice for a control's label). TalkBack hears the button: "Instrument, Guitar, Drop D, button, double-tap to change instrument or tuning" (the picked tuning as its state); the name is skipped, as it says the same. A long-press on the button shows "Instrument" in a tooltip. Why not a button with the name in it: a long name made a big button, and two buttons (instrument, tuning) crowded the row or added one (the mock-up's options 1 to 3, all "cluttery"). Only in guitar mode, where the strings show; chromatic mode shows no instrument or tools button, which mean nothing there (owner, 2026-10-25) (E₂ A₂ D₃ G₃ B₃ E₄, target highlighted and `selected`; each string a button, "Lock to this string" / "Pick strings automatically"; a locked string gets an outer ring, "Locked", and the hint "Play the A2 string"). Chips take the style's outline shape and a see-through backing.
- **Instrument and tuning** (`InstrumentTuningPanel.kt`, as the approved mock-up `docs/design/combined-picker-preview.html`): opened from the instrument button above the strings (*Guitar controls*, above), and over the Practice tools from theirs ([Other screens](ui-other-screens.md), *Practice*); from the bar's Instrument item between 2026-10-15 and 2026-10-19. `TunerContent` shows the panel while `pickerOpen` and the state has a tuning (not over an error, which closes it). Without the microphone `TunerScreen` shows the same panel over the permission prompt, from `TunerViewModel.panelTuning` (the saved tuning and its notation, known without listening). Picking an instrument or a tuning in chromatic mode switches to guitar mode, where the strings show. The panel is a Material 3 modal bottom sheet over the dimmed tuner (at most 74 % of the window with its handle; in a short window, under 500 dp, a phone on its side, the whole height up to the status bar with the title beside the instruments, Phase 63's review; top corners from the style's cards, Futuristic's cut): the title "Instrument and tuning", every instrument in one row that scrolls sideways (the picked one in `primaryContainer` with a 2 dp accent outline, scrolled into view on opening; Ancient's chips alternate pebbles, Retro's are shaded push buttons), a divider, then that instrument's presets (`TuningRow`: name over its open strings on the app's choice row, `ChoiceRow`, the current one in `primaryContainer` with a 2 dp accent outline and ticked), in a column that scrolls and opens scrolled to the ticked tuning (a `BringIntoViewRequester` on its row, preset or own, asked one frame after the sheet's content is composed, from inside it: the sheet composes its content in a window of its own, later than the code round it, so asked from outside, the rows are often not there yet and the panel opens at the top), so a bass's or a long list of own tunings never hides it below the fold. Picking an instrument switches the tuner to its standard tuning and keeps the panel open, so the strings change behind it; picking a tuning closes it, as do a tap outside, a swipe down or Back. The player's own tunings for that instrument follow the presets (`MyTunings.kt`) under the small muted heading "My tunings" (in capitals with wide letter spacing as the mock-up; TalkBack hears it as written, a heading), which also stands alone over "New tuning" while there are none. Each is a `TuningRow` (picked and ticked like a preset) with a ⋮ button at its end, outside the part that picks it (TalkBack: "Options for Drop D loose"); it opens a small menu, Edit and Delete. Edit closes the panel and opens the tuning editor for that tuning. Delete first asks in a dialog ("Delete this tuning?" / "“Drop D loose” will be gone for good.", Delete in the error colour, Cancel; Back or a tap outside also keep it), so a stray tap deletes nothing; confirmed, the tuning goes (`TunerViewModel.deleteCustomTuning`; a picked one gives way to the instrument's standard tuning) and the panel stays open. The name is in the dialog's text, not its title, and the title is set at 115 % of `titleMedium`: in the wider title faces (Space Age) the dialog's own headline size left the question mark alone on a line at 2× text. A confirmation was chosen over undo: it needs no restore in the repository and no snackbar over the tuner. The list ends in "New tuning" (a + in the accent), which closes the panel and opens the tuning editor ([Lifecycle](lifecycle.md), *Custom tunings*). For an instrument the tuner leaves something out for, a quiet line in `bodySmall` and the muted colour follows (`instrumentNoteRes`, `TunerTestTags.PICKER_NOTE`): the 12-string's "The strum check and the chord name are off for the 12-string: its twelve strings ring too close together to tell apart." (`TunerContentTest`). **Lower by** (Phase 51, the owner's combination of A and B in `docs/design/lower-tuning.html`: "Most casual guitar players will rarely use this and it should not be on top"): under the list, outside its scrolling, so it is always at the panel's bottom, a divider and one quiet row (`LowerByRow`): "Lower by" in the muted colour, then − (back up a half step, off at none), the amount ("None", "½ step" … "2½ steps") and + (a half step lower, off where the tuning can go no lower): the shared stepper (`StepperButton`, a 36 dp face in the style's button shape, 48 dp to touch; plain ⌃ ⌄ until Phase 63), − on the left in every language. The amount takes the room of the widest one, so the buttons never move; TalkBack hears "Lower by, ½ step" (a polite live region, said again on a change) and the buttons as "Back up a half step" and "Lower a half step". A tap keeps the panel open and lowers the picked tuning (`SettingsRepository.setLoweredBy`, saved; in chromatic mode it switches to guitar mode as a pick does); the ticked row shows it lowered, with its name and strings ("Drop C", C G C F A D). Another tuning or instrument picked starts as written (a tap on the ticked row keeps it lowered). The row is left out where the tuning can't go lower at all (the five-string bass's B0). The tuner's label and button, the Practice tools' line and the home-screen widget name a lowered tuning the same way (`tuningName`, `PickerNames.kt`), TalkBack with its ♯ and ♭ in words. `TunerContentTest` checks the row and the names in the panel, `LowerByRowTest` the steps, the buttons' steady place and every word at 2× text (Dutch by day, every language overnight). `StringRowStylesTest` checks the strum check's switch (and the instrument button beside the name) for every instrument, upright and on its side, at 1× and 2× text (wholly shown, in the window, above the strings and ending in line with them). `MyTuningsStylesTest` checks the own-tunings part in every style in Dutch at 2× text (heading, a 40-character name and its ⋮ button wholly shown, the button beside the name, also with "2½ toon lager" after it); it is drawn outside the panel there, because the panel, the menu and the dialog are windows of their own that take the phone's text size and language rather than a test's override, so those were checked on the phone.
- **On its side** (the two panes, `TwoPaneLayout`): the controls pane's header (the mode switch, focus mode's button, the signal bars) is as wide as the string buttons' faces (`HeaderOverSlot`: the guitar controls write the faces' width into a `StringFacesSpan` as they measure, the header reads it, in chromatic mode too; `StringRowStylesTest.theStrumSwitchSitsAboveTheStringsAtTheirEnd` checks focus mode's button ends where the switch does), so focus mode's button ends where the tools button and the last string do and the signal bars start where the instrument button does (Phase 65: the header took the pane's whole width, its button a few dp further out). Upright the button keeps its place at the column's text margin.
- **Strings ticked off as they're tuned** (Phase 65; the owner skipped the mock-up, 2026-11-01): in guitar mode each string that has been in tune since the tuner opened carries the 12th-fret check's "fine" badge (`stringMarkBadge`: a check on a disc in the "in tune" colour, ringed in the background, at the button's top end corner, outside its box so no button moves or changes size), growing in once over 400 ms; TalkBack hears "Tuned" ("Locked, tuned" on the locked string; NL "Gestemd") as the button's state, so a beginner sees and hears which strings are done. The rule is the tuner's own in-tune rule (`TunerUiState.Detected.inTune`: in the window for 300 ms, `InTuneDetector`), so a needle sliding through 0 ticks nothing. `AllStringsInTune` (`RatingMoment.kt`), which already followed this for Google's rating window, keeps the strings (`tuned`, a bit each) on the DSP thread; `TunerViewModel.tunedStrings` publishes them (`TunedStrings`: the tuning and the bits) only when they change. A ticked string played clearly out of tune again loses its tick (owner, 2026-11-01), by the tuner's own out-of-tune rule: the hint line says "Tune up" or "Tune down" (`SteadyHint`: only past the in-tune window's exit margin, after settling) and the reading is past that margin (7 cents, Precise 3: `InTuneDetector.exitCentsFor`), without a break for `AllStringsInTune.UNTICK_SECONDS` (1 s) on that string. So a pluck's sharp attack, a wobble or the gap between plucks keeps it, nothing flickers, and a peg turned away shows within a second or so; back in tune, it is ticked again. Merely not held in tune (a new pluck settling, a reading inside the exit margin) keeps it. They start afresh with a new tuning or A4 (seen in any listening or detected state) and go when listening stops (the tuner left for more than the 5 s `WhileSubscribed` grace, the app in the background, the microphone lost); the screen shows them only for the tuning it shows. Only the plain tuner shows them (the pitch trail too): the strum check, the 12th-fret check (whose own badges take that corner) and the chord name hide them, and the compact layout's small chips leave them out, like the octave mark. On a 12-string the tick and the octave mark both show: the badge at the top right, the "8" at the top left. No switch of its own: it follows *Show tuning help* (`showTuneHint`, [Lifecycle and settings](lifecycle.md)), with the up/down hint (owner, 2026-11-01: so experienced players can turn both off); off, `TunerViewModel.tunedStrings` stays null and `TunerContent` shows no tick and TalkBack says no "Tuned" (the ticks are still kept, for Google's rating window, and show as soon as it is on again). `TunerViewModelTest` (held in tune, a glide through the note, chromatic mode, a new tuning or A4, leaving, drifting off and back, the switch off), `RatingMomentTest` (the tracker, the untick rule), `TunerContentTest` (state, Dutch, own tuning only, not under a tool, nothing moves, the switch off), `SettingsContentTest` (the switch's words in English and Dutch); `StyleScreenshots` shows the low E and the A ticked.
- **String buttons' colours** (Phase 65): the name in full ink, the edge `controlOutline` (the muted colour, or Futuristic's accent, strong enough for 3:1 against the background), on `stringChipFill` (the high surface container at 85 % on the background), all checked by `StylePaletteTest` in every style, light and dark; until then the name was muted and the edge the track colour, about 1.3:1, fainter than the round buttons above them.
- **String row, 4 to 8 strings** (`StringRowFit.kt`, JVM-tested): while every button fits at full size in its 48 dp touch target, the row spreads them evenly over its width (`SpaceEvenly`), so a bass's four sit wider apart than a guitar's six. Where they don't fit (7 or 8 strings upright on a narrow phone, 8 in the controls pane on its side), each button gets an equal share of the row and shrinks with its lock-ring gap (at least 2 dp) so neighbours stay 4 dp apart: 41 dp shares for 8 strings on a 360 dp phone. Its touch area is its share by 48 dp tall; Compose widens a touch just beside a button to the nearest one. The compact layout's small row packs its 32 dp chips 4 dp apart and shrinks the same way only if its column can't hold them. Every string's name keeps its size where it fits and otherwise shrinks to fit its button (`TextAutoSize`, down to 8 sp; the octave and line height are relative, so they shrink with it): squeezed buttons, and wide faces such as Space Age's "Mi" at 2× text. Strings stay in instrument order (a re-entrant ukulele's G4 first). `StringRowStylesTest` checks 4, 5, 7 and 8 strings, the ukulele, the 12-string and the banjo upright, on its side and compact, at 1× and (solfège) 2× text.
- **The banjo's short string** (Phase 57, option A of `docs/design/phase57-preview.html`): a string that starts partway up the neck (`Tuning.shortStrings`, the banjo's fifth, first in the row) gets the same button and a small extra gap after it, 12 dp (6 dp between the compact layout's small chips), as padding outside the button, so it is no part of what a tap reaches. The gap is taken from the row's width before the buttons are spread (`stringRowFitFor`, `stringFaceInset`, so the strum switch and the instrument button still line up with the end faces) and dropped where the row would be squeezed (`shortStringGapFor`). `StringRowStylesTest.theBanjosShortStringStandsALittleApart`: in each banjo tuning the first gap is 12 dp wider than the others, which are alike, upright and on its side, 1× and 2× text; every language at 2× text (`@EveryLanguage`).
- **The 12-string's octave pairs** (Phase 57, option A of `docs/design/phase57-preview.html`): a course with a second string an octave up (`Tuning.hasOctaveString`) carries a small "8" (as music marks an octave up, 8va; not translated) in its button's top left corner, inside the face (in right-to-left languages too, as the badges stay at the top right), labelSmall at 75 % in the text colour at 80 %; TalkBack hears "E 2, with octave string" (`tuner_spoken_octave_course`, NL "E 2, met octaafsnaar"), the badge hidden from it. The compact layout's small chips leave it out. A tick or a 12th-fret result's badge sits across from it, so both show (until Phase 65's last step the "8" sat at the top end, under the badge). `StringRowStylesTest.theTwelveStringMarksItsOctavePairs`: four marks, each wholly inside its button, upright and on its side, 1× and 2× text; `theTwelveStringsEightShowsBesideItsTick`: with every course ticked each "8" clear of its badge, TalkBack hearing "Tuned", no button moved.
- **Mode slot** (`ModeSlot`): measures both modes' content all the time, takes the taller height (at least 104 dp) and places only the current mode's, so a mode switch moves nothing, also at 2× font. In chromatic mode with the wave it shows `ChromaticReadout` (cents and frequency, hidden from TalkBack since the note's live region says them); with the needle the slot stays empty.
- **In tune:** meter marker, centre mark, note, cents text and highlighted string fade to the style's "in tune" colour (`StyleTokens.inTune`, read through `InTuneColor.kt`; Material 3 has no success role). A check mark appears at the pill's start, so it doesn't depend on seeing green (in the wave view too, see [Meter, wave and styles](ui-meter-and-styles.md), *Wave view*), and with the needle display the pill then says "In tune" instead of the cents (`tuner_in_tune`, TalkBack's own words, with a capital: "Zuiver", "Gestimmt"): in tune holds until 7 cents (Precise 3), and "7 cents flat" beside a check mark read as a contradiction (store screenshot 8, review of 2026-10-24). The cents go, rather than moving to the frequency line: the needle already shows where in the in-tune window the note sits, and a line that grows and shrinks as the note comes in and out of tune is busier. The switch follows the tuner's in-tune hysteresis, nothing new, and the pill keeps the width of its widest text (cents or "In tune"), so nothing moves. The wave view's pill holds only the check and is unchanged. The pill fills, a soft glow lights behind the note and the note pops (grows 8 % and springs back). Each runs once per change, read in the draw phase.
- **Pill turns green gradually** (2026-10-15, owner: the jump from grey to green was abrupt): before the note is in tune the pill already takes some of the "in tune" colour as it comes close (`pillTint`, JVM-tested): none from 25 cents off, rising with a smoothstep to 40 % at the target, the same flat or sharp, so it says "getting close" without repeating the meter's direction. Only in tune fills it fully (with the check mark), so a full pill still means in tune. The fill glides on a very slow spring (`StiffnessVeryLow`), so it follows the note, not every jitter of the cents; past 40 % the text switches (150 ms fade) to whichever of `onSurface` and `onInTune` has more contrast on the fill at that moment, so it stays readable all the way (a blend of the two was grey on mid-green in the phone check); below 40 % it never changes colour.
- **Bloom and sheen** (2026-10-15, owner, as the mock-up shown in chat): the fill is drawn as a bloom (`drawPillBloom` in `PillTint.kt`), a soft oval in the pill's proportions that grows from the middle and deepens with the fill (`bloomReach`, `bloomCentre`, JVM-tested), the same both sides; past 70 % a flat layer fades in, so in tune the pill is even to its ends. As the note gets in tune one soft white band (32 %) crosses the pill once, 250 ms after, over 900 ms (`drawPillSheen`), then all is still. A real string drifts a few cents and drops in and out of tune while it rings, so the sheen follows `InTuneTickRule` (like the haptic tick) with a 6 s quiet time: the same note sheens again only after 6 s out of tune; another note at once. The sheen runs in its own coroutine, so the note leaving tune never freezes it half way; none in the wave view or with "Remove animations". Phone check (Xiaomi, Futuristic): a tone sliding into tune, wobbling ±3 cents, drifting 11 cents flat for a second and back sheened once.

## Strum check view
Strum once and every string of the tuning shows flat, in tune or sharp at once (the detector: [Strum check and metronome](strum-and-metronome.md), *Strum check (polyphonic)*).
- **Entry point:** a switch above the strings, at the end of a row of its own, its outline ending where the last string button's face does (`StrumCheckToggle`: strings with a sweep across them, in the style's button outline and shape, filled with `primaryContainer` while on), so it lives with guitar mode's controls and leaves the top corners empty. The string row spreads its buttons evenly, so its edges are not the buttons': `GuitarControls` takes the faces' inset from the same fit rules the row uses (`stringFaceInset`, JVM-tested; the row is symmetric, so one inset serves both ends) and lines the switch's outline and the tuning's name up with them (at the row's edge the switch sat up to 22 dp past a bass's last string; reading the buttons' positions as alignment lines during layout left the row at stale positions when the tuning changed). Not saved: the app always opens on the tuner. Switching to chromatic turns it off; switching it on from chromatic goes to guitar mode. In the compact layout on its side it is reached like the other controls (tap the screen).

## Tour
On first start a short tour points out the tuner's main parts, one at a time, as the approved mock-up [docs/design/tour-preview.html](../design/tour-preview.html) (its top comment is the design; owner decided 2026-10-14). Code: `:feature:tuner` `tour/` (`TourHost` and the overlay in `TourOverlay.kt`, the pure `TourGeometry.kt`, `TourPart`, `TourTargets`), wired in `MainActivity` (its rules in `FirstStartTour.kt`). Four steps before the first string is tuned (Phase 43; it had eight, two of them pointing at an empty meter and note): the meter and the note together, the instrument button, the strings, the tools button. The mode switch, focus mode and the navigation tabs explain themselves and have none. Its strings step ("Tap: tune only this string. Hold: hear it") is how people find the string lock, and the only place that says a long press plays the reference tone (owner, 2026-10-25: no extra hint or mark on the strings).
- **Layer:** `TourHost` wraps the whole window (screen and navigation bar or rail) and lays the tour on top, never in the layout, so nothing moves when it comes or goes; under the dim layer the balloon may cover any part. Taps outside the balloon are swallowed (the lit part isn't live, so the tour never changes a setting): the layer only has to be hit, which keeps them from the screen below, and consumes nothing, because consuming cancelled the buttons' taps whenever a finger moved a pixel (v1.0.1 on the S26); Back = Skip tour. While it runs the screen below is `clearAndSetSemantics {}`: TalkBack (and UI Automator) find only the balloon.
- **Parts:** each marks itself with `Modifier.tourTarget(part)` (a `Modifier.Node` that reports its unclipped bounds in the window to `TourTargets`, found through `LocalTourTargets`; the pieces of one part are joined): `READOUT` (the meter look, also with the wave display, or the strum panel in its place, and the note's block under it: its name with the cents pill and frequency, or with the wave; lit together as one, "How far off, and which note"), `PICKER` (the instrument button's outline, only in guitar mode, like the strum switch), `STRINGS`, `STRUM` (the "more tools" button's outline, or the strum check switch's in its place). The mode switch, focus mode's button and the bar's or rail's three tabs mark themselves with `tourControl()`: no step's part, but controls the balloons keep clear of. The strum switch and strings mark themselves only in guitar mode (`inTour`), since chromatic mode keeps them composed but unplaced and their old bounds mustn't count. A part out of view in a scrolling column (very large fonts) is brought into view (`bringIntoView`). Steps (`TourPart.stepsFor`): all four in guitar mode (the tools button left out where strum check isn't offered; every instrument has it today), also in focus mode, whose compact layout shows its controls while the tour runs; in chromatic mode (only when started from Settings) `READOUT` alone, "1 of 1" with Done (no strings, instrument button or tools button there).
- **Order** (`tourOrder`; owner, 2026-10-15: the balloons jumped around): the reading order of the parts in the current layout, the tools button always last (what tunes the guitar first, the extras after it; until Phase 43 the bar's Metronome and Settings pair was last). `readingOrder` is an XY cut on the parts' bounds: blocks side by side with a clear gap from top to bottom (the two panes) left to right, each on its own; inside a block, rows (boxes whose heights overlap) top to bottom; in a row, left to right. Side by side goes first so a pane scrolled on its own (large fonts) never reorders the tour; upright the full-width meter keeps everything one block. Upright: meter and note, instrument button, strings, then the tools button. On a phone on its side (the compact tuner shows the two panes during the tour) and in the two-pane layout: the readout pane's meter and note, then the controls pane's instrument button and strings, then the tools button. The overlay derives the order from the live bounds; parts not laid out yet go after the others, before the tools button. The step index is saved, so a turned phone carries on at the same step number in its own order. In a right-to-left language (Arabic) the screen is mirrored and `readingOrder(rightToLeft = true)` reads side-by-side blocks right to left, so the order stays meter and note, instrument button (now at the strings' right end), strings, tools button (now at their left end); the balloons are placed in window coordinates (`place`, not `placeRelative`), so they follow the mirrored parts, while Skip tour and Next swap sides with the layout.
- **Dim and spotlight:** on a layer of its own, dark themes the style's background mixed 50 % with black at 80 % (it keeps the style's hue), light themes the background mixed 60 % with ink at 80 % (a clear grey in the hue; 40 % at 72 % measured only 2× on the Xiaomi); with a lighter dim the owner found the lit part hardly stood out. The target, checked in phone screenshots: the lit part at least 3× as bright as the same kind of part under the dim. The spotlight is cut out (`DST_OUT`): the part plus 8 dp, kept in the window, its corner the part's (the mock-up's button, string-button or card radius per style, `tourCornerDp`) plus 8 dp, at most half the hole; Futuristic's corners cut (at most 12 dp); a soft 14 dp edge (`BlurMaskFilter`, Android 9+; before that a sharp edge). A soft halo hugs the edge outside: the style's accent, 28 % at the edge in dark themes and 20 % in light, fading to nothing over 12 dp (`BlurMaskFilter.Blur.OUTER` on the hole's outline, so it follows Futuristic's cut corners; Android 9+, none before). It is steady, glides with the spotlight and fades with the dim; no outline, no pulse.
- **Balloon:** no outline; filled with the accent (`primary`), text in on-accent; above the text the style's mark (`NavMark`, as under the open tab) and the step count ("2 of 4", 12 sp, at 78 %); the text 16 sp; Skip tour (a text button) and Next / Done (filled on-accent with accent text; Futuristic a neon outline) side by side, or on two rows where they don't fit (`TourButtonsPolicy`, whose reported height follows the same rule, so the placement knows a wider balloon is shorter); the last step has nothing left to skip, so Done stands alone at the end. `shapes.medium` corners; Next the style's button shape with at most 20 dp corners. A soft two-layer shadow (Futuristic: an accent glow) under the body and the 14 × 8 dp pointer, drawn with `setShadowLayer` (Android 9+). Its width hugs the text (`IntrinsicSize.Max`), at most min(window less its side margins, 260 dp × font scale); it grows with the font without a cap. The buttons keep 48 dp touch targets, so the bottom padding is 4 dp instead of the mock-up's 8.
- **Placement** (`placeBalloon`, JVM-tested): measured from the part's own element, not the spotlight: the pointer's tip ends 2 dp from the element (the balloon may lie over the spotlight's 8 dp pad and soft edge, never over the element). The step's preferred side (the meter and note and the strings below; the instrument button and the tools button above) if the balloon fits between 40 dp from the top and 24 dp from the bottom (or the system bars, if more); else the other side. Where the preferred side would cover another interactive control (`TourTargets.controlsBesides`: the instrument button, tools button and string row, and the controls with no step: the mode switch, focus mode's button and the bar's tabs) and the other side fits without covering one, the other side. Where neither fits: beside the part (right, then left, again a clear one first: the rail on a phone on its side, an addition to the mock-up); where it fits on no side at its full width, it narrows to the room beside the part (at least 200 dp, `MIN_BESIDE_WIDTH`), taller, if it then fits there (the strings' step at 2× text on a phone on its side); else it may come 8 dp from the screen's edges (`NEAR_EDGE`, never into the system bars): above and below, and on the sides too where the 16 dp it gains in width is what lets it fit, wider being shorter (`balloonWidth`; the note's step in German and Indonesian at 2× text on a 360 × 800 dp phone with navigation buttons, Space Age's tall note leaving 220 dp below it: at 328 dp Skip tour and Next missed one row by 0.3 and 10 dp, so the balloon was 232 dp tall; at 344 dp they share a row and it is 180 dp, measured 2026-10-20); else over the part on the side with more room, without a pointer. Under the dim it may still cover the meter and the note. Where no side is clear it takes the one that fits: upright the strings' balloon goes above over the instrument button, since below there is no room, and the meter and note's goes below, into the row above the strings, since above it there is no room under the mode switch (on the Xiaomi it fits between the instrument and tools buttons, covering neither). Centred on the part, at least 16 dp (or the cutout) from the sides; the pointer at the element's middle (the meter and note: the middle of the pair), at least 27 dp from the balloon's ends; the tools button at the row's end clamps the balloon, the pointer stays over the button. `TourTargets.shown` (`TourShown`) says which step is on screen and where its balloon and pointer tip are, for tests.
- **Motion:** the dim fades in (300 ms), then the balloon (200 ms); Next fades the balloon out (150 ms) while the spotlight glides (300 ms, cubic ease in-out: position, size and corners together), then the next balloon fades in; Skip tour, Done and Back fade it all out (250 ms). With "remove animations" every change is instant (`rememberAnimationsOff`). The balloon fades with `CompositingStrategy.ModulateAlpha`, so its pointer and shadow, outside its box, aren't clipped.
- **Once:** `tourDue` = asked for from Settings, or not `tourSeen` and not ended in this session; `tourShown` = due, on the tuner, and the tuner itself shows (the microphone allowed: `TunerScreen(onMicGrantedChange)`), so it comes after the microphone question. Skip tour, Back and Done end it for good: `tourSeen` is saved. Updates never show it: `decideTourOnce()` runs at every start and, while `tour_seen` is missing, saves it as seen if any other setting is saved (an install from before the tour, or one restored from a backup; an old key such as `string_lock_tip_seen` counts too) and as not seen on an empty file, so a fresh user who changes a setting before allowing the microphone still gets the tour. The step is saved (`rememberSaveable`), so a turned phone carries on where it was; after it ended it starts again at step 1. ## Display
Setting "Display": `TunerDisplay` **needle** (cents in a pill and the frequency) or **wave** (the default, in the picked wave look). The meter is there with both. The wave takes the cents' place, so `NoteDisplay` and `TunerContent` lay it out differently: no pill colour, no frequency line, the check mark in the "in tune" colour, `ChromaticReadout` in chromatic mode, the compact note on a phone on its side.
