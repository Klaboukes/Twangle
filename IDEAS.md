# Twangle — Ideas for after v1.0

Not planned yet: ideas the owner wants written down, to pick up after launch only if their condition is met. Each becomes a numbered phase in [ROADMAP.md](ROADMAP.md) once it's chosen, and leaves this file. The music tools became Phases 28–34 on 2026-10-16.

## Custom themes (idea, 2026-10-16)
Let people make their own look on top of the six styles. **Condition:** start only if several closed-test testers or early users ask to change the look. Rules that stay: no internet, no account, readable by the same rule as the six palettes (`StylePaletteTest`), calm by default. The six built-in backgrounds stay drawn in code (they fit every screen, have light, dark and quiet versions, and weigh almost nothing).
1. ☐ **Your own background photo with matching colours:** a "Make your own" card at the end of the style choices; the photo comes from Android's photo picker (no permission), is copied into the app's own storage, shrunk to the screen once, dimmed or softly blurred under a veil of the theme's colour (stronger behind Settings) so the needle and note stay readable; the theme's colours are taken from the photo, like Material You does from the wallpaper. The widget and watch keep their built-in look.
2. ☐ **A small theme builder** around that card's live preview: background (a built-in picture or your photo), main colour, lettering, meter look, light / dark / follow the phone. Twangle works out the other colours and keeps them readable; no editing of every colour one by one.
3. ☐ **On the shelf, only if 1 and 2 prove popular:** a theme code (a short text to save and share themes; a theme with a photo needs a small file instead) and a round trip with the user's own AI app: Twangle shares instructions through Android's share menu, the user chats with any AI, copies the code it gives, and Twangle offers to preview it from the clipboard. Never a live connection (that would need internet and an account).

## After the drone and ear training (idea, 2026-10-26)
What is left of these two ideas now that they are Phases 48 and 55. **Condition:** only once both have landed, and if players use them.
1. ☐ **The drone and the metronome together:** both sounding while you practise, which needs them on one screen (leaving the metronome stops its clicks today)
2. ☐ **A scale or mode by ear:** Twangle plays a scale over the drone, the player names it (major, Dorian, Mixolydian, …). Phase 55 stops at three exercises against bloat, so this would replace or join them only if players and teachers ask, under the same rules: a tool, not a game, no scores kept

## Twangle on the iPhone (idea, 2026-10-26)
One codebase for both phones: Kotlin Multiplatform, with Compose Multiplatform drawing the screens on the iPhone. **Condition:** only after v1.0 is on Play; the restructuring is the wrong thing to do in the weeks before a release. A friend of the owner has offered to build the iPhone side (2026-10-26): he has the Mac, the iPhone and the Apple developer account, which removes the cost, so after the launch this becomes a phase on the owner's say-so rather than waiting for reviews to ask for it (the feasibility look of 2026-10-26).

**What carries over as it is:** the pitch detector and all the music maths (`:core:dsp` has no Android in it), and all sixteen languages (the translations keep their file format). **Shared after a mechanical move:** every screen, the six styles, the meter and wave looks, the metronome and the practice tools; their texts and fonts move to Compose's multiplatform resources (`composeResources`, the same `values-<language>/strings.xml` layout). **Needs an iPhone twin:** the microphone and the sound output (`:core:audio`, behind the same interfaces; `AVAudioSession`'s measurement mode is the iPhone's counterpart of `UNPROCESSED`), haptics, keep screen on, the in-app language switch, the app shell and its navigation. **Written in Swift, not shared:** the home-screen widget (WidgetKit) and an Apple Watch app (watchOS has no Compose; it could still use the Kotlin detector as a framework). The UI-test scripts, the overnight run, the store screenshots and the release scripts speak to Android phones and to Play; each needs an Apple counterpart, and every visible change is then checked on two platforms.

**The arrangement with the friend** (to settle before he starts):
- **One codebase, not a second app.** He works in this repository, on branches and pull requests like every change here, with access to the private repository (the free plan allows collaborators); the iPhone app is the same Kotlin code with an iPhone shell. Not his own Swift app around the pitch detector: two apps would drift apart, and the iPhone one would live only as long as his spare time.
- **Who does which part.** Step 1, the modules becoming multiplatform, touches every module and collides with the daily Android work, so it is a roadmap phase done here on Windows: the Android app keeps building, and the shared code compiles for the iPhone on Windows, though only a Mac can produce the app. His part starts when it lands: steps 2 and 3, the audio twins, the shell, the Xcode project and the App Store side. He checks the iPhone app on his phone the way the owner checks the Android one; the owner can't. His Mac is the gate for iPhone changes, as the owner's local run is for Android; no iPhone build in CI (GitHub's Mac runners cost ten times the minutes). A change here can break the iPhone build unnoticed: he keeps the iPhone side in step, and a broken iPhone build never holds up an Android fix.
- **Whose name on the App Store.** The developer account's name is the seller: it owns the listing, the reviews and the crash reports, and carries the legal responsibility, privacy policy included. Advice: the owner enrols (about €99 a year, also for a free app; Apple's identity check may need an iPhone, borrow his) and adds him to the account, so he builds and uploads while the app stays in the owner's name and under the free-forever promise. If it is his account instead, the iPhone app is his in Apple's eyes; a transfer later is possible but formal. Either way every release passes Apple's review (usually a day), and the store listing needs a privacy label and a sentence on why the app uses the microphone; both are easy, Twangle records nothing and sends nothing.
- **The licence** (Phase 58). Today the owner is the only author, which lets a GPL 3 app go on the App Store (others could not republish the code there). His contributions are his copyright: a short written note that they are under Twangle's licence and that the owner may publish the app on the App Store keeps Phase 56 simple.

**Without him** it would also need a Mac (Apple only lets iPhone apps be built on one; a Mac mini is the cheapest, a rented cloud Mac does for building but not for the daily phone check), an iPhone for the device checks (the simulator can use the Mac's microphone, but the tuner is tested against a real phone's microphone and audio path) and the developer programme.

**Rejected routes:** redrawing every screen natively in Swift over the shared Kotlin maths (months of work, two user interfaces to keep in step forever), or a full Swift rewrite (two of everything). Compose draws its own pixels, so Twangle looks the same on both phones, which suits a tuner with six custom styles.
1. ☐ **Here: the modules become multiplatform,** one at a time, with the Android app working unchanged at every step (the UI tests prove it): `:core:dsp` first (its tests keep running on the JVM), then `:core:settings` (DataStore is multiplatform), then the design system and the feature modules with their texts and fonts in `composeResources`.
2. ☐ **His, on the Mac: the iPhone twins in `:core:audio`:** the recorder (AVAudioEngine in measurement mode, float frames into the same `Flow<FloatArray>`) and the three players (reference tones, clicks, drone), checked on his iPhone against the laptop's test tones.
3. ☐ **His, on the Mac: the iPhone shell:** the app, navigation bar or rail, microphone permission, keep screen on, haptics, the language switch, opening screen and icon; then the Mac side of the scripts (UI tests on the simulator, screenshots, a release script for App Store Connect) and the App Store listing.
4. ☐ **On the shelf, only if the phone app finds users:** the widget (WidgetKit) and the Apple Watch app (Swift, with the Kotlin detector as a framework).

## From the tuner comparison, left out (the owner's review, 2026-10-26)
The comparison of eight popular Play tuners (2026-10-25, Phase 51) listed features Twangle lacks. The owner's review of 2026-10-26 ("just because other tuners have a feature doesn't make it good or useful; maybe Twangle can offer a better replacement") left these four out. Each stays here with why, and with the condition that brings it back.
1. ☐ **Transposing instruments** (clarinet, trumpet, saxophones, horn: the tuner showing the note as the wind player reads it). A feature of the professional chromatic tuners, not of the tuners compared, and not what someone searching for a guitar tuner needs. **Condition:** wind players ask. Then not as a key in Settings (once set, a guitarist sees the wrong notes with nothing on screen saying why) but as instruments in the instrument panel, "Trumpet in B♭", "Alto sax in E♭", which put the tuner in chromatic mode and say on the button what is going on; the reference tone and strings mode unchanged
2. ☐ **Which way to turn the peg** (a drawn headstock with a curved arrow on the string's peg, after Simply Tune's step-by-step guidance): the needle shows within a second whether the peg went the right way, and the drawing is wrong for half the instruments (three-a-side, six-in-line, classical, bass, ukulele friction pegs and violin pegs all turn differently, and it depends on how the string was wound). Hint clutter, against the owner's rule. **Condition:** beginners say, unprompted, that they turned a peg the wrong way and the needle didn't tell them
3. ☐ **On to the next string by itself** (GuitarTuna: the lock moves to the next string once one is in tune). GuitarTuna needs it because its mode is "pick a string, tune it, next"; Twangle's Auto mode already follows the string plucked, and a player locks a string exactly when Auto picked wrong, so moving the lock by itself undoes the lock, and it is the kind of surprise the calm rule forbids. **Condition:** testers who lock strings ask for it
4. ☐ **Temperaments** (just, Pythagorean, meantone, as gStrings and DaTuner Pro have): frets are built for equal temperament, and the chord charts and capo helper assume it; a just or meantone tuning puts a guitar in tune in one key and out in the others, and those temperaments serve harpsichord and early-music players, who use specialist apps. What guitarists do ask for are **sweetened tunings** (Peterson sells them): each string offset by a few cents so chords sound sweeter, as James Taylor's well-known tuning does. That fits Twangle's tuning model with a cent offset per string in the tuning editor, free where Peterson charges. **Condition:** players ask for sweetened tunings or James Taylor's; the temperaments themselves only if early-music players ask
