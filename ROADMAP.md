# Twangle — Roadmap

Status legend: ☐ todo · ◐ in progress · ☑ done

Each phase ends in a state that builds and can be demoed. Phases are worked in the order they are listed here, not by number. See [ARCHITECTURE.md](ARCHITECTURE.md) and its topic files for design details.

Every finished phase is in [ROADMAP-ARCHIVE.md](ROADMAP-ARCHIVE.md), with the overview of the music tools that went into v1.0 and the finished part of Phase 23; only the open phases are below. Move a phase there once all its items are ☑, its owner checks moved to *Owner checks* below, and finished owner items too. Ideas not planned yet live in [IDEAS.md](IDEAS.md).

**Phase numbers:** a new phase takes the next free number and goes where it belongs in the order. If that leaves the open phases' numbers out of order, renumber them so they count up again (finished phases keep theirs) and add the mapping to the archive's opening note, which lists every renumbering so far; commits, PRs and branches from before a renumbering use the old numbers.

## Owner checks (live tests)
Done by the owner by hand, with a real guitar or headset. They are not roadmap increments: `/roadmap-step` skips them. The first two come before production.

- ☐ **TalkBack's own voice** (Phase 81, ~5 min). **Failed on 2026-11-06** on the Xiaomi with 2.0.0 from Play: the tuner showed notes while only TalkBack spoke, and its spoken readings ("x cents sharp/flat") set off the next ones in a loop. Phase 81 built the fix on 2026-11-07, so with a build from `main` the whole check again: TalkBack on (volume keys held 3 s, or *Settings → Accessibility*), no headphones, phone on the table, the tuner open with the speaker loud. Play nothing and let it talk, then tune two strings: the tuner never shows a note while only TalkBack speaks, the reading doesn't set off the next one in a loop, and a reading is said once per note ("E 2, 12 cents flat, Tighten", then "E 2, in tune"), not again as its cents move. Then the metronome's Listen, Chords' Listen and Ear training's Play it back the same way. Watch for a stray reading right after each spoken one: in Claude's spot check (2026-11-07) readings of the room's sounds ("E 4, 221 cents flat") were said every 5–10 s after a tone, one 0.55 s after TalkBack stopped speaking, so the 0.4 s tail after TalkBack's voice (`ScreenReaderSpeech.TAIL_SECONDS`) may be too short. Tell Claude what you hear
- ☐ **The vibration guide, eyes closed** (Phase 85): TalkBack on, the guide on (*While tuning → Vibration guide*, on by itself with TalkBack), phone on the table or in a pocket; tune all six strings without looking, first in a quiet room, then with music playing: each string reaches in tune at least as quickly as with speech alone (the bar: a blind Roadie user tunes a whole electric guitar by vibration in under 25 s, and a player on stage has 10–20 s between songs, AppleVis 2026), the direction is never mistaken (one tap: tighten, two: loosen), and the guide never makes the tuner show a note by itself. Then on the Galaxy Watch with the guide's switch on: the wrist taps are felt while playing and tell the direction the same way
- ☐ **The high-contrast look at a glance** (Phase 86): on the Xiaomi at low brightness, the phone at arm's length (or through the phone's magnifier): tune a string and start the metronome; the note, the direction and in tune read at a glance; then the same outside in daylight; and whether the picked one in a row of choices (the metronome's 4/4, a grey fill in this look) stands out enough
- ☐ **When you have the gear** (postponed 2026-10-16, no hurry):
  - Phase 5, a Bluetooth headset: with one connected, the tuner still listens through the phone's mic and reads a string correctly
  - Wired earbuds with a mic (3.5 mm plug, or USB-C), for a noisy room (2026-10-31): with them plugged in, Android listens through the earbud mic, which Twangle allows (only a Bluetooth headset is overruled), so the mic can lie close to the guitar. Lay the mic on the guitar's top a few centimetres from the soundhole (not in it), hands off the cable, and tune all six strings, first in a quiet room, then with loud music from the laptop: the note and cents follow each string, the low E included, at least as quickly and steadily as with the phone's own mic, and better in the noise; the reference tone plays in the earbuds; pulling the plug goes back to the phone's mic without a restart. If it passes, one line on the website's help page in English and Dutch ("Noisy room? Plug in wired earbuds and lay the mic on the guitar"), no hint in the app; if the low strings fail, the line names guitar and ukulele only
  - Phase 15, a bass (or a 7-string guitar or cello): the lowest strings read correctly (B0, E1, B1, C2), and the tuner shows no note while the amp only hums
- ☐ **Phases 63 and 65 on the tablet and the watch** (2026-11-01, no rush; both were checked only in forced window sizes, on the phone and on the Wear OS emulator):
  - On the Lenovo tablet, upright and on its side, and on the Galaxy Watch: the buttons, pickers and switches look as on the phone (Phase 63)
  - On the Lenovo tablet, upright and on its side, in a light and a dark style (Phase 65, *Contrast and edges*): the string buttons and the round buttons stand out clearly from the background, the style's background runs under the navigation buttons (no white or black strip), and Settings' rows fade out under the title as you scroll
  - The upright tablet store screenshots (Phase 65, *The store pictures*): with the tablet connected, Claude runs `scripts/store-screenshots.sh` for English and Dutch; the pictures show one scaled-up column with no empty middle, every word readable
  - On the Galaxy Watch, TalkBack on (Phase 65, *TalkBack on the watch*): the dial is spoken in words, e.g. "A 2, 12 cents flat, Tighten" or "E 2, in tune", calmly (not on every small change), and "Another app is using the microphone." is spoken when one has it (a voice recorder open on the watch)

## Phase 87 — More languages, after 2.0
Goal: the biggest groups of people who can't read Twangle in any of its 25 languages get their own. Asked by the owner on 2026-11-05, after counting that the 25 languages reach roughly two-thirds of the world's people (about 55–60 % who can read). It waited for 2.0 to reach `main` (2026-11-06), so each language is translated once, against 2.0's texts. Urdu, Persian, Filipino and Malay stay out, as decided on 2026-10-28 and confirmed on 2026-11-05 (Phase 50's *More languages* in the archive: many phones in Pakistan and the Philippines are set to English, Indonesian is close to Malay, Google Play doesn't serve Iran). Each language is one `/add-language` (its texts in the phone app, the watch and the widget, its store listing, the note-name default, TalkBack's note names, digits, right to left, fonts, the phone check at 2× text, its store screenshots, the meaning review by a helper other than the one that translated it) and gets every release's "What's new" from then on. Every language added makes the night's `/translate`, the full check's language checks and the store screenshots a little longer (about 2 minutes a language for the screenshots). Before each one, check that Android phones offer it as a phone language and that Play has a store listing for it; a language with neither goes to [IDEAS.md](IDEAS.md) with that reason, not into the app. The website in these languages is Phase 56's *The website in more languages*, not this phase. In this order, the most readers first:
- ☐ **Tamil, Telugu and Marathi** (`ta`, `te`, `mr`, India, about 80–95 million each): for players who read neither Hindi nor English; each is its own `/add-language`; Marathi shares Hindi's script (Devanagari), so Hindi's guitar loanwords are the place to start, and Android may write its numbers in Devanagari digits
- ☐ **Swahili, Amharic and Hausa** (`sw`, `am`, `ha`, East and West Africa): Swahili for Kenya and Tanzania, Amharic (its own Ethiopic script) for Ethiopia, Hausa for Nigeria and Niger only if Android and Play both offer it (see the check above)
- ☐ **Burmese and Khmer** (`my`, `km`, Myanmar and Cambodia, almost all Android): both scripts stack letters above and below the line and put no spaces between words, so line breaks need the care Thai's got (languages.md, Thai); for Burmese, check that the phones there show standard Unicode Burmese, not the older Zawgyi encoding some still use
- ☐ **Romanian, Czech, Greek and Hungarian** (`ro`, `cs`, `el`, `hu`, Europe, about 10–25 million each): check that the app's Nunito, which may be cut down to the letters it needs (`scripts/subset-font.py`), keeps ș ț, ě ř ů and ő ű; Greek has no letters in Nunito, so it comes from the phone's font like the other scripts

## Phase 56 — Getting found, after launch
Goal: the rest of the growth plan, once Twangle is public on Play. The plan, its message and its rules (no paid ads, no tracking, no bought reviews, no other app named) are in Phase 50's goal. In order:
- ☐ **Store listing experiments:** after launch, **the owner** starts Play Console experiments one at a time, about two weeks each, Claude preparing the variants in every language the experiment covers: first the short description (the chosen one against the TalkBack one, already in *Being found*), then the first screenshot, then the icon. - ☐ **Where players ask:** Claude drafts one honest post per community ("I made a free tuner, no ads, no internet permission; what it does; I'm the developer"), short, in that community's language and tone, for r/guitar, r/Bass, r/ukulele, r/androidapps, r/WearOS (with the watch app), r/privacy, and a few big guitar forums in other languages; and short answers for the frequent "which tuner app do you use?" threads. **The owner** posts them, one per community, and answers questions. ## Phase 58 — Open the code after v1.0
Goal: the Twangle repository goes public as open source, once v1.0 is live on Play. The licence is the **GPL version 3** (owner, 2026-10-17): anyone may copy, change and share the code, but what they build from it stays open under the same licence, so nobody can turn Twangle into a closed app with ads. The bundled fonts keep their own SIL Open Font License, and the AndroidX and Kotlin libraries' Apache 2.0 fits inside GPL 3.
- ☑ `LICENSE` with the full GPL 3 text, and a *Licence* section in README.md naming it (2026-11-05)
- ☑ README.md says the name "Twangle" and its icon are not covered by the licence, so copies must use their own name and icon
- ☑ The code read as an outsider would (2026-11-05): no leftovers of the old name InTune (every `InTune*` name means *in tune*, as CLAUDE.md says; renaming them would also reset the saved "vibrate when in tune" choice, whose key carries the words), no TODO or FIXME notes, no keys or personal paths in the code; the one misnamed test is now `InTunePrecisionDetectorTest`
- ☐ The owner decides which planning files go public with the code: `.claude/` (Claude's skills), CLAUDE.md, ROADMAP.md, ROADMAP-ARCHIVE.md, IDEAS.md and the mock-ups in `docs/design/` (67 pages on 2026-11-05). Nothing in them is secret, and keeping them shows openly how the app was made with Claude; leaving one out means deleting it before opening (and from the history, if the history is kept). If CLAUDE.md stays private, its hard rules move to a CONTRIBUTING.md, since code comments cite them ("CLAUDE.md, hard rule 2") and README's *Contributing* points there
- ☐ Before opening: check the history for anything that shouldn't be public (the owner's personal email in old commits, `local.properties`, keys); the owner decides whether to keep the history or start the public repository fresh. The launch review (2026-11-01) found 294 commits under the owner's Gmail address (244 under the no-reply one) and no key, keystore, `local.properties` or token anywhere in the history; CLAUDE.md and the docs also name the test devices' adb serials, the watch's network name and the owner's phone habits: the owner decides whether those stay
- ☑ The CI workflow's actions pinned to commit SHAs instead of tags (`actions/checkout@v7` and so on), so a moved tag can't change what runs once outsiders can open pull requests. **Done (2026-11-08):** checkout v7.0.1, setup-java v6.1.0, setup-gradle v6.4.1 and upload-artifact v7.0.2, each by its commit with the version in a comment; the workflow's head says how to update one
- ☐ The owner makes the repository public; later commits use the GitHub no-reply email
- ☐ While the owner is the only author, the licence can still change; once others contribute, it can't without them, so settle any change before accepting outside code
- ☐ Google's rating window (Phase 50) comes from a library under Google's own terms, not open source: F-Droid and IzzyOnDroid flag such a library, and GPL 3 alone doesn't allow it in a copy someone else builds. Settle it before opening:
  - ☑ **A build without it for F-Droid** (2026-11-08; lifecycle.md, *Google's rating window*): `-Ptwangle.foss=true` builds the phone app with no rating window (`RatingWindow.NONE`) and without the library, a guard stopping that build if anything of Google Play, Play services or Firebase comes in; a Gradle property rather than a flavour, so no task, script or the release changes name; the nightly CI builds it. Checked: its runtime libraries hold none of Google's (the Play build's five as before), the guard stops a build that has one, and the free release build runs on the Xiaomi
  - ☐ **The owner decides** (before opening, no hurry): either a short GPL exception for that one library in `LICENSE`, so anyone may build the Play version too, or the Play build only ever made by the owner, the published code's own build being the free one
