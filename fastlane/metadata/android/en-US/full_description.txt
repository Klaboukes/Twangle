A tuner that does what you need it to do. And nothing else.

Twangle gets out of your way. Open it, play a string, and you see the note and how far off it is, right down to a single cent. No ads popping up mid-tune. No account. Nothing to unlock. Every feature is free, and it stays that way.

Only you hear it. Twangle doesn't even have permission to use the internet, so the sound from your microphone stays on your phone: analysed live, never recorded, never sent. The code is open source, so anyone can check.

And it's not just for guitar. It's a bass tuner, ukulele tuner, violin tuner and chromatic tuner too. Once you're in tune, it keeps going: a metronome, chord charts, scales, a capo helper and ear training.

TUNE ANYTHING THAT PLAYS A NOTE
• Guitar (6, 7, 8 or 12 strings), bass (4 or 5 strings, down to the low B), ukulele (low G and baritone too), violin, viola, cello, mandolin and 5-string banjo
• Standard, Drop D, half step down, full step down, DADGAD, Open G, Open D, Open E, Open C, or a tuning you make yourself
• Anything else that plays one clear note: your voice, a sax, a trumpet, a harmonica, a kalimba… the chromatic tuner has you covered
• Tune by ear: touch and hold a string on the screen to hear its note
• Playing with an orchestra? Set A4 anywhere from 415 to 466 Hz

IT KEEPS TIME, TOO
• A metronome from 30 to 250 beats per minute: slide the tempo ruler or just tap the beat
• 2/4, 3/4, 4/4, 5/4, 6/8 and 7/8, with an accent on the first beat and lights to follow
• Clicks between the beats in eighths, triplets or sixteenths
• Speed trainer: the tempo climbs step by step until you hit your goal
• Keeps clicking while your own music or backing track plays, and can vibrate on the beat

PRACTISE
• Chord charts for your instrument and your tuning, even the ones you made yourself
• Scales on the fretboard: major, minor, pentatonic and blues, every note named
• Capo helper: pick the song's key and the shapes you like, and see where the capo goes
• Ear training: Play it back (hear the notes, play them on your instrument), Intervals and Chord type

TOOLS THAT LISTEN
• 12th-fret check: play a string open, then at the 12th fret. Twangle tells you if the saddle should move, which way, and on a guitar about how far
• Pitch trail: watch your bends, your vibrato and your long notes as a line. Works for singers too
• Chord name: strum a chord on guitar, ukulele or mandolin and see what it's called
• Strum check: strum all the strings at once and see which ones are off

ON YOUR WRIST
• A Wear OS app that tunes with the watch's own microphone, no phone needed
• Swipe left for a metronome that taps the beat on your wrist
• A tile and a watch-face complication, so it's always one tap away

TUNE WITHOUT LOOKING
• Vibration guide: one tap means tighten, two mean loosen, a long buzz means you're in tune
• Sound guide: short sounds tell you too high or too low, and a bell rings when you're in tune
• TalkBack says what to do first ("Tighten, E 2, 12 cents flat") and never talks over your string

MAKE IT YOURS
• Light, dark, or whatever your phone does, in your wallpaper's colours on Android 12 and newer
• Large text up to twice the normal size, and a high-contrast look
• Three meters: the classic needle, the light arc or the strobe
• Focus mode: turn your phone on its side and the tuner fills the screen, easy to read from across the room
• A home-screen widget that opens the tuner in one tap
• In 36 languages, with note names the way you learned them: C D E, H for B, or Do Re Mi

Free and offline. For good.
