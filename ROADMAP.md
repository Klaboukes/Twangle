## Owner checks (live tests)
Done by the owner by hand, with a real guitar or headset. They are not roadmap increments: `/roadmap-step` skips them.

- ☐ **When you have the gear** (postponed 2026-10-16, no hurry):
  - Phase 5, a Bluetooth headset: with one connected, the tuner still listens through the phone's mic and reads a string correctly
  - Wired earbuds with a mic (3.5 mm plug, or USB-C), for a noisy room (2026-10-31): with them plugged in, Android listens through the earbud mic, which Twangle allows (only a Bluetooth headset is overruled), so the mic can lie close to the guitar. Lay the mic on the guitar's top a few centimetres from the soundhole (not in it), hands off the cable, and tune all six strings, first in a quiet room, then with loud music from the laptop: the note and cents follow each string, the low E included, at least as quickly and steadily as with the phone's own mic, and better in the noise; the reference tone plays in the earbuds; pulling the plug goes back to the phone's mic without a restart. If it passes, one line on the website's help page in English and Dutch ("Noisy room? Plug in wired earbuds and lay the mic on the guitar"), no hint in the app; if the low strings fail, the line names guitar and ukulele only
  - Phase 15, a bass (or a 7-string guitar or cello): the lowest strings read correctly (B0, E1, B1, C2), and the tuner shows no note while the amp only hums
- ☐ **Phase 63 on the tablet and the watch** (2026-11-01, no rush): on the Lenovo tablet, upright and on its side, and on the Galaxy Watch, the buttons, pickers and switches look as on the phone (the review saw them only in forced window sizes and on the emulator)
- ☐ **TalkBack's own voice** (the launch review, 2026-11-01; before launch if you can, ~5 min): TalkBack on (volume keys held 3 s, or *Settings → Accessibility*), no headphones, phone on the table, the tuner open with the speaker loud. Play nothing and let it talk, then tune two strings: the tuner never shows a note while only TalkBack speaks, and the reading doesn't set off the next one in a loop. Then the metronome's Listen and Ear training's Play it back the same way. If TalkBack's voice shows up as a note, Phase 65's TalkBack item sets the readings aside while it speaks

## Phase 58 — Open the code after v1.0
Goal: the Twangle repository goes public as open source, once v1.0 is live on Play. The licence is the **GPL version 3** (owner, 2026-10-17): anyone may copy, change and share the code, but what they build from it stays open under the same licence, so nobody can turn Twangle into a closed app with ads. The bundled fonts keep their own SIL Open Font License, and the AndroidX and Kotlin libraries' Apache 2.0 fits inside GPL 3.
- ☐ `LICENSE` with the full GPL 3 text, and a *Licence* section in README.md naming it
- ☐ README.md says the name "Twangle" and its icon are not covered by the licence, so copies must use their own name and icon
- ☐ Before opening: check the history for anything that shouldn't be public (the owner's personal email in old commits, `local.properties`, keys); the owner decides whether to keep the history or start the public repository fresh. The launch review (2026-11-01) found 294 commits under the owner's Gmail address (244 under the no-reply one) and no key, keystore, `local.properties` or token anywhere in the history; CLAUDE.md and the docs also name the test devices' adb serials, the watch's network name and the owner's phone habits: the owner decides whether those stay
- ☐ The CI workflow's actions pinned to commit SHAs instead of tags (`actions/checkout@v7` and so on), so a moved tag can't change what runs once outsiders can open pull requests
- ☐ The owner makes the repository public; later commits use the GitHub no-reply email
- ☐ While the owner is the only author, the licence can still change; once others contribute, it can't without them, so settle any change before accepting outside code
- ☐ Google's rating window (Phase 50) comes from a library under Google's own terms, not open source: F-Droid and IzzyOnDroid flag such a library, and GPL 3 alone doesn't allow it in a copy someone else builds. Settle it before opening: a build without it for F-Droid (`RatingWindow.NONE`, e.g. a `foss` flavour), and either a short GPL exception for that one library in `LICENSE`, or the Play build only made by the owner

## Phase 64 — Must-fix before the next release (the launch review, 2026-11-01)
Goal: the last full review before launch (2026-11-01: code, UX and TalkBack, production readiness, DSP, tests and security, and the look on the S26 with that day's build) found no blocker, but two ways the app can crash, a question that sounds after Ear training is left, no automatic guard on the "no internet" promise, and a privacy policy behind the saved settings. They go into the version that goes to production: `/roadmap-step 64` before the next `/release`. No switch of their own: they are repairs inside the tools (Ear training's go with its switch). In order:
- ☑ **Ear training survives losing the microphone:** `EarTrainingViewModel.hear()` (behind the `heard` flow) catches the recorder's errors as the tuner, Listen and the watch already do. Today an `AudioRecorderException` during Play it back reaches `viewModelScope` uncaught and Android closes the app: the phone locked or Home pressed within the 5 s grace (Android 10+ silences the mic and `ChunkReader` throws `MicrophoneBusy`), a call, the Assistant, Android 12's microphone switch, or the mic failing to open. Instead the exercise shows one short line that the microphone stopped (a polite live region for TalkBack) and listens again when the screen comes back (`ON_START`), as the watch's `shown()`. `EarTrainingViewModelTest` with a fake listener that throws, while listening and on opening
- ☑ **No question after Ear training is left:** hiding the screen (`ON_STOP` → `stopSound()`) also cancels `nextJob`, so the next question, due 1.5–2.5 s after an answer, no longer sounds in the background and takes the audio focus from the player's music; it is asked when the screen shows again. `EarTrainingViewModelTest` in virtual time
- ☑ **A failed save never crashes:** reading the settings is guarded (`retryWhen`, `ReplaceFileCorruptionHandler`), writing isn't: every `dataStore.edit` throws `IOException` on a full phone or a storage error, and its ~49 callers launch it bare, the first-use notes in `MainActivity.onCreate` among them, so a full phone would crash at every start. Every write in `DataStoreSettingsRepository` goes through one private helper that catches `IOException` and logs it, so the app keeps running with the old value; the tuning editor's Save says it couldn't save (new text) instead of closing as if it had. JVM test with a store whose writes throw: each setter returns normally, the editor shows its line
- ☐ **The "no internet" promise checked by the build:** today only a comment in `gradle/libs.versions.toml` says the merged manifest was checked by hand, so a library update that brings `INTERNET` along would ship unnoticed and break the README, the store text and the Data safety answer. A check on the merged release manifests of `:app` and `:wear` (e.g. a task after `process*ReleaseMainManifest` that `check` and `test` run, so every local run and the nightly CI see it) fails on any permission outside an allowlist (today `RECORD_AUDIO`, `VIBRATE`, AndroidX's signature-level `DYNAMIC_RECEIVER_NOT_EXPORTED_PERMISSION`, and what the watch has now), naming `INTERNET`, `ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE` and `AD_ID` in its message; `scripts/release.sh bundle` refuses a bundle with one (`bundletool dump manifest`). The comment in the catalog then points to the check, and performance.md's *Release build* says it
- ☐ **The privacy policy names the metronome's new settings:** PRIVACY.md and PRIVACY.nl.md, *Settings stay on your phone*, add the clicks between the beats and the speed trainer's settings (Phase 62, `metronome_subdivision` and `metronome_trainer_*`), with a new date; `scripts/publish-privacy.sh` after the merge
- ☐ New texts in English and Dutch (the night languages by `/translate`); `./gradlew test lint assembleDebug`; Ear training's and the tuning editor's UI tests; on a test phone: Play it back listening, lock the phone, wait 10 s and unlock: no crash, the line shows, and it listens again; answer and press Home at once: no question sounds, the music plays on
