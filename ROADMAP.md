## Phase 58 — Open the code after v1.0
Goal: the Twangle repository goes public as open source, once v1.0 is live on Play. The licence is the **GPL version 3** (owner, 2026-10-17): anyone may copy, change and share the code, but what they build from it stays open under the same licence, so nobody can turn Twangle into a closed app with ads. The bundled fonts keep their own SIL Open Font License, and the AndroidX and Kotlin libraries' Apache 2.0 fits inside GPL 3.
- ☐ `LICENSE` with the full GPL 3 text, and a *Licence* section in README.md naming it
- ☐ README.md says the name "Twangle" and its icon are not covered by the licence, so copies must use their own name and icon
- ☐ Before opening: check the history for anything that shouldn't be public (the owner's personal email in old commits, `local.properties`, keys); the owner decides whether to keep the history or start the public repository fresh
- ☐ The owner makes the repository public; later commits use the GitHub no-reply email
- ☐ While the owner is the only author, the licence can still change; once others contribute, it can't without them, so settle any change before accepting outside code
- ☐ Google's rating window (Phase 50) comes from a library under Google's own terms, not open source: F-Droid and IzzyOnDroid flag such a library, and GPL 3 alone doesn't allow it in a copy someone else builds. Settle it before opening: a build without it for F-Droid (`RatingWindow.NONE`, e.g. a `foss` flavour), and either a short GPL exception for that one library in `LICENSE`, or the Play build only made by the owner

## Phase 61 — The watch: quick to open, felt on the wrist, a finer look (owner, 2026-10-30)
Goal: the watch tuner stays a plain, useful tuner (owner, 2026-10-30: "useful features", not advanced ones; don't blow up the app), for the update that brings the watch app to Play (decision 3). A watch tuner is worth it only if it opens faster than finding the phone, and the watch can tap the wrist, which a phone can't. Picked from the mock-up [docs/design/watch-ideas-preview.html](docs/design/watch-ideas-preview.html) (cards 1 and 6); its other ideas are in [IDEAS.md](IDEAS.md), *More for the watch*. Not tied to launch: `/roadmap-step 61` can run any time. The owner's real-watch check (*Owner checks*, Phase 20) comes first, as it may change or add items.
- ☑ **A finer look, a bigger marker:** the marker on the ring larger (today 2.6× the ring's line, about 14 dp across on the Watch 5), and a little more finish to the ring and the in-tune glow, in the same calm style and colours — the owner asked for mock-ups after all and picked C, a needle about 30 dp long pointing in from the ring over a tick every 5 cents with ♭ and ♯ at the scale's ends ([docs/design/watch-look-preview.html](docs/design/watch-look-preview.html)), and C1, the phone's ribbon wave small under the cents ([docs/design/watch-wave-preview.html](docs/design/watch-wave-preview.html)), which moves by the phone's own `WaveMotion` (moved to `:core:dsp` for it). Also the owner's find: the cents line no longer shifts sideways as the number changes (a fixed slot for the number). Checked live on the Galaxy Watch 5 with a guitar (A2 −13, G3 −2, E2 0: needle, pulses, still in tune) and by 8 UI tests on the emulator
- ☑ **A Twangle tile:** a Wear OS tile beside the watch face with Twangle's name and one Tune button that opens the tuner, in the watch's Contemporary colours. The tile never listens: the microphone starts only once the tuner is open (Hard rule 4) — the name, the mark in the accent and a Tune edge button; checked on the Wear OS emulator (no recording while the tile shows, one once the tuner opens; an open tuner comes to the front). Found on the way: the screen keeper listened to the tuner in the background, so the mic stayed open behind the watch face until Android silenced it and the tuner came back saying another app had the mic; fixed, and coming back after a failure now tries again by itself
- ☑ **A watch-face shortcut:** a complication (the small round slot a watch face offers) with Twangle's icon that opens the tuner with one tap — the mark with "Tune" in a short-text slot ("Twangle" was cut to "TWAN…"), the mark alone in an icon slot, the launcher icon in a small-image slot; checked on the emulator's Utility face
- ☐ **Feel it in tune:** one soft tap on the wrist the moment the note comes in tune, when the ring starts to glow; once per tuned note, again only after the note leaves tune or changes (as the phone's `InTuneHaptic`). The watch's microphone must not hear the motor: the reading stays steady across the tap (a JVM test on the pipeline, then the real watch). Whether the watch needs a way to turn it off is decided with the owner after trying it — built: the system's click at 0.6 strength (the plain click where the motor can't), by the phone's rule (`InTuneTickRule`, now in `:core:dsp`); the pipeline looks away for about 190 ms as the note comes in tune, so the buzz can't move the reading (JVM: a 150–250 Hz motor buzz mixed in at the tap, every string, up to twice the note's level: still in tune, within a cent); taps checked on the emulator, which has no motor. Left: the try on the Galaxy Watch 5 and the off-switch decision
- ☐ Check on the Galaxy Watch 5 with a real guitar (and the Wear OS emulator): the new look, the tile and the shortcut open the tuner, the tap comes once per tuned note; texts in English and Dutch (the night languages by `/translate`); the watch's UI tests; the watch part of [ui-other-screens.md](docs/architecture/ui-other-screens.md) brought up to date

## Phase 62 — The metronome for practice: subdivisions and a speed trainer (owner, 2026-10-31)
Goal: the two things a practice metronome is expected to have and Twangle's lacks. Today it clicks once per beat; players keep strumming patterns and picking even, and feel triplets and swing, with softer clicks between the beats. And the usual way to learn a fast passage is to start slower and let the metronome speed up bit by bit. Both stay calm and plain: a tool, not a game, nothing scored or kept but the settings. Not tied to launch: `/roadmap-step 62` can run any time. **Switches:** subdivisions are an option of the metronome and need none; the speed trainer is something the player picks on its own, so it is a tool with its own switch in `ToolSwitches`, off in release builds until the owner's check passes. In order:
- ☐ **Where they go, a rough mock-up first** (Contemporary, one row per option, as `docs/design/more-tools-preview.html`): the metronome screen keeps its row of three buttons (Sound · Tap tempo · Listen, no fourth) and its dial, so the subdivision choice and the speed trainer must find room elsewhere, e.g. the subdivision beside the meter picker or in the Sound panel, and the trainer from a long press on Start or a panel of its own. **The owner** picks
- ☐ **Subdivisions in `:core:dsp`:** none (the default), 2 (eighths), 3 (triplets), 4 (sixteenths); in 6/8 and 7/8, where a click is already an eighth, only none and 2. Every click sound gets a fourth, softest accent for the clicks between beats, told apart from the plain beat by level and pitch. `ClickTrack` places them sample-exact by the same integer formula as the beats (no adding up of rounded intervals), and a tempo or meter change still takes effect from the next beat. At 250 BPM in sixteenths a click comes every 60 ms, shorter than today's 80–180 ms clicks, so the in-between click is short enough (or cuts the one before) to keep `ClickTrack`'s one voice; allocation-free. `ClickTrackTest` (every click of ten minutes on its ideal sample, each subdivision, odd tempos) and `ClickSoundsTest` (the new accent: no clipping, ends on 0, quieter than the plain beat)
- ☐ **Subdivisions saved and shown:** the choice in `SettingsRepository` (none when unset), handed to the player by `MetronomeViewModel`, heard from the next beat while playing; the beat lights and "Vibrate on the beat" stay on the beats, Listen and Tap tempo unchanged; TalkBack names each choice; steady sizes at 2× text; `MetronomeViewModelTest`, `MetronomeScreenTest`
- ☐ **The speed trainer:** a start tempo, a goal tempo and a step ("+5 BPM every 4 bars", the step and the number of bars each picked from a short list); the tempo rises on a bar's downbeat, the dial following, and holds at the goal. Changing the tempo by hand, Tap tempo or Listen ends it; Stop and Start begin again from the start tempo. Its settings saved; pure logic in `:core:dsp` with tests (each step on its downbeat, never past the goal), `MetronomeViewModelTest`, `MetronomeScreenTest`
- ☐ Texts in English and Dutch (the night languages by `/translate`); the metronome part of [strum-and-metronome.md](docs/architecture/strum-and-metronome.md) and [ui-other-screens.md](docs/architecture/ui-other-screens.md); the website's metronome page (`website/pages/en` and `nl`), then `scripts/publish-website.sh` after the merge
- ☐ Check on a test phone: each subdivision at slow and fast tempos in every meter, in the speaker and in headphones (no clicks lost, no crackle at 250 BPM in sixteenths); the trainer climbing to its goal; the metronome's UI tests
- ☐ **Owner check** added under *Owner checks*: play along with eighths, triplets and sixteenths, and practise a passage with the speed trainer: easy to follow, the beat still stands out, nothing tiring after a few minutes
