# People who recommend apps to others: emails to send

Phase 92, *People who recommend apps to others*. Notes for the owner, who picks the people, writes
each email **in their own words** under their own name, and sends them one at a time. Online only
(owner, 2026-10-28: no printed flyers). Written 2026-11-16.

Three kinds of people pass apps on to many others: **YouTube channels for beginners**, **guitar
teachers who teach online** and **Android sites and blogs**. One honest email from the maker, short
and clearly written for them, is the whole idea. A few a week at most, each one personal.

## The rules (every email)
- **Personal, short, one person at a time.** Their name, one real line about their work (a video, a
  lesson, an article you actually looked at), then Twangle. No mass mailing, no BCC, no mail-merge,
  no "Dear content creator". If you can't write the personal line, don't send it.
- **Say you're the developer** in the first lines.
- **One honest line on how it's made:** the code is written with Claude (an AI), to your design, and
  you test it on real phones (and a watch) with a real guitar.
- **The safe claim:** every feature free, no ads, no purchases, no account, no internet permission.
  Never "the only free tuner", "the best" or "the most accurate", nor "the only one with" a feature.
- **Lead with what Twangle does for their readers, not with "free, no ads".** New tuners from solo
  developers arrive every week and nearly all pitch "free, no ads" first, so editors and channels
  have heard it; it goes after the lead, in a line (communities.md, *Standing out*, checked
  2026-11-27). The privacy angle is the one exception: for a privacy site, no internet permission
  *is* the lead.
- **No other app named**, compared or criticised, not even "unlike the big tuners".
- **Nothing in return.** No payment, no gift, no "I'll share your channel", no asking for a review,
  a rating, a video or a mention. "If it's useful to you, here it is" is the whole ask. (Google
  removes apps whose makers reward reviews; it's also simply fair.)
- **Their way of being contacted.** Use the address or form they publish for this. If they only
  list social-media DMs, or say "no requests", skip them.
- **Links written out** at the end: Play, the website, the press page (below).

## Who, and how to find them

### 1. YouTube channels for beginners (guitar, ukulele, bass)
- **Who fits:** channels that teach beginners (first lessons, "how to tune your guitar", "gear for
  beginners", ukulele for kids), roughly a few thousand to a couple of hundred thousand
  subscribers, with a video in the last month. Small and mid-sized channels read their own mail and
  like hearing from viewers; the very big ones go through agencies and get hundreds of pitches.
- **Where they show a tuner anyway:** a channel whose tuning video uses a phone app is the most
  natural fit, as Twangle answers the beginner's own question ("which way do I turn the peg?").
- **How to find them:** search YouTube for *how to tune a guitar for beginners*, *beginner guitar
  lesson 1*, *ukulele for beginners*, *bass for beginners* (Dutch: *gitaar stemmen beginners*,
  *gitaarles beginners*), filter by *This year*, and watch a little before writing.
- **How to contact:** the channel's *About* (*More about this channel*) shows *View email address*
  when they list a business email; YouTube asks you to sign in and pass a check first, which you do
  yourself. Or a contact form on the channel's own website. If neither exists, skip them; don't
  write in the comments.
- **Angle:** beginners. Hold a string to hear its note and tune by ear, chord charts for the tuning
  they play in, a metronome with a speed trainer, ear training; nothing to sign up for and no ads in
  a child's lesson. (Not "it says which way to turn": every tuner shows flat or sharp.) The watch
  angle suits channels that review gear.

### 2. Guitar teachers who teach online
- **Who fits:** teachers with their own website who give lessons by video call or sell a course,
  especially those with a "resources", "handy apps" or "what you need" page for students. Start in
  the Netherlands and Belgium, in Dutch (the Dutch email below); English-speaking teachers after.
- **How to find them:** search for *online gitaarles*, *gitaarles via Zoom*, *gitaarles op afstand*,
  *ukelele les online*; in English *online guitar lessons resources apps*. Look at the site: a real
  person, recent lessons, a contact form or address.
- **How to contact:** their contact form or the general address on their site. Not their private
  number, not a social-media DM.
- **Angle:** students practising alone between lessons. Tuner, metronome with the speed trainer,
  chord charts and scales for the student's own tuning, ear training; free for every student (no
  parent pays), no account (nothing to set up for a class), no ads, nothing leaves the phone.

### 3. Android sites and blogs
- **Who fits:** sites with an "app of the week", "new apps" or roundup column; sites and blogs about
  privacy-friendly or open-source apps; sites that cover Wear OS apps. Big news sites rarely write
  about a small app, so send one tip each and expect nothing; smaller blogs and columns are likelier.
- **Examples that take tips** (checked 2026-11-16 on their own pages; use the site's own route, not
  a writer's personal address):
  - *Android Police*: the contact page, under *Editorial Inquiries* (topic ideas and suggestions).
  - *Android Authority*: the contact page lists a news address for tips, and a general form.
  - *9to5Google*: *Submit a Tip / Contact Us* on its contact page.
  - *Android Planet* (Dutch): the contact page sends news tips to its *Tip de redactie* form.
  - *Androidworld* (Dutch): its contact page names an editorial address for tips (seen through a
    search; check the page before sending).
- **How to check:** the site's contact or "tips" page; a column's recent posts (do they include
  small apps, and from developers?). Skip sites that only write about paid placements ("sponsored",
  "submit your app for a fee"): Twangle never pays for a mention.
- **Angle:** one per site, whichever fits its readers: privacy, the watch app or accessibility
  (below). For blind and low-vision readers there are separate notes:
  [accessibility-communities.md](accessibility-communities.md).

## The three angles
Lead with one, mention another in a line at most.
- **Privacy:** no internet permission at all, so the microphone's sound can't leave the phone; it is
  analysed live and never recorded. No ads, no account, no analytics or tracking. The code is public
  under GPL 3 (GitHub `Klaboukes/Twangle`), and the Play page's permission list shows it.
- **The watch app:** tuning by feel on the wrist: the vibration guide taps once to tighten, twice to
  loosen, quicker as the note gets close, and one long buzz in tune, so the player's eyes stay on the
  guitar; swipe left for a metronome that taps the beat on the wrist, set with the bezel or crown.
  Then: it runs on the watch alone with the watch's own microphone, with a tile and a watch-face
  shortcut. Several other Wear OS tuners also listen on the watch alone (communities.md, *Standing
  out*), so that part isn't the news. Wear OS 3 or newer, tested on a Galaxy Watch 5. On Play since
  2026-11-27 (search Twangle in the watch's own Play Store).
- **Accessibility:** TalkBack says the reading in words ("Tighten, E 2, 12 cents flat") without
  talking over the string; the vibration guide lets you tune by feel alone; a high-contrast look; every
  screen works with text twice the normal size; 36 languages.

## After sending
- **One follow-up at most,** and only to a person (a channel or a teacher), never to a site's tip
  form: about two weeks later, two lines ("Just checking this reached you; no worries if it's not
  for you."). After that, let it go.
- **A "no" or silence is fine.** Don't argue, don't write again, and don't write to someone else at
  the same channel or site.
- **A yes or a question:** answer kindly and plainly. If they ask for something you don't know
  (figures, a feature's details, a larger screenshot), bring it to Claude. Full-size screenshots
  are in the store-listing folder on your PC (`fastlane/`, docs/store-listing.md).
- **If they mention Twangle,** a thank-you is enough. Never ask them to change what they wrote,
  except a plain factual error.
- **What they tell you** (a bug, a wish) goes to Claude for the roadmap or [IDEAS.md](../../IDEAS.md),
  as in [communities.md](communities.md), *How to answer questions*.
