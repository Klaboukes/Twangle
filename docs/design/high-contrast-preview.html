What low-vision players say

  • High contrast is not dark mode. Dark modes are greys for dim rooms; high contrast is pure black and a few strong colours (Perkins School for the Blind). Apple warns that grey-on-black "may be more difficult to read for those with low vision" (App Store Connect). Ink dark is a dark mode: its scale's arc is 1.35:1 on its background, its light twin's 1.18:1 (table below).
  • No one scheme suits all. One player with retinitis pigmentosa found light backgrounds intolerable and inverted every app without a dark theme (AppleVis, May 2026); another reads best as black on mustard or yellow (AppleVis, 2025); a dark screen is hard to read in bright light (halation, Perkins). Yellow on black is the old favourite of magnifiers and large-print keyboards (HI VIZ on AppleVis).
  • They turn it on for the whole phone. Apple: most users who need higher contrast switch on Increase Contrast and Bold Text phone-wide (App Store Connect, above). Samsung's high-contrast theme had passed 15 million downloads by One UI 2 (Samsung). An app that follows the phone is found; one that hides it in its settings is not.
  • What tuners for them advertise: "high contrast, high visibility" (APTuner), a high-contrast display (Airyware), a high-contrast dark mode (AccessMusic). A clip-on buyer with poor eyesight praised big characters, colour contrast and clear coloured bands. Big, bold, few, and nothing faint.
  • Not to rely on colour: colour inversion turns green to pink and amber to blue, and many low-vision players see colour poorly (Perkins). So in tune is a mark plus the word, and the direction is the needle's side.
  • Reddit couldn't be read this time (the built-in browser refuses reddit.com now); Phase 89's post asks low-vision players directly after launch.

What phones offer by themselves

  • Android 14 and up: a contrast level (on Pixels Accessibility › Colour and motion › Colour contrast: Standard, Medium, High). Apps can read it, UiModeManager.getContrast(), 0 standard to 1 high, with a listener for changes (Android). Material's own colours follow it; Twangle's Ink doesn't, as its colours are its own. To check on the S26.
  • High-contrast text (every Android since 5; "Maximise text contrast" on newer Pixels): the phone outlines the text itself, in every app. The Xiaomi 12X (Android 13, HyperOS 1.0, read by adb today) has this and colour inversion, but no contrast level: on it, high-contrast text is the only sign an app can read.
  • Samsung: Visibility enhancements holds a high-contrast theme (from Galaxy Themes), high-contrast fonts (outlined), a high-contrast keyboard, colour inversion and colour adjustment (Samsung). The theme recolours Samsung's own apps; an app can't ask whether it is on.
  • Colour inversion and bold text work on any app by themselves. The high-contrast look inverted is still high contrast (white on black becomes black on white).

How it is chosen

a · A fourth choice in Light or dark

One list, no new row. But high contrast then has one face only: the player who needs white on black and the one who needs black on white (or dark at night, light in daylight) can't both have it.

b · A row of its own in Look (Claude's pick)

A switch beside Light or dark: high contrast follows light or dark, so white on black in dark and black on white in light, the phone's or the player's choice. Players differ (above), and the same player differs between a dark stage and daylight. lifecycle.md already puts it in Look.

Comes on by itself?

Claude's pick: yes, as the vibration guide does with TalkBack. While the player has never flipped the switch, it is on when the phone's contrast is High (Android 14+) or its high-contrast text is on, and off otherwise; the first flip saves their choice. Medium stays Ink (Ink already reads at 4.5:1, its secondary text 7:1). Why: the players who need it set the phone, not every app (Apple, Samsung's 15 million), and on the Xiaomi's Android 13 high-contrast text is the only sign there is. Samsung's theme can't be read; its players find the switch in Look.
