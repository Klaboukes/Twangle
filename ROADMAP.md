Done by the owner by hand, with a real guitar or headset. They are not roadmap increments: `/roadmap-step` skips them.

- ☐ **When you have the gear** (postponed 2026-10-16, no hurry):
  - Phase 5, a Bluetooth headset: with one connected, the tuner still listens through the phone's mic and reads a string correctly
  - Wired earbuds with a mic (3.5 mm plug, or USB-C), for a noisy room (2026-10-31): with them plugged in, Android listens through the earbud mic, which Twangle allows (only a Bluetooth headset is overruled), so the mic can lie close to the guitar. Lay the mic on the guitar's top a few centimetres from the soundhole (not in it), hands off the cable, and tune all six strings, first in a quiet room, then with loud music from the laptop: the note and cents follow each string, the low E included, at least as quickly and steadily as with the phone's own mic, and better in the noise; the reference tone plays in the earbuds; pulling the plug goes back to the phone's mic without a restart. If it passes, one line on the website's help page in English and Dutch ("Noisy room? Plug in wired earbuds and lay the mic on the guitar"), no hint in the app; if the low strings fail, the line names guitar and ukulele only
  - Phase 15, a bass (or a 7-string guitar or cello): the lowest strings read correctly (B0, E1, B1, C2), and the tuner shows no note while the amp only hums
- ☐ **Phases 63 and 65 on the tablet and the watch** (2026-11-01, no rush; both were checked only in forced window sizes, on the phone and on the Wear OS emulator):
  - On the Lenovo tablet, upright and on its side, and on the Galaxy Watch: the buttons, pickers and switches look as on the phone (Phase 63)
  - On the Lenovo tablet, upright and on its side, in a light and a dark style (Phase 65, *Contrast and edges*): the string buttons and the round buttons stand out clearly from the background, the style's background runs under the navigation buttons (no white or black strip), and Settings' rows fade out under the title as you scroll
  - The upright tablet store screenshots (Phase 65, *The store pictures*): with the tablet connected, Claude runs `scripts/store-screenshots.sh` for English and Dutch; the pictures show one scaled-up column with no empty middle, every word readable
  - On the Galaxy Watch, TalkBack on (Phase 65, *TalkBack on the watch*): the dial is spoken in words, e.g. "A 2, 12 cents flat, Tighten" (2.0: the word on screen; 1.1 says "tune up") or "E 2, in tune", calmly (not on every small change), and "Another app is using the microphone." is spoken when one has it (a voice recorder open on the watch)
- ☐ **TalkBack's own voice** (the launch review, 2026-11-01; before launch if you can, ~5 min): TalkBack on (volume keys held 3 s, or *Settings → Accessibility*), no headphones, phone on the table, the tuner open with the speaker loud. ## 2.0 — the app designed again
Decided on 2026-11-02, from the from-scratch design of 2026-11-01 ([IDEAS.md](IDEAS.md), *A fresh start*, with the first mock-up [docs/design/fresh-start-preview.html](docs/design/fresh-start-preview.html): the navigation, the metronome, the Practice cards, Settings, the watch) and the mock-ups of 2026-11-02: [docs/design/v2-tuner-screens-preview.html](docs/design/v2-tuner-screens-preview.html) (the tuner: six single designs, then the chosen combination with each of the three looks in the lens, and focus mode on its side) and [docs/design/v2-one-look-preview.html](docs/design/v2-one-look-preview.html) (four candidate looks, Ink chosen, and the picker sheet). Each page's top comment lists its decisions. **Listed first because it is what sessions work on now,** on the `v2` branch (CLAUDE.md, *The v2 branch*); Phases 53, 56 and 58 below are the owner's launch and after-launch work on `main`.

**The design, settled (owner, 2026-11-02):**
- **One look, Ink:** blue-grey dark, near-white light, a plain sans, rounded corners; light, dark or the phone's choice (the switch stays in Settings); the phone's wallpaper colours as the one personalisation (on by default, Android 12 and up; amber and green stay the app's own). The six styles, the meter looks and the wave looks go; a saved one reads as the one look.
- **The tuner screen:** the picker chip "Guitar · Standard ▾" at the top between the gear (top left) and the focus mode button (top right); the ruler (the chromatic scale sliding under a fixed pointer: which note); the lens below it (how far: left flat, right sharp, the tuner's own in-tune zone, ±5 cents or ±2 with Precise, green in tune, quiet when the string dies); the whole screen softly tinted amber far off and green in tune (how close); one word at the bottom, Tighten · Loosen · In tune, with the cents small under it; the pegs as a plain row, the one heard ringed, tap to lock, hold to hear; the signal bars, behind their switch in Settings as today, beside the ruler's heard note. No mode switch, no big note (the ruler is the note), no cents pill, no hertz, no wave view, no strings backdrop.
- **Three looks in the lens, the one choice a player gets (Settings):** the pitch trail (the default: the newest reading at the top, the last seconds falling under it), the Light arc (as today: 17 rays of 5 lights on the stretched scale), the strobe (three bands drifting flat or sharp, still in tune). The lens is the contract; only what is drawn inside it changes. Stop at three. The pitch trail tool goes as a tool of its own: the trail is the meter, and its switch stays as the trail look's brake.
- **The string tick-off** (a tuned string's peg gets a check, "In tune. Play." once all have one) is a beginner helper: present, behind a toggle in Settings, off by default.
- **Focus mode:** one button, no switch in Settings; the same screen laid out wide on its side (the ruler across the full width, the lens wide, the word and the string small under it, the exit button in the same top-right corner), the pegs and tabs back with a tap; the turning of the whole app, Back and the tap stay as they are.
- **The one picker:** the chip opens "What are you tuning?": instrument pills, that instrument's tunings with their strings, Edit tunings, Any note as a switch. Instrument, tuning and chromatic in one place.
- **Navigation:** three tabs, Tune · Metronome · Practice; Settings and About behind the gear; Practice as four big cards (Chords, Scales, Capo, Ear training) and a "last time" row; no tour.
- **The 12th-fret check** is a tuner feature, not a Practice tool: it stays on the tuner, reached from the tuner screen behind a switch in Settings (its way in still to be mocked up, Phase 71).
- **Strum check and chord name happen by themselves** (the tuner hears one string or six), no mode to switch on.
- **Under the hood nothing changes:** `:core:dsp`, `:core:audio`, `:core:settings`' storage, the tunings, the reference tones, the drone, the metronome clock, the charts, Ear training's questions, no internet, no account, no ads. The watch and the widget follow the one look.

## Phase 58 — Open the code after v1.0
Goal: the Twangle repository goes public as open source, once v1.0 is live on Play. The licence is the **GPL version 3** (owner, 2026-10-17): anyone may copy, change and share the code, but what they build from it stays open under the same licence, so nobody can turn Twangle into a closed app with ads. The bundled fonts keep their own SIL Open Font License, and the AndroidX and Kotlin libraries' Apache 2.0 fits inside GPL 3.
- ☐ `LICENSE` with the full GPL 3 text, and a *Licence* section in README.md naming it
- ☐ README.md says the name "Twangle" and its icon are not covered by the licence, so copies must use their own name and icon
- ☐ Before opening: check the history for anything that shouldn't be public (the owner's personal email in old commits, `local.properties`, keys); the owner decides whether to keep the history or start the public repository fresh. The launch review (2026-11-01) found 294 commits under the owner's Gmail address (244 under the no-reply one) and no key, keystore, `local.properties` or token anywhere in the history; CLAUDE.md and the docs also name the test devices' adb serials, the watch's network name and the owner's phone habits: the owner decides whether those stay
- ☐ The CI workflow's actions pinned to commit SHAs instead of tags (`actions/checkout@v7` and so on), so a moved tag can't change what runs once outsiders can open pull requests
- ☐ The owner makes the repository public; later commits use the GitHub no-reply email
- ☐ While the owner is the only author, the licence can still change; once others contribute, it can't without them, so settle any change before accepting outside code
- ☐ Google's rating window (Phase 50) comes from a library under Google's own terms, not open source: F-Droid and IzzyOnDroid flag such a library, and GPL 3 alone doesn't allow it in a copy someone else builds. Settle it before opening: a build without it for F-Droid (`RatingWindow.NONE`, e.g. a `foss` flavour), and either a short GPL exception for that one library in `LICENSE`, or the Play build only made by the owner
