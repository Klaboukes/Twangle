Twangle, if we started over

Same engine, same tools: YIN, cents, tunings, reference tones, the metronome, the charts. A new face, designed around one moment: a player with both hands on the guitar, glancing at a phone an arm's length away. The big phone is live. Tap a peg on the phone to hear that string and lock to it. "Hear my guitar" uses this PC's microphone and a small pitch detector in the page, so the needle, the word and the colour behave as they would in the app.

  1. The tuner is the app.It opens listening. No mode to pick, nothing to tap. The two other tabs are one tap away and always bring you back here.
  2. Three answers in one glance.Which string (the lit string and its peg), what note (the biggest thing on the screen), which way to turn (a word, a needle and a colour). Readable at arm's length in a dark room, while you look at the headstock.
  3. Words a hand understands."Tighten" and "Loosen" are what you do with the peg; ♭ and ♯ still need translating. The needle shows how much, and "a little" warns before you overshoot. In "Any note" mode, where there is no peg, the words become "Too low" and "Too high".
  4. Colour means done.Blue while you are far off, amber when close, green when in tune, over the whole screen, so your eye reads it without focusing. No red anywhere: an out-of-tune string is not an error.
  5. Progress you can see.A peg gets its tick when its string holds in tune for a moment. When all six are ticked the screen says so. You always know what is left and when you are finished.
  6. Hear it.Tap a peg to hear that string and to lock the tuner to it, for a loud room, for tuning by ear, or when the app keeps guessing the neighbouring string.
  7. Strum once to check all six.No separate mode. Strum the open strings and every peg shows ↑, ↓ or ✓. Strum a fretted chord and its name appears where the string name is.
  8. A trail under the needle.The last two seconds of pitch, fading away. A wobble reads as a band instead of a jumping needle, so you trust what you see and stop chasing it.
  9. One question for setup: what are you tuning?Guitar standard, drop D, bass, ukulele, or any note. Instrument, tuning and chromatic in one picker, under the chip at the top.
  10. The metronome gets its own tab.It is the second most used tool, so it is one tap, not two. Tune · Metronome · Practice. Settings sit behind the gear, because you set them once.
  11. Practice is four big cards.Chords, Scales (the drone lives there), Capo and Ear training, each named for what it does, with a picture that shows it. The intonation check is a row below, next to what you used last.
  12. One look, light and dark.Dark by default (stages, bedrooms, OLED), light when the phone is. One accent colour. All the polish goes into the one screen players see every day.
  13. Nothing to learn.When it hears nothing it says "Listening…" and the pegs tell you what to do. There is no tour: a hint appears once, in place, and then never again.

What I would leave out, starting fresh

Visual styles and meter looks

One look, done very well, that follows the phone's light or dark setting. Every hour spent on a sixth style is an hour not spent on the one screen everybody sees. Fewer choices also mean one set of screenshots, one set of tests and one thing to get right in 27 languages.

Modes for things that can just happen

Strum check and chord naming are not tools you switch on. The tuner hears one string or six and shows what fits. The same for the pitch trail: it is part of the meter, not a screen of its own.

A tour

If the first screen needs explaining, the screen is wrong. "Listening…", the lit string and the pegs that say "tap to hear" carry a first-time player through without a single overlay.

A Settings tab

Settings are set once. A gear in the corner of every screen is enough, and it frees the bottom bar for the two things people actually switch between while playing.

What stays exactly as it is

Everything under the hood

  • YIN pitch detection, smoothing, the cents maths
  • Tunings, A4, note names in 27 languages
  • Reference tones, the drone, the metronome clock
  • Chord, scale and capo charts, Ear training's questions
  • No internet, no account, no ads, nothing stored

Why it would still feel like Twangle

Calm, a single accent colour, green only when it is earned. The app does one thing extremely well and the rest without fuss.
