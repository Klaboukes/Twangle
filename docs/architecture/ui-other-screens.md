## Metronome screen
`:feature:metronome`, after the approved mock-up [docs/design/metronome-nav-preview.html](../design/metronome-nav-preview.html) (its top comment is the spec). **Owner decided (2026-10-14):** keep the round tempo dial; everything else as shown; bar variant B (Frameless) for the navigation bar ([UI basics](ui.md), *Navigation bar*), which it shares the window with (it opens from the Metronome tab; switching tabs stops it).
- **Roomier layout (2026-10-18):** the owner found the screen too crowded and picked option A of the rough mock-up [docs/design/metronome-roomy-preview.html](../design/metronome-roomy-preview.html): the dial as large as the screen allows (upright it fills the width, 361 dp on the Xiaomi against 288 before, and the spare height goes to the gaps round it; on its side it fills its half), everything about the tempo inside it (− and + either side of the figures, a round Start / Stop in the opening at the bottom), and Tap tempo, Listen and "Vibrate on the beat" as one row of three icon buttons under it (72 × 52 dp each, 16 dp apart), each named for TalkBack and in a tooltip on a long press. The beat lights are 48 dp tall upright (64 before).
- **Listen:** after the approved mock-up [docs/design/metronome-listen-preview.html](../design/metronome-listen-preview.html) (owner decided 2026-10-15; its top comment is the spec). A tap on Listen asks for the microphone if needed ([Lifecycle](lifecycle.md)), then `MetronomeViewModel.listen` pauses the clicks (the microphone would hear them) and feeds `OnsetTempoDetector` ([Strum check and metronome](strum-and-metronome.md), *Tempo from music*) on `Dispatchers.Default` (`hear`, `withTimeoutOrNull(15 s)` round the recorder's chunks). Listening ends by itself once the detector is `STEADY`, after 15 s (a tempo if `SETTLING`, else "no beat"), or on Stop, another control (the player took over: − + Tap tempo, the meter, the dial's ring, Vibrate), leaving the screen. While it listens: Listen shows a stop square in the accent (TalkBack "Stop listening"), its outline dims and a 1.25 dp trace in the accent at 60 % runs clockwise from its top centre over the 15 s (`ListenTrace`, on the outline's own path, its last 8° fading; it fades out over 300 ms where it is when listening ends); Start / Stop rests at 38 % and takes no taps; the status line under the beat lights says "Listening…", then "Listening… 96 BPM" once the detector has a tempo; a small hollow ring on the dial's arc marks that tempo (fading in 400 ms, gliding 500 ms); the beat lights follow the heard beats (the detector's beat grid moved to the `System.nanoTime()` clock by the earliest chunk arrival, `HeardBeats`) at a softer level (0.25 + 0.45). **Found:** a balloon over the screen pointing at Listen (`ListenBalloon`, the tour's balloon: accent fill, soft lift, Futuristic glow and cut corners, at most 260 dp; beside Listen when a phone on its side has no room above or below), with the style's mark and "Heard", the tempo in the number face with BPM and its marking, ½× 1× 2× (only those within 30–250), Not now and Use. Nothing changes until Use: then the dial's arc, needle and number glide there in 700 ms with one soft glow behind the number, and the clicks come back after the glide only if they played before Listen. Not now, Back or another control closes it and brings the clicks back if they played. **No beat:** "No steady beat heard  Try again" for 8 s; the tempo stays. The status line keeps its 24 dp (one line at larger fonts) so nothing moves, crossfades its changes (160 ms out, 160 ms in; a new tempo while listening changes in place, `contentKey`) and is a polite live region that holds its text (merged, the words fading out left unheard), so TalkBack says each change; while listening it says paced words instead (`pacedSpeech`, a fresh pace per listening: "Listening…" at once, the first tempo after 400 ms, a tempo still settling at most every 2 s), with the shown words hidden but still there for the language checks; its action is at least 24 dp tall, as tall as the line, with Compose's 48 dp touch area. Words and action keep their size while they fit side by side; where they don't (large text, long languages) the action takes at most 45 % of the line and each shrinks to stay on one line (the words down to 6 sp, which Dutch at 2× text on a 360 dp phone needs).
- **Layout, upright** (one column, at most 480 dp, scrolls with very large fonts): the meter picker (2/4 3/4 4/4 5/4 6/8 7/8, where the tuner's mode switch is; one radio group "Beats in a bar"; 50 dp buttons so six fit a 360 dp phone; where they don't (a phone on its side beside navigation buttons, as the S26: about 300 dp) each touch area is its share of the row and 48 dp tall, as the chord buttons, its label set smaller where it must; otherwise wrapping at very large fonts), the beat lights (48 dp), Listen's status line (24 dp), the tempo dial with − and + and Start / Stop inside it, and the row of Tap tempo, Listen and Vibrate. The dial takes all the height the rest leave (`FitColumn`'s shrinking part), up to the width less the 16 dp margins (at most 440 dp), down to 200 dp (below that, at very large text, the column scrolls); on a tall phone the spare height is shared by the gaps above the dial, between it and the row and under the row. **On its side** (≥ 600 dp wide and wider than tall): the dial filling its half (up to 440 dp) at the start, the rest in a column beside it (16 dp apart, as the other Practice tools), centred; on a short window the beat lights get lower (56 down to 40 dp, `FitColumn`) so it fits without scrolling (the S26 with navigation buttons, 2026-10-18: 650 × 325 dp beside the rail); only very large text scrolls. The quiet background, as Settings. Before 2026-10-18 the dial was 288 dp with 52 dp − and + in its bottom corners, and Tap tempo and Listen, a 196 dp Start / Stop and a "Vibrate on the beat" row with a switch stood under it ([history](history.md)).
- **Press effect** (`Modifier.pressFeedback` in `:core:designsystem`, `PressFeedback.kt`): every metronome button lights up instead of showing Material's ripple (`indication = null` on its `clickable`, `selectable` or `toggleable`, the same `MutableInteractionSource` given to the modifier). The modifier draws the button's face itself (fill, the light, outline, then the content), so the light lies above the background and under the text, and scales the whole drawing for the sink (never the layout). Level: 1 from the frame of touch-down, held while pressed, then (1 − t/280 ms)³; sink 1 − sink × s (s smoothed over 35 ms; 0.03, Ancient and Retro 0.02, Classic and the vibrate row 0.015). The light per style: Contemporary and Space Age the accent at 0.20; Ancient a radial smudge of the ink mixed 45 % into the accent at 0.24; Classic a shadow from the top over the accent at 12 %; Retro a 9 dp shadow from the top (the pressed-in push button) over the accent at 10 %; Futuristic the accent at 14 % with the outline lit and an 8 dp accent glow (Android 9+). `PressTone` picks flat colours on accent fills (Start / Stop: on-accent at 0.20; in the balloon on-accent, on its filled buttons the accent). Text buttons and the vibrate row light a 32 dp pill (`lightShape`, `lightInset`). Tap tempo uses `crisp = true`: full for 70 ms from touch-down whatever the finger does, a 40 ms linear fade, no sink. "Remove animations": no sink or fade, lit while held (at least 100 ms; crisp: 70 ms). TalkBack's double tap sends no touch, so each button calls `MutableInteractionSource.pressFlash()` in its click (ignored within a second of a real touch). `PressFeedbackTest` (JVM) checks the timing. The rest of the app has the same effect ([Meter, wave and styles](ui-meter-and-styles.md), *Visual styles*, **Press effect everywhere**).
- **Tempo dial** (`TempoDialArt` in `:core:designsystem` `metronome/`, gestures and TalkBack in `TempoDial`): frameless on the meter's calm field, 30–250 BPM clockwise over 300° (`dialDegrees`), the gap at the bottom; a tick every 10 BPM with the meter's scale rules (major ticks and numbers at 30, 50, 100, 150, 200, 250; numbers kept 3u clear of their tick's end whichever way it points), the style's arc finish unlit over the range and lit in the accent up to the tempo, the style's needle inside the arc pointing at it. The tempo sits in the middle in the style's number face (46 units, its middle 14 units above the dial's, fixed like the tuner's note; the mock-up draws it in the number face, which also spares Ancient's display face its diamond 0), "BPM" and the Italian marking under it (growing with the font up to 1.3×). − and + stand level with the figures, faces 32 units across (at least 32 dp, in a 48 dp touch area) with their middles 58 units out, so they reach 76 units out at most, inside the needle's path (from 78); 58 units was too large a figure for them to fit beside it. Start / Stop (54 units, at least 56 dp) stands 104 units below the middle, in the opening between the arc's ends (47 units either side, 81 down). Each takes its own touches before the ring. A touch on the ring sets the tempo it points to, a drag follows the finger (a touch in the gap keeps to the nearer end, `bpmAtTouch`), saved when the finger lifts. Only the ring reacts (`isOnDialRing`: from 74 units out, 20 inside the arc and just clear of the tempo's figures and marking at their widest, to the dial's edge at 144), so a tap on the tempo or "BPM" in the middle does nothing (the middle's angle is anything, so it would jump the tempo to 30 or 250). A drag that started on the ring follows the finger anywhere but the middle (`isClearOfDialMiddle`), past the dial's edge too. `TempoDialTest` (JVM) checks the angles, the gap, the ring and its scaling. TalkBack: one slider "Tempo, drag to change", "96 beats per minute, Andante" (Dutch "96 slagen per minuut", the usual Dutch for BPM), 30–250 in 1 BPM steps (`progressBarRangeInfo` + `setProgress`).
- **Beat lights** (`BeatLightsArt`): one mark per beat of the bar in the style's own wave mark (Ancient dab, Classic engraved band, Retro phosphor band, Contemporary rounded bar, Futuristic neon bar, Space Age star pair), beat 1 largest, group starts (`BeatAccent.GROUP`) middle sized, the plain beats smallest; beat numbers under them. The beat heard lights up in the accent (never in-tune green) with a soft glow and eases (200 ms) to a resting 40 % until the next. Hidden from TalkBack.
- **The beat** (`BeatClock`): `MetronomePlayer.beats` gives each click with the moment it is heard; a frame loop (`withInfiniteAnimationFrameNanos`, only while playing, skipped by UI tests) moves only the lights and one soft accent glow behind the tempo (220 ms decay, full on a downbeat, 75 % on a group start, 55 % otherwise), read in the draw phase, so playing never recomposes. Changes are heard from the next beat (≈ 0.12 s).
- **Controls**: see-through buttons in the style's shape (`shapes.small`, round − and + and Start in Contemporary and Space Age) with a muted outline; picked ones (Vibrate while on) in the container colour with an accent outline; Start in the accent, a play or stop icon only (TalkBack "Start", "Stop"), one size whatever it does; Futuristic's as neon outlines (Start / Stop's 14 % accent tint lies on the background colour, so the grid's lines don't show through it). The meter buttons are radio buttons heard by name ("Six-eight time", Dutch "Zesachtstemaat"), as TalkBack would read "3/4" as a date or a fraction. − / + step 1 BPM ("Slower", "Faster"); Tap tempo (`TapTempo`, [Strum check and metronome](strum-and-metronome.md), *Metronome*), a fingertip tapping a surface (`ic_tap`), counts the touch with its crisp flash, and TalkBack's double tap counts too. "Vibrate on the beat" (off by default) buzzes the motor directly (as `InTuneHaptic`): `EFFECT_HEAVY_CLICK` on the downbeat, `EFFECT_CLICK` otherwise (35 / 18 ms one-shots before Android 10).
- **State** (`MetronomeViewModel`): tempo, meter and vibration, each saved at once (a dial drag when the finger lifts); opens stopped at the saved values (passed in from the settings already read, so the first frame is right). It stops the clicks when cleared; the screen stops them when it leaves ([Lifecycle](lifecycle.md)), but not for a configuration change (the activity's ViewModel and the app's player live on, so the clicks play on and the new screen shows them).
- **Tests**: `MetronomeViewModelTest`, `MetronomeListenTest` (a fake microphone playing click tracks and silence to the real detector, in virtual time: clicks paused and the microphone held only while listening, the steady tempo suggested before 15 s and nothing changed until Use, half and double, Use bringing the clicks back only if they played, Not now, no beat after 15 s and its 8 s line, a failing microphone, Stop, another control, leaving, the estimate changing at most a few times), `TapTempoTest`, settings tests (JVM); `TempoDialTest`, `PressFeedbackTest` (JVM); `MetronomeScreenTest` (opens stopped, Start / Stop, ± and meter reach the player and are saved, − and + beside the tempo and Start in the dial's opening, the dial by touch on its ring and by TalkBack, a touch on its middle does nothing, labels, vibration switch, tap tempo, Listen holding the fake microphone only while listening with Start / Stop resting, another control or leaving ending it, the suggestion's choices and buttons, the status line, leaving stops it, Dutch at 2× on its side) and `MetronomeStylesTest` (every style light and dark: fits without scrolling on a 360 dp phone, the Xiaomi at 1.25× and on its side, nothing overlaps but − and + and Start inside the dial, − and + clear of the figures and Start of the marking; the suggestion wholly shown above Listen, beside it on its side; at 2× every control wholly shown).

## Practice tab
Phase 32, after the approved rough mock-up [docs/design/practice-preview.html](../design/practice-preview.html) (its top comment is the spec). **Owner picked (2026-10-16): A, D, G, I.** The Metronome tab becomes Practice (the bar: Tuner · Instrument · Practice · Settings, [UI basics](ui.md), *Navigation bar*), holding the Metronome, Chords and, with its own switch (`ToolSwitches.capoHelper`, Phase 33), the Capo helper, opening on the tool used last (saved); with its switch (Phase 28, `ToolSwitches.practiceTab`) off it stays the plain Metronome tab, with no switch, and nothing about Practice is shown or saved.
- **Where it lives:** `:feature:practice` (decided 2026-10-17) holds the tool switch and the tools that don't listen and need no audio, Chords and the Capo helper, so `:feature:metronome` stays about the metronome and its microphone. `:feature:*` modules never depend on each other, so `:app` (`MainActivity.PracticeTab`) puts them together: it picks the tool, saves it and hands each tool the switch as its `header`. `practiceToolsFor(tools)` gives the tools offered (none with the switch off; the Capo helper only with its own switch on too), `practiceToolToOpen` the one to open on (the saved one while offered, else the metronome).
- **Tool switch (A):** `PracticeToolSwitch`, the tuner's mode switch itself (`SegmentedSwitch` in `:core:designsystem`, which `ModeSwitch` uses too: selected segment in `primaryContainer`, labels shrinking to fit), Metronome · Chords · Capo. Every tool draws it at its very top, 8 dp down as the tuner's mode switch, centred upright; on its side (≥ 600 dp wide and wider than tall) at the top of the start half, which on the metronome is the dial's pane: the dial shrinks to leave room, so the controls' column keeps its height and nothing has to scroll. Upright the metronome's meter picker moves down under it. It has room for three tools only (with three, each segment keeps less padding, so Dutch "Metronoom" still fits a 320 dp phone at 2× text; no segment shows a check mark, the picked one's fill tells it, the owner's choice of 2026-10-18): a fourth (ear training, a drone, a practice timer) needs another look, the mock-up's text tabs (B) or one button with a list (C), and a new mock-up. TalkBack hears its segments as radio buttons by name ("Chords, selected").
- **Opens on the tool used last:** the pick is saved at once (`SettingsRepository.setPracticeTool`, key `practice_tool`, [Lifecycle](lifecycle.md), *Saved settings*); a fresh install opens on the metronome, the tab it used to be, so someone who mostly uses the metronome still reaches it in one tap. Each tool keeps its own saved UI state while the other shows (a `SaveableStateHolder`); leaving the metronome for chords stops its clicks, as leaving the tab does (clicks under the chord charts would need the metronome to play with no screen of its own: not now).
- **Chords (built 2026-10-17; calmer pickers 2026-10-18):** `ChordsScreen`. Upright one column (at most 480 dp): the switch, the instrument and tuning in the muted colour ("Guitar · DADGAD", a custom tuning by its own name; heard "Guitar, DADGAD"), the Chords · Scales choice, the two pickers in one row (root, and chord type or scale), the chart in all the height left, and the pager. There is no separate name line: the pickers say what is shown. `FillColumn` (a small custom layout) gives the chart what the rest leaves, at least 156 dp (1 dp a unit, its smallest); only below that (large text) the column scrolls. A screen under 610 dp tall (a narrow phone with navigation buttons) gets the gaps a quarter as wide and the pager no taller than its 48 dp buttons, so the chords fit a 360 × 720 dp phone with navigation buttons (570 dp for the screen). On its side (≥ 600 dp wide and wider than tall) the switch, tuning line, choice and pickers fill the start half and the chart and pager (or the neck) the end half.
  - **Picking** (`ChordPicker`), the owner's pick C in [docs/design/chords-calm-preview.html](../design/chords-calm-preview.html) (2026-10-18: "too cluttered with buttons"; C, as it leaves room for more chord types and scales later): `ChordPickers`, two buttons of one steady size side by side, each half the row (at most 180 dp, 8 dp apart), with the Capo helper's shapes button's face and ⌄ (see-through, `shapes.small`, 40 dp in a 48 dp touch row). The left shows the root ("C"), the right the chord in words ("C minor seventh"; its symbol alone would be "C" beside the root's "C") or the scale by its name alone ("Minor pentatonic": with its root too, Korean "A♭ 메이저 펜타토닉" needed three lines at 2× text); two lines rather than small, never breaking a word. A tap opens its grid in a Material dropdown right under the row, as wide as the row: the twelve roots six to a row, the seven chord types four and three ("C", "Cm", "C7", "Cmaj7", "Cm7", "Csus2", "Csus4"), or the five scales two to a row; a pick closes it, so do Back and a tap beside it. The dropdown asks its content's intrinsic size, so the grids are given the row's width instead of measuring their room (`BoxWithConstraints` there crashed). The grid buttons are the earlier see-through buttons, as the metronome's meter buttons (the picked one in `primaryContainer` with a 2 dp accent outline, Futuristic's neon outlines); each touch area is its share of the row (whole pixels, rounded down, so a row never wraps) and 48 dp tall, so six roots fit a 320 dp phone. The roots are written as the chord would be with the type picked (B♭, E♭, A♭ for major, but F♯, C♯m, G♯m; `ChordSpelling.rootOf`); a long name (Sol♯m7 at 2× text) is set smaller, down to 8 sp, and below that only where it would otherwise be cut off (La♭maj7 in Classic at 2× text on a 320 dp phone: 7.5 sp). A hyphen between letters may end a line (`breakableAtHyphens`, a zero-width space after it): Android breaks a compound such as Dutch "majeur-pentatonisch" at its hyphen only with hyphenation on. All follow the notation setting (letters, flats, solfège, German). TalkBack: each picker a list with what is picked ("Root, B flat", "Chord type, B flat minor seventh", "Scale, A minor pentatonic"; the scale's is a polite live region, so a new scale is said briefly), each grid a radio group ("Root", "Chord type", "Scale"), roots by their spoken names ("B flat"), types in words. The pickers' texts stay in the semantics tree (only described), so the tests' text checks see them. Both picks are saved at once (`practiceRoot`, `practiceChordQuality`, [Lifecycle](lifecycle.md), *Saved settings*) and the tool opens on them; a fresh install shows C major.
  - **Shapes** come from `ChordShapeFinder.shapesOf(chord, tuning)` (up to six, easiest first) on `Dispatchers.Default`: up to ~5 ms a chord on a PC for 6 and 8 strings, more on a phone, so never on the main thread; the last shapes stay shown meanwhile. The tuning is the one picked in the Instrument item (`AppSettings.tuning`, the player's own tunings included). A chord with no shape within reach shows "No shape within reach in this tuning" instead of the chart, the pager hidden but keeping its place.
  - **Chart (D)** (`ChordChart`, drawn in one `drawWithCache`): units of the mock-up (22 between strings, 26 a fret row, 4 rows, 34 at each side for "5fr", 26 above for o and ×, 26 below for the notes), as large as the room allows up to 2.4 dp a unit (`ChartGeometry.unitFor`); its lettering is set in units, not scaled again by the font setting (the chart grows instead). The nut 4.5 units thick in the ink colour when the shape starts at the 1st fret, else "5fr" beside the first row (`practice_chart_fret`: "5F" in Japanese, "5品" in Chinese, "5 лад" in Russian, the number alone in Arabic; set smaller to fit, right of the barre's end). o (an ink ring) over an open string, × (muted) over one not played; dots in the ink colour with the finger in the background colour, a barre as one rounded bar with its finger at its first string; the notes under the strings in the player's notation, the root bold in the accent. Strings and frets in the muted colour; a soft oval field of the background colour behind it, so the style's pattern never runs through the chart. Always drawn with the low string on the left, in right-to-left languages too. Finger numbers in the language's digits (`localDigits`).
  - **Several shapes (G):** ‹ "2 of 3" › under the chart: 48 dp round see-through buttons (chevrons, mirrored in right-to-left languages), resting at 38 % at the first and last shape (no wrapping round); a swipe across the chart of 48 dp steps too (to the left for the next, to the right in right-to-left languages). A new chord or tuning starts at its first shape.
  - **TalkBack:** the chart is one item in words, string by string from the thickest (`spokenChart`): "C major, shape 1 of 6, E 2 string, not played, A 2 string, fret 3, finger 3, C, D 3 string, fret 2, finger 2, E, G 3 string, open, …"; a barre once before the strings ("barre, fret 3, finger 1, strings A 2 to E 4"). Strings by their open note in the tuning's own spelling, notes as the chart writes them, both through `NoteNameSpeech`. It is a polite live region, so a new shape or chord is said when it shows. Chord words are the tuner's (`practice_chord_*` copies `tuner_chord_*`, as `:feature:*` modules don't share resources).
  - **Scales (built 2026-10-17)** are a second view of the same tool rather than a third segment of the Practice switch (which keeps its room for the Capo helper, Phase 33). A small Chords · Scales choice (`ChartChoice`, two picker buttons, at most 132 dp each; TalkBack a radio group "Chords or scales") sits under the tuning line, upright and on its side. The root picker is shared (one saved root), written as the scale writes its root (`Scale.nameOf`: C♯ minor pentatonic but D♭ major); the right picker holds the five scales by name (Major, Natural minor, Major pentatonic, Minor pentatonic, Blues). Their long names take two lines rather than getting small (3 dp clear of the outline above and below, so Classic's and Retro's letters don't touch it), never breaking a word (`WholeWordsAutoSize`, a `TextAutoSize` that also lets Chinese and Japanese break between characters). Then the neck in all the height left (`FillColumn`, at least 140 dp), with no pager. The choice and the scale are saved at once (`practiceChart`, `practiceScaleType`, [Lifecycle](lifecycle.md), *Saved settings*); a fresh install shows chords, and scales open on major.
  - **Neck (I)** (`ScaleNeck`, one `drawWithCache`): every note of `ScaleFinder.notesOf(scale, tuning)` on frets 0 to 12, in the mock-up's units (30 before the nut for the open strings' notes, 20.5 a fret, 19 between strings, room for the fret numbers 3 5 7 9 12 in the language's digits beside the thickest string; dots 8.5 units round with the name inside, set smaller to fit). The root's dots filled in the accent (`primary`, name in `onPrimary`), the others in the background colour with a muted outline and the name in the ink colour; strings and frets muted, the nut thick in the ink colour; a soft oval field of the background behind it, as the chord chart. It lies **upright** (nut at the top, thickest string on the left, as the chord charts) where that makes it at least 1.15× bigger than lying sideways (`NeckGeometry.lieFor`), up to 2.4 dp a unit: since the pickers became one row (2026-10-18) that is every phone held upright, the Xiaomi included, and a tablet; the owner confirmed upright on the phone (2026-10-18: "bigger and easier to read"). Otherwise (a phone on its side) it lies **sideways**, the thickest string at the bottom, as the owner picked in the mock-up. Lettering in units, not scaled again by the font setting; always drawn left to right, in right-to-left languages too. Instruments of 4 to 8 strings and the player's own tunings, by the tuning picked in the Instrument item.
  - **TalkBack (scales):** the neck is one item in words (`spokenScale`): "A minor pentatonic, notes A, C, D, E, G, E 2 string, open E, fret 3 G, fret 5 A, root, fret 8 C, …", string by string from the thickest, each from the nut up, strings named as in the chord charts (`spokenStringName`), notes as the scale writes them through `NoteNameSpeech`. Not a live region (it is long); the scale's name is a polite one instead, so a new pick is said briefly.
- **Capo helper (built 2026-10-17):** Phase 33, after the rough mock-up [docs/design/capo-helper-preview.html](../design/capo-helper-preview.html) (its top comment is the spec). **Owner picked (2026-10-17): B**, the capo stepper with a chord table (not A, the song's key with one big answer, nor C, every capo place for a key). `CapoScreen`, the Practice switch's third segment, behind its own switch (`ToolSwitches.capoHelper`, live in v1.0 since Phase 34). Maths: `:core:dsp` `capo/` ([Notes, tunings and audio](notes-and-audio.md), *Capo*).
  - **Layout:** upright one column (at most 480 dp): the switch, the instrument and tuning line (as Chords; the table doesn't depend on the tuning, but a row opens its shape on it), "Shapes I play" over a 132 dp button showing the shapes picked ("C ▾") that opens the list, the capo stepper ‹ "Capo 3" › (48 dp round see-through buttons as the chord pager, resting at no capo and at fret 11; "Capo" set smaller where a long word would push the buttons out, Russian "Каподастр" at 2× text), the table, then "Sounds in E♭" (`titleMedium`). A screen under 610 dp tall (a narrow phone with navigation buttons) gets the gaps half as wide and the stepper no taller than its buttons, so it fits a 360 × 720 dp phone with navigation buttons; the six 48 dp rows don't shrink. On its side (≥ 600 dp wide and wider than tall) the switch, tuning line, shapes, stepper and "Sounds in" fill the start half and the table the end half, so both fit a 330 dp-high window without scrolling at normal text; at large text the halves scroll.
  - **Shapes:** `CAPO_SHAPES`, the open keys from C up: C, D, E, G, A (`Capo.COMMON_MAJOR_SHAPES`), then Dm, Em, Am (`COMMON_MINOR_SHAPES`), in a Material dropdown list (`TwangleMenuItem`s, the picked one bold in the accent). A saved choice it doesn't offer opens on C (`capoShapesToOpen`).
  - **Table:** "Shape · Sounds" over the shape key's six usual chords (`capoChordsOf`: a major key's I ii iii IV V vi, a minor key's i III iv v VI VII; no diminished chord, rarely played open), each row "shape → chord it sounds": the shape muted, a small arrow (mirrored in right-to-left languages), the sounding chord in ink and semibold; every other row on a faint band (the ink at 6 %), no lines. 48 dp rows, at most 320 dp wide. Names in the player's notation, written as their key writes them (`CapoPlan.shapeSymbolOf`, `soundingSymbolOf`): C shapes at 3 read C → E♭, Dm → Fm, Em → Gm, F → A♭, G → B♭, Am → Cm; D shapes at 4 sound in F♯, whose vi is D♯m. The song's key under it is the shapes' key moved up by the fret (`capoPlanOf`).

## Home-screen widget
`:app` `widget/`. A tile for the home screen, three cells by one (resizable across, down to two): the navigation bar's tuning fork in the accent, the instrument and tuning as the tuner names them ("Guitar · Standard", a custom tuning by its own name, "Chromatic" in chromatic mode) in ink, and "Open the tuner" ("Open de stemmer") in the muted colour under it, on a rounded tile in the style's background colour (corners: the launcher's own on Android 12+, 16 dp before). No border, no art: calm beside other widgets. A tap anywhere opens Twangle on the tuner.
- **Tech: plain `RemoteViews`** (`layout/widget_tuning.xml`, an `AppWidgetProvider`), not Jetpack Glance. The widget is one static layout with three values to fill in; Glance would add a library, a second Compose runtime for widgets and a WorkManager-backed session for no gain here. Text sizes come from the layout (the title shrinks from 16 to 11 sp to fit, `autoSizeTextType`), the face is the system's medium sans: `RemoteViews` can't take the styles' bundled faces without a layout per style, and the colours already carry the style.
- **Colours** (`TuningWidget.paint`): the style's scheme as the app ends up with it (`colorSchemeFor` in `:core:designsystem`, wallpaper colours for Contemporary with Material You on, `usesWallpaperColors`): background, `onBackground` (ink), `onSurfaceVariant` (muted) and `primary` (accent), set as colour filters on the tile and the fork and as text colours. With the theme on "System" on Android 12+ both sets go in (`setColorInt` light/night), so the launcher follows the phone's dark mode without a redraw; otherwise the set for the current brightness. The picker's preview (Android 12+, `previewLayout`) is the layout itself in Contemporary light or dark (`values/widget.xml`).
- **Kept up to date by the app, never on a timer** (`updatePeriodMillis` 0): `TwangleApplication` follows the settings (`TuningWidget.follow`) for as long as its process lives and redraws the widgets when what they show changes (`WidgetState`: mode, instrument, tuning, custom name, style, theme, wallpaper colours; A4 or the meter's look change nothing), and when the configuration changes (language, dark mode before Android 12). `TuningWidgetReceiver` draws a widget when it is placed and redraws on the phone's `LOCALE_CHANGED`, reading the settings off the main thread (`goAsync`). With no widget placed, a redraw is one `getAppWidgetIds` call.
- **One tap opens the tuner** (`TuningWidget.openTuner`): an immutable `PendingIntent` to `MainActivity` with `ACTION_OPEN_TUNER` and `NEW_TASK | CLEAR_TOP | SINGLE_TOP`. A cold start opens on the tuner anyway; an open app gets `onNewIntent`, which counts a request, and `TwangleApp` switches to the tuner (an open tuning editor closes as with Back, dropping its draft).
- **Never the microphone:** the widget and its receiver touch only the settings. The mic opens only when the tuner it opens is shown, exactly as from the launcher ([Lifecycle](lifecycle.md)), and the tuner asks for the permission there.
- **TalkBack:** the tile is one button; the title reads as the tuner's button does ("Guitar, Standard"), then "Open the tuner".
- **Tests:** `WidgetStateTest` (JVM: names, custom tuning, chromatic, which changes redraw); `TuningWidgetTest` (device: the views applied as a launcher does show the names in the style's colours and are tappable; drawing them records nothing; the tap's intent brings the open app from Settings back to the tuner).

## Watch app (Wear OS)
`:wear`. A standalone Wear OS app (`com.google.android.wearable.standalone`): it listens with the watch's own microphone and needs nothing from the phone. Compose for Wear OS (`androidx.wear.compose` Material 3, `AppScaffold` with the time at the top), minSdk 30 (Wear OS 3).
- **Same application ID as the phone** (`io.github.klaboukes.twangle`), as Wear OS and Play expect for a phone app and its watch app; namespace `…twangle.wear`. **`versionCode` = the phone's + 1,000,000** (1,010,000 for 1.0.0), because Play needs a different code for each build; both come from `twangle.version` ([Threading and performance](performance.md), *Release build*). The release build is signed with the phone's upload key. The launcher icon is the phone's adaptive icon (copied into `wear/src/main/res`).
- **The pitch engine is the phone's**: `WatchPitchPipeline` runs `:core:dsp`'s `FrameAssembler` → `SignalGate` → `SustainCheck` (with its own noise floor for the room rule) → YIN (`AnalysisSettings.newPitchDetector`) → `PitchSmoother` → `InTuneDetector` (the phone's default window: within 5 cents for 300 ms, released past 7), with frame sizes from the recorder's rate, so a 16 kHz watch mic gets the same window and hop as 48 kHz. On a watch-class core it costs about 13–20 % of the CPU ([Threading and performance](performance.md), *Watch CPU and the native detector*). It leaves out the phone's extras (SNR, reception, tips, strum check, low-note window, precision setting): chromatic, A4 = 440 Hz, sharps. It allocates nothing per frame and sends a new `WatchTunerState` (`Listening`, `Detected(note, cents, inTune)`, `Failed`) only when the note, the whole cents or "in tune" change. A note lost for a moment stays on screen 0.6 s beyond the smoother's own hold (as long as the phone's glow bridges a dropout) and keeps "in tune" if the same note comes back, so the watch never flickers between a reading and "Play a note".
- **The microphone is `:core:audio`'s `AndroidAudioRecorder`**, unchanged: unprocessed source, then voice recognition, the watch's native rate, the built-in mic while a Bluetooth headset is connected.
- **Microphone lifetime** as on the phone: `WatchTunerViewModel.state` is `stateIn(WhileSubscribed(5_000))` and the screen collects it with `collectAsStateWithLifecycle()`, only once `RECORD_AUDIO` is granted. The permission is asked for when the app first opens; afterwards a button asks again, or opens the app's page in the watch's settings once Android stops showing its dialog. `rememberWatchMicPermission` follows the phone's `MicPermissionRules` ([Lifecycle](lifecycle.md)): a first dialog dismissed without an answer keeps "Allow microphone", and only a real "don't ask again" leads to settings. A refusal is remembered in the watch's own small `SharedPreferences` file (`twangle_wear`, `mic_refused`), as the phone keeps it in its settings. `WatchMicPermissionTest` on the JVM. A busy or broken mic shows a message with "Try again".
- **Screen off and ambient let go of the mic at once.** On Wear OS the activity stays *resumed* when the screen dims to ambient (always-on) or goes off (seen on the emulator: display `DOZE`, Twangle still the top resumed activity), so the lifecycle alone would keep the mic open. `ScreenOnEffect` listens for `ACTION_SCREEN_OFF` / `ACTION_SCREEN_ON` (sent when the display leaves or enters interactive mode, ambient included) and checks `PowerManager.isInteractive` on start; the view model then switches its upstream to a plain `Listening`, without the 5 s grace, and opens the mic again when the screen comes back. The app doesn't declare ambient support, so the watch shows the system's ambient view. Measured on the emulator: the mic is released within 0.9 s of `KEYCODE_SLEEP` (most of it adb) and reopened on wake; Home still keeps the 5 s grace.
- **Screen:** a ring along the edge (inset 6 % of the smaller side, so it sits the same on round and square screens), with the cents scale at its top: ±50 cents over ±60° either side of 12 o'clock, a tick every 10 cents and a longer one in ink at the target. A dot in the accent marks the reading and glides there (a low-stiffness spring), drifting back to the centre as it fades when the note stops. The note is large in the middle (30 % of the dial, light weight, the octave small beside it on the same baseline, a ♯ raised and smaller in the phone's cut of Noto Sans Symbols, bundled with its licence; the watch has no About page, so its licence texts, the OFL and Apache 2.0 for AndroidX and Kotlin, ship as assets, `WatchLicencesTest`), the cents under it ("+12 cents", "−3 cents"), and "Play a note" while nothing is heard; reading and "Play a note" cross-fade (400 ms). Sizes follow the screen, not the font setting, so the reading always fits inside the ring (checked from 170 to 225 dp). The tuner leaves out the time, where the ring runs; the permission and error messages keep it.
- **In tune:** the ring, the dot and the note ease to the in-tune colour over 700 ms (`FastOutSlowInEasing`) with a soft band of light around the ring (a radial gradient, 35 % on the ring, fading to nothing 5 % of the dial either side), hold while the note is in tune and fade the same way. No pulse, no sparks. TalkBack hears the note, the cents and "in tune".
- **Colours** are the phone's default style, Contemporary dark (`WatchColors`: ink, muted, track, accent, in tune), on black. They are copied rather than shared because `:core:designsystem` brings the phone's settings, fonts and Material 3; `WatchDialTest` fails if they drift from `StylePalettes.kt`. The watch has no style choice.
- **Tests:** `WatchPitchPipelineTest` (JVM, synthetic notes through the fake recorder: A4 in tune, cents within 1 of the played pitch, a 16 kHz mic, silence, few states for a held note, in tune only after 300 ms, never at 20 cents, a new note earns it again, a 400 ms dropout keeps reading and glow, back to listening after the note stops), `WatchTunerViewModelTest` (mic closed until collected, released 5 s after, released at once when the screen goes off and reopened when it comes on, error and retry, note names) and `WatchDialTest` (angles, colours match the phone's). On the Wear OS emulator (AVD `Wear_OS_Large_Round`, Wear OS 6, 454 px = 227 dp round): `WatchTunerTest` (6 Compose tests: "Play a note", the reading, TalkBack's "in tune", cross-fade both ways, "Try again", the reading inside the ring from 170 to 225 dp). By hand it installs, records through voice recognition at 48 kHz (the emulator has no unprocessed source), shows the ring with "Play a note", lets go of the mic on sleep and takes it back on wake. The PC's microphone did not reach the emulator (host mic switched on, two short 440 Hz beeps from the laptop: nothing heard), so a live note was only seen through `WatchTunerScreenshots`. A real watch is the owner's check (ROADMAP, *Owner checks*).
