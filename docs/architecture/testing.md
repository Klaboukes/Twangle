| `:core:dsp` | JVM tests with `TestSignals` (`testFixtures`, shared via `testImplementation(testFixtures(project(":core:dsp")))`): sines, saw/square, weak fundamentals, white noise, mains hum, band-limited audio at any rate, decaying plucks. Assert ±1 cent. `EdgeCaseTest` runs whole recordings through the app's chain (`TunerChain`) and checks what would be shown ([Signal pipeline](signal-pipeline.md), *Edge cases*). Recorded WAV fixtures go in `src/test/resources/`. Allocation-free paths have byte-counting tests. `StringSynthTest`: the reference tone in every instrument's voice: every preset string exactly on pitch by YIN at any A4 (the voice without its stiffness) and, with it, within 1 cent 3 s in and 2.5 cents at 1 s (steel strings' upper partials sit sharp on purpose, which YIN reads early in a low note); a pluck starts a few cents sharp and settles, dies away and ends on silence; a bowed note swells in, holds its level within 2 dB and rings out; never clips at any pitch; no click at start, end or release; the same samples in chunks or at once; no allocation; and the instrument in it: the pick's noise and the body's knock only at the start, the pluck point's hole in the spectrum, the body raising the partials on its resonances, the mandolin's pair beating where a single string doesn't, a steel string's 10th partial 5 cents sharp and nylon's not. `StringSynthReport` (with the benchmarks) prints what YIN reads from every open string of every instrument and writes each to a WAV file to listen to. `PluckSynthTest` checks `PluckSynth`, the ideal plucked string in the test fixtures that the strum, chord, 12th-fret and store-screenshot tests play. `ClickTrackTest` and `ClickSoundsTest`: every click on its exact sample, no drift over ten minutes, accents per meter, tempo clamped and changed while playing, clicks that start and end on silence and never clip, for every `ClickSound` at 16–96 kHz: each under a beat at 250 BPM, beat 1 higher and ≥ 4.5 dB louder at the peak, as loud as the wood block, and at 48 kHz every 16-bit sample of the approved samples in `docs/design/click-sounds-preview/`. `ClickCandidatesReport` (with the benchmarks) writes those samples from the app's own clicks, a bar of 4/4 at 100 BPM each and the same under two bars of strummed guitar (`StringSynth`), prints how the sounds differ, and checks the recipes' listening places. `DroneCandidatesReport` (with the benchmarks, Phase 48) writes the candidate drone sounds (`DroneCandidates`, in the test sources, round 2: a re-plucked tanpura, a harmonium, a flute organ, a warm pad) into `docs/design/drone-sounds-preview/`, the root alone, with its fifth and octave and under a guitar scale, all equally loud (K-weighted), and prints what YIN reads from each (failing past 1 cent on average), how much its level and brightness move and how much of it a phone's speaker plays. `DroneSynthTest` checks the app's drone (`DroneSynth`, [Notes, tunings and audio](notes-and-audio.md), *Drone*): every sound and voicing within 1 cent by YIN at any A4, lower or not; an octave down when lower; no peak above 0.8 at any root or seed; all equally loud within 1.5 dB; a fade in from silence and every release ending on exactly 0; no click (a 14 kHz high-pass) at a start, a crossfade of root, voicing, octave or sound, a quick run of changes or a release; ten minutes at the same level and pitch, the tanpura's cycles never alike; the same seed the same samples; no allocation. `DroneSynthReport` prints each sound's loudness, worst peak and reading and the gains for `DroneSynth.LEVELS`, and each sound against its candidate; `DroneSynthBenchmark` times a 10 ms chunk. `StrumDetectorTest`, `StrumListenerTest` and `StrumVerdictTest` on synthetic strums from `StrumChords` (`PluckSynth` strings, or stiff strings with any harmonic levels); `PartialFitTest` on plain sinusoids; `StrumAccuracyReport` prints the strum check's accuracy tables (with the benchmarks, not in CI). `ChordDetectorTest` and `ChordNamerTest` on synthetic strums of 41 open and barre shapes, 16 power chords and 12 ninths from `ChordShapes` (chord-chart frets in standard tuning, played through `StrumChords`) and on sounds that are no chord (single notes, thirds, octaves, muddles, noise); `ChordAccuracyReport` prints how often chords are named right, not at all or wrongly. `OnsetTempoDetectorTest` on synthetic music from `BeatSignals` (click tracks, kick/snare/hi-hat grooves with swing and loose timing, a plucked bass line); `TempoAccuracyReport` and `TempoDetectorBenchmark` run with the benchmarks. |
| `:core:audio` | JVM tests for source, rate and Bluetooth selection and chunk sizes; `ChunkReaderTest`, `MicOpenerTest` and `BlockingTransferTest` with a fake mic: silenced mid-session, 2 s of zeros (and a quiet room or a slow start that are not), stuck and failing reads and writes, the next rate when the first won't open. `FakeAudioRecorder` can also open at another rate (`openedConfig`), for the pipelines' tests. Instrumented tests record from the real mic (don't play test tones during them). `FakeAudioRecorder` (`testFixtures`) plays a `FloatArray` as chunks paced like a mic (instant under `runTest`), can loop or fail, and reports `isRecording` / `startCount`, so everything downstream, mic lifetime included, runs without a device. `FakeTonePlayer` (`testFixtures`) records `play`/`stop` and drives `isPlaying` (`finish()` = the tone died away). Instrumented `AndroidTonePlayerTest` plays short tones through the speaker: it ends by itself after the whole tone, `stop` ends it quickly, a new note replaces the old one, losing audio focus stops it. `FakeMetronomePlayer` follows tempo and meter and emits beats on request; instrumented `AndroidMetronomePlayerTest` plays clicks: beats heard 250 ms apart at 240 BPM with the right accents and told on time, tempo and meter change while playing, `stop` ends it quickly, another app taking audio focus doesn't stop it. `FakeDronePlayer` records each `play` (`DronePlay`: root, sound, voicing, lower) and `stop`, and `interrupt()` stops it as a call would; instrumented `AndroidDronePlayerTest` plays the drone about a second at a time: it never ends by itself, `stop` turns `isPlaying` false at once and lets the track go after the fade, a change of root, sound and voicing (the pad's dearest case included) crossfades with no underrun, `play` during the fade brings it back, losing audio focus stops it, a root outside 40–1000 Hz is refused. `CallModeTest` (JVM): only `MODE_NORMAL` lets it play. `MetronomeDriftMeasurement` (on demand, `scripts/metronome-drift.sh`) plays ten minutes and reports drift and jitter ([Strum check and metronome](strum-and-metronome.md), *Metronome*). |

| `:baselineprofile` | On demand, device needed ([Threading and performance](performance.md)). |
| Running UI tests | Always `scripts/ui-tests.sh` (the [ui-test skill](../../.claude/skills/ui-test/SKILL.md)), never Gradle's `connectedDebugAndroidTest`, which is 15–25 s slower per module and uninstalls the app. The script builds every test APK in one Gradle call, then installs and runs each module with `adb shell am instrument` under a time limit, prints one line per module and runs only the modules a branch touches. `-a key=value` passes an instrumentation argument. One run at a time holds the phone (a lock folder on it, which a live run touches every 10 s; a run killed outright, which skips its clean-up, is taken over a minute later: the next run puts back the rotation settings it saved in the lock and stops its tests and MIUI loop; every run stops its test processes at the end, since `am instrument` can leave one behind), its MIUI app-op loop ends with the run, and it wakes the screen before each module: a run beside another one, a screen that is off, or leftover loops (each about a third of a core) make the first test of a run time out, which in a `--thorough` run is always an Ancient one. `--cool` runs one test class at a time and waits while the phone is 37 °C or warmer; `--languages` adds the `@EveryLanguage` checks. A module that passes whole (no `-f`, `-c` or `-a`) from a clean working tree is noted as passed at that commit in `build/ui-tests-passed/` (this checkout only); `--since-pass` runs just the modules with no such note or with a change since it that they can see (the same mapping as the branch scoping), so a release after a good overnight run often runs nothing on the phone. `scripts/overnight-ui-tests.sh` is the overnight run (the [overnight-run skill](../../.claude/skills/overnight-run/SKILL.md)): every module in every style with `--cool`, then every language, one style at a time with cooling pauses, then the store screenshots in every language (`scripts/store-screenshots.sh`), and one summary. |
| Device | Manual checklist per release: real guitar, noisy room, Bluetooth headset, permission denial paths. |

## Fast UI tests
Compose UI tests use `createSharedActivityComposeRule()` (`:core:testing`): one activity for the whole run, a fresh composition (own recomposer, test clock and `ViewModelStore`) per test. `SharedActivityTestRunner` stops AndroidJUnitRunner closing every activity after each test, which took ~0.7 s per test on the Xiaomi 12X; together they cut the tuner suite from ~8½ to ~3 minutes. Pixel checks take one capture and cut it up (a capture costs a frame), and build assertion messages only on failure. A test that changes the activity itself (rotation, recreation, window flags) still uses `createComposeRule()` or `ActivityScenario`. How to write them: [writing-tests.md](../../.claude/skills/ui-test/writing-tests.md).

## Any phone
Every on-device UI test gives the same answer on any phone and fails only when Twangle itself is wrong (owner, 2026-10-20: users have all kinds of phones). The first overnight run on the Galaxy S26 (360 × 780 dp, three-button navigation, edge to edge forced by Android 15+) gave 99 failures in 21 tests that passed on the Xiaomi 12X (393 × 873 dp, gestures): the tests had quietly assumed the Xiaomi. The rules since:

- **A test that sets a window size goes through `PhoneScreen`** (`:core:testing`): `PhoneScreen(area, bars, fontScale, locale) { … }`. The window is `area` plus the `SystemBars` around it, forced with `DeviceConfigurationOverride.ForcedSize`. Where the phone's screen is smaller, everything is drawn at a lower density (a 393 dp window on a 360 dp phone at 2.74 instead of 3.0) but laid out in the window's own dp, so every size is checked on every phone and none is skipped. Bounds read through `getBoundsInRoot()` and the like use the node's own density, so they are in the window's dp too; px values (text layouts, captures) are the phone's own. A test without a size of its own gets a fixed one (`TunerContentTest.PHONE`, 360 × 780; `SettingsContentTest.PHONE`, 360 × 730), never the phone's screen.
- **Fake system bars, the same on every phone.** The shared activity is edge to edge, as the app and every phone from Android 15 on. `PhoneScreen` hands the content its bars as window insets in the window's dp (`DeviceConfigurationOverride.WindowInsets`), replacing the phone's own: a 24 dp status bar, a 24 dp gesture bar or 48 dp of navigation buttons (at the bottom upright, on the right on its side), the camera on the left on its side. `SystemBars.tab()` is what one tab's screen gets in the app (the app's bar or rail takes the bars on its side), `SystemBars.phone()` the whole screen (the navigation bar and tour tests); `inside()` turns a whole phone's size into the area. Fit tests alternate buttons and gestures (light and dark). Checks of "inside the window" use `safeArea()`, the part the bars leave clear. Screens that pad for the bars themselves (Metronome, Settings, Practice) get the tab's bars; `TunerContent` gets none, as `TunerScreen` pads for it.
- **One root more.** The fake bars come through a view of their own, so a `PhoneScreen` test has two compose roots: `onRoot()` is ambiguous, `onPhoneScreen()` is the window (captures, touches across it).
- **Menus escape the forced size.** A Material dropdown (the Chords and Scales panel) is a window of its own, laid out on the phone's real screen, so whether it fits under its button differs from phone to phone: in Dutch at 2× text the chord panel fits under the name on the Xiaomi 12X but not on the S26, and Material moves it up there. `assertMenuUnder` (`:core:testing`) checks a menu opened under its button wherever it fits (its height against the room down to the app window's bottom, less Material's 48 dp margin) and lets it move up only where it doesn't.
- **Menus follow their screen.** A `DropdownMenu` is a window of its own and takes the phone's density, not the forced one: on the S26 the Scales panel came one to a row at 393 dp. The app's menus keep their screen's density inside (`ScreenDensity`, `:core:designsystem`), which changes nothing in the app, where the two are the same. Dialogs and the tuning panel still take the phone's text size and language (checked on the phone).
- **Px rounding.** At a forced density text and boxes round to whole px differently: a 48 dp row exactly as tall as its 2× line is a px short at 1.44. `assertWhollyShown` allows one px each way (`isCutOffAtTheBottom`: a line lost, or the lines more than a px taller than the box; a line at most a px wider than its box). A line the line breaker could have broken (it holds a space) but judged to fit may be up to 2 % wider: Samsung's Hindi font draws such lines a little wider than their measure (4 px of 984, 8 of 444, drawn whole); a word too long for its box gets the px alone. Pixel checks measure in dp where a phone's density moves a feature across a px boundary (`MeterLooksTest`: a clip is a step along at least 3 dp of the box's edge; Classic's in-tune tick, 0.8 dp under its top, landed in the sampled row at 3.0 and not at 2.75).
- **The test's language is the text's language.** Compose lays text out (line breaks, Japanese line-breaking rules) by the process's default language, not the configuration's, so `PhoneScreen` sets the default to the language it shows and the shared rule puts the phone's back after each test. Without it the screen showed Japanese in 6 lines while the layout TalkBack reads (and the checks) broke it into 7, on a phone set to English.
- **UI Automator on the real app:** wait until a list has come to rest before tapping in it (a tap on a gliding list only stops it); scroll with swipes rather than the first scrollable it finds.

What the S26 found in the app itself: focus mode flickered (the layout chose compact or not by width, and hiding the rail and bars changed the width: 578 ↔ 663 dp with navigation buttons, ~40 switches a second; [Tuner screen and tour](ui-tuner.md)), the menus above, and the Practice name button: its height, two and a half lines of 12 sp, was turned into dp in one go (30 sp), and from Android 14 large text sizes grow less than small ones (nonlinear font scaling), so at 2× text the button was shorter than its two lines (Space Age's Dutch "A♭ majeur-pentatonisch" on a 320 dp phone; the Xiaomi, Android 13, scales linearly). One line's size is turned into dp first now. With realistic bars (72 dp less height on a 360 × 800 dp phone with navigation buttons) the tour's note balloon in Space Age, German and Indonesian at 2× text, found room on no side and lay over the note; it now widens to 8 dp from the screen's sides where that lets it fit ([Tuner screen and tour](ui-tuner.md), *Tour*).

## Every screen in every style
`TunerStylesTest`, `SettingsStylesTest` and `MetronomeStylesTest` check each screen in each of the six styles, light and dark, because a style brings its own fonts and button shapes: the tuner upright (a short narrow phone at 1×: 360 × 570 dp, what a 360 × 720 dp phone with navigation buttons leaves under its bars and the app's; the owner's Xiaomi 12X at 1.25×, less 80 dp for the navigation bar) and in its full layout on its side (less the 80 dp rail) fits without scrolling, nothing overlaps; at 2× text every part, Settings row, card name and About text can be scrolled to and is then wholly shown (`assertWhollyShown` in `:core:testing`: nothing clipped, no line cut off or ellipsized), and the string buttons fit the longest names (solfège flats); TalkBack hears the reading once, nothing in the meter or wave, the mode switch, strings, style/meter/wave cards and light-or-dark choices by name with which one is picked. `CompactLandscapeStylesTest` covers the compact layout, `MeterLooksTest` and `WaveViewTest` every look.

## Every language
`@EveryLanguage` tests (`:core:testing`) show the tuner (a note with the meter, listening with the wave), Settings, About, the metronome (with each of Listen's status lines, under the Practice tab's tool button), the Practice tab's Chords tool, the tour's balloons and the navigation bar (Metronome and Practice) in each of the languages in `twangle.languages` (`shippedLanguages`, from `BuildConfig` of `:core:testing`) at 2× text, upright on a 360 dp phone and on its side, and check every text TalkBack can find, scrolled to in turn, is wholly shown (`assertEveryTextWhollyShown`: no letter cut off, no line lost, no ellipsis); a text taller than its scrolling column must fill it. Texts that are only measured, never placed (a status line keeping the room of its longest wording, `TallestOf` and `Kept` in the tuner's tools), are not on screen and are skipped. The Practice checks also fail a word broken across two lines (`assertNoWordBroken`; Chinese and Japanese may break anywhere): Android breaks "majeur-pentatonisch" at its hyphen only with hyphenation on, so Practice puts a zero-width space after hyphens (`breakableAtHyphens`). A failure saves a picture of the screen to the run's extra output, which the script copies to the module's `build/outputs/connected_android_test_additional_output`. In every language they take about 20 minutes per style, so all 16 run only with `--all-languages`, in the overnight run (every style). By day `--languages` runs them in the testers' languages only, English and Dutch (`checkedLanguages`: the script passes `-e languages en,nl`, `DAY_LANGUAGES`), about a minute per module: Dutch has the long words, and a text too long in another language waits for the night (owner, 2026-10-26). Dialogs, menus and the tuning panel are windows of their own that take the phone's language and text size rather than a test's, so those are checked on the phone.

## Quick and thorough UI runs
A normal run is quick: it leaves out the double checks. Style test classes (`TunerStylesTest`, `SettingsStylesTest`, `MeterLooksTest`, `CompactLandscapeStylesTest`, `StringRowStylesTest`, `TourStylesTest`, two checks in `WaveViewTest`) run in the default style only (`allIfThorough(VisualStyle.entries, VisualStyle.DEFAULT)`), and `@Thorough` tests (variants of a quicker test in another font size, language, orientation or view) are skipped; `--thorough` runs everything. This cut the tuner suite from ~3 minutes to ~1. Each family keeps its hardest case in the quick run (e.g. Dutch at 2× font on a phone on its side), so most layout breaks still show at once. The thorough run belongs to the overnight run (`scripts/overnight-ui-tests.sh`, which passes `--thorough`), so daytime work never waits for it (owner, 2026-10-16: limited tests during normal work, the full suite and its fixes overnight). Thorough runs do find what quick runs miss: the first one (overnight 2026-10-15/16) found tests written while their runs were deferred, and the tour stuck on step 2.
