What makes a meter read precisely

  • More movement near the note. The middle of the scale is stretched: a cent near in tune moves the marker 2½ times as far, and the far ends (where only the direction matters) are squeezed.
  • Something fixed to compare against. A few quiet marks without numbers (5, 10 and 25 cents). Without them the eye can tell left from right, but not 3 cents from 8.
  • Thin edges, not soft blobs. The eye lines up two thin lines very precisely; it can’t find the middle of a glow or a bubble nearly as well.
  • Steps that mean something. Arrows light at 2, 5, 10, 20 and 40 cents, so one lit arrow means “almost there”.
  • The in-tune window shows. You see the ±5 cents (±2 in Precise) the glow waits for, so you know how close you are.
