What chord is this?

Strum a chord and the tuner names it. Three ways it could look on the tuner, on a phone the size of the Xiaomi 12X. Rough on purpose: flat colours of the default style; the real build takes each style's colours and faces. Pick a letter, and answer the wording questions at the end.

A · A tool in the ^ menu, the chord big recommended

Like the strum check and the 12th-fret check: picked from the ^ "more tools" menu above the strings, in guitar mode. The chord's symbol takes the meter's place, big and calm; its name in words sits under it. The line in the note's place says what to do. Tapping a string leaves it and tunes that string, as in the strum check.

Good

  • Same place and habits as the other two tools: nothing new to learn.
  • The calmest screen: one big name, nothing else moving.
  • The symbol is what song books print; the words help beginners and TalkBack.

Less good

  • Two taps to reach (^, then the tool).
  • Shows only the last chord, not a sequence.

B · As A, plus the chord's notes and the last chords

Same entry and place as A. Under the name: the chord's notes (A · C · E) with the bass note filled. Along the bottom of the panel, faint, the last three chords, so a chord change can be checked.

Good

  • Teaches what a chord is made of.
  • The slash chord explains itself: the filled note is the bass.
  • The history helps to work out a song's chords.

Less good

  • Busier: three kinds of thing in one panel.
  • The note circles look a bit like the string buttons below.
  • The history keeps changing while you play: more movement on screen.

C · Its own mode: Strings / Chromatic / Chords

A third part in the mode switch at the top. The whole tuner becomes the chord view: a very big name, no strings row (no tuning needed), in any instrument.

Good

  • Found at a glance, one tap.
  • The biggest name; also for players who use chromatic mode.

Less good

  • Goes against the "tools in the ^ menu" choice of 2026-10-16.
  • Three parts in the switch get tight ("Chromatisch", Russian, at 2× text).
  • Changes a v1.0 control: the third part must vanish in release builds until 1.1.

How the name settles (every option)

The detector names each reading (every 0.1 s) on its own, and a ringing chord gives a few odd readings. So the screen never shows one reading: a name appears once it wins 4 of the last 6 readings (about a second after the strum), stays at least 1.5 s, and is replaced only when another chord wins 4 of 6. Readings with no chord don't erase it: as the strum dies away it stays, faded. A strum that names nothing for 1.5 s says "Not a clear chord" (for at least 2 s), never a guess. The bass note joins the name ("C/E") only when it, too, agrees in that majority, and only when it isn't the root.

How the chord is written

Big: the symbol, in the player's note names (letters, German H/B or solfège, with ♯/♭ as the note-name setting says; German letters write Bm as Hm and B♭ as B). Small under it: the name in words in the app's language. TalkBack reads the words.

Questions for you

1. Big symbol, or big words?
"Am" big with "A minor" small under it (recommended: short, fits every language and style face), or "A minor" big on its own.
2. The tool's name in the menu
"Chord name" / "Akkoordnaam" (recommended), "What chord?" / "Welk akkoord?", or "Chord finder" / "Akkoordzoeker".
3. Dutch words for the chord types
"A mineur", "C majeur", "G septiem", "C groot septiem", "A mineur septiem" (recommended), or "A klein", "C groot"…? And is a plain major just "C" big with "C majeur" under it?
4. Slash chords
"C/E" with "C major, E in the bass" (Dutch "C majeur, E in de bas") as drawn, or leave the bass out and just say "C major"?
5. Sharps and flats
Follow the note-name setting (A♯ with sharps, B♭ with flats; recommended, like the rest of the screen), or always the usual chord spelling (B♭, E♭, F♯m) whatever the setting?
