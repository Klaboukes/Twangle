Chords by key

The circle of fifths as a key picker inside Chords. Pick a key, get the six chords that belong together in it, and play their charts. Tap any key on the circle (the outer ring is major, the inner ring minor). On the circle, those six chords are always the key and its two neighbours, in both rings.

Your pick

  1. Where it goes. Recommended: inside Chords, as a “Key” side of the chord picker, as drawn. The alternative is a fifth Practice card, “Keys”, which adds a card for something Chords already half does.
  2. What it shows. Recommended: the six chords only, with no Roman numerals (I, IV, V) and no music theory words, the key's own chord ringed. The seventh chord of a key (the diminished one, B° in G) is left out: beginners rarely play it and it would need a seventh button.
  3. Minor keys. Recommended: yes, from the inner ring. The same six chords, with the minor one as home (E minor shares G major's chords).
  4. On to Scales. Recommended: the row at the foot opens Scales on the key with the drone ready, so “play along in G” is one tap away.
  5. Hearing the chords. Not in this mock-up. Each chart could get a play button that strums it with Twangle's reference-tone sound; worth it only if the strum sounds good, so that is a later step.
