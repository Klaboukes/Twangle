## Notes, tunings and audio
- **Reference tone until 2026-10-24:** one ideal plucked string for every instrument (`PluckSynth`): harmonics at 1/k, each a plain exponential with time constant 1.5 s / (1 + (k − 1)/2), at most 32 below 6 kHz, a 5 ms attack, the harmonic amplitudes adding up to 0.8. No body, no pluck point, no attack noise, no stiffness, and a violin's string plucked like a guitar's: it sounded like a cheap keyboard (the owner, 2026-10-24), so `StringSynth` with a `StringVoice` per instrument took its place ([Notes, tunings and audio](notes-and-audio.md), *Reference tone*). `PluckSynth` moved to the test fixtures, where the strum, chord, 12th-fret and store-screenshot tests still play it, so their measured figures stand.

  - **How the watcher decided** (`StrumWatcher` until the owner's review of 2026-11-03, when the unused rules and their tests went; it cost two or three `ChordDetector` passes a pluck, 0.07 ms a hop on average on the Xiaomi 12X): **Deciding, first built for no button at all** (the Phase's first step). Until the guitar check the strum check and the chord name needed no mode: the watcher decided what each sudden sound was, by the rules below, and named chords too. On a real guitar that flickered between a string, a strum and a chord, so the app no longer asks for it (`everySoundIsAStrum` on, chords not named); `StrumWatcher` still has the rules and their tests. The tuner's own readings are the same with it or without (`HeardStrumFlowTest` compares them reading for reading), so a strum never changes how one string reads.
  **One sound, decided.** `StrumListener` notices the sound (its *Timing* above) and since Phase 74 keeps the frame that ended as it began (`readBeforeStrum`: what rang just before). Each of the sound's first frames goes through `ChordDetector`, which finds its notes whatever they are (`ChordResult.noteSalience`, new, keeps each note's salience in the frame's own units so two frames compare). A note is **a string of its own** when it is at least 15 % of the strongest (`ChordDetector.SURE`) and lies on no harmonic of another note found (`ChordDetector.isHarmonicInterval`: 12, 19, 24, 28, 31, 34 … semitones up). Every such note must have been **struck by this sound**: at least 1.5 times (+3.5 dB) its salience at its note, or a semitone either side, in the frame before. Then the frame is
- **a strum of the open strings** with at least 3 such notes, each exactly an open string of the tuning (within half a semitone of it after the strum's own tuning offset), and `StrumDetector` hearing at least 3 strings and half of them;
- **a fretted chord** when `ChordDetector` names one from at least 3 such notes, or from 2 as a power chord's root with its fifth above (a fourth, such as two open strings side by side, is none);
- else **nothing** (`StrumKind.NONE`).
The sound's kind is what 2 of its first 3 frames say (0.49–0.54 s after it), and holds until the next sound; a sound with no frame by its end (a knock) is nothing. A rise before the sound's first frame belongs to it (a slow strum, a quick arpeggio, or a bass's low strings beating, which dips and lifts the level enough for `StrumListener` to notice a new strum). Then a strum of open strings is read by `StrumDetector` and a chord named by `ChordDetector` every 0.1 s while it rings, as 1.1's tools did, and nothing more is read of a sound that was nothing.
  **Why each rule.** On the real guitar (`src/test/resources/fixtures/`) `ChordDetector` names a lone G string **G5** and a finger-plucked low E **E5**: the string's 3rd partial (D5 over G3, B3 over E2) is left over at 0.3–0.8 of the string's salience, enough to count in a chord. It lies on the string's harmonic, so it is no string of its own. A strum and one string plucked while others ring are told apart by the struck rule: the low E, then the A string a second later is E2 and A2 in one frame, which `ChordDetector` names **A5/E**, and the D, G and B strings in turn are **G/D**; the strings from before have not risen, so the sound is nothing. In the noisy recordings the TV's notes (up to 10 of them, some at half the string's salience) are not struck either. The open strings must be exact, not an octave up, or a Dsus2 (xx0230: D3 A3 D4 E4, all open strings or their octaves) would be a strum of open strings.
  **Tested** (`StrumWatcherTest`, `:core:dsp`): never a strum or a chord on any of the 30 real plucks (in standard tuning, drop D and chords only), on the six real strings plucked one after another 0.6 or 1 s apart while the others ring with two re-plucks after (pick and finger), on the reference tone of every string of the guitar, 12-string, ukulele and 4-string bass, on single `PluckSynth` notes E2–E5 and stiff ones with a phone's weak fundamental, on two open strings side by side together or 0.3 s apart, on noise and clicks. A strum of the open strings in tune, two strings off, every string within ±30 cents, struck unevenly, with the B left out, again over the first one still ringing, in drop D, DADGAD, open G and open D, the top three strings, a ukulele and a 4-string bass (its first sound: later rises of its beating strings are no new strum) is one, its first reading within a cent on the clear strings; a single string plucked over a ringing strum is nothing. All 41 open and barre shapes and the 16 power chords are chords, named right in most readings; a chord with chord names off, and the open strings with the open-string check off, are nothing. Decided 0.4–0.6 s after the strum; nothing allocates after `start`.

It fades in and out with the reading, moves only while it is shown (an infinite animation frame loop; each frame redraws only its own layer) and is hidden from TalkBack.
