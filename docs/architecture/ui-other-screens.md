## Metronome screen
`:feature:metronome`, after the approved mock-up [docs/design/metronome-nav-preview.html](../design/metronome-nav-preview.html) (its top comment is the spec). **Owner decided (2026-10-14):** keep the round tempo dial; everything else as shown; bar variant B (Frameless) for the navigation bar ([UI basics](ui.md), *Navigation bar*), which it shares the window with (it opens from the Metronome tab; switching tabs stops it).
- **Roomier layout (2026-10-18):** the owner found the screen too crowded and picked option A of the rough mock-up [docs/design/metronome-roomy-preview.html](../design/metronome-roomy-preview.html): the dial as large as the screen allows (upright it fills the width, 361 dp on the Xiaomi against 288 before, and the spare height goes to the gaps round it; on its side it fills its half), everything about the tempo inside it (− and + either side of the figures, a round Start / Stop in the opening at the bottom), and Tap tempo, Listen and "Vibrate on the beat" as one row of three icon buttons under it (72 × 52 dp each, 16 dp apart), each named for TalkBack. The beat lights are 48 dp tall upright (64 before).
- **Names and a beat you can see (Phase 43, the UI review of 2026-10-24):** the three buttons were icons only (the name in a long-press tooltip), and the beat lights small and far above the dial with an empty band between. Now each button has its short name under it ("Tap tempo", "Listen", "Vibrate"; `metronome_vibrate_label`, as "Vibrate on the beat" doesn't fit, until Sound took Vibrate's place in Phase 45; TalkBack still hears the buttons' own names, so the words are hidden from it, and the tooltips went): one line in `labelMedium`, muted, in an 84 dp column centred under its button, all three in one size, set smaller where the widest doesn't fit (`rowLabelSize`, down to 8 dp whatever the font size: Turkish "Dokunarak tempo", Dutch at 2× text); Listen keeps its word while listening; the buttons keep their 72 × 52 dp. Upright the beat lights stand right over the dial (only Listen's status line and the dial's 8 dp margin between, `LightsOverDial`), and the band moved above them, under the meter picker; where a tall screen has height to spare after the dial reaches its widest, the lights take half of it, from 48 up to 80 dp (the Xiaomi: 80 dp), and the marks, their glow and numbers grow in proportion (`BeatLightsArt` draws for a 48 dp row and scales past it, never smaller). On its side they take what the column leaves: 40–56 dp on a phone, up to 80 dp on a tall window. The heights depend only on the room, so nothing moves while the lights flash (`aBeatLightingUpMovesNothing`). The beat numbers sit a little lower (90 % down, the marks at 36 %), clear of the downbeat's mark at every size.
- **Sound (Phase 45, the owner's pick A of [docs/design/metronome-sound-preview.html](../design/metronome-sound-preview.html), round 2; its top comment is the spec):** the row reads **Sound · Tap tempo · Listen**; Sound took Vibrate's place and comes first (the owner expects it to be used most), and "Vibrate on the beat" moved into its panel as a switch, its behaviour unchanged. The button (`SoundButton`, `SoundParts.kt`) is the row's 72 × 52 dp see-through button, its icon the click picked, in the row icons' line style (1.8 dp round lines on a 24 dp grid): the wood block a block with wood grain and a stick striking its edge (`ic_sound_wood_block`, variant A3), the cowbell (`ic_sound_cowbell`), the claves one stick level and the other coming down onto it at about 30° (`ic_sound_claves`, A5), the rim click a drum with a stick across its rim (`ic_sound_rim_click`: the drum is clipped round the stick by a hole in its `clip-path`, wound the other way, as a tinted icon can't fill the stick to hide it). While vibration is on, ic_vibrate's two shaking lines stand either side of the icon (`ic_sound_vibrate_marks`, a 38 × 24 dp grid centred on it). The word under it is always "Sound" (`metronome_sound`), so it never changes size; TalkBack hears "Sound: Cowbell" (", vibrates on the beat" while on) as a drop-down list. A tap opens **the panel** (`SoundPanel`) on a layer over the screen (with the Listen balloon; the layer is always there, so its place is known before either shows): the raised surface colour (`surfaceContainerHigh`, as the tuner's pill; plain `surface` was nearly the background on the Xiaomi with wallpaper colours) in `shapes.large`, a soft 12 dp lift (shadow), no border, 248 dp wide (less on a narrow window), 8 dp padding; four 48 dp rows of icon and name (the instrument's common name in each language, one line, set smaller down to 8 dp where it must), the picked one in the container colour with a 2 dp accent outline in `shapes.medium` (as the app marks picked items), then "Vibrate on the beat" with a Material switch (the whole 56 dp row toggles it). Upright it opens 8 dp above Sound, its start edge in line with Sound's; on its side (≥ 600 dp wide and wider than tall) beside Sound towards the dial (the start side, so the right in right-to-left languages), its bottom level with the button; it keeps 8 dp from the window's edges, and where the room is short (very large text) it scrolls. A tap on a sound calls `MetronomeViewModel.previewSound`: stopped, one bar of it plays once (never looping); playing, it is heard from the next beat; the panel stays open for another try. Back or a tap beside it (which does nothing else) closes it; closing needs no stop, as the bar ends by itself, and leaving the screen or the tab stops a preview as it stops the clicks. Its open state survives the phone turning (it opens again in its place for the new layout). TalkBack hears the panel by name ("Sound", a pane title), the sounds as a radio group "Sound" by name, the switch by its name. Listen, now third, keeps its balloon pointing at it (above it upright, beside it over the dial on its side).
- **Listen:** after the approved mock-up [docs/design/metronome-listen-preview.html](../design/metronome-listen-preview.html) (owner decided 2026-10-15; its top comment is the spec). A tap on Listen asks for the microphone if needed ([Lifecycle](lifecycle.md)), then `MetronomeViewModel.listen` pauses the clicks (the microphone would hear them) and feeds `OnsetTempoDetector` ([Strum check and metronome](strum-and-metronome.md), *Tempo from music*) on `Dispatchers.Default` (`hear`, `withTimeoutOrNull(15 s)` round the recorder's chunks). Listening ends by itself once the detector is `STEADY`, after 15 s (a tempo if `SETTLING`, else "no beat"), or on Stop, another control (the player took over: − + Tap tempo, the meter, the dial's ring, Vibrate), leaving the screen. While it listens: Listen shows a stop square in the accent (TalkBack "Stop listening"), its outline dims and a 1.25 dp trace in the accent at 60 % runs clockwise from its top centre over the 15 s (`ListenTrace`, on the outline's own path, its last 8° fading; it fades out over 300 ms where it is when listening ends); Start / Stop rests at 38 % and takes no taps; the status line under the beat lights says "Listening…", then "Listening… 96 BPM" once the detector has a tempo; a small hollow ring on the dial's arc marks that tempo (fading in 400 ms, gliding 500 ms); the beat lights follow the heard beats (the detector's beat grid moved to the `System.nanoTime()` clock by the earliest chunk arrival, `HeardBeats`) at a softer level (0.25 + 0.45). **Found:** a balloon over the screen pointing at Listen (`ListenBalloon`, the tour's balloon: accent fill, soft lift, Futuristic glow and cut corners, at most 260 dp; beside Listen when a phone on its side has no room above or below), with the style's mark and "Heard", the tempo in the number face with BPM and its marking, ½× 1× 2× (only those within 30–250), Not now and Use. Nothing changes until Use: then the dial's arc, needle and number glide there in 700 ms with one soft glow behind the number, and the clicks come back after the glide only if they played before Listen. Not now, Back or another control closes it and brings the clicks back if they played. **No beat:** "No steady beat heard  Try again" for 8 s; the tempo stays. The status line keeps its 24 dp (one line at larger fonts) so nothing moves, crossfades its changes (160 ms out, 160 ms in; a new tempo while listening changes in place, `contentKey`) and is a polite live region that holds its text (merged, the words fading out left unheard), so TalkBack says each change; while listening it says paced words instead (`pacedSpeech`, a fresh pace per listening: "Listening…" at once, the first tempo after 400 ms, a tempo still settling at most every 2 s), with the shown words hidden but still there for the language checks; its action is at least 24 dp tall, as tall as the line, with Compose's 48 dp touch area. Words and action keep their size while they fit side by side; where they don't (large text, long languages) the action takes at most 45 % of the line and each shrinks to stay on one line (the words down to 6 sp, which Dutch at 2× text on a 360 dp phone needs).
- **Layout, upright** (one column, at most 480 dp, scrolls with very large fonts): the meter picker (2/4 3/4 4/4 5/4 6/8 7/8, where the tuner's mode switch is; one radio group "Beats in a bar"; 50 dp buttons so six fit a 360 dp phone; where they don't (a phone on its side beside navigation buttons, as the S26: about 300 dp) each touch area is its share of the row and 48 dp tall, as the chord buttons, its label set smaller where it must (down to 9 sp); where even that wouldn't fit (Futuristic's and Space Age's wide faces at 2× text on its side, overnight run 2026-10-18) and at very large fonts the full-size buttons wrap onto a second row), the beat lights (48–80 dp), Listen's status line (24 dp), the tempo dial with − and + and Start / Stop inside it, and the row of Sound, Tap tempo and Listen with their names. The lights, the line and the dial are `FitColumn`'s shrinking part together (`LightsOverDial`): the dial takes all the height the rest leave, up to the width less the 16 dp margins (at most 440 dp), down to 200 dp (below that, at very large text, the column scrolls); on a tall phone the lights take half of the spare height (up to 80 dp) and the gaps above the lights, between the dial and the row and under the row share the rest. **On its side** (≥ 600 dp wide and wider than tall): the dial filling its half (up to 440 dp) at the start, the rest in a column beside it (16 dp apart, as the other Practice tools), centred; on a short window the beat lights get lower (56 down to 40 dp, `FitColumn`) so it fits without scrolling (the S26 with navigation buttons, 2026-10-18: 650 × 325 dp beside the rail); only very large text scrolls. The quiet background, as Settings. - **Upright on a tablet (Phase 52):** the column stopped at 480 dp and the dial at 440 dp, so on the Lenovo Tab M11 upright (800 dp) the meter picker, the beat lights, the dial (290 dp across there) and the row kept a phone's size with a wide empty band either side, as the tuner had. Now the upright column follows the tuner's rule (`columnScaleFor` and `ScaledUp`, in `:core:designsystem`'s `ScaledUp.kt`; [Tuner screen and tour](ui-tuner.md), *Upright tablet*): it is laid out as on a phone of the window divided by the scale and drawn that much bigger, so every part, text and gap grows together (1.43 on the Tab M11: a 686 dp column, the dial about 413 dp across at its 1.3x text); the Sound panel and the suggestion's balloon, on their layer over the screen, are drawn at the same scale, so they open from Sound and Listen at the column's size. Their anchors, the dial's drag (angles from the dial's middle in px) and the beat lights work in px and need nothing else. The Practice tab's tool button keeps its own size and place there, at the start of a phone's 480 dp column 8 dp down, as over Chords and Scales, so it doesn't move when the tool changes; the scaled column takes the height under it. On its side (two halves) and on every phone the scale is 1 and nothing changes. `MetronomeStylesTest.anUprightTabletShowsThePhonesColumnBigger` checks every part on an 800 x 1100 dp tablet at 1.3x text against the phone-sized window that much bigger, no text cut off, the tool button's place, and the Sound panel.
- **Press effect** (`Modifier.pressFeedback` in `:core:designsystem`, `PressFeedback.kt`): every metronome button lights up instead of showing Material's ripple (`indication = null` on its `clickable`, `selectable` or `toggleable`, the same `MutableInteractionSource` given to the modifier). The modifier draws the button's face itself (fill, the light, outline, then the content), so the light lies above the background and under the text, and scales the whole drawing for the sink (never the layout). Level: 1 from the frame of touch-down, held while pressed, then (1 − t/280 ms)³; sink 1 − sink × s (s smoothed over 35 ms; 0.03, Ancient and Retro 0.02, Classic and the vibrate row 0.015). The light per style: Contemporary and Space Age the accent at 0.20; Ancient a radial smudge of the ink mixed 45 % into the accent at 0.24; Classic a shadow from the top over the accent at 12 %; Retro a 9 dp shadow from the top (the pressed-in push button) over the accent at 10 %; Futuristic the accent at 14 % with the outline lit and an 8 dp accent glow (Android 9+). `PressTone` picks flat colours on accent fills (Start / Stop: on-accent at 0.20; in the balloon on-accent, on its filled buttons the accent). Text buttons and the vibrate row light a 32 dp pill (`lightShape`, `lightInset`). Tap tempo uses `crisp = true`: full for 70 ms from touch-down whatever the finger does, a 40 ms linear fade, no sink. "Remove animations": no sink or fade, lit while held (at least 100 ms; crisp: 70 ms). TalkBack's double tap sends no touch, so each button calls `MutableInteractionSource.pressFlash()` in its click (ignored within a second of a real touch). `PressFeedbackTest` (JVM) checks the timing. The rest of the app has the same effect ([Meter, wave and styles](ui-meter-and-styles.md), *Visual styles*, **Press effect everywhere**).
- **Tempo dial** (`TempoDialArt` in `:core:designsystem` `metronome/`, gestures and TalkBack in `TempoDial`): frameless on the meter's calm field, 30–250 BPM clockwise over 300° (`dialDegrees`), the gap at the bottom; a tick every 10 BPM with the meter's scale rules (major ticks and numbers at 30, 50, 100, 150, 200, 250; numbers kept 3u clear of their tick's end whichever way it points), the style's arc finish unlit over the range and lit in the accent up to the tempo, the style's needle inside the arc pointing at it. The tempo sits in the middle in the style's number face (46 units, its middle 14 units above the dial's, fixed like the tuner's note; the mock-up draws it in the number face, which also spares Ancient's display face its diamond 0), "BPM" and the Italian marking under it (growing with the font up to 1.3×). Both are measured, so every style's faces keep clear (overnight run 2026-10-18): the figures shrink, the same at every tempo, where the widest tempo would come within 3 units of − and + (Space Age's and Futuristic's wide faces), and "BPM" and the marking shrink (to 0.7× at least) where the marking would come within 3 units of Start (Start keeps its 56 dp on a small dial, and some styles set "BPM" in a tall line). − and + stand level with the figures, faces 32 units across (at least 32 dp, in a 48 dp touch area) with their middles 58 units out, so they reach 76 units out at most, inside the needle's path (from 78); 58 units was too large a figure for them to fit beside it. Start / Stop (54 units, at least 56 dp) stands 104 units below the middle, in the opening between the arc's ends (47 units either side, 81 down). Each takes its own touches before the ring. A touch on the ring sets the tempo it points to, a drag follows the finger (a touch in the gap keeps to the nearer end, `bpmAtTouch`), saved when the finger lifts. Only the ring reacts (`isOnDialRing`: from 74 units out, 20 inside the arc and just clear of the tempo's figures and marking at their widest, to the dial's edge at 144), so a tap on the tempo or "BPM" in the middle does nothing (the middle's angle is anything, so it would jump the tempo to 30 or 250). A drag that started on the ring follows the finger anywhere but the middle (`isClearOfDialMiddle`), past the dial's edge too. `TempoDialTest` (JVM) checks the angles, the gap, the ring and its scaling. TalkBack: one slider "Tempo, drag to change", "96 beats per minute, Andante" (Dutch "96 slagen per minuut", the usual Dutch for BPM), 30–250 in 1 BPM steps (`progressBarRangeInfo` + `setProgress`).
- **Beat lights** (`BeatLightsArt`, drawn for a 48 dp row and in proportion past it): one mark per beat of the bar in the style's own wave mark (Ancient dab, Classic engraved band, Retro phosphor band, Contemporary rounded bar, Futuristic neon bar, Space Age star pair), beat 1 largest, group starts (`BeatAccent.GROUP`) middle sized, the plain beats smallest; beat numbers under them. The beat heard lights up in the accent (never in-tune green) with a soft glow and eases (200 ms) to a resting 40 % until the next. Hidden from TalkBack.
- **The beat** (`BeatClock`): `MetronomePlayer.beats` gives each click with the moment it is heard; a frame loop (`withInfiniteAnimationFrameNanos`, only while playing, skipped by UI tests) moves only the lights and one soft accent glow behind the tempo (220 ms decay, full on a downbeat, 75 % on a group start, 55 % otherwise), read in the draw phase, so playing never recomposes. Changes are heard from the next beat (≈ 0.12 s).
- **Controls**: see-through buttons in the style's shape (`shapes.small`, round − and + and Start in Contemporary and Space Age) with a muted outline; picked ones (the sound picked in the Sound panel, the meter) in the container colour with an accent outline; Start in the accent, a play or stop icon only (TalkBack "Start", "Stop"), one size whatever it does; Futuristic's as neon outlines (Start / Stop's 14 % accent tint lies on the background colour, so the grid's lines don't show through it). - **State** (`MetronomeViewModel`): tempo, meter, vibration and the click's sound (`sound`, Phase 45), each saved at once (a dial drag when the finger lifts); opens stopped at the saved values (passed in from the settings already read, so the first frame and the first beat are right). `previewSound` picks a sound: while playing it is heard from the next beat; while stopped one bar of it plays once at the tempo and meter shown (`isPreviewing` meanwhile, `isPlaying` stays false), and the next pick, Start, Listen or leaving the screen ends it. ## Practice tab
Phase 32, after the approved rough mock-up [docs/design/practice-preview.html](../design/practice-preview.html) (its top comment is the spec). **Owner picked (2026-10-16): A, D, G, I.** The Metronome tab becomes Practice (the bar: Tuner · Practice · Settings, [UI basics](ui.md), *Navigation bar*), holding the Metronome, Chords, Scales (a tool of its own since Phase 47) and, with its own switch (`ToolSwitches.capoHelper`, Phase 33), the Capo helper, opening on the tool used last (saved); with its switch (Phase 28, `ToolSwitches.practiceTab`) off it stays the plain Metronome tab, with no switch, and nothing about Practice is shown or saved.
- **Where it lives:** `:feature:practice` (decided 2026-10-17) holds the tool button and the tools that don't listen, Chords, Scales and the Capo helper, so `:feature:metronome` stays about the metronome and its microphone. **Sound (Phase 48, decided 2026-10-26):** for the drone under Scales it depends on `:core:audio`, as `:feature:metronome` does, but uses only the `DronePlayer` interface, playback: it never opens the microphone (the app's `AppContainer.dronePlayer`, the real `AndroidDronePlayer`, handed to the Scales screen by `MainActivity.PracticeTab` with the A4 setting and the saved drone choices, as the metronome gets its player; tests use `FakeDronePlayer` from `:core:audio`'s test fixtures; see *Drone* under *Scales*). Depending on the module rather than having `:app` hand over lambdas keeps the screen's own tests on the same interface the app uses. `:feature:*` modules never depend on each other, so `:app` (`MainActivity.PracticeTab`) puts them together: it picks the tool, saves it and hands each tool the tool button as its `header`. `practiceToolsFor(tools)` gives the tools offered (none with the switch off; the Capo helper only with its own switch on too), `practiceToolToOpen` the one to open on (the saved one while offered, else the metronome).

On its side (≥ 600 dp wide and wider than tall) the row stands at the top of the start half, which on the metronome is the dial's pane: the dial shrinks to leave room, so the controls' column keeps its height and nothing has to scroll. A tap opens a short list of the tools (a Material menu: a soft lift with no border, 200 dp wide), each a 48 dp row of icon and name, the picked one in the container colour with a 2 dp accent outline as the metronome's Sound panel; a pick switches the tool and closes it, as do Back and a tap beside it. ## Home-screen widget
`:app` `widget/`. A tile for the home screen, three cells by one (resizable across, down to two): the navigation bar's tuning fork in the accent, the instrument and tuning as the tuner names them ("Guitar · Standard", a custom tuning by its own name, a lowered one as "Guitar · Drop C", "Chromatic" in chromatic mode) in ink, and "Open the tuner" ("Open de stemmer") in the muted colour under it, on a rounded tile in the style's background colour (corners: the launcher's own on Android 12+, 16 dp before). No border, no art: calm beside other widgets. A tap anywhere opens Twangle on the tuner.
- **Tech: plain `RemoteViews`** (`layout/widget_tuning.xml`, an `AppWidgetProvider`), not Jetpack Glance. The widget is one static layout with three values to fill in; Glance would add a library, a second Compose runtime for widgets and a WorkManager-backed session for no gain here. Text sizes come from the layout (the title shrinks from 16 to 11 sp to fit, `autoSizeTextType`), the face is the system's medium sans: `RemoteViews` can't take the styles' bundled faces without a layout per style, and the colours already carry the style.
- **Colours** (`TuningWidget.paint`): the style's scheme as the app ends up with it (`colorSchemeFor` in `:core:designsystem`, wallpaper colours for Contemporary with Material You on, `usesWallpaperColors`): background, `onBackground` (ink), `onSurfaceVariant` (muted) and `primary` (accent), set as colour filters on the tile and the fork and as text colours. With the theme on "System" on Android 12+ both sets go in (`setColorInt` light/night), so the launcher follows the phone's dark mode without a redraw; otherwise the set for the current brightness. The picker's preview (Android 12+, `previewLayout`) is the layout itself in Contemporary light or dark (`values/widget.xml`).
- **Kept up to date by the app, never on a timer** (`updatePeriodMillis` 0): `TwangleApplication` follows the settings (`TuningWidget.follow`) for as long as its process lives and redraws the widgets when what they show changes (`WidgetState`: mode, instrument, tuning, custom name, style, theme, wallpaper colours; A4 or the meter's look change nothing), and when the configuration changes (language, dark mode before Android 12). `TuningWidgetReceiver` draws a widget when it is placed and redraws on the phone's `LOCALE_CHANGED`, reading the settings off the main thread (`goAsync`). With no widget placed, a redraw is one `getAppWidgetIds` call.
- **One tap opens the tuner** (`TuningWidget.openTuner`): an immutable `PendingIntent` to `MainActivity` with `ACTION_OPEN_TUNER` and `NEW_TASK | CLEAR_TOP | SINGLE_TOP`. A cold start opens on the tuner anyway; an open app gets `onNewIntent`, which counts a request, and `TwangleApp` switches to the tuner (an open tuning editor closes as with Back, dropping its draft).
- **Never the microphone:** the widget and its receiver touch only the settings. The mic opens only when the tuner it opens is shown, exactly as from the launcher ([Lifecycle](lifecycle.md)), and the tuner asks for the permission there.
- **TalkBack:** the tile is one button; the title reads as the tuner's button does ("Guitar, Standard"), then "Open the tuner".
- **Tests:** `WidgetStateTest` (JVM: names, custom tuning, chromatic, which changes redraw); `TuningWidgetTest` (device: the views applied as a launcher does show the names in the style's colours and are tappable; drawing them records nothing; the tap's intent brings the open app from Settings back to the tuner).

## Watch app (Wear OS)
`:wear`. A standalone Wear OS app (`com.google.android.wearable.standalone`): it listens with the watch's own microphone and needs nothing from the phone. Compose for Wear OS (`androidx.wear.compose` Material 3, `AppScaffold` with the time at the top), minSdk 30 (Wear OS 3).
- **Same application ID as the phone** (`io.github.klaboukes.twangle`), as Wear OS and Play expect for a phone app and its watch app; namespace `…twangle.wear`. **`versionCode` = the phone's + 1,000,000** (1,010,000 for 1.0.0), because Play needs a different code for each build; both come from `twangle.version` ([Threading and performance](performance.md), *Release build*). The release build is signed with the phone's upload key. The launcher icon is the phone's adaptive icon (copied into `wear/src/main/res`).
- **The pitch engine is the phone's**: `WatchPitchPipeline` runs `:core:dsp`'s `FrameAssembler` → `SignalGate` → `SustainCheck` (with its own noise floor for the room rule) → YIN (`AnalysisSettings.newPitchDetector`) → `PitchSmoother` → `InTuneDetector` (the phone's default window: within 5 cents for 300 ms, released past 7), with frame sizes from the recorder's rate, so a 16 kHz watch mic gets the same window and hop as 48 kHz. On a watch-class core it costs about 13–20 % of the CPU ([Threading and performance](performance.md), *Watch CPU and the native detector*). It leaves out the phone's extras (SNR, reception, tips, strum check, low-note window, precision setting): chromatic, A4 = 440 Hz, sharps. It allocates nothing per frame and sends a new `WatchTunerState` (`Listening`, `Detected(note, cents, inTune)`, `Failed`) only when the note, the whole cents or "in tune" change. A note lost for a moment stays on screen 0.6 s beyond the smoother's own hold (as long as the phone's glow bridges a dropout) and keeps "in tune" if the same note comes back, so the watch never flickers between a reading and "Play a note".
- **The microphone is `:core:audio`'s `AndroidAudioRecorder`**, unchanged: unprocessed source, then voice recognition, the watch's native rate, the built-in mic while a Bluetooth headset is connected.
- **Microphone lifetime** as on the phone: `WatchTunerViewModel.state` is `stateIn(WhileSubscribed(5_000))` and the screen collects it with `collectAsStateWithLifecycle()`, only once `RECORD_AUDIO` is granted. The permission is asked for when the app first opens; afterwards a button asks again, or opens the app's page in the watch's settings once Android stops showing its dialog. `rememberWatchMicPermission` follows the phone's `MicPermissionRules` ([Lifecycle](lifecycle.md)): a first dialog dismissed without an answer keeps "Allow microphone", and only a real "don't ask again" leads to settings. A refusal is remembered in the watch's own small `SharedPreferences` file (`twangle_wear`, `mic_refused`), as the phone keeps it in its settings. `WatchMicPermissionTest` on the JVM. A busy or broken mic shows a message with "Try again".
- **Screen off and ambient let go of the mic at once.** On Wear OS the activity stays *resumed* when the screen dims to ambient (always-on) or goes off (seen on the emulator: display `DOZE`, Twangle still the top resumed activity), so the lifecycle alone would keep the mic open. `ScreenOnEffect` listens for `ACTION_SCREEN_OFF` / `ACTION_SCREEN_ON` (sent when the display leaves or enters interactive mode, ambient included) and checks `PowerManager.isInteractive` on start; the view model then switches its upstream to a plain `Listening`, without the 5 s grace, and opens the mic again when the screen comes back. The app doesn't declare ambient support, so the watch shows the system's ambient view. Measured on the emulator: the mic is released within 0.9 s of `KEYCODE_SLEEP` (most of it adb) and reopened on wake; Home still keeps the 5 s grace.
- **Screen:** a ring along the edge (inset 6 % of the smaller side, so it sits the same on round and square screens), with the cents scale at its top: ±50 cents over ±60° either side of 12 o'clock, a tick every 10 cents and a longer one in ink at the target. A dot in the accent marks the reading and glides there (a low-stiffness spring), drifting back to the centre as it fades when the note stops. The note is large in the middle (30 % of the dial, light weight, the octave small beside it on the same baseline, a ♯ raised and smaller in the phone's cut of Noto Sans Symbols, bundled with its licence; the watch has no About page, so its licence texts, the OFL and Apache 2.0 for AndroidX and Kotlin, ship as assets, `WatchLicencesTest`), the cents under it ("+12 cents", "−3 cents"), and "Play a note" while nothing is heard; reading and "Play a note" cross-fade (400 ms). Sizes follow the screen, not the font setting, so the reading always fits inside the ring (checked from 170 to 225 dp). The tuner leaves out the time, where the ring runs; the permission and error messages keep it.
- **In tune:** the ring, the dot and the note ease to the in-tune colour over 700 ms (`FastOutSlowInEasing`) with a soft band of light around the ring (a radial gradient, 35 % on the ring, fading to nothing 5 % of the dial either side), hold while the note is in tune and fade the same way. No pulse, no sparks. TalkBack hears the note, the cents and "in tune".
- **Colours** are the phone's default style, Contemporary dark (`WatchColors`: ink, muted, track, accent, in tune), on black. They are copied rather than shared because `:core:designsystem` brings the phone's settings, fonts and Material 3; `WatchDialTest` fails if they drift from `StylePalettes.kt`. The watch has no style choice.
- **Tests:** `WatchPitchPipelineTest` (JVM, synthetic notes through the fake recorder: A4 in tune, cents within 1 of the played pitch, a 16 kHz mic, silence, few states for a held note, in tune only after 300 ms, never at 20 cents, a new note earns it again, a 400 ms dropout keeps reading and glow, back to listening after the note stops), `WatchTunerViewModelTest` (mic closed until collected, released 5 s after, released at once when the screen goes off and reopened when it comes on, error and retry, note names) and `WatchDialTest` (angles, colours match the phone's). On the Wear OS emulator (AVD `Wear_OS_Large_Round`, Wear OS 6, 454 px = 227 dp round): `WatchTunerTest` (6 Compose tests: "Play a note", the reading, TalkBack's "in tune", cross-fade both ways, "Try again", the reading inside the ring from 170 to 225 dp). By hand it installs, records through voice recognition at 48 kHz (the emulator has no unprocessed source), shows the ring with "Play a note", lets go of the mic on sleep and takes it back on wake.
