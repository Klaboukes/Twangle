# Twangle on Google Play: the store listing

The plan for how Twangle is presented on Google Play: the texts, the pictures, and how people find it. Phase 38 of the [roadmap](../ROADMAP.md) builds it, and Phase 53 puts it on Play. The English texts are the source; every language in `twangle.languages` has its own translation (see *The texts, ready to paste*).

## What makes Twangle stand out
Most tuners in the store are free to download but show ads, sell a subscription, ask for an account or track their users. Twangle's strong points, in the order the listing tells them:

1. **It tunes well:** a clear needle, within ±1 cent, from a 5-string bass's low B to the top frets, with "Tune up" / "Tune down" and a reference tone to tune by ear.
2. **Many instruments:** guitar (6, 7, 8 strings), bass, ukulele, violin, viola, cello, mandolin, chromatic for everything else, and your own tunings.
3. **Practice in the same app:** a metronome, chord charts and scales on the fretboard for your instrument and tuning (your own tunings included), and a Capo helper that works out where the capo goes and what each shape sounds.
4. **Tools that listen with the tuner:** strum check (every string at once), chord name (strum a chord and see its name), pitch trail (a line that shows how steady a held note, a bend or vibrato is) and the 12th-fret check (does the guitar play in tune up the neck).
5. **Made for everyone:** TalkBack speaks the note and how far off it is, every control works at 2× text, a vibration when in tune, 16 languages including Arabic (right to left). Few tuners can say this, and blind and low-vision musicians look for exactly this.
6. **Nothing to pay, nothing to sign, nothing leaves the phone:** no ads, no account, no purchases, no tracking, and no internet permission at all. Play's *Data safety* section will show "No data collected" and "No data shared", which backs this up.
7. **Yours to style:** six styles, six meter looks, light and dark, and focus mode for one big meter on a phone on its side.
8. **A home-screen widget.** The Wear OS watch app follows in an update after v1.0 (owner, 2026-10-16, **decision 3**), so v1.0's listing leaves it out.

## Google's rules that shape the listing
Checked against the Play Console when entering the listing, since Google changes them now and then.
- **No price or promotion words in the title, icon, developer name, feature graphic or screenshots:** no "free", "no ads", "best", "#1", "download now". Those claims go in the short and full description only, stated as plain facts.
- **"Free" stays out of the short description** (checked 2026-10-29): Google's *Metadata* policy itself bans price and promotion words only in the title, icon and developer name, but its store-listing best practices say of the short description (which it also calls the promotional description, the text shown in search results): don't include promotional offers like "free" or "discount", nor claims like "#1" or "best". So the short description says "no ads, no account, offline" (facts about the app, not offers) and leaves "free" to the full description, whose first line says it. Play shows the price ("Free" / "Install") beside every listing anyway.
- **No other apps named** anywhere in the listing, and no keyword lists: search words appear in normal sentences.
- **Lengths:** title 30 characters, short description 80, full description 4000.
- **Screenshots:** 2 to 8 per device type, JPEG or 24-bit PNG (no alpha), each side 320 to 3840 px, the long side at most twice the short side. The Xiaomi 12X's screen is 1080 × 2400 (2.2 : 1), too long as it is, so screenshots are placed on a 1080 × 1920 canvas with a caption above (see below). At least 4 screenshots of 1080 px or more make the app eligible for Play's recommendations.
- **Icon** 512 × 512 PNG; **feature graphic** 1024 × 500 PNG or JPEG, shown behind the promo video or at the top of the listing on some devices, so nothing important near its edges.

## Title and texts

**App name in the store (30):** `Twangle: Guitar Tuner` (21). "Guitar tuner" is by far the most searched phrase in this category and the title weighs most in Play search. The launcher label stays "Twangle". In other languages the part after the colon is translated ("Twangle: Gitaarstemmer", "Twangle: Gitarrenstimmgerät"), as long as it fits in 30.

**Short description (80):** shown under the title, and the second strongest place for search words. Since Phase 50 (2026-10-29) it leads with what sets Twangle apart, as well as saying what it is:
> Tuner for guitar, bass, ukulele and violin. No ads, no account, fully offline.

(78 characters.) Why: players who already have a tuner switch when they are fed up with ads, accounts and needing the internet (the growth plan, ROADMAP Phase 50), so those three facts go where every search result shows them. The search words the owner chose stay (tuner, guitar, bass, ukulele, violin); "accurate" and "metronome" made room, and both are in the full description's first lines and the title's "Guitar Tuner" carries the strongest phrase. "Free" is left out on purpose (*Google's rules*, above); "fully offline" says in two words what "no internet permission" says in four, and the full description's first lines spell it out. Other languages follow the same pattern in their app words, each within 80 code points; where the three facts don't all fit, the internet one stays (Italian: "Senza pubblicità né internet"), and Japanese, Korean and Chinese, with room to spare, keep the metronome.

Replaced (the owner's pick of 2026-10-17, on Play until the next `scripts/release.sh listing`):
> Accurate tuner for guitar, bass, ukulele and violin, with a metronome. No ads.

The alternative, which leads with accessibility, stays the candidate for a Play *store listing experiment* after launch (see *Being found*):
> Guitar tuner and metronome that speaks with TalkBack. No ads, no account.

(73 characters.)

**Full description (4000):** plain text, short paragraphs, a few bullet lines; no emoji rows. Its first paragraph carries the message on its own, since many readers stop after two or three lines: what Twangle is (a guitar tuner that also tunes bass, ukulele, violin and more), every feature free, no ads, no account, no in-app purchases, no tracking, and no internet permission, so the sound is analysed on the phone and never recorded or sent. The second paragraph says how it tunes (precise to a cent, "Tune up" / "Tune down") and what else it does. Then six capitalised sections in the order of the strong points above: tune any instrument, listening tools, practice, made for everyone, make it yours, also. It closes with one short promise, "Twangle will stay free and offline, for good", without repeating the list of the first paragraph (until Phase 50 the opening told what it does and the closing listed "no ads, no account, no purchases" a second time). The English text is [fastlane/metadata/android/en-US/full_description.txt](../fastlane/metadata/android/en-US/full_description.txt) (about 3,000 characters); the longest translation, German, is about 3,600. The Wear OS line comes back with the update that brings the watch app (**decision 3**). The words people search for (guitar tuner, bass tuner, ukulele tuner, violin tuner, cello tuner, chromatic tuner, metronome, chord chart, chord name, scales, capo, Drop D, strobe) each appear in a real sentence; none is repeated just for search.

### The texts, ready to paste
Every language's title, short and full description are plain-text files in `fastlane/metadata/android/<Play locale>/` (`title.txt`, `short_description.txt`, `full_description.txt`): the layout fastlane and the Gradle Play Publisher read, and F-Droid too once the repo is public. `scripts/release.sh listing` sends them to Play, every language at once, with the pictures ([release.md](release.md), *The store listing*); pasted by hand instead, one language at a time, the trailing newline doesn't count. Each release's "What's new" sits beside them, `changelogs/<versionCode>.txt` in every language (at most 500 characters), pasted as one block ([release.md](release.md)).
- **Folders** use Play's locale codes, derived from the Android qualifier in `twangle.languages`: `pt-rBR` → `pt-BR`, `zh-rCN` → `zh-CN`, `in` → `id`, `b+zh+Hant` → both `zh-TW` and `zh-HK` (Play has no Traditional Chinese of its own; the two hold the same texts but for Hong Kong's words, 結他 for guitar; `StoreListingTest`'s `PLAY_FOLDERS` names them, and `scripts/release.sh listing` sends every folder), `nb` → `no-NO` (Play's Norwegian; also in `PLAY_FOLDERS`, and the store screenshots set the app to `nb-NO` for it), and a plain language takes Play's folder for it with its region (`en` → `en-US`, `nl` → `nl-NL`, `de` → `de-DE`, `fr` → `fr-FR`, `es` → `es-ES`, `it` → `it-IT`, `ja` → `ja-JP`, `ko` → `ko-KR`, `hi` → `hi-IN`, `ru` → `ru-RU`, `tr` → `tr-TR`, `pl` → `pl-PL`, `bn` → `bn-BD`, `sv` → `sv-SE`, `da` → `da-DK`), or without one where Play has none (`ar`, `id`, `vi`, `th`, `uk`).
- **Words** follow each language's app texts (`res/values-<language>/strings.xml`): the tab, tool and style names, "Tune up"/"Tune down", the register (du, tu, vous, ты …), and the TalkBack example is what Twangle says in that language, with its note names (Mi 2 in the Do Re Mi languages). In the title only the part after the colon is translated; French puts a no-break space before the colon, like the app.
- **Guard:** `StoreListingTest` (JVM, `:app`, part of `./gradlew test`) fails when a language in `twangle.languages` has no folder (or two, but for the languages its `PLAY_FOLDERS` gives two), a folder is for a language that isn't listed, a file is missing or empty, a text is over Play's limit (counted in Unicode code points), the title doesn't start with "Twangle" or has "free", "no ads", "best" or "#1", or a text has emoji. Adding a language therefore needs its store texts too.

## Pictures

### Icon and feature graphic
- **Icon:** the launcher icon (*twang to tune*, PR #206) at 512 × 512, full square: Google adds the round corners and shadow itself. It shows the middle 90 dp of the adaptive icon's 108 dp layers, so the mark fills about three quarters of the width: the whole 108 dp left it small and thin in the yellow square, the launcher's own 72 dp crowded it against Play's round corners. A 32-bit PNG (Play's format), about 10 KB.
- **Feature graphic:** 1024 × 500 in Contemporary, light (as screenshot 1), on the style's quiet background: the store icon (with Play's round corners and a soft shadow) and the name "Twangle" under it in the style's display face on the left; on the right the tuner's own meter, the default look, holding a note in tune: the needle upright in the "in tune" colour with its calm glow. No other text: the meter's small "CENTS" label (translated in the app) is left out, and there is no note name (A in English, La in the Do Re Mi languages). So one version serves every language: it is sent with the default language (en-US) only, and Play shows the default language's graphics in every language without its own. Everything keeps about 100 px from the edges, which Play may crop or cover. A 24-bit PNG without alpha (Play's format), about 230 KB.
- **Making them:** `scripts/store-graphics.sh` (about a minute, phone connected). The `StoreGraphics` test (`:app`, androidTest, opt-in with `storeGraphics=true`) draws both on the phone from the app itself: the icon from the real adaptive icon's layers, the feature graphic in Compose with the app's theme and the real `MeterFrame` (`FeatureGraphic`, drawn and read back like the screenshots' canvas). They go to `fastlane/metadata/android/en-US/images/icon.png` and `featureGraphic.png`, where fastlane and F-Droid look for them, and unlike the screenshots they are committed (small, and the same in every language). Make them again after the launcher icon, the meter or Contemporary's look change; `StoreListingTest` checks their exact sizes and formats.

When the repository goes public with F-Droid, the release's set can be committed once.

## Being found
- **Category:** Music & Audio.
- **Tags:** in the Play Console (*Store settings*), up to five of Google's tags: the ones closest to tuner, metronome and musical instruments.
- **Contact details:** the public contact email (**decision 5**) and the website's home page, https://klaboukes.github.io/, as website (the privacy-policy page until Phase 54; the policy's address in *App content* stays); `scripts/release.sh listing` sets both, with English (United States) as the default language (`playAppDetails` in `app/build.gradle.kts`), and leaves the contact phone empty.
- **Ratings:** good ratings early help search more than anything else. Ask the closed-test testers to rate it once it is public. In Twangle, a calm "Rate Twangle" row in About that opens the Play page, and Google Play's own rating window (In-App Review), asked for at most once: right after every string of the tuning has been in tune in one go, never while tuning, never on the first day, with no question of Twangle's own before it (owner's pick, 2026-10-28; Phase 50). Google decides whether it actually shows, and it never shows in an install from the PC, so its effect shows only in Play Console's ratings after release.
- **Translations:** every language's title, short and full description and captions, translated by Claude and checked by a native speaker where possible (the Phase 22 owner check covers this).
- **Accessibility communities:** once live, submit Twangle to the app directories and forums of blind and low-vision Android users (for example *Accessible Android*), where a tuner that speaks is rare. Ask the closed-test testers whether one of them uses TalkBack.
- **After launch:** Play's *store listing experiments* can compare the chosen short description with the TalkBack one or the first screenshot; *custom store listings* can give e.g. bass players their own listing later.
- **"What's new":** short release notes in every language with each update, in the same plain style.

**Which days:** the daily figures for 2025 show it is Christmas Day itself: worldwide 100 on 25 December against 40–60 on an ordinary day (about 2×), in the US 100 against about 33 (3×); 26–28 December stay about 1.2–1.6× normal, and by 2 January it is an ordinary day again. So "late December into January" is really **one sharp week**, 24–28 December: new guitars unwrapped, tuners searched for that day. January is no busier than autumn.

**What it means for launch:** Twangle has to be in production, and high in Play search, *before* 24 December; ratings gathered after Christmas come too late for that week. By Phase 53's dates the date itself looks safe: the closed test's 14 days end around 5–6 November (1.0.1 on the closed track since 22 October), Google's review of the production-access application takes up to about a week, so production around mid-November, with the staged rollout at 100 % before December. The tighter part is the next version, 1.1.0, which waits for a clean overnight run and every language complete; if the new languages or Phase 46's recordings would push it past early December, send **1.0.2, already on Play, to production first** and put 1.1.0 out as its first update. A few hundred ratings by Christmas is the harder goal: it takes a few thousand installs in the first weeks, so the closed testers' ratings and Phase 50's listing work matter more than the exact date.
