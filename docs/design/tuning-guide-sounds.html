The tuning guide's sounds

The blind guitarist who reviewed Twangle asked for sounds: a tone that glides up while the string is sharp and down while it is flat, and a steady note once it is in tune. Here are a few of each to compare, then a string nearing pitch with the guide playing over it. Every sound is short and stops by itself.

–

Why the sounds sit so high

A guitar's strings sound from the low E (82 Hz) to the high E's 24th fret (about 1,300 Hz), and the tuner listens for pitches up to 1,500 Hz. So the glides sit at 2,000–3,600 Hz: they never sound like a wrong note against the string, and the tuner doesn't take them for one. The phone's speaker still goes into its microphone, so the tuner sets each sound and a short tail aside (about 0.1 s here, measured on the phone when it is built), as it does the reference tones. While it does, it doesn't hear the string either; that is why every sound is short.

The pace, the vibration guide's: one sound every 700 ms when the string is 30 cents off or more, easing in a straight line to one every 300 ms at 5 cents (start to start). Within 5 cents it is in tune: the in-tune note once, then quiet while the string holds; the guide starts again once it drifts more than 7 cents off. Sharp: the glide goes up. Flat: it goes down.

Glides

Each pair plays one glide up (too high, loosen) and one down (too low, tighten). Speeding up plays 3 s of a string coming from 30 cents sharp to 5, at the pace above. "Near pitch" is how much of the time the tuner still hears the string at the quickest pace (300 ms apart).

A · Short glide. 0.1 s, 2,000 ↔ 2,500 Hz (a major third). Clearly a glide, still short. Near pitch the tuner hears the string a third of the time.
B · Longer glide. 0.16 s, 1,900 ↔ 2,850 Hz (a fifth). Sings more, but near pitch the tuner hears the string only an eighth of the time.
C · Chirp. 0.06 s, 2,400 ↔ 3,600 Hz (a fifth). The quickest; up and down are harder to tell apart. Near pitch the tuner hears the string half the time.
For comparison only · Inside the strings. A's glide at 440 ↔ 550 Hz, where the strings ring: it sounds like a note of its own, and the tuner would read it.

The in-tune note

Played once when the string becomes in tune. The reviewer suggested middle C. It sits on the B string's first fret, so it clashes with whatever string rings, and a phone's small speaker plays it thinly. High C (three octaves up) is the same note, clear of every string and above what the tuner listens for.

Round 2: softer sounds

Round 1's glides at 2,000–3,600 Hz sounded sharp; that band is where the ear is most sensitive. These sit lower, at 700–1,300 Hz: still above a phone speaker's floor and, as steady notes, off every open string's pitch. The tuner sets its own sounds aside anyway, so they needn't stay above its 1,500 Hz top. Each is a WAV file made by scripts/guide-sounds.py, peak −12 dBFS, the same files the public page (website/pages/en/sound-guide.html) plays for the blind reviewer. Each demo is a string coming up from 40 cents flat at the vibration guide's pace, then the bell at 0.3 s (about 6.6 s; only the guide, no string). Every file starts after 0.6 s of silence, so a screen reader (TalkBack says “Pause” as Play is double-tapped) can finish speaking first; then a sharp or flat sound plays three times, one every 0.7 s (the guide's far-off pace), and an in-tune sound three times with 0.6 s of quiet after each; a demo plays once. The lengths below are one play's. Not yet heard by anyone: they were checked by their samples only. The Stop button above doesn't stop these; each player has its own.
