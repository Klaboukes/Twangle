# Blind and low-vision musicians: notes for posting

Phase 92, *Blind and low-vision musicians*. Notes for the owner, who submits and posts **in their own
words** and answers the replies. The ground rules, the order (one community a week or so) and *How to
answer questions* are in [communities.md](communities.md); this file adds what is different here.
Checked 2026-11-16.

**Why here:** a tuner that speaks well is rare, and blind players say the most about tuners: they
praise vibration and complain about noise (Phase 85's research). Their answers to the two questions
below shape the vibration guide and the high-contrast look.

**Tone:** practical and exact. Readers use screen readers, so: plain text, no emoji, no pictures
that carry meaning, links written out at the end, short paragraphs. Say what TalkBack actually says.
Be honest that you are sighted and tested it with TalkBack yourself; that is why you ask.

## What to say about Twangle (pick from it)
**TalkBack in the tuner**
- The reading in words: "E 2, 12 cents flat, tighten", "F sharp 2, in tune", "Play a string".
- It never floods you: a new note or "in tune" is said after 0.4 s, small changes in cents aren't
  said at all, so TalkBack isn't talking over the string.
- TalkBack's own voice isn't taken for a note: the microphone ignores what the screen reader says
  (and 0.4 s after), so a spoken reading can't set off the next one.
- The string buttons say "A 2", "selected" or "Locked"; hold one to hear its note ("Play A 2") and
  tune by ear. The instrument button says "Guitar, Standard" and opens the list of instruments and
  tunings.
- The strum check says how many strings are in tune ("4 of 6 in tune"); the pitch trail adds how the
  note runs ("E 2, 10 cents sharp, wavering").

**TalkBack in the tools**
- Metronome: the tempo ruler is a slider ("Tempo", one beat a minute a step), the meters in words
  ("Four-four time"), "Vibrate on the beat" a switch; Listen suggests your tempo.
- Chords: the chart is read string by string, from the thickest: "A 2 string, fret 3, finger 3, C".
  Scales: the neck the same way, every note named.
- Ear training: the answer buttons in words ("minor seventh", "5th").
- The metronome and the Practice tools work without the microphone; the permission screen says so.
- On the watch: the dial in words, "A 2, 12 cents flat, Tighten".

**The vibration guide** (Settings → *While tuning* → *Vibration guide*; on by itself while TalkBack is
on): while the tuner hears a string the phone taps, **one tap to tighten, two to loosen**, quicker as
the string gets close (from about every 0.7 s to every 0.3 s), and **one long buzz when it's in
tune**. The taps make no sound, so the tuner isn't disturbed; best felt with
the phone on your lap or in a pocket. The watch does the same on the wrist, with stronger, slower
taps. Explained on the website: https://klaboukes.github.io/help.html#without-looking

**The high-contrast look** (Settings → *Look* → *High contrast*; on by itself when the phone's contrast
is set high, Android 14 and newer, or its high-contrast text is on): pure black and white with thicker lines, every text and
every line that means something at least 7:1, white on black or black on white with the phone's light
or dark. Every screen also works with text up to twice the normal size.

**The rest:** every feature free, no ads, no purchases, no account, no internet permission; 36
languages; guitar, bass, ukulele, violin, viola, cello, mandolin, banjo, chromatic. And the line on
how it's made: the code is written with Claude (an AI), to your design, and you test it on real
phones and a watch.

## The two questions (in every post)
Ask them plainly; what people answer goes to Claude, and adjusts the vibration guide and the look or
goes into [IDEAS.md](../../IDEAS.md).
1. **While you tune, what works best for you: speech, vibration, or sounds like ticks?** (Sounds are
   the hard one: the microphone hears them, so say whether you'd use them with earbuds.)
2. **For low-vision players: what does the high-contrast look lack?** Something too thin or small,
   or a place where you can't tell what's picked. (One known spot: the picked chord root in Practice
   is a grey fill with a thicker edge, not white.)

## Accessible Android (accessibleandroid.com): week 1
- **What it is:** a site of blind and visually impaired Android users with an app directory (picked by
  its editors), articles, reviews and Telegram and WhatsApp groups.
- **How to submit (read 2026-11-16):** *Suggest an app*, https://accessibleandroid.com/suggest-an-app/
  . **Developers may submit their own apps**; the site says it helps promote the app and its
  accessibility. An account is optional (it credits you as the author; use the same email). The form
  asks: your name and email, the app's name, a category (pick the closest, or *Not sure*), the Play
  link, a description, **accessibility and user comments** (required: is it usable, which screen
  reader you tested with, any known issues), the last tested app version (Settings → About) and
  Android version, and *Free*.
- **What to write in it (say it your way):** the description from the facts above, short; in the
  comments, honestly: you are the developer and sighted, you tested it with TalkBack yourself (name
  the phones and Android versions you used), the known spot above, and the two questions, so their reviewers know you want to hear back.
- **Opening of the description (say it your way):**
  > Twangle is a free guitar tuner (also bass, ukulele, violin and more) that speaks with TalkBack and
  > can guide you by vibration alone: one tap to tighten, two to loosen, a long buzz when you're in
  > tune. I'm the developer. The code is written with Claude, an AI, to my design, and I test it
  > with TalkBack on real phones.
- **Their groups:** fine to join and answer when someone asks about tuners; don't post an advert
  there unless the group's rules allow it.
- **Submitted 2026-11-16** by the owner through *Suggest an app*: category *Music and Audio apps*,
  Twangle 2.2.0, the description and accessibility comments from the facts above (with the known
  chord-root spot and the two questions), tested with TalkBack on the owner's phones. The watch app
  was left out (still in Google's review). Their editors review by hand; what they or their readers
  answer goes to Claude. Once the watch app is on Play, a line on its vibration guide is worth
  sending them.
- **Reviewed** (shared by the owner 2026-11-23): a blind guitarist's review on the site, warm. What
  works for them: the 12th-fret check (setting up their guitar's intonation without sighted help),
  clear labels everywhere, "Locked" on the string button telling them where they are, the
  metronome's meters and tap tempo, the accuracy. Their answer to question 1 is **sounds**: a tone
  gliding up for sharp and down for flat, a steady note in tune for a length they set; failing
  that, TalkBack saying tighten, loosen and in tune quicker. They felt the taps only with the phone
  laid against the guitar. One wish more: the guitar on a 6.35 mm to USB-C cable with the phone's
  sounds still on the phone. All three are in [IDEAS.md](../../IDEAS.md): *A sound guide*, *The
  word first*, *A guitar on a cable*. Worth asking back: were the taps too weak with the phone on
  the lap or in a pocket, and would the sounds come from the phone's speaker or earbuds.

## Blind Android Users: week 4
- **What it is:** a long-running podcast for blind and low-vision Android users, with a mailing list
  (https://groups.io/g/blindandroidusers), a Telegram group and a Facebook group.
- **The podcast:** its contact page (https://www.blindandroidusers.com/contact/) invites developers
  of apps for the visually impaired or of accessible mainstream apps to come on an episode and talk
  about their app. A short note there: what Twangle is, the vibration guide, that you're the
  developer, how it's made, that you'd be glad to come on or just send it for them to try.
- **The mailing list:** its rules couldn't be read from here; after joining, read the group's
  guidelines and a few days of posts before one short post with the two questions.
- **Opening (say it your way):**
  > Hi all, I'm the developer of Twangle, a free Android tuner for guitar, bass, ukulele and more. It
  > reads the note through TalkBack without talking over the string, and has a vibration guide: one
  > tap to tighten, two to loosen, a long buzz when in tune. I'm sighted and tested it with TalkBack
  > myself, so I'd really like to hear from people who use it every day. (The code is written with
  > Claude, an AI, to my design.)
