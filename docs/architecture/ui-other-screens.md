## Metronome screen
`:feature:metronome`, after 2.0's first mock-up [docs/design/fresh-start-preview.html](../design/fresh-start-preview.html), *Metronome*, in Ink (Phase 75): **the tempo is the screen.** It is the Metronome tab ([UI basics](ui.md), *Navigation bar*; switching tabs stops it). 1.1's screen round the tempo dial (Phase 19 to Phase 75) is in [history](history.md), *Metronome, widget and watch*.
- **Layout, upright** (one column, at most 480 dp, `FitColumn`; with very large fonts it scrolls): the top row, every tab's `TopBar` ([UI basics](ui.md), *One top row*): the app's gear at the start (`header`, handed down by `AppNavigationFrame`), the tab's name in the middle (`title`, the bar's "Metronome") and the options button at the end (`MoreButton`, ⋯, the gear's 44 dp face; three sliders on a 52 dp face until 2026-11-04); the tempo (`TempoHeading`, the column's one shrinking part, so it takes the height the rest leave); the ruler 8 dp under it (`TempoRuler`); the beat lights (48 dp, at most 360 dp wide) with Listen's status line under them; the meter and the sound as segmented choices 8 dp apart; Tap the beat and Start side by side; the speed trainer's row 12 dp under them, 12 dp from the bottom. Three gaps share what is left: over the tempo, over the choices and over the buttons. Fits a 360 × 570 dp tab at 1× and the Xiaomi at 1.25× without scrolling (`MetronomeStylesTest`).
- **On its side** (from 600 dp wide, wider than tall): two halves 24 dp apart, 16 dp from the window's sides (24 until 2026-11-04), each a `FitColumn` that scrolls if it must: the start half holds the gear (when the screen has it; the app puts it on the rail there), the tempo, the ruler, the beat lights and the status line; the end half the options button at its top end (its outline 16 dp from the window's side, as the tuner's focus mode button there; no name, the rail names the tab), the two choices, the big buttons and the trainer's row.
- **Upright on a tablet (Phase 52's rule):** the column is laid out as on a phone of the window's size divided by `columnScaleFor` and drawn that much bigger (`ScaledUp`), and the panels and the suggestion with it; the gear keeps its own size at the start of a phone's 480 dp column, 8 dp down, over the column.
- **The tempo** (`TempoHeading`, `TempoParts.kt`): the figures in the phone's own face, extra bold, a little tighter (−0.04 em), tabular, as high as the room allows from 56 to 120 dp and as wide as the row leaves between − and + (sized by the widest tempo, 250, so they keep one size at every tempo); they do not grow with the font size, like the tuner's note. − and + either side (`StepperButton`, 56 dp faces with 28 dp icons, 8 dp from the figures' room), one BPM a tap, slower on the left in every language (the row is laid out left to right). Under them, muted, "beats per minute · Allegro" (`metronome_tempo_line` with the Italian marking, `TempoMarking`, `markingName`; Dutch "slagen per minuut"), 16 dp growing with the font up to 1.5×, one line, set smaller where it must. The figures and words are hidden from TalkBack: the ruler says the tempo. While a tempo Listen found is used, one soft accent glow lights behind the figures (in 300 ms, held 400 ms, out 900 ms) and they glide there (`TempoMotion`).
- **The ruler** (`TempoRuler`, in the look of the tuner's ruler): 64 dp high across the column, a drawing whose sizes don't follow the font size. One mark per BPM 10 dp apart (12 dp long, in `outlineVariant`), every 5th 20 dp and every 10th 26 dp in the muted colour with its number over it (12 dp, the app's digits), standing on a line 54 dp down; the scale ends at 30 and 250, and marks and numbers fade out over 40 dp at each end. A fixed cursor in the middle (3 dp, the accent, from under the numbers to the line) marks the tempo, with a soft glow 10 dp either side that brightens on each beat (`BeatClock.glow`); a number passing under the cursor fades to 25 %. A drag slides the scale with the finger, a whole BPM at a time (left for faster, as a ruler slides; the touch slop first, as Compose's `draggable`), saved when the finger lifts; a flick carries on, slowing (`FloatExponentialDecaySpec`, friction 1.5), stops at 30 or 250 or settles on a whole tempo with a soft spring, and then it is saved; a new touch catches a flick. A tap on a mark jumps to it. With "Remove animations" a flick stops where the finger lifts. While Listen listens a small hollow ring (5 dp, the accent) over the scale marks the tempo it settles on; while the speed trainer is on a small accent dot under the line marks its goal. TalkBack: one slider "Tempo", "96 beats per minute, Andante", 30–250 in 1 BPM steps (`progressBarRangeInfo` + `setProgress`), which TalkBack's own gestures move. Drawing allocates nothing (the numbers are measured once).
- **Beat lights** (`BeatLightsArt` in `:core:designsystem` `metronome/`, drawn for a 48 dp row and in proportion past it; the mock-up's `.beats`): a round light per beat of the bar, beat 1 the largest (26 dp), group starts (`BeatAccent.GROUP`) middle sized and plain beats smallest (`TIER_SCALES` 1, 0.86, 0.73: the mock-up's 30 and 22), at most 48 dp apart; on a narrow row, and with clicks between the beats, the lights shrink to keep at least 6 dp from each other and from the small dots. Ink's ink at rest (20 % light, 26 % dark, a little more for the larger tiers), the beat heard in the accent (never in-tune green) with a soft glow, easing (200 ms) to a resting 40 % until the next. No beat numbers since Phase 75 (1.1's bars with numbers are history). **The clicks between the beats** (Phase 62): with a subdivision picked (`Subdivision.within(meter)`, shown stopped too) a small dot (5 dp) stands for each click between the beats, evenly in the gaps at x(i) + j · spacing / k, the last beat's to its right, at 65 % of a plain beat's resting strength; `beatSpacing` sets the beats closer only where the last beat's dots wouldn't fit inside the row's 8 dp end (`BeatSpacingTest`). Each lights as its click sounds, blending to the accent at 70 % with no glow and fading back (70 ms decay, `BetweenLights.kt`), worked out in the draw phase from the beat's `heardAtNanos`, `bpm` and `clicksPerBeat`; clicks closer than 75 ms (`MIN_BETWEEN_LIGHT_MILLIS`) leave the dots unlit. Hidden from TalkBack.
- **The choices** (`MeterChoice`, `SoundChoice`, `MetronomeControls.kt`): the tuner's `SegmentedSwitch` across the column (at most 400 dp), the picked segment filled in the accent's container colour. The meter: 2/4 3/4 4/4 5/4 6/8 7/8 in the app's digits, TalkBack radio buttons by their spoken names ("Six-eight time", Dutch "Zesachtstemaat", as it would read "3/4" as a date) in a group "Beats in a bar". The sound: the four click sounds as icons (the switch's `icon`, 24 dp; wood block, cowbell, claves, rim click), radio buttons by their names in a group "Sound"; a pick while stopped plays one bar of it, while playing it is heard from the next beat (`MetronomeViewModel.previewSound`). The mock-up's fourth sound, Vibrate, stays a switch of its own in the options panel, as vibration goes with any sound.
- **Tap the beat and Start** (`BigButtons`): side by side across the column (at most 400 dp, 12 dp apart), 64 dp tall whatever the language or font size (the mock-up's 72, lower so a short phone fits), pill shaped (`styleButtonFace`). Tap the beat (`ic_tap` and its words, see-through with the muted outline) counts the moment the finger touches, with its crisp flash ([Strum check and metronome](strum-and-metronome.md), *Metronome*); TalkBack's double tap counts too. Start (1.3 times as wide, the accent, a play or stop icon and "Start" / "Stop") rests at 38 % and takes no taps while Listen listens. Their words are one line, 18 sp bold, set smaller where they must (down to 8 dp).
- **The options panel** (the shared `FloatingPanel` in `:core:designsystem` since Phase 77, the metronome's own in `OptionsParts.kt` until then; the options button's, Phase 62): on its own layer over the screen, `surfaceContainerHigh`, `shapes.large`, a 12 dp lift, no border, 280 dp wide (less on a narrow window), under the button with its end edge in line with the button's (over it for an anchor in the window's lower half; where the room is short it slides along to stay inside the window, scrolling only if the window is shorter than it). Rows: **Between the beats** (`SubdivisionChoice`, under the shared small label `PanelLabel`: none, eighths, triplets, sixteenths as note-value icons; in 6/8 and 7/8 only none and two to a beat, named and drawn as sixteenths, the others dimmed in place and the choice kept for the next meter that offers it), **Vibrate on the beat** (the shared `SwitchRow`; the motor directly, as `InTuneHaptic`: `EFFECT_HEAVY_CLICK` on the downbeat, `EFFECT_CLICK` otherwise, 35 / 18 ms one-shots before Android 10) and **Listen** (below). Back or a tap beside it closes it, and the tap does nothing else; TalkBack hears its pane title "Options". What is on in it shows as small icons on the status line (`StatusMark`: the note value playing between the beats, then vibration), heard by name.
- **The speed trainer** (Phase 62, behind `ToolSwitches.speedTrainer`, its brake; without it nothing of it shows): a row at the foot of the screen, as the mock-up's `.trainer` (`TrainerRow`, `TrainerParts.kt`): a card (the surface at 60 % with a thin `outlineVariant` edge, `shapes.large`, at least 56 dp tall, at most 400 dp wide) with its rising-line icon (the accent while on), "Speed trainer" bold over its words in the muted colour, and its switch at the end. Its words say its plan while stopped or off ("80 → 120 · +5 every 4 bars"), where it stands while it climbs ("95 → 120 · +5 in 2 bars") and "At the goal · 120 BPM" (`trainerStatus`); TalkBack hears the row as a button with them ("Speed trainer, from 80 to 120 BPM, +5 every 4 bars") and the switch as "Speed trainer". Not a live region: it never talks over the clicks. A tap on the row opens its settings (`TrainerSettings`) in a floating panel of the same kind over the row (TalkBack "Speed trainer"): its name and switch, the start and goal tempos with − and + 5 apart (the goal kept at least 5 above the start), the step (+1 +2 +5 +10) and the bars (1 2 4 8); Back or a tap beside it closes it (1.1's ‹ back to the options went with the trainer's place in the options panel, and its text `metronome_trainer_back`). Turning it on the first time opens the settings, already filled in from the tempo. Until Phase 75 its words stood on the status line and its goal was a ring on the dial.
- **Listen:** after the approved mock-up [docs/design/metronome-listen-preview.html](../design/metronome-listen-preview.html) (owner decided 2026-10-15; its top comment is the spec). Listen is the options panel's last row (since Phase 75). A tap on it closes the panel and asks for the microphone if needed ([Lifecycle](lifecycle.md)), then `MetronomeViewModel.listen` pauses the clicks (the microphone would hear them) and feeds `OnsetTempoDetector` ([Strum check and metronome](strum-and-metronome.md), *Tempo from music*) on `Dispatchers.Default` (`hear`, `withTimeoutOrNull(15 s)` round the recorder's chunks). Listening ends by itself once the detector is `STEADY`, after 15 s (a tempo if `SETTLING`, else "no beat"), or on Stop, another control (the player took over: − + Tap the beat, the meter, the ruler, Vibrate), leaving the screen. While it listens: the panel's Listen row shows a stop square in the accent and "Stop listening" (1.1's trace round a Listen button is history); Start / Stop rests at 38 % and takes no taps; the status line under the beat lights says "Listening…", then "Listening… 96 BPM" once the detector has a tempo; a small hollow ring over the ruler marks that tempo (fading in 400 ms, gliding 500 ms); the beat lights follow the heard beats (the detector's beat grid moved to the `System.nanoTime()` clock by the earliest chunk arrival, `HeardBeats`) at a softer level (0.25 + 0.45). **Found:** a balloon over the screen pointing at the options button, where Listen is (`ListenBalloon`, under the button since Phase 75, as the button is at the top, drawn as 1.1's tour balloon was: accent fill, soft lift, Futuristic glow and cut corners, at most 260 dp), with the style's mark and "Heard", the tempo in the number face with BPM and its marking, ½× 1× 2× (only those within 30–250), Not now and Use. Nothing changes until Use: then the ruler and the tempo's figures glide there in 700 ms with one soft accent glow behind the figures, and the clicks come back after the glide only if they played before Listen. Not now, Back or another control closes it and brings the clicks back if they played. **No beat:** "No steady beat heard  Try again" for 8 s; the tempo stays. **Microphone busy:** a recorder that fails to open or stops (a call or another app has it; on Android 8–9 often the tuner not quite done with it) says "Microphone is busy  Try again" for 8 s instead (`ListenPhase.MIC_BUSY`, Phase 65), or "Microphone is off" when the permission went missing. The status line keeps its 24 dp (one line at larger fonts) so nothing moves, crossfades its changes (160 ms out, 160 ms in; a new tempo while listening changes in place, `contentKey`) and is a polite live region that holds its text (merged, the words fading out left unheard), so TalkBack says each change; while listening it says paced words instead (`pacedSpeech`, a fresh pace per listening: "Listening…" at once, the first tempo after 400 ms, a tempo still settling at most every 2 s), with the shown words hidden but still there for the language checks; its action is at least 24 dp tall, as tall as the line, with Compose's 48 dp touch area. Words and action keep their size while they fit side by side; where they don't (large text, long languages) the action takes at most 45 % of the line and each shrinks to stay on one line (the words down to 6 sp, which Dutch at 2× text on a 360 dp phone needs).
- **The beat** (`BeatClock`): `MetronomePlayer.beats` gives each click with the moment it is heard; a frame loop (`withInfiniteAnimationFrameNanos`, only while playing or listening, skipped by UI tests) moves only the lights and the ruler cursor's glow (220 ms decay, full on a downbeat, 75 % on a group start, 55 % otherwise), read in the draw phase, so playing never recomposes. Changes are heard from the next beat (≈ 0.12 s).
- **Press effect** (`Modifier.pressFeedback` in `:core:designsystem`, `PressFeedback.kt`): every metronome button lights up instead of showing Material's ripple (`indication = null` on its `clickable`, `selectable` or `toggleable`, the same `MutableInteractionSource` given to the modifier). The modifier draws the button's face itself (fill, the light, outline, then the content), so the light lies above the background and under the text, and scales the whole drawing for the sink (never the layout). Level: 1 from the frame of touch-down, held while pressed, then (1 − t/280 ms)³; sink 1 − sink × s (s smoothed over 35 ms; 0.03, Ancient and Retro 0.02, Classic and the vibrate row 0.015). The light per style: Contemporary and Space Age the accent at 0.20; Ancient a radial smudge of the ink mixed 45 % into the accent at 0.24; Classic a shadow from the top over the accent at 12 %; Retro a 9 dp shadow from the top (the pressed-in push button) over the accent at 10 %; Futuristic the accent at 14 % with the outline lit and an 8 dp accent glow (Android 9+). `PressTone` picks flat colours on accent fills (Start / Stop: on-accent at 0.20; in the balloon on-accent, on its filled buttons the accent). Text buttons and the vibrate row light a 32 dp pill (`lightShape`, `lightInset`). Tap tempo uses `crisp = true`: full for 70 ms from touch-down whatever the finger does, a 40 ms linear fade, no sink. "Remove animations": no sink or fade, lit while held (at least 100 ms; crisp: 70 ms). TalkBack's double tap sends no touch, so each button calls `MutableInteractionSource.pressFlash()` in its click (ignored within a second of a real touch). `PressFeedbackTest` (JVM) checks the timing. The rest of the app has the same effect ([Meter, wave and styles](ui-meter-and-styles.md), *Visual styles*, **Press effect everywhere**).
- **State** (`MetronomeViewModel`, unchanged by Phase 75): tempo, meter, vibration, the click's sound and the clicks between the beats, each saved at once (a ruler drag when the finger lifts or its flick stops); the speed trainer's plan and switch; opens stopped at the saved values (passed in from the settings already read, so the first frame and the first beat are right). `previewSound` and `setSubdivision` while stopped play one bar once at the tempo and meter shown (`isPreviewing` meanwhile, `isPlaying` stays false); the next pick, Start, Listen or leaving the screen ends it. It stops the clicks when cleared; the screen stops them when it leaves ([Lifecycle](lifecycle.md)), but not for a configuration change (the activity's ViewModel and the app's player live on, so the clicks play on and the new screen shows them). The options panel's and the trainer panel's open state survive a turn of the phone (`rememberSaveable`).
- **Tests (Phase 75):** `MetronomeScreenTest` checks: opens stopped at the saved tempo and meter with its words; Start / Stop, − + and the meter reach the player and are saved; a drag on the ruler slides the tempo both ways and saves it on lift, a flick carries it on to a whole tempo, a tap on a mark jumps to it, TalkBack's slider steps one BPM; every control heard by name (Tap the beat, the options button as a drop-down list, the meters and sounds as named radio buttons); the screen reads top to bottom as the mock-up; the options panel under its button with the note values, the switch and Listen, the vibration and note-value marks on the status line, 6/8 dimming triplets and sixteenths; − left of + in Arabic while the top row and the big buttons mirror; Listen, its suggestion (under the options button) and its status line. `MetronomeStylesTest` (light and dark): every part inside the window and nothing overlapping upright on a 360 × 570 dp tab at 1×, the Xiaomi at 1.25× and on its side, with the gear and without; ## Practice tab
Phase 32, after the approved rough mock-up [docs/design/practice-preview.html](../design/practice-preview.html) (its top comment is the spec). **Owner picked (2026-10-16): A, D, G, I.** Since 2.0 (Phase 69) a tab of its own beside the metronome's (the bar: Tune · Metronome · Practice, [UI basics](ui.md), *Navigation bar*), holding, each with its own switch (`ToolSwitches.chords`, `scales`, `capoHelper` and `earTraining`), Chords, Scales (a tool of its own since Phase 47), the Capo helper (Phase 33) and Ear training (Phase 55), shown as four big cards that each open their tool under the bar (Phase 69's third step, below; full screen until 2026-11-03); with its switch (Phase 28, `ToolSwitches.practiceTab`) off, or every tool's, there is no Practice tab, the metronome keeps its own, and nothing about Practice is shown or saved. From Phase 32 to 68 the metronome was its first tool and the tab was the Metronome tab renamed, and until Phase 69's third step a tool button at the top of each tool picked the tool ([history](history.md)).
- **Where it lives:** `:feature:practice` (decided 2026-10-17) holds the cards and the tools that don't listen, Chords, Scales and the Capo helper, so `:feature:metronome` stays about the metronome and its microphone. **Sound (Phase 48, decided 2026-10-26):** for the drone under Scales it depends on `:core:audio`, as `:feature:metronome` does, but uses only the `DronePlayer` interface, playback: it never opens the microphone (the app's `AppContainer.dronePlayer`, the real `AndroidDronePlayer`, handed to the Scales screen by `MainActivity.PracticeToolScreen` with the A4 setting and the saved drone choices, as the metronome gets its player; tests use `FakeDronePlayer` from `:core:audio`'s test fixtures; see *Drone* under *Scales*). Depending on the module rather than having `:app` hand over lambdas keeps the screen's own tests on the same interface the app uses. `:feature:*` modules never depend on each other, so `:app` puts them together: the cards (`PracticeCards`) under every tab's top row, the app's gear upright at its start and the title in its middle (`settingsButton`, [UI basics](ui.md), *The gear*), and each tool as a screen of its own (`Screen.PRACTICE_CHORDS`, `PRACTICE_SCALES`, `PRACTICE_CAPO`, `PRACTICE_EAR_TRAINING`; `MainActivity.PracticeToolScreen`), which it opens from a card or the "last time" row, saves as the tool used last and hands its back arrow (`onBack`). `practiceToolsFor(tools)` gives the tools offered (none with the tab's switch off; each tool with its own switch on, the Capo helper only with Chords too, whose charts its rows open), `lastTimeTool` the "last time" row's (the saved one while offered, else none, and no row).
- **Cards (Phase 69's third step, 2.0's first mock-up [docs/design/fresh-start-preview.html](../design/fresh-start-preview.html), *Practice*, in Ink):** `PracticeCards`. The page's top row is the gear (upright; on its side it is at the rail's top) and the title "Practice" (`nav_practice`, the tab's own word, 22 sp bold, a heading for TalkBack), 8 dp down as the tools' rows; under it one big card per tool offered (each tool's switch hides its card, `practiceToolsFor`), in the tools' order: two to a row upright (at most 480 dp wide on a phone, 600 dp on a tablet), all four in one row on its side (a tablet's at most 960 dp), each the surface with a 1 dp line round it and 22 dp corners, its picture at the top in the accent (`practiceToolPicture`, half the card's width within 64 to 120 dp, so 86 dp on the Xiaomi and 120 dp on a tablet, 56 dp on a phone on its side, where height is short; owner, 2026-11-03: bigger, after 88 dp and 64 dp on a card under 190 dp wide, which most phones got: the mock-up's chord box under a thick nut with three fingers, the neck lying down with the scale's dots, an ear with two waves; for Capo the Capo icon's nut, five strings and a solid bar across them drawn large, not the mock-up's bar with a lever, which is how 1.1's icon read as a chair), its name (19 sp bold) and a line saying what it does at the bottom (13.5 sp, muted: "Every shape, left-handed too", "With a drone to play along", "Which fret for the key you want", "Hear a note, play it back"). Each card is at least 1.08 times as tall as wide upright (0.9 on its side), so a row is one height at the usual text size, and grows where large text needs it; where a card's line would hold fewer than about seven letters (large text on a narrow window) there are fewer to a row, two on its side and one upright, and the name is set smaller on its one line and the line keeps its words whole (`WholeWordsAutoSize`, a break allowed after a hyphen); a phone on its side closes up the gaps (12 dp) and the cards' insides, so the page fits a 330 dp-high window without scrolling at the usual text size; the page scrolls where even that doesn't fit (2× text). A tap on a card opens its tool **under the bar** (or beside the rail), the Practice tab still the open one (`Screen.destination`: every tool is `Destination.PRACTICE`; owner, 2026-11-03: the player goes back and forth between a chord chart, the tuner and the metronome, and Android's own guidance keeps the bar on the screens a player works in, hiding it only for short focused ones like Settings; from Phase 69 until then the tools were full screen, [history](history.md)). The tool's top row has a **back arrow** in the top start corner where the cards have the gear (`PracticeBackButton`, the gear's 44 dp face, `ic_arrow_back`, turned round in right-to-left languages, TalkBack "Back"), and the arrow and Back lead back to the cards (`Screen.back`; Chords opened from a row of the capo helper's table back to the capo helper). **A tap on the open Practice tab** in a tool leads to the cards too (`Screen.reselected`; from Chords opened from the capo helper as well), and **the tab opened from another tab** comes back to the screen it was left on, the cards or a tool with its state (`Destination.screenFrom(practiceLeftOn)`, `MainActivity`'s `practiceLeftOn`, kept over a rotation but not a fresh start), as Android's tabs keep their place. The tools themselves show no gear upright (Claude's pick: one corner, one button; Settings is a back arrow and a tap away); on its side the rail's gear is there as on every tab, and Settings opened from it goes back to the tool (`settingsOpener` is the screen, not the tab). Under the cards the **"last time" row** (`LastTimeRow`) reopens the tool used last: the tool's small icon (`practiceToolIcon`) in the accent, "Last time" (muted) over the tool's name and what was picked in it ("Chords · A minor", "Scales · A minor pentatonic"; the capo helper and Ear training by name), and › at the end; with nothing used yet (a fresh install, 1.1's metronome) or the tool's switch now off there is no row (`lastTimeTool`). TalkBack hears each card as one button with its name and line ("Chords, Every shape, left-handed too") and the row as one button ("Last time, Scales, A minor pentatonic"). The 12th-fret check has no row here: it stays on the tuner ([v2-plan](v2-plan.md), *The two tools*). The tools' small icons (`practiceToolIcon`, from 1.1's tool button) are 1.8 dp round lines on a 24 dp grid: a chord box (four strings under the nut, two frets, two fingers), a staircase for Scales, a capo across four strings, an ear. A new tool is a new `PracticeTool` entry with its name, card line, icon and picture (the `when`s say so), its place in `practiceToolsFor` and its `Screen`. **Five cards (Phase 78):** upright the fifth, alone in its row, is a wide, low card across it (its picture, 56 dp, beside its name and line); on an upright window under 640 dp (the S26) the grid's cards are 0.95 as tall as wide with their pictures at 64 dp, and the wide card's picture 40 dp; on its side all five share one row. `PracticeCardsTest` checks it fits without scrolling on the S26, the Xiaomi, a phone on its side and the tablet both ways.
- **Saved, and each tool's own state:** a card or the row saves the tool at once (`SettingsRepository.setPracticeTool`, key `practice_tool`, [Lifecycle](lifecycle.md), *Saved settings*), for the "last time" row; the tab never opens on a tool by itself: on a fresh start its cards, later the cards or the tool it was left on in this visit (above). Each tool keeps its own saved UI state while another shows (`MainActivity`'s `SaveableStateHolder`, by screen), but Chords and Scales are one screen (`ChordsScreen(scales = …)`) under one saved state, so the root picked in one is the other's too. The metronome's tab of its own stops its clicks when left, so they never sound under the chord charts.
- **Chords (built 2026-10-17; calmer pickers 2026-10-18; one name button 2026-10-19; short name 2026-10-20; as wide as the row 2026-10-26):** `ChordsScreen`. Upright one column (at most 480 dp): the back arrow (the tool button until Phase 69) and the tool's name, "Chords" or "Scales", in the middle of the row (`BackAndTuningRow(tool)`, every tab's top row, [UI basics](ui.md), *One top row*; Phase 77's fifth step, the owner's "each screen should just have the screen's name there", 2026-11-04), and on its own centred line right under it the picker chip (`PickerLine`, where the tuner has its chip; in the row's middle from 2026-11-04's first top rows until then, beside the arrow before that; `TuningLine`; Phase 70, the tuner's `PickerChip`, [Tuner screen](ui-tuner.md), *Picker chip*; until then a headstock button and the name in the muted colour): "Guitar · DADGAD ▾", a custom tuning by its own name, on one line ending in "…" where it is longer. A tap opens the sheet "What are you tuning?" over the tool (`MainActivity`'s `PracticeTuningPanel`, the tuner's own sheet without its Any note switch, since the charts need an instrument; saving as the tuner does and switching the tuner to guitar mode); its New tuning and Edit open the tuning editor, which goes back to Practice. ## Home-screen widget
`:app` `widget/`. A tile for the home screen, three cells by one (resizable across, down to two), the top of the tuner in small (2.0, Phase 75): the instrument and tuning as the tuner names them ("Guitar · Standard", a custom tuning by its own name, a lowered one as "Guitar · Drop C", "Any note" in Any note) in a chip like the tuner's picker chip (a pill in the surface colour with a fine line round it in the outline colour, the name in ink, 14 sp medium, ending in "…" when it doesn't fit; no ▾, since the widget opens the tuner, not the picker), and "Open the tuner" ("Open de stemmer") under it in the quiet face of the tuner's word ("Play a string": medium, muted), both centred on a rounded tile in the app's background colour (corners: the launcher's own on Android 12+, 16 dp before). No border round the tile, no art: calm beside other widgets. A tap anywhere opens Twangle on the tuner. (Until Phase 75 the navigation bar's tuning fork stood in the accent at its start, the two lines beside it.)
- **Tech: plain `RemoteViews`** (`layout/widget_tuning.xml`, an `AppWidgetProvider`), not Jetpack Glance. The widget is one static layout with three values to fill in; Glance would add a library, a second Compose runtime for widgets and a WorkManager-backed session for no gain here. The chip is two tinted images under the name in a `FrameLayout` (its line, then its fill 1 dp inside it, `drawable/widget_chip_line.xml` and `widget_chip.xml`), as `RemoteViews` can't tint a shape's fill and stroke apart. Text sizes come from the layout, the face is the system's medium sans: `RemoteViews` can't take the styles' bundled faces without a layout per style, and the colours already carry the style.
- **Colours** (`TuningWidget.paint`): Ink's scheme as the app ends up with it (`colorSchemeFor` in `:core:designsystem`: the wallpaper's colours on Android 12+ with that setting on, as in the app; in 1.1 only Contemporary's): background on the tile, `outlineVariant` on the chip's line, `surface` on its fill, `onSurface` (ink) on the name and `onSurfaceVariant` (muted) under it, set as colour filters on the images and as text colours. With the theme on "System" on Android 12+ both sets go in (`setColorInt` light/night), so the launcher follows the phone's dark mode without a redraw; otherwise the set for the current brightness. The picker's preview (Android 12+, `previewLayout`) is the layout itself in Ink light or dark (`values/widget.xml`, `values-night/widget.xml`).
- **Kept up to date by the app, never on a timer** (`updatePeriodMillis` 0): `TwangleApplication` follows the settings (`TuningWidget.follow`) for as long as its process lives and redraws the widgets when what they show changes (`WidgetState`: mode, instrument, tuning, custom name, style, theme, wallpaper colours; A4 or the meter's look change nothing), and when the configuration changes (language, dark mode before Android 12). `TuningWidgetReceiver` draws a widget when it is placed and redraws on the phone's `LOCALE_CHANGED`, reading the settings off the main thread (`goAsync`). With no widget placed, a redraw is one `getAppWidgetIds` call. A redraw that fails (the launcher gone, a drawing too big to send) is logged, never a crash: `follow` catches it and goes on with the next change, and both the app's scope and the receiver's log any exception that escapes them (`loggingScope`, a `CoroutineExceptionHandler`; Phase 65, `TuningWidgetFollowTest`).
- **One tap opens the tuner** (`TuningWidget.openTuner`): an immutable `PendingIntent` to `MainActivity` with `ACTION_OPEN_TUNER` and `NEW_TASK | CLEAR_TOP | SINGLE_TOP`. A cold start opens on the tuner anyway; an open app gets `onNewIntent`, which counts a request, and `TwangleApp` switches to the tuner (an open tuning editor closes as with Back, dropping its draft).
- **Never the microphone:** the widget and its receiver touch only the settings. The mic opens only when the tuner it opens is shown, exactly as from the launcher ([Lifecycle](lifecycle.md)), and the tuner asks for the permission there.
- **TalkBack:** the tile is one button; the title reads as the tuner's button does ("Guitar, Standard"), then "Open the tuner".
- **Tests:** `WidgetStateTest` (JVM: names, custom tuning, chromatic, which changes redraw); `TuningWidgetTest` (device: the views applied as a launcher does show the names in the chip and "Open the tuner" under it, in Ink's colours (the tile, the chip's fill and line, the texts), dark and light, and are tappable; the picker's preview colours are Ink's; drawing them records nothing; the tap's intent brings the open app from Settings back to the tuner).

## Watch app (Wear OS)
`:wear`. A standalone Wear OS app (`com.google.android.wearable.standalone`): it listens with the watch's own microphone and needs nothing from the phone. Compose for Wear OS (`androidx.wear.compose` Material 3, `AppScaffold` with the time at the top), minSdk 30 (Wear OS 3).
- **Same application ID as the phone** (`io.github.klaboukes.twangle`), as Wear OS and Play expect for a phone app and its watch app; namespace `…twangle.wear`. **`versionCode` = the phone's + 1,000,000** (1,010,000 for 1.0.0), because Play needs a different code for each build; both come from `twangle.version` ([Threading and performance](performance.md), *Release build*). The release build is signed with the phone's upload key. The launcher icon is the phone's adaptive icon (copied into `wear/src/main/res`).
- **The pitch engine is the phone's**: `WatchPitchPipeline` runs the phone's own frame chain, `:core:dsp`'s `PitchChain` in the standard range (`FrameAssembler` → `SignalGate` → `SustainCheck` → YIN → noise floor; [Signal pipeline](signal-pipeline.md), *One frame chain*), then `PitchSmoother` → `InTuneDetector` (the phone's default window: within 5 cents for 300 ms, released past 7), with frame sizes from the recorder's rate, so a 16 kHz watch mic gets the same window and hop as 48 kHz. On a watch-class core it costs about 13–20 % of the CPU ([Threading and performance](performance.md), *Watch CPU and the native detector*). It leaves out the phone's extras (SNR, reception, tips, strum check, low-note window, precision setting): chromatic, A4 = 440 Hz, the note names in the watch language's usual notation (`Notation.forLanguage`: A♯, La♯ or H; no notation setting). It allocates nothing per frame and sends a new `WatchTunerState` (`Listening`, `Detected(note, cents, inTune)`, `Failed`) only when the note, the whole cents or "in tune" change. A note lost for a moment stays on screen 0.6 s beyond the smoother's own hold (as long as the phone's glow bridges a dropout) and keeps "in tune" if the same note comes back, so the watch never flickers between a reading and "Play a note".
- **The microphone is `:core:audio`'s `AndroidAudioRecorder`**, unchanged: unprocessed source, then voice recognition, the watch's native rate, the built-in mic while a Bluetooth headset is connected.
- **Microphone lifetime** as on the phone: `WatchTunerViewModel.state` is `stateIn(WhileSubscribed(5_000))` and the screen collects it with `collectAsStateWithLifecycle()`, only once `RECORD_AUDIO` is granted. The permission is asked for when the app first opens; afterwards a button asks again, or opens the app's page in the watch's settings once Android stops showing its dialog. `rememberWatchMicPermission` follows the phone's `MicPermissionRules` ([Lifecycle](lifecycle.md)): a first dialog dismissed without an answer keeps "Allow microphone", and only a real "don't ask again" leads to settings. A refusal is remembered in the watch's own small `SharedPreferences` file (`twangle_wear`, `mic_refused`), as the phone keeps it in its settings. `WatchMicPermissionTest` on the JVM. A busy or broken mic shows a message with "Try again". **Coming back on screen** (from the watch face, the tile or the shortcut) after a failure tries again by itself (`WatchTunerViewModel.shown`, on `ON_START`): Android silences a recording the moment its app leaves the screen, which reads as a busy mic, and within the 5 s grace that greeted the player on their return (seen on the emulator, 2026-10-30). **The screen coming on counts only with the tuner on it** (`screenReport` in `ScreenState.kt`, `ScreenStateTest`): waking from always-on lands on the watch face, not the tuner, and within the 5 s grace the screen-on opened the microphone behind the watch face for some 0.4 s until Android silenced it (the Galaxy Watch 5, 2026-10-31); now the tuner catches up on the screen when it comes back (`ON_START`). On the Galaxy Watch 5 the microphone lets go 0.2 s after the screen goes dark or dims to always-on, and comes back as the screen wakes onto the tuner.
- **The screen stays on while the tuner hears playing**, as the phone's Keep screen on does (owner, 2026-10-30: the Galaxy Watch 5 went dark mid-tuning after its own 15 s or so): `WatchScreenAwake` runs the phone's `ScreenAwakeRule` (`:core:dsp`, `awake`) on the watch's clock and holds `View.keepScreenOn`, a window flag, not a wake lock. A note counts once it has sounded 1 s and holds the screen however long it sounds; 3 minutes after the last one, or after the screen came on, the watch's own timeout has the screen again. No warning first, unlike the phone: a raised wrist or a tap wakes the watch and the tuner listens again at once, and the watch has no room for it. No setting either; the watch app has none. It follows the tuner's state only while the tuner is on screen (`repeatOnLifecycle(STARTED)`): until 2026-10-30 it collected it in the background too, so the mic stayed open behind the watch face until Android silenced it, and the tuner came back saying another app had the microphone (`WatchScreenAwakeLifecycleTest` on the emulator). JVM-tested in `WatchScreenAwakeTest`; on the Galaxy Watch 5 the hold came off 3:00 after the tuner opened in a quiet room and the watch went dark on its own timeout, while a TV's music, heard as notes of a second or more, kept it on, as it does on the phone.
- **Screen off and ambient let go of the mic at once.** On Wear OS the activity stays *resumed* when the screen dims to ambient (always-on) or goes off (seen on the emulator: display `DOZE`, Twangle still the top resumed activity), so the lifecycle alone would keep the mic open. `ScreenOnEffect` listens for `ACTION_SCREEN_OFF` / `ACTION_SCREEN_ON` (sent when the display leaves or enters interactive mode, ambient included) and checks `PowerManager.isInteractive` on start; the view model then switches its upstream to a plain `Listening`, without the 5 s grace, and opens the mic again when the screen comes back. The app doesn't declare ambient support, so the watch shows the system's ambient view. Measured on the emulator: the mic is released within 0.9 s of `KEYCODE_SLEEP` (most of it adb) and reopened on wake; Home still keeps the 5 s grace.
- **Screen** (2.0, Phase 75; the watch of [docs/design/fresh-start-preview.html](../design/fresh-start-preview.html), *On the wrist*): the top half of the phone's tuner, nothing else, on black. **An arc** along the edge, open at the bottom (inset 9 % of the smaller side, its line 4 % wide with round ends, in the track colour): ±50 cents over ±135° either side of 12 o'clock (2.7° a cent), a small muted mark on it at the top for the note, and ♭ and ♯ (the phone's music font, muted) inside its flat and sharp ends at ±118°, which stay on their sides in right-to-left languages as the arc does. **A dot** on the arc marks the reading (8 % across, a soft light round it): it glides there (a low-stiffness spring) and, when the note stops, fades where it was. **The note** large in the middle (36 % of the dial, extra bold and set close, the mock-up's; the octave small and muted beside it on the same baseline, a ♯ raised and smaller in the phone's cut of Noto Sans Symbols, bundled with its licence; the watch has no About page, so its licence texts, the OFL and Apache 2.0 for AndroidX and Kotlin, ship as assets, `WatchLicencesTest`), fading in and out with a reading (400 ms). **The word** under it, bold, between the arc's ends: the phone's "Tighten", "Loosen" or "In tune" (`WatchWord`, the phone's `tunerHintFor` and `tuneWordFor` with strings, copied since they live in `:feature:tuner`: in tune while the watch says so, otherwise by the whole cents shown, but within 5 cents of the note it keeps this note's direction, so a reading wobbling across the note never swaps Tighten and Loosen; nothing until a direction is known, as a note first heard right on the mark; set smaller where a language's word doesn't fit half the dial), and **the cents** small and muted under it, as the phone writes them there: "12 cents flat", "3 cents sharp", in tune only how many, "2 cents", and "0 cents" right on the note before it counts (`WatchCents`: the number in a slot as wide as the widest from 0 to 99, in tabular digits and set against the words, the line as wide as its widest reading of that kind, flat, sharp or the amount alone, and starting at the same place, so nothing shifts as the number changes, owner 2026-10-30; the words are the texts TalkBack says, `wear_spoken_cents_flat`, `wear_spoken_cents_sharp` and `wear_cents`, split at the number, so each language keeps its order). **The tone:** the dot and the word take the phone's tone (`WatchTone`, a copy of `readingToneShare` at the phone's default window, `WatchToneTest` keeping it equal): the amber of a note far off, easing toward green as the note nears the in-tune window, green in tune. While nothing is heard "Play a note" stands in the word's place, a little smaller and muted, as the phone's "Play a string" does, and the middle is empty. No pegs, no tabs, no settings: the watch is chromatic and needs nothing from the phone. Sizes follow the screen, not the font setting, so the note fits inside the arc and the word and cents between its ends (checked from 170 to 225 dp). The tuner leaves out the time, where the arc runs; the permission and error messages keep it. (Until Phase 75: a ring with a needle and ticks, the note and signed cents, and a small sound ribbon; [History](history.md), *Metronome, widget and watch*.)
- **In tune:** the arc eases to green (45 % of the way from its grey to the in-tune green, so the dot stays the bright one), the dot and the word to the in-tune green, over 700 ms (`FastOutSlowInEasing`), hold while the note is in tune and fade the same way, and the wrist feels one tap (below). No pulse, no sparks.
- **TalkBack** (Phase 65): the dial is one polite live region, a layer under the arc (`WatchDial.SPOKEN_TAG`), that says the reading in words as the phone does: "A sharp 2, 12 cents flat, Tighten", "E 2, 1 cent sharp, Loosen", "E 2, in tune", "E 2, 0 cents" right on the note before it counts as in tune, or "Play a note" (`spokenWatchReading`, `WatchSpokenReading.kt`). The word after the cents is the word on screen (since Phase 75; until then "tune up" and "tune down", texts of their own), so near the note it keeps its direction as the screen does: "E 2, 2 cents sharp, Tighten". The note is named as on screen and said through the phone's `NoteNameSpeech` (`:core:dsp` `speech/`): "ais 2" in Dutch, the German way in German; its per-language settings are a copy of the phone's `speech.xml` (`wear_spoken_letter_names`, `wear_spoken_solfege`; `WatchSpokenReadingTest` fails if they drift), the sharp's word `wear_spoken_sharp` is the watch's own text (the watch writes only sharps). - **The tap on the wrist** (Phase 61): the moment the shown note comes in tune, when the arc starts to turn green, the motor taps once (`WatchInTuneTapEffect` in `WatchTunerApp`). Once per tuned note by the phone's own rule, `InTuneTickRule` (moved to `:core:dsp` `smoothing/` so phone and watch share it; `WatchInTuneTap` feeds it the shown note): a note wobbling out of tune and back within 1.5 s doesn't tap again, another note taps at once, and the tuner appearing on a note already in tune doesn't tap (the rule's state is saved). **The tap:** one pulse of 150 ms at full strength (`VibrationEffect.createOneShot`, `TAP_MILLIS`), on every watch. Tried on the owner's Galaxy Watch 5 with a guitar (2026-10-31; its motor has amplitude control but no primitives): the system's click (`EFFECT_CLICK`, the phone's tick; it was a `PRIMITIVE_CLICK` at 0.6 where supported) ran on the motor but wasn't felt at all, a 40 ms pulse neither, 80 ms only with the arm still (the first string, not the next ones, though the log shows a tap on every one), and a double tap of 80 ms not while playing either: the guitar's own vibration and the moving arm hide a short pulse, and 150 ms comes through. It drives the motor directly, as the phone does (the normal `VIBRATE` permission, granted at install; the microphone stays the only permission asked for); Android files it as touch feedback (`dumpsys vibrator_manager`: `Step=150ms(amplitude=1.00)`, usage TOUCH), so the watch's own touch-vibration strength applies to it. The first guess (60 ms to start, 20 ms to ring out: 320 ms of frames) let the buzz's tail through, and the needle jumped as the note came in tune (owner, 2026-11-03). `WatchPitchPipelineTest` closes the loop as on a watch: its recorder mixes a motor buzz (150–250 Hz, the tap's 150 ms at full strength, then a 7 ms decay for 50 ms) into the following audio when the tap rule fires, 0 to 150 ms late, on every string held and plucked, at half the note's level and as loud as it, twice or ten times: one tap, the same note still in tune, the cents within 1 of the clean reading. The emulator has no motor; on the Galaxy Watch 5 with a guitar the tap came once per tuned string and the needle held still across it (owner, 2026-10-31).
