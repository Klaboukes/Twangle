## Phase 46 — Stiff strings: the tuner's last few cents (the accuracy review of 2026-10-24)
Goal: the tuner and the 12th-fret check read a real, stiff string as well as they read a synthetic one. A real string is stiff, so its overtones sit a little sharp of exact multiples of the fundamental (inharmonicity, Fletcher 1964: partial k at `k·f₀·√(1 + B·k²)`), and YIN, which uses the whole waveform, is pulled sharp by them. Modelled on 2026-10-24 with `StrumChords.stiffStrum` and the stiffness the strum tests already assume (B = 1.5·10⁻⁴ for the low E, down to 2·10⁻⁵ for the high E), what the needle shows after a hard pick attack on the low E: **+8.5 cents at 0.25 s, +3.6 at 0.5 s, +1.0 at 1 s, +0.5 at 1.5 s**, sliding down as the overtones die away (the same pluck without stiffness: −0.2 to 0.0; a finger pluck, partials at 1/k², stays under 0.5 throughout). A note at the 12th fret has half the string's length and so four times its B, so the 12th-fret check, which takes 0.25–1.25 s of each note, would report a well set-up guitar played with a pick as **+4.3 cents on the low E** ("move the saddle back 1.5 mm"), +3.2 on the A and +3.1 on the G, all past its 3-cent tolerance; with a finger, under 1 cent on every string. The strum check and the chord name are tested with stiff strings (`StrumAccuracyReport`, `ChordAccuracyReport`); the tuner isn't. The owner's live checks passed, so this matters most for players with a pick and for the Precise setting (2 cents). Everything here is `:core:dsp` (hard rules 1, 2 and 6); nothing changes on screen. In order:
- ☐ **Stiff-string tests first:** `EdgeCaseTest` cases from `StrumChords.stiffStrum`'s model for every string of the standard tuning at the strum tests' B values and at three times them, plucked with a pick (partials at 1/k) and a finger (1/k²): the shown reading 0.5 s and 1 s after the pluck within 1 cent of the fundamental (`f₀·√(1 + B)`, as `StrumDetectorTest` defines it); and `IntonationTest` with the fretted note at 4× the open string's B: `FINE` on every string, the offset within the 3-cent tolerance. They fail today (the low E reads +3.6 at 0.5 s); they define done for the next item
- ☐ **The refinement on the lowest partials:** after YIN has chosen the period (its threshold, its whole-period checks and its above-range check stay exactly as they are, so the octave decisions don't change), the sub-sample refinement (step 5) runs again on the frame limited to the lowest three or four partials of that note: a low-pass at about 3.5 × f₀ (a Butterworth's start-up transient is long at such a cutoff, so likely through the frequency domain instead, since `FftDifference` already holds the frame's spectrum: the band-limited cross-correlation's peak near τ, refined with the same cubic fit). Measured in this review: limiting E4 to three partials took its pick-attack bias from +2.5 to +0.5 cents at B = 1.5·10⁻⁴, with the 20 dB SNR precision still under 0.5 cent. Allocation-free; every existing accuracy test still within its limit (sines ≈ 0.1 cent, 20 dB SNR under 1, 10 dB under 5, no octave errors, every sample rate, the low range); the stiff-string tests pass; `YinBenchmark` and `scripts/dsp-phone-benchmark.sh` for the cost per frame against [docs/architecture/performance.md](docs/architecture/performance.md); the YIN section of [docs/architecture/signal-pipeline.md](docs/architecture/signal-pipeline.md) and the Intonation section of [docs/architecture/notes-and-audio.md](docs/architecture/notes-and-audio.md) say how the refinement works and lose their stiff-string known limits
- ☐ **Real recordings in the tests:** every accuracy figure so far rests on synthetic sound (`core/dsp/src/test/resources/fixtures/` is empty). First a recorder for the test phone that is not Twangle (an instrumented test in `:core:testing` run by `scripts/record-plucks.sh`, writing 16-bit mono WAV files to the phone's storage and pulling them with adb), so hard rule 7 stands untouched. Then **the owner** (Claude says when the script is ready) plays each open string of a real guitar a few times in front of the Xiaomi, hard with a pick and softly with a finger, and in the same session a few plucks in a noisy room (talk, a TV on) for the next item, and the files go into the fixtures folder (a few MB, in git). A test runs them through `TunerChain`: the right note from the first shown frame, no octave flips, and the reading 0.5 s after the pluck within 2 cents of the reading 2 s in (the settled fundamental); `RealPluckReport` (print-only, with the benchmarks) prints each pluck's trajectory for the docs. The *Accuracy target* line of the signal-pipeline doc gets the measured figure in place of the ±3 cents target, and the pick and finger partial models in the tests are checked against what the recordings show
- ☐ **Quick and steady on real strings** (from Phase 51, moved here on 2026-10-26: it uses the same recordings and the same session with the owner, and it answers the most common complaint about the big tuners, a needle slow to catch the note or stuck): from the recordings, measure for each pluck how long until the first note shows and until the needle settles, and that the noisy-room plucks read the right note without a wandering needle. A test holds the measured figures; where a figure is worse than the synthetic one, fix it in `:core:dsp` first (hard rules 1, 2, 6), without undoing the no-flicker rules (the steady hint, the sticky string, the in-tune hold), which are part of why Twangle feels calm. The figures go into the *Accuracy target* part of [docs/architecture/signal-pipeline.md](docs/architecture/signal-pipeline.md)
- ☐ **Mains hum notch for the low range** (verified for real only with the bass from *Owner checks*, Phase 15; until then synthetic): in `PitchRange.LOW` alone, where 50 and 60 Hz are inside the range (today hum 10 dB below a B0 string pulls it up to 8 cents, 2–3 cents from E1 up, and hum alone shows as G1 or B♭1), narrow notches at 50 and 60 Hz before YIN, and at their 2nd and 3rd harmonics only where the measurements show they are needed, each so narrow that the nearest notes (G1 49.0 Hz, B1 61.7, G2 98.0, B2 123.5) stay within 0.1 cent and a semitone-flat string still reads. `LowPitchRangeTest`: hum 6 dB below every low string within 1 cent, hum alone nothing, every existing low-range figure kept; the *Low notes* and *Edge cases* sections of the signal-pipeline doc updated; the guitar's standard range is left as it is (60 Hz already lies below it)
- ☐ **Owner check added** under *Owner checks*: with a real guitar and a pick, play each string hard: the needle lands within a cent of where it settles within half a second, instead of sliding down from several cents sharp; run the 12th-fret check on a guitar a shop has set up: every string `FINE`

## Phase 55 — Ear training (owner, 2026-10-26; a tool, not a game, 2026-10-28)
Goal: one Practice tool, *Ear training*, with three exercises: **Play it back** (Twangle plays a note, the player finds it on the instrument, and the tuner hears whether it is right), **Intervals** and **Chord type** (answered with buttons). Play it back is what only Twangle can do among the free tuners, because it already hears. Intervals and chord types are what guitar teachers teach first, and they work away from the instrument, in headphones; with them, a teacher can recommend Twangle as a beginner's whole practice kit, free and without ads (Phase 56's flyer for teachers). Against bloat: one tool with three exercises, nothing added to the navigation, and no fourth exercise (a scale by ear stays in [IDEAS.md](IDEAS.md)). **A tool, not a game** (owner, 2026-10-28), in every exercise: no scores kept, no rounds, no levels, streaks or reminders. Questions keep coming until the player leaves; right and wrong are shown as gently as the tuner's in-tune effect; a quiet tally counts this visit only ("7 of 9") and is gone, never saved, when the tool closes; a question missed comes back a few questions later in the same visit. The player chooses what is asked (which notes, which intervals, which chord types), so a teacher can say "fourths and fifths this week"; Twangle never sets a level or says one is passed. No account, nothing leaves the phone (hard rules 7 and 9). It needs the microphone, which `:feature:practice` doesn't use by design, so it is a module of its own that holds the mic only while it is shown (hard rule 4). Built behind its own switch in `ToolSwitches`, hidden in release builds until the owner's check passes (the music tools' rule). Visible changes land with their UI tests, every language and every style. In order:
- ☐ **Rough mock-up first** (Contemporary), for **the owner** to pick: picking one of the three exercises, one question of each (the ▶ to hear it again; "Play it on your instrument" with the note the tuner hears, or the answer buttons), right and wrong, the tally for this visit, a missed question coming back, and where the player picks what is asked (e.g. the open strings, the natural notes up to the 5th fret or every note up to the 12th; which intervals; which chord types)
- ☐ **The questions in `:core:dsp`:** pure and seeded, so tests can repeat a visit: a note from the player's choice within the instrument's range for its tuning, sequences of two to four notes, an interval from the player's choice (minor second to octave, up, down or together), a chord type from the player's choice of the Chords tool's sixteen; never the same question twice in a row; a missed question asked again a few questions later; and the answer checks (the same note in any octave or the exact one, by the tuner's own note maths). `EarTrainingTest`
- ☐ **The tool and its module:** `:feature:eartraining` (the CLAUDE.md modules table and ARCHITECTURE.md updated), `PracticeTool.EAR_TRAINING` in Phase 47's tool list, the microphone asked for as the tuner does and held only while the tool is shown (`WhileSubscribed`, the tuner's pipeline reused, `UNPROCESSED` as hard rule 5 says). The question's sound plays in the instrument's voice through `TonePlayer`, and listening waits until it has faded, so the speaker's sound is never taken for the answer. Tests with a fake recorder and a fake player
- ☐ **Play it back:** Twangle plays a note, the player finds it on the instrument and holds it; the tuner says right once the note is steady (the strings mode's lock rules, no flicker), or plays the question again; the tally for this visit and the missed notes coming back; then two to four notes in a row. The exercise used last and the player's choices of what is asked are kept in `SettingsRepository` like the other Practice settings (settings, not scores), and [PRIVACY.md](PRIVACY.md) and its Dutch copy add them to the *Practice* line
- ☐ **Intervals:** two notes in the instrument's voice, the player picks the interval from buttons (steady sizes, the names in every language, TalkBack); the player's choice of intervals and of up, down or together; no microphone needed, so it works without the instrument
- ☐ **Chord type:** a chord strummed in the string voice from `ChordShapeFinder`'s shapes for the tuning picked; the player picks its type from buttons, from a few (major, minor, 7) up to all sixteen, as the player chooses
- ☐ **Owner check** added under *Owner checks*: with a real guitar, a few minutes of each exercise from the speaker and in headphones: Play it back hears right and wrong notes reliably and never takes the question for the answer, the sounds are clear, the buttons are easy to tell apart, and nothing flickers
- ☐ **Live:** after the owner's check, the switch on in release builds too

## Phase 59 — Chord charts and scales for the banjo (owner, 2026-10-27)
Goal: the 5-string banjo from Phase 57 gets the Practice tools the guitar has, so a beginner can tune and then learn the first chords (G, C, D7) without leaving Twangle. Today Chords, Scales and the Capo helper show a quiet line in their place for it (`ShortStringLine`, gated on `Tuning.hasShortString`), because the charts and the shape finder treat every string as starting at the nut, and on a guitar a string outside the chord is muted.
