## Strum check (polyphonic)
Phase 18. Three parts in `:core:dsp` `strum/`, all allocation-free (byte-counting tests), used by the strum check view ([Tuner screen and tour](ui-tuner.md), *Strum check view*). Offered for every instrument but the 12-string (`StrumInstruments.offers`, Phase 57): its octave strings sound right on the lower strings' partials (E3 on the low E's second), which the one partial series per string below can't tell apart. The chord name leaves it out for the same reason (`ChordInstruments`).
- **`StrumListener`** turns mic chunks into frames timed from each strum (below, *Timing*).
- **`StrumDetector`** reads every string of one strum from one frame, given the frequencies to expect: the tuning's open strings at the current A4, for any instrument up to 8 strings, in any order. `detect(frame, expectedHz, count, result)` fills a `StrumResult` per string: Hz, cents from the expected note, level in dB below the loudest string, `present` and `shadowed`.
- **`StrumVerdict.of(result, string, toleranceCents, previous)`** turns a reading into `NOT_HEARD`, `FLAT`, `IN_TUNE` or `SHARP` (below, *Verdict*).

**Method.** A Hann window over the frame, a 2× zero-padded FFT, then five steps per string, steps 2–5 repeated 4 times because each string's result depends on the others':
1. **Coarse search:** weighted harmonic summation (Hermes 1988; Klapuri 2006), `Σ_k |X(k·f)| / k` over the first 8 harmonics, on a 1-cent grid ±100 cents around the expected note. Searching near the expected strings keeps the other fundamentals out. The 1/k weight was needed: unweighted, A2 at −22 cents locked onto E4's fundamental landing on its 3rd harmonic.
2. **Partial peaks:** each harmonic of the estimate is followed to its peak within half a main lobe; quadratic interpolation of the log magnitude (Smith & Serra 1987) gives its frequency and amplitude. A peak further off belongs to another string. **Joint fit** (`PartialFit`): when other heard strings' partials lie ½ to 3 main-lobe half-widths away (2.9–17.6 Hz at 341 ms), close enough to bend the peak but far enough apart to tell, the partial is fitted together with them instead. The complex spectrum around it is modelled as Hann-windowed sinusoids at the others' known frequencies plus ours; the amplitudes come from linear least squares and a golden-section search finds our frequency with the smallest residual (nonlinear least squares with all frequencies but one known; Stoica & Moses 2005, §4.3). The window's exact transform comes from the Dirichlet kernel, its angles stepped by rotation from bin to bin. Partials closer than half a lobe (1/T, 2.9 Hz at 341 ms) can't be told apart and stay one merged peak; fitting them too (tried down to a tenth of a lobe) only added errors. If the fit finds nothing between the others, the plain peak is used.
3. **Isolation:** each partial gets weight 0 within one main-lobe half-width (2 bins of the unpadded FFT: 5.9 Hz at 341 ms) of another heard string's partial, 1 from two. The joint fit could allow closer, but these weights gave the best clear strings.
4. **Estimate:** the mean of `f_k / k` weighted by power × isolation (power favours the low partials, which inharmonicity bends least). A string with no clear partial is **shadowed** and reads its lowest partial as it is: the other string's partial there is one of its own higher, weaker harmonics.
5. **Presence**, judged on the first two partials: a clear partial counts with its amplitude, a shadowed one with what is left after the other strings' expected share of the partials merged with it (their nearest clear partial, falling as 1/j), and not at all when the other string's harmonic number is ≤ ours (else a missing string's search settles where its upper harmonics hit other fundamentals). Partials fitted apart in step 2 are already out of its amplitude, so they don't count in that share. Heard: within 25 dB of the loudest string (a Hann window's highest sidelobe is −31 dB, so a sidelobe never counts) and above −74 dBFS.

**Overlapping harmonics are the core problem.** Equal temperament puts many partials of different strings within 2 cents. In standard tuning B3 ≈ 3 × E2 (1.96 cents apart), E4 = 4 × E2 and ≈ 3 × A2, A2's 4th ≈ D3's 3rd, D3's 4th ≈ G3's 3rd. Telling apart two partials 2 cents apart at 247 Hz (0.3 Hz) would take a window of seconds. So:
- **B3 and E4 are shadowed** in standard tuning: every partial of theirs sits on one of E2's. They read the merged peak, pulled towards E2's harmonic by its share: in tune +0.4 to 0.6 cents (part of the 2-cent gap). A lower string's partial within half a lobe is merged too and pulls them by up to 2.3 cents at 341 ms (0.5 at 683 ms): A2 10 cents flat puts its 3rd harmonic 0.33 lobe from E4. From half a lobe on, the joint fit separates it. A detuned string itself separates: 20 cents is 2.9 Hz at 247 Hz but 11 Hz at its 4th harmonic.
- **Octave strings** (drop D, DADGAD, open D, open G: D2 D3 D4, A2 A3, G2 G3) are shadowed the same way. An octave pair detuned against each other (A2 −20, A3 +15 in DADGAD) read 7.3 cents off at 341 ms in the research spike: A3's fundamental sat 0.75 lobe from A2's 2nd harmonic. The joint fit brings it to **0.44**.
- **Uneven octave strings** (the research spike's detector did the same): when the upper string of an octave pair is within half a lobe of the lower one's harmonic (35 cents at 147 Hz, D3 over D2) and clearly softer, the merged peak is mostly the lower string's harmonic, and the reading lands between the two. In drop D, with every string random within ±30 cents and struck at 0.3–1 of full strength, D3 read up to 18.6 cents off (rms 3.2) while the other strings stayed within 0.2 (E4 3.6). The 683 ms frame halves that (10.2) but loses more strings; masking the lower string's partials and searching again didn't help. The owner's check with a real guitar (*Owner checks* in ROADMAP.md) will show how much it matters in real strums. `shadowed` marks these readings.
- **Unison strings** (one note on two strings, as on a 12-string's unison pairs or in a custom tuning) have every partial in common, so no frame tells them apart. `StrumDetector` reads them as one course (expected frequencies within 1 cent) and gives both strings the course's reading: both show the pair's pitch, and a pair with one string left out still reads as heard. `StrumDetectorTest`: a G3 pair in tune, +15 and −20 cents, one of it left out, and an E4 pair listed first and last.
- **A missing shadowed string** is filled in by the lower string's partials, and coinciding partials add with unknown phases (anywhere from the difference to the sum of their amplitudes). The 1/j share catches a missing B3 or E4 in synthetic strums, but a missing D4 in DADGAD or open D reads as heard, and real guitars' harmonics don't fall as 1/j. Counting less of the share (tried 0.4–0.8 of it) or the merged amplitude as the share both made things worse. In DADGAD, open D and open G with uneven strums, 16–23 of 600 present strings read as missing (43–44 with the spike's detector) and 7–12 of 100 missing strings as heard (3–6).

A five-string bass needs the long frame: 2.5 cents on B0 at 341 ms, 0.03 at 683. `StrumDetectorTest` holds these with a little room (clear strings within 0.5 cent, shadowed within 3) and checks every verdict where the reading's error can't cross its limit.

**Frame and latency.** Accuracy comes from resolving partials: a Hann main lobe is 2/T wide each side (11.7 Hz at 171 ms, 5.9 at 341, 2.9 at 683). Default **16 384 samples (341 ms, four tuner windows)**; `frameLengthFor(rate, PitchRange.LOW)` doubles it for instruments below the guitar, like the tuner's window. 171 ms is too short for the shadowed strings.

**Timing** (`StrumListener(sampleRate, range)`). Mic chunks go in through `push` (like `FrameAssembler`, it stops as soon as a frame is ready) and are checked one tuner hop (1024 samples, 21 ms) at a time. A **strum** is a hop at or above −50 dBFS (the tuner's gate) and 8 dB louder than the quietest of the 6 hops before it (130 ms), at least 150 ms after the last strum (a strum's strings start up to ≈ 0.1 s apart). The first hop after construction or `reset` only sets that history to its own level, so whatever already sounds when listening starts (strings still ringing when strum check is switched on, a steady note, a fan) is never taken for a strum. The first frame comes once 50 ms of attack and a whole frame have arrived after the hop the strum was noticed in: it starts 50–93 ms after the strum and is ready **0.39–0.44 s after it** (0.73–0.78 s for `PitchRange.LOW`). If that first frame (its RMS, `frameLevelDbfs`) is under the gate (`MIN_FIRST_FRAME_DBFS`, −50 dBFS), the strum is not read at all: on the phone, the click of a sound stopping at once and a dying strum's beating near the gate were taken for strums, and their one faint reading replaced a good one. Then a new frame every **0.1 s** while the strings ring, until the level falls 6 dB below the gate. A new strum, also over a ringing one, starts over. `readFrame(out)` copies the frame out; `strumCount`, `readingsSinceStrum`, `samplesSinceStrum` (a `Long`: as an `Int` it overflowed 12.4 hours after a strum at 48 kHz, and no strum counted after that), `isRinging`, `levelDbfs` and `frameLevelDbfs` (the frame's level, which weighs readings, [Tuner screen and tour](ui-tuner.md), *Strum check view*) tell the view where it is. The `FrameAssembler` inside holds one frame, and its new `copyLatest(out, length)` reads it at any hop without taking the hop's own frame. `StrumListenerTest`: no frame from room noise alone, nor from a sound already there when listening starts, the frame's start and readiness, a frame every 0.1 s while ringing and none after, a re-strum 10 dB over a ringing one, a click over strings dying away noticed but not read, a soft strum 3 dB over the first frame's limit read, any chunk size, chunks through to verdicts, a strum still noticed 13 hours after the last.

**Verdict** (`StrumVerdict`). In tune within the tolerance, 5 cents by default (the tuner's Relaxed; pass 2 for Precise), else flat or sharp; not heard when the string isn't present. A shadowed string gets 2.5 cents more (the merge's measured pull with equal strings), so it is only called out of tune when the merge alone can't explain it. A string that was in tune in the previous reading of the same strum stays so 1 cent further, so readings every 0.1 s don't flicker at the edge (like the tuner's 5 / 7). A string more than 100 cents off reads at the edge of its search, which still gives the right direction, or as not heard.

**CPU.** One detection: JVM 2.6 ms (341 ms frame) / 4.4 ms (683 ms). Xiaomi 12X (`scripts/dsp-phone-benchmark.sh`): **2.6 / 5.3 ms** (1.9 / 4.2 before the joint fit). At a reading every 0.1 s that is about 3 % of a big core while the strings ring, so a native detector isn't needed for this ([Threading and performance](performance.md), *Watch CPU and the native detector*). Most of it is the 32 768-point FFT and the fits.

**Phone check** (`scripts/strum-check.sh`). `scripts/strum-wav.py` writes synthetic strums (the same ideal plucked string as `PluckSynth`, strings 15 ms apart, all equally loud, 4 s, ending at once); the script plays them from the computer's speaker to the phone, reads what each string shows from the screen's TalkBack labels (`uiautomator dump`) and saves screenshots in `build/strum-check/`. `scripts/strum-check.sh bass` does the same for a 4-string bass. The guitar rows were read with readings weighed together (below); the bass rows come from the earlier run that showed the last reading, already right, and the weighing only changes how readings are put together and which sounds count as strums. The room got noisy late in the check (below), so the last guitar row was read while the strum still rang.

The readings behind it (a temporary log of every reading): over the first ≈ 0.5 s every string was within 0.5 cent of what was played and a missing string was missing. As the strings died towards the gate (−50 to −56 dBFS, 1.5–2 s on) E2 wandered by up to ±7 cents, a 30-cent-flat B3 came and went, and once a missing A2 read +60 (probably the speaker's difference tone G3 − E2 = 113.6 Hz, 56 cents above A2). The panel showed the last reading, so it often ended on those: E2 played −10 showed −17, B3 played −30 "+99" or not heard. Weighing every reading by its frame's power fixed it: put together that way, the logged readings of every strum gave each string within 0.5 cent of what was played from the third reading on (0.6 s after the strum), and every missing string missing. The script also showed sounds taken for strums: the speaker's click as a strum stops at once, beating at the end of a strum, and, once the room got noisy (for the last half hour of the check), up to several "strums" a second, onsets at −28 to −49 dBFS over a −70 floor, as loud as the test strums or louder, some of them read. The first two are gone (the first-frame limit above). A first reading must now also hear half the strings, which keeps knocks off; other sounds as loud as a strum, such as a voice next to the phone, can still be read as one and start a strum over, so strum check wants a quiet room.

**Not modelled yet:** pick noise and buzz, sympathetic strings, the body's resonances, and the phone mic's weak response at 82 Hz beyond the weak-fundamental case; the owner's check with a real guitar (*Owner checks* in ROADMAP.md) will tell. Inharmonicity bends partial k sharp by ≈ 866·B·(k² − 1) cents; the low-partial weighting keeps it to +0.6 cents with weak fundamentals (+1 at three times those B values); fitting B per string would remove it. Higher partials decay first, so a late frame has fewer clear partials (fine 1.5 s after the strum in synthetic strums).

## Chord naming
Phase 30. `:core:dsp` `chord/`, allocation-free (byte-counting tests). Shown by the tuner's chord name, behind its switch (Phase 28): [Tuner screen and tour](ui-tuner.md), *Chord name view*, which also covers `ChordSettler` (readings settled into what shows), `ChordSpelling` (symbols and the usual chord spelling) and `ChordInstruments` (which instruments get it).
- **`ChordDetector(sampleRate, frameLength, lowestMidi, highestMidi)`** names the chord in one frame: `detect(frame, result, a4Hz)` fills a `ChordResult` and returns its `chord`, or null. Frames come from `StrumListener`, the same as strum check's (341 ms, a new one every 0.1 s while the strings ring); notes are looked for from B1 to E6.
- **`Chord`** is a root `PitchClass` and a `ChordQuality`: major, minor, 7th, major 7th, minor 7th, sus2, sus4, and since Phase 49 the power chord, 7♯9, m9 and maj9 (the eleven `detected` ones, which the tuner names), and for the chord charts only 7sus4, 6, m6, add9, 9, dim, dim7, m7♭5 and aug (2026-10-20). Those share their tones with the named ones or with each other (C6 = Am7, Cm7♭5 = E♭m6, a dim7 is four chords at once), so hearing them would rest on the bass alone, and the detector wasn't measured on them. Every chord exists once (`Chord.of`), so naming allocates nothing. The UI names it in the player's notation (A minor, La minore, a-Moll …). `ChordResult` also holds the `bass` (lowest note's pitch class, for a slash chord such as C/E), the notes heard with their strengths, each pitch class's strength, the strum's `tuningOffsetCents`, and `crowded`.
- **`ChordNamer.name(pitchClasses, bass)`** is the template match on its own, pure and tested chord by chord.

**Why not `StrumDetector`.** Strum check knows which note each string should be. A chord can put any note on any string, leave strings out and double notes, so this expects nothing and finds the notes itself. A plain chroma (the spectrum folded into 12 pitch classes, matched against templates) is not enough: every note's 3rd and 5th harmonics land on its fifth and major third, so an E5 power chord (E2 B2 E3) reads as E major, and the 3rd harmonic of a chord's third adds its seventh (C → Cmaj7). The notes have to be found first, with their harmonics accounted for.

**Method** (`ChordDetector`):
1. **Peaks:** a Hann window, a 2× zero-padded FFT, and its peaks up to 4 kHz: local maxima that are the highest within 0.75 main-lobe half-widths (a Hann sidelobe sits 1.25 away, so it is never one), within 50 dB of the loudest, above −74 dBFS; frequency and amplitude by quadratic interpolation of the log magnitude (Smith & Serra 1987).
2. **Tuning:** the peaks' power-weighted circular mean distance from the semitone grid at the given A4. A guitar tuned to itself at A4 = 432 Hz reads −32 cents and is named against 440. Every string may also be ±10 cents off on its own.
3. **Notes, by iterative estimation and cancellation** (Klapuri 2003 and 2006): every semitone gets a salience, `Σ_k r_k / √k` over its first 10 partials, where `r_k` is what is left of the strongest peak within ±30 cents of `k·f₀`. The strongest is a note; its partials are taken out of the peaks, and the search repeats until the best salience is under 8 % of the first note's. Then:
   - *A note's fundamental must reach 15 % of its strongest partial.* Otherwise C2 would be found under C3 E3 G3, whose frequencies are its 4th, 5th and 6th harmonics. A low string heard through a phone mic, whose 2nd harmonic is three times its fundamental (`TestSignals.WEAK_FUNDAMENTAL_HARMONICS`), still counts.
   - *Partial k is taken out only up to the geometric mean of the note's own partials k − 1 and k + 1, as first measured* (spectral smoothness, Klapuri 2003), so another note's partial on top of it stays. With the larger neighbour instead, a real G#4 went with E2's 5th partial because B4 sat on the 6th. With the smaller one, B2's 3rd partial (F#4) was left over strongly enough to turn G into Gmaj7.
   - *1/√k weights, not 1/k:* with 1/k the octave above a weak-fundamental low string won, and the string's odd partials were left over as false notes.
   - *More than 10 notes* is noise or a muddle (`crowded`), no chord.
4. **Name:** a note under 30 % of the first note's salience that lies on a harmonic (2 to 16, within 35 cents) of another note found is taken for that note's leftover and doesn't count. When a note an octave up merges with a lower one, its own odd partials are left over: C#6 = 6 × F#3 in Bm. The rest go to `ChordNamer` as pitch classes, with the lowest note as the bass. Notes from 15 % of the first note's salience are sure. Weaker ones may be a string played softly or a leftover, so the chord is named only if it stays the same with and without each of them (every subset). A strum that is nearly two chords, or nearly none, gets no name.

**Template match** (`ChordNamer`). The pitch classes must be exactly a chord's tones. The 7th and 9th chords may leave out their fifth, as guitar voicings often do (C7 as x32310). Two notes are a chord only as root and fifth, the power chord (Phase 49): E and B alone are E5 (022xxx or 02xxxx), never E or Em from a third that isn't there; B under E (a fourth) is E5/B. Any other two could be several chords: C and E could be C major or A minor. The 7♯9, m9 and maj9 are heard since Phase 49 too: their tones are no other chord's, and the figures below show they took no names from the chords heard before. An extra note makes a chord this doesn't know yet (add9, 6, diminished, augmented), so no name. Inversions are named by their chord. Two chords share their tones only among the sus chords (Dsus2 = Asus4 = D E A, Dsus4 = Gsus2), and the bass tells them apart: xx0230 is Dsus2, x02230 Asus4, and with neither root in the bass there is no name. C6 has Am7's tones and is named Am7, since C6 isn't among the detected chords (`ChordNamer` matches only `ChordQuality.detected`).

**Measured** (`ChordAccuracyReport`, synthetic `PluckSynth` strums like strum check's, 41 shapes in standard tuning: 25 open (E, A, D, G, C, Em, Am, Dm, E7, A7, D7, G7, C7, B7, Cmaj7, Dmaj7, Fmaj7, Am7, Em7, Dm7, Dsus2, Asus2, Dsus4, Asus4, Esus4) and 16 barre (F, B♭, G#, C, Bm, F#m, Cm, Am, F#7, C7, B♭maj7, C#m7, Bm7, Gm7, Csus4, Csus2)). Right / no name / wrong:

|---|---|
| As played; upstroke; frame from the strum's start, 1 s or 2 s later | 41 / 0 / 0 each |
| Each string ±10 cents and struck at 0.5–1 of full strength, strings 5–30 ms apart; or struck at 0.3–1 (410 each) | 410 / 0 / 0 each |
| Same, struck at 0.2–1 (410) | 397 / 13 / 0 (a chord tone struck softly, or a leftover, under 15 %) |

The 41 shapes' figures were the same before and after the power chord and the 9ths were heard (2026-10-26).

**Power chords** (16: E5, A5, D5, F5, F♯5, G5, A5, B♭5, B5, C5, D5, E♭5 on three strings, E5, G5, A5, C5 on two): 16 / 0 / 0 as played and every way above, but 158 / 2 / 0 struck at 0.2–1; never a major or minor chord. **7♯9, m9 and maj9** (12 chord-book shapes: Cm9 x31333, Em9 020002, Am9 575557, Dm9 x53555, Cmaj9 x32430, Amaj9 x02424, Emaj9 021102, Fmaj9 102010, E7♯9 076780, C7♯9 x3234x, A7♯9 575658, B7♯9 x2123x): 10 / 0 / 2 as played, 105 / 0 / 15 uneven; the wrong ones are all the 7th chord of the same root (Am9 575557 as Am7, Em9 as Em7, Emaj9 as Emaj7): the ninth there is the fifth's 3rd harmonic (B4 over E3), so it is taken for the fifth's leftover, as before the 9ths were heard.

No name for: 37 single notes (E2–E5), 21 thirds, 21 minor thirds with the octave, 21 octaves (0 of 100), 100 white-noise frames from −70 to −10 dBFS (0 of 100), two chords at once, a chord with a wrong note and random clusters (1 of 246; 1 of 269 before the power chord and the 9ths, whose sets now count as chords). That one: a soft G#4 added to Esus4, on E2's 5th harmonic, was taken for E2's leftover. A wrong note on a harmonic of a chord note and under 30 % (a soft added 9th sits on the fifth's 3rd harmonic) is heard as that note's harmonic, so the triad is named. `ChordDetectorTest` holds every shape as played and through `StrumListener`, three random strums per shape, stiff, weak-fundamental, noisy and retuned strums, very uneven strums (right or none, at least 90 % named), power chords on two and three strings at every root from E2 to A3 (never a major or minor chord, however struck), the 9ths (right or their 7th chord, at least 80 % right), and the no-chord cases.

**CPU.** One detection: 1.4 ms on the JVM, most of it the 32 768-point FFT; strum check's takes 2.6 ms at the same pace of one every 0.1 s.

**Not modelled yet:** real guitars (body resonances, pick noise, sympathetic strings, harmonics that don't fall as 1/k), chords beyond the eleven named types, and readings combined in the detector (each frame is named on its own; `ChordSettler` settles the view on a name over several). The owner's check with a real guitar (*Owner checks* in ROADMAP.md, later in Phase 30) will tell.

## Metronome
The engine, the screen ([Metronome, widget and watch](ui-other-screens.md), *Metronome screen*) and the navigation bar ([UI basics](ui.md), *Navigation bar*).
- **Meters** (`Meter`, `:core:dsp` `metronome/`): 2/4, 3/4, 4/4, 5/4, 6/8 and 7/8. One click per beat of the bar, and the tempo counts clicks, so in 6/8 and 7/8 it counts eighth notes (as most practice metronomes do). Beat 1 is the accent (`BeatAccent.DOWNBEAT`); the start of each later beat group gets a middle accent (`GROUP`): 5/4 as 3 + 2 (beat 4), 6/8 as 3 + 3 (beat 4), 7/8 as 2 + 2 + 3 (beats 3 and 5), the most common groupings. The other beats are `PLAIN`.
- **Clicks** (`ClickSound`, `ClickSounds`): four instruments, the owner's choice of 2026-10-25 (Phase 45), each in its own register and with its own nature: `WOOD_BLOCK` (the default), `COWBELL`, `CLAVES` and `RIM_CLICK`. All synthesised, no recordings, so nothing to licence. Every sound has a click per accent, told apart by pitch and level: beat 1 is higher (an octave, the claves a fifth) and at least 4.5 dB louder at the peak, a group accent in between, and the clicks between beats (`SUBDIVISION`, Phase 62) a fourth lower and the softest, at least 3 dB under a plain beat at the peak and by energy (at 48 kHz: wood block 4.1 dB, cowbell 4.3, claves 3.4, rim click 6.8 at the peak). No click passes 0.8 (about −2 dBFS), and every one starts and ends on exactly 0.
  - *Wood block*: a 40 ms "tock", two decaying modes of a struck free bar at 1 : 2.756 (Fletcher & Rossing §2.15), the upper one decaying twice as fast, with a 0.8 ms raised-cosine attack and an 8 ms fade. Downbeat A6 (1760 Hz) at peak 0.8, group E6 at 0.6, plain A5 at 0.45, between beats E5 (659.3 Hz) at 0.28.
  - *Cowbell* (180 ms), *claves* (120 ms) and *rim click* (80 ms): struck by `PercussionSynth` from the recipes in `ClickRecipes`. A hit is a raised-cosine contact pulse (Hertz's law as Chaigne & Doutaut 1997 use it: a harder hit is shorter, so louder and brighter), driving a bank of two-pole resonators, one per mode, plus a band-passed strike noise. The cowbell: a thin bell shell's ring modes (560 Hz tone, a clashing cluster to 3 kHz, every mode a beating pair), 40 dB down at 132 ms. The claves: a thick rosewood rod's bending modes (Timoshenko estimate) at 2.2 kHz, damped by the hand. The rim click: a noisy crack around 3 and 6 kHz, the stick's own modes, a short thump of the drum's membrane modes (Bessel zeros, 180 Hz) and the hoop's ring at 1.2 kHz, faint on plain beats and between them. A click between beats is struck at 0.4 (a plain beat 0.66) and rings at 0.75 × a plain beat's pitch, every sound alike. Every hit is the same: the mode signs and the noise come from `java.util.Random` seeded by the recipe's name and a fixed "listening place" per accent (the one of 300 with the least peak for its energy whose energy still peaks within 1.6 ms of the onset), so every bar is identical on every device. Each sound is scaled so a bar of 4/4 has the wood block's RMS, unless a click would pass 0.8 (the rim click: 0.7 dB under).
  - *The approved samples* are `core/dsp/src/test/resources/clicks/` (one bar of 4/4 at 100 BPM each), written from the app's own code by `ClickCandidatesReport`, which also writes them, alone and under a strummed guitar, into `docs/design/click-sounds-preview/` to listen to (not committed) (with the benchmarks), which also checks the listening places are still the ones its search picks. `ClickSoundsTest` checks that `ClickTrack` at 48 kHz gives every 16-bit sample of them exactly. The struck sounds are made at 48 kHz, the samples' rate, and at any other rate resampled from it (`Resampler`, a Hann-windowed sinc after J. O. Smith, with 0.1 ms and 1 ms fades so the ends stay on exactly 0): their noise is drawn one value per sample, so made at another rate it would come out different, and the rim click lost up to 3 dB.
  - *Rendered once per sample rate*, so playing a click is a copy: the wood block when the `ClickSounds` is made (31 KB at 48 kHz), each other sound the first time it is asked for (`prepare`, or `ClickTrack.sound`'s setter), 1.5–2.5 ms on the PC at 48 kHz, 3–7 ms where it is resampled. All four, four clicks each, together hold 80,640 floats at 48 kHz, 323 KB.
- **Sample-exact timing** (`ClickTrack`): beat n starts on sample `A + round((n − a) · 60 · rate / bpm)`, worked out in integer arithmetic from the beat a and sample A where the tempo was last set, never by adding up rounded intervals. Each beat is within half a sample of its ideal time and the error never builds up (`ClickTrackTest`: every beat of ten minutes at 97 BPM / 44.1 kHz, 233 BPM / 48 kHz and 31 BPM / 16 kHz, whatever the chunk size). The tempo is an integer, clamped to 30–250 BPM, so beats are ≥ 0.24 s apart (a tempo change never brings the next beat closer than one new interval) and without subdivisions the longest click, the cowbell's 0.18 s, ends before the next starts. `ClickTrack.sound` changes the instrument from the next click; a click still sounding plays out (`ClickTrackTest`: every sound whole at 250 BPM, also across a tempo change at the worst moment).
- **Subdivisions** (`Subdivision`, `ClickTrack.subdivision`, Phase 62): `NONE` (the default), `TWO`, `THREE` or `FOUR` clicks per beat, the beat's own and the others the softest click. In 2/4–5/4 that is eighths, triplets or sixteenths; in 6/8 and 7/8, where a click is already an eighth, only `NONE` and `TWO` (sixteenths) are offered (`offeredIn`), and the others play as `NONE` there (`within`), so switching meters back keeps the choice. Click j of beat n's k starts on sample `A + round(((n − a) · k + j) · 60 · rate / (bpm · k))`, the same formula, so the beats fall on the same samples whatever the subdivision and every click is within half a sample of its ideal time (`ClickTrackTest`: every click of ten minutes in each subdivision at the three tempos above). A new subdivision is heard from the next beat; the meter, too, still changes from the next beat. A new tempo moves the clicks left in the beat sounding to the new tempo, leaving out any already past. `BeatListener.onSubdivision` tells about the in-between clicks; `onBeat` is told only beats, so vibration stays on the beats; the beat lights show the clicks between as small marks, lit from each beat's heard time (below). **Saved and chosen** (Phase 62): `AppSettings.metronomeSubdivision` (`metronome_subdivision`, by name; none when unset or unknown), the choice as picked, so triplets picked in 4/4 stay saved through 6/8 (where they play as none) and come back in 4/4. `MetronomeViewModel.setSubdivision` hands it to the player (`setSubdivision` while playing, heard from the next beat; while stopped one bar of it plays, as a picked sound does) and saves it; the player always gets the choice and `ClickTrack` applies `within(meter)`. The player's `beats` stay beats only.
- **One voice, also at 250 BPM in sixteenths** (a click every 60 ms, shorter than every sound but the wood block): a click still sounding when the next starts is cut, faded out along half a cosine over the 3 ms before it (`CUT_FADE_SECONDS`), reaching 0 just as the next begins, so there is never more than one click to copy and nothing crackles. A tempo change never schedules the next click so soon that the fade has no room, and a fade already begun goes on to its end. Without subdivisions nothing is ever cut, so the metronome sounds as before (the approved samples still match). `ClickTrackTest`: at 250 BPM in 2, 3 and 4 every sound's clicks start whole on their samples and end within 0.001 of 0; 20 s of random tempo and subdivision changes at random moments in every sound never step further between samples than the clicks themselves do (+0.02); rendering and every setter but a new sound allocate nothing.
- **Changes while playing** take effect from the next beat: a new tempo puts it one new interval after the last beat (or at once if that moment has passed), and the tempo counts on from there; a new meter makes it a downbeat without moving it. `render` and the setters allocate nothing (byte-counting test).
- **The speed trainer** (`SpeedTrainer`, `ClickTrack.startTrainer`, Phase 62; a tool with its own switch, `ToolSwitches.speedTrainer`, live in every build since the owner's check passed on 2026-10-31): a start tempo, a goal tempo and a step (+1, +2, +5 or +10 BPM every 1, 2, 4 or 8 bars; start and goal on a 5 BPM grid, the goal at least 5 above the start, `SpeedTrainer.of`). Bar b of the climb, counted from its first downbeat (bar 0), plays at `min(goal, start + step · ⌊b / bars⌋)`, so it never passes the goal and holds there. **Where the step is decided: in the click track, on the audio thread.** On every downbeat `ClickTrack` counts a bar (`trainerBar`) and asks the plan for that bar's tempo; a new one takes effect from the downbeat itself: the downbeat keeps the old tempo's place (one old interval after the beat before) and the anchor of the integer formula moves to it, so the beats and the clicks between them after it follow the new tempo, each on its exact sample. Deciding it in the ViewModel from the beats it hears would not be reliable: a beat is told as it is *heard*, about one buffer (≈ 0.12 s) after it was rendered, plus the main thread's own delay, and at 250 BPM the next beat may already be rendered by then, so a step asked for then could land a beat late. In the click track it costs a counter and a little integer arithmetic per downbeat (`bpmOfBar` allocates nothing; `SpeedTrainerTest`, `ClickTrackTest`: every downbeat a step falls on is on the old tempo's sample and every beat and in-between click after it on the new tempo's, in 4/4 eighths, 3/4 triplets and 7/8, never past the goal; a trainer started while playing takes its start tempo from the next beat and counts bars from the next downbeat; a reset begins it again; climbing allocates nothing). The player hands a plan over once per `start(…, trainer)` or `setTrainer` and, while it climbs, stops copying its wanted tempo to the track; `setTrainer(null)` ends it and the tempo stays where it climbed to until the next `setTempo`. Each `MetronomeBeat` tells the tempo it plays at (`bpm`) and the trainer's bar (`trainerBar`), so the ViewModel only follows: the dial takes each beat's tempo as it is heard, moving with the downbeat. **Saved** (`AppSettings.metronomeTrainer`, four ints `metronome_trainer_start`, `_goal`, `_step`, `_bars`, null until first turned on; `metronomeTrainerOn`, `metronome_trainer_on`). `MetronomeViewModel`: turned on the first time it is filled in from the tempo shown (`SpeedTrainer.startingAt`: from there to 20 BPM above, +5 every 4 bars) and saved; while on, Start plays from the start tempo and the climb follows; Stop puts the dial back at the start (Stop and Start begin again from it); a new plan while it climbs begins again from its start from the next beat; a tempo by hand (dial, − +), Tap tempo or Listen ends it (the switch goes off, the tempo stays where it was and is saved); the climbing tempos are never saved as the metronome's tempo. `MetronomeViewModelTest`, `MetronomeListenTest`.
- **Playback** (`MetronomePlayer`, `AndroidMetronomePlayer` in `:core:audio`): like the reference tone ([Notes, tunings and audio](notes-and-audio.md), *Reference tone*; both share `AudioOutput`), a mono float `AudioTrack` at the native rate, `USAGE_MEDIA` / `CONTENT_TYPE_SONIFICATION`, on its own single writer thread, existing only while it plays. It keeps 12 chunks of 10 ms queued: a busy moment can't starve the speaker (a gap would shift every later beat), and a change is heard ≈ 0.12 s after it is asked for. Underruns are logged. `start(bpm, meter, sound, subdivision, trainer)`, `setTempo`, `setMeter`, `setSound`, `setSubdivision`, `setTrainer` (Phase 62), `previewBar` and `stop` only set fields under a lock, read before every chunk; a sound not played yet is rendered there, on the writer thread (a few ms, once). It never touches the microphone.
- **Preview** (`previewBar(bpm, meter, sound, subdivision)`, Phase 45; the subdivision since Phase 62): while stopped, one bar of a sound, with its clicks between the beats, from beat 0, once, then silence until the playback head has passed the bar's end, and the session ends by itself (`isPreviewing`; `isPlaying` stays false and no beats are told, so no lights or vibration). A new preview, or Start during one, starts the click track over in the same session and track, its frames counted from where the track has got to (the ≈ 0.12 s already queued plays out first); `stop` ends it. While playing, `previewBar` only changes the sound and subdivision. `AndroidMetronomePlayerTest`: a sound changed while playing comes within a beat at 240 BPM and stays; a preview lasts its bar and tells no beats; a second preview starts over; Start after a preview begins at beat 0 with its own sound.
- **Plays over other music: no audio focus** (owner's decision, 2026-10-15). Players often practise with a backing track from another app, which must keep playing at full volume, so the metronome asks for no focus at all: `AUDIOFOCUS_GAIN_TRANSIENT_MAY_DUCK` would still make Android lower the other app's music (or pause it, for apps that pause when ducked), and any `GAIN` pauses it. Without focus nothing tells it about a call, so the session reads the phone's audio mode (`AudioManager.getMode()`, `isCallMode`: anything but `MODE_NORMAL`, i.e. ringing, a phone call, a voice or video chat) before it starts (then it stays silent) and every 25 chunks (4× a second) while it plays, and stops; it doesn't come back by itself after the call. Another app taking focus doesn't stop it. `addOnModeChangedListener` would tell sooner, but only from Android 12; the check costs one binder call per 250 ms on the writer thread. The reference tone keeps its transient focus ([Notes, tunings and audio](notes-and-audio.md), *Reference tone*): tuning by ear needs the tone alone.
- **Headphones unplugged** (`ACTION_AUDIO_BECOMING_NOISY`, also when a Bluetooth headset goes): a receiver registered for each session (not exported from Android 13; only the system sends it) stops the clicks, so they never carry on out of the speaker, as music players do.
- **Beats for the screen** (`beats: SharedFlow<MetronomeBeat>`, with `index`, `beatInBar`, `accent`, `heardAtNanos` on the `System.nanoTime()` clock, the `sound` it clicked in, and since Phase 62 the `bpm` it plays at, the speed trainer's `trainerBar` and `clicksPerBeat`, the subdivision that beat is split into as the click track sets it, so the beat lights light each click between the beats at j / k of the beat after its heard time without a flow of their own): each rendered beat waits in a small ring; once `AudioTrack.getTimestamp` runs, its moment is known about one buffer ahead and a timer emits it then (the writer thread only wakes when the speaker takes a block, every 10–40 ms, which told beats up to 35 ms late); before the first timestamp it is emitted when the playback head reaches it. So beat lights and vibration can match the sound. `FakeMetronomePlayer` (`testFixtures`) emits beats on request, climbing as the click track does when a trainer is on.
- **Tap tempo** (`TapTempo`, pure, allocation-free): the tempo of the last taps, the time from the oldest of the last six to the newest over the intervals between them (so one late tap only shifts it by its share), whole BPM within 30–250; a pause of more than 2 s (slower than 30 BPM) starts afresh. A tap counts when the finger touches, not when it lifts. **Tempo markings** (`TempoMarking`): the Italian name under the tempo, from Grave (under 40) through Largo, Larghetto, Adagio, Andante, Moderato, Allegro (120–167), Presto to Prestissimo (200 and up), the mock-up's bounds. `TapTempoTest`.
- **Measured on the Xiaomi 12X** (`AndroidMetronomePlayerTest`, 240 BPM): a float track at 48 kHz, no underruns; beats heard 250 ms apart within ±0.02 ms by the output's timestamps, and told 2–4 ms after their sound.
- **Ten minutes on the Xiaomi 12X** (`scripts/metronome-drift.sh`, on demand: `MetronomeDriftMeasurement` in `:core:audio`'s instrumented tests, skipped unless `-a metronomeDrift=true`; the real player at 120 BPM in 4/4 at media volume 1 of 150, put back after; 1,200 beats, 2026-10-14):
  - *Sample clock*: every beat of ten minutes rendered at 48 kHz in the player's 10 ms chunks lands on its ideal sample (largest error 0.000 samples): no drift, by construction.
  - *Speaker clock against the system clock*: the heard moments fitted to a line give one beat every 500.000005 ms of `System.nanoTime()`, **0.0 ppm** (+0.1 ppm in a one-minute run); after 1, 5 and 10 minutes the beats are +0.02, −0.01 and +0.00 ms from a perfect wall-clock grid. The output's frame clock and the system clock come from the same crystal on this phone, so the metronome keeps time with the phone's clock, not just with itself.
  - *Jitter of the reports*: heard gaps 500 ms −0.28 … +0.26 ms (rms 0.05 ms), scatter about the line at most 0.27 ms (rms 0.04 ms), which is the timestamps' own noise, not the sound; each beat reaches the screen 1.0–6.0 ms after its sound (median 2.4, 95 % within 3.4). No missed beats, no underruns.
  - Not measured acoustically: the microphone runs off the same audio clock, so recording the clicks could not tell more than the timestamps do. The owner's play-along check is under *Owner checks* in ROADMAP.md.
- **Subdivisions and the speed trainer on the Xiaomi 12X** (Phase 62, 2026-10-31; on demand: `MetronomeSubdivisionCheck` in `:core:audio`'s instrumented tests, skipped unless `-a metronomeSubdivisions=true`, run with `MODULE_TIMEOUT=900 scripts/ui-tests.sh :core:audio -c MetronomeSubdivisionCheck -a metronomeSubdivisions=true`, about 6 minutes; the real player at media volume 1 of 150, put back after):

## Tempo from music
The metronome listens to music nearby through the microphone and finds its tempo. The detector is pure `:core:dsp` (`metronome/`): `TempoDetector` (interface) and `OnsetTempoDetector(sampleRate)`, any rate from 8 kHz. The metronome screen's Listen uses it ([Metronome, widget and watch](ui-other-screens.md), *Metronome screen*).
- **Feeding and reading.** `process(samples, offset, length)` takes the recorder's chunks as they come (any size; the tuner's one-hop 1024-sample chunks at 48 kHz are the usual), allocates nothing, and is not thread-safe (one thread feeds and reads). Every 0.25 s of audio it updates: `status` (`LISTENING` for the first 3 s, then `NO_BEAT`, `SETTLING` or `STEADY`), `bpm` (whole, 30–250, 0 for none), `exactBpm`, `halfBpm` / `doubleBpm` (0 out of range) and `alternativeBpm` (whichever of the two the music came closer to), `confidence` 0..1, `steadySeconds`, and the beat grid: `lastBeatSample` (counted from the first sample fed, −1 for none) and `beatIntervalSamples`, so the next beat is due at their sum. `reset()` starts afresh.
- **Onset strength** (Bello et al. 2005; Ellis 2007): every ≈ 5 ms (a hop of `round(rate / 200)` samples: 240 at 48 kHz) a Hann-windowed FFT of the last ≈ 21 ms (1024 samples at 44.1–48 kHz, 256 at 8 kHz) is summed into log-spaced bands, three per octave from 40 Hz to 12 kHz (each at least two bins), in dB with a −70 dB floor; the envelope is the sum of each band's rise since the previous frame (half-wave rectified spectral flux), high-passed at 0.4 Hz and smoothed over 25 ms.
- **Tempo** (Scheirer 1998; Ellis 2007): the autocorrelation of the last 8 s of envelope (4096-point FFT, unbiased, lags up to 4.5 s) is scored at every whole tempo by its peaks at the beat lag and up to 4 multiples, weighted by a log-Gaussian around 120 BPM 1.5 octaves wide. The winner's period is refined from the peaks at up to 16 multiples (parabolic interpolation, least-squares slope through the origin), far finer than 1 BPM.
- **Half or double.** Every doubling repeats as well, so the beat is picked from the envelope: from the slowest level in range, the tempo doubles while the in-between beats are nearly as strong as the beats (the best-fitting grid at twice the tempo over the last 6 s, its two interleaved halves compared; each point averaged over ±10 ms so a loose player still counts). "Nearly" is a ratio of 0.15 for a faster level up to 80 BPM, rising through 0.35 (120), 0.5 (160), 0.55 (200) to 0.6 (250), with ±0.07 in favour of the level already shown. So a click every 2 s is 30, not 60; even clicks at 250 are 250; kick, snare and eighth-note hi-hat at 120 stay 120; swung eighths never count as beats. Rock at 50 with eighth-note hi-hats reads as 100, a fair hearing that the screen's ½× / 2× choice covers.
- **Steady to show**: the estimate is the median of the last 5 updates; `bpm` only moves once it is 0.75 BPM away; `STEADY` once the estimate has stayed within ±1 BPM for 4 s. Confidence (the comb score at the chosen tempo) must reach 0.35 for a tempo, and falls back to "no beat" under 0.25 (click track ≈ 0.95, drums 0.6–0.95, white noise ≤ 0.16).
- **Beat grid**: the phase whose beats over the last 6 s are strongest at the estimate's period (parabolic between envelope samples), moved back by the measured onset delay (2 hops of smoothing plus 0.3 of the FFT window). Within ±6 ms of the true beats for clicks, drums and plucks at 45–250 BPM and 16–48 kHz.
- **Measured** (`OnsetTempoDetectorTest`, 14 s of audio each, white noise at 10–15 dB SNR): click tracks at 18 tempos from 30 to 250 (97.3 included), the metronome's own accented clicks, rock and four-on-the-floor grooves with chords from 70 to 200, a plucked bass line without drums, swing with an 8 ms loose drummer, clicks 12 ms loose, 8–96 kHz: all within ±1 BPM, most within 0.1; a first tempo by 3–5 s, steady by 7–10 s, at most one change of the shown number; a change from 100 to 130 BPM followed within 8 s; silence, quiet and loud noise and a held chord never show a tempo. `TempoAccuracyReport` (with the benchmarks): 52 of 54 cases within ±1 BPM at 16 s (largest error 0.09 BPM), the other 2 at double tempo (rock and four-on-the-floor at 50).
- **CPU**: 0.10 ms per 1024-sample chunk on average on the JVM (0.07 median, 0.35 for the one chunk in twelve that runs an estimate), 0.5 % of a core while listening (`TempoDetectorBenchmark`; `DspPhoneBenchmark` has the case for the phone).
