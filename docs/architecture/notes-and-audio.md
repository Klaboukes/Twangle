## Note math
```
midi  = 69 + 12 · log2(f / A4)        (A4 default 440 Hz, configurable 415–466)
note  = ceil(midi − 0.5)               (round half down, so exactly half-way reads +50)
cents = 100 · (midi − note)            range (−50, +50]
```
Scientific pitch notation (MIDI 60 = C4). `Note` is a value class over the MIDI number. `Notation` (`SHARPS`, `FLATS`, `SOLFEGE`, `GERMAN`) names a pitch class with real ♯ and ♭ (C♯ / D♭ / Do♯; fixed-do, B is Si); each entry holds its names in both spellings so `nameOf()` returns the same instances every frame. `GERMAN` calls B "H" and B♭ "B" (Half step down reads E♭ A♭ D♭ G♭ B E♭); its other black keys keep the signs (C♯, E♭), as German players write them even though they say Cis and Es, so only the B/H letters differ from `SHARPS`/`FLATS`.

**Notation by language.** The notation is a default that follows the app's language until the player picks one. `Notation.forLanguage(language)` (`:core:dsp`, JVM-tested) gives `GERMAN` for `de`/`pl`/`ru` (Polish and Russian players write H for B too), `SOLFEGE` for `fr`/`it`/`es`/`pt`/`tr`/`ar` (Turkish and Arab players learn Do Re Mi; Arabic shows the Latin syllables, like every note name, and TalkBack says them in Arabic script), and `SHARPS` for every other language (a new language with its own habit is one line there; owner's rule: what is most common in that country). The saved setting is `AppSettings.notationChoice: Notation?`: null (no saved key) means Automatic, so existing installs that never touched the setting simply get their language's default, and an explicit pick, including the one that equals the default, sticks across language changes. `AppSettings.notationFor(language)` resolves it. The language comes from the screen's configuration (`appLanguage()` in `:core:designsystem`, `LocalConfiguration.locales[0].language`, which follows the per-app language): `TunerViewModel` takes it at creation (so the first frame is right) and the screen passes changes with `setLanguage`; the tuning editor and Settings read it directly. Settings lists Automatic first, labelled with a sample of what it gives ("Automatic: C, C♯, D … B", "Automatique : Do, Do♯, Re … Si", "Automatisch: C, C♯, D … H"; built from `settings_notation_sample`, B's name last because that's where the notations differ); choosing it clears the key. The watch has no notation setting and follows its own locale (`WatchNoteName.letter`), drawing a solfège syllable smaller (`sizeFor`) so "Sol♯4" fits the ring. The home-screen widget shows no note names.

**Tuning spelling.** A preset is written the way players write it, and that wins over the sharps/flats setting: Half step down is E♭ A♭ D♭ G♭ B♭ E♭, Open D keeps F♯. `Tuning.spelling` comes from how the preset is written in `Tunings` (`"Eb2"` flats, `"F#3"` sharps; mixing is refused), and `Notation.nameIn(tuning, …)` applies it in the string row, tuning menu, hint and big note in guitar mode. Chromatic mode follows the setting alone.

**Auto (guitar) mode** targets the closest string of the tuning, in cents; **Chromatic** the nearest equal-tempered note.

## Tunings
`Tuning(id, strings)` lists open-string `Note`s lowest first; `id` is the saved key (never rename). Names live in string resources, so `:core:dsp` has no UI text. `Tunings` holds the presets (Standard, Drop D, Half-step down, Full-step down, DADGAD, Open G, Open D, Open E), `DEFAULT` = Standard, `byId()` to restore.
- `nearestStringIndex(midi)` follows A4 calibration; smallest |Δ| in semitones, ties to the lower string, no allocation.
- `centsFromString(midi, index)` is **not** wrapped to ±50, so a string far off shows the full distance.
- Strings are in instrument order, thickest first. That is lowest pitch first except on re-entrant tunings (standard ukulele G4 C4 E4 A4), so `lowestNote` / `highestNote` scan all strings.

## Instruments
`Instrument(id, stringCount, tunings, voice)` is a stringed instrument with its presets, the first being its standard tuning (`defaultTuning`), and its `voice`, how its strings sound when the tuner plays a reference tone (*Reference tone* below); every preset must have `stringCount` strings. `Instruments` holds them in picker order, `DEFAULT` = six-string guitar, whose presets are exactly `Tunings.all` with their original ids, so saved settings keep working. Tuning ids are unique across instruments (`<instrument>_standard`, `ukulele_low_g`), so `Instruments.tuningById()` / `ofTuning()` restore a saved tuning and its instrument. A mandolin counts its four pairs as four courses.

| Instrument (`id`) | Strings | Presets | Range |
|---|---|---|---|
| Guitar (`guitar`) | 6 | the eight `Tunings` | D2–E4 |
| 7-string guitar (`guitar_7`) | 7 | B1 E2 A2 D3 G3 B3 E4 | B1–E4 |
| 8-string guitar (`guitar_8`) | 8 | F♯1 B1 E2 A2 D3 G3 B3 E4 | F♯1–E4 |
| Bass (`bass_4`) | 4 | E1 A1 D2 G2 | E1–G2 |
| 5-string bass (`bass_5`) | 5 | B0 E1 A1 D2 G2 | B0 (30.9 Hz)–G2 |
| Ukulele (`ukulele`) | 4 | G4 C4 E4 A4 (re-entrant), low G: G3 C4 E4 A4 | G3–A4 |
| Violin (`violin`) | 4 | G3 D4 A4 E5 | G3–E5 (659 Hz) |
| Viola (`viola`) | 4 | C3 G3 D4 A4 | C3–A4 |
| Cello (`cello`) | 4 | C2 G2 D3 A3 | C2–A3 |
| Mandolin (`mandolin`) | 4 courses | G3 D4 A4 E5 | G3–E5 |

`lowestNote` / `highestNote` span all of an instrument's presets. Tunings whose lowest string goes below the guitar's range (B1, F♯1, E1, B0, and the cello's C2) switch the detector to the low-note window ([Signal pipeline](signal-pipeline.md), *Low notes*). Players pick the instrument and its tuning in one panel ([Tuner screen and tour](ui-tuner.md), **Instrument and tuning**); the instrument is saved with the tuning ([Lifecycle](lifecycle.md), *Saved settings*), and the panel lists only its presets.

**Names** (`PickerNames.kt`, `instrumentNameRes` / `tuningNameRes`, JVM-tested: every instrument and preset has one, none shared within an instrument): English and Dutch — Guitar/Gitaar, 7-string guitar/7-snarige gitaar, 8-string guitar/8-snarige gitaar, Bass/Bas, 5-string bass/5-snarige bas, Ukulele/Ukelele, Violin/Viool, Viola/Altviool, Cello, Mandolin/Mandoline. Every instrument's first preset is "Standard"; the ukulele's other is "Low G"/"Lage G".

## Chord shapes and scales
Pure `:core:dsp` music maths for the Practice tab's charts ([Metronome, widget and watch](ui-other-screens.md), *Practice tab*), worked out for whatever tuning is picked, a preset or the player's own, so a DADGAD player sees DADGAD shapes. Nothing is stored: a chord's shapes take well under a millisecond, so the screen asks again when the chord or tuning changes.

**Chord shapes** (`chord/ChordShapeFinder.shapesOf(chord, tuning, limit = 6)` → `List<ChordShape>`, easiest first). A `ChordShape` holds per string, in instrument order (thickest first), `frets` (`ChordShape.MUTED` = −1, 0 open, else the fret) and `fingers` (1 index … 4 little, 0 for open or muted), and an optional `Barre(fret, firstString, lastString, finger = 1)`. It writes itself as chord books do (`toString()`: "x32010", frets from 10 in brackets), gives `notesIn(tuning)` / `bassIn(tuning)`, and `startFret()` for the chart: 1 (drawn with the nut) when it fits the first four fret rows (`CHART_ROWS`), else its lowest fret ("3fr").
- **Search:** every string muted, open (when its open note is a chord tone) or pressed on a chord tone within `MAX_SPAN` = 3 frets above the lowest fret pressed (four frets in all), up to the 12th. A shape sounds every chord tone and nothing else (7th and 9th chords may drop the fifth), mutes strings only on the thick side and plays at least four (all of them on four-string instruments). On five strings or more the bass is the root, except an open tuning's own chord: every string open or under one barre with the fifth in the bass (open G's G is 000000, its C 555555). On four strings an inversion is allowed (ukulele G 0232) but ranks lower. A chord with no such shape on five strings or more is looked for again with the top string muted too (Phase 49: D♭maj9 x4354x, the only one in standard tuning). **Power chords** (Phase 49) are their own search: two or three neighbouring strings, the rest muted, the root lowest and on one of the thick strings (the lowest three of a guitar, the first two on four strings): E5 022xxx, F5 133xxx, G5 355xxx, A5 x022xx, C5 x355xx, D5 xx023x; ukulele C5 00xx.
- **Fingers:** one finger per fret from the 1st fret when open strings ring and that fits exactly (C x32010 = 3 2 1), else from the lowest fret pressed. A barre when more than four strings are pressed (the E and A shapes, F 133211, Bm x24432), or across the last two strings or more pressed side by side at the lowest fret (xx3211, ukulele B♭ 3211). Five strings pressed without that barre may take a short one by a later finger across the last strings at the highest fret, at most two frets above the lowest (Phase 49: Cm9 x31333, index on the D string, middle on the A, ring across G, B and E; E♭m9 x64666). Never more than four fingers, no thumb.
- **Ranking** (`effortOf`, lower is easier): 1 a finger, 0.5 for a barre (1.5 by a finger other than the index, so C6 stays x32210 before x35555), 0.5 a fret of stretch, 0.5 a fret up the neck, 0.7 a string left out, 0.25 for a fifth left out, 1 for an inversion, 2 a fret for open strings ringing while a finger is above the 3rd fret, 1 a fret the fingers climb while arching over open strings (`archOf`); a power chord's muted strings cost nothing, but one on two strings 2 more (E5 022xxx before 02xxxx). Weighed by reading every chord on every preset (`ChordShapeReport`, print-only, `./gradlew :core:dsp:benchmark --tests '*ChordShapeReport*'`) until the first shape is the one chord books show first: standard C x32010, G 320003, D xx0232, A x02220, E 022100, Am x02210, Em 022000, Dm xx0231, F 133211, Bm x24432, E7 020100, C7 x32310, Cmaj7 x32000, Fmaj7 xx3210, Dsus4 xx0233, Cm9 x31333, Em9 020002, Am9 575557, Cmaj9 x30000, Fmaj9 102010, E7♯9 020133; ukulele C 0003, G 0232, F 2010, Am 2000; mandolin G 0023; DADGAD D 004200.
- **Tests** (`ChordShapeFinderTest`): those first shapes and their fingers, barres and chart start, open tunings and a tuning of the player's own, and for every chord on every preset of every instrument: at least one shape, only chord tones and all the needed ones, within the span, at most four fingers, a finger exactly on each pressed string, the root in the bass on five strings or more. Every tuning has every root's chords of up to four tones; the five-tone 9ths and the chart-only chords are asked for only on the strummed instruments' standard tunings (a few lie out of reach elsewhere, DADGAD's C♯m9, and a ukulele has no G♯m9 or F♯maj9 with the root in it). Power chords on every preset: two or three neighbouring strings, the root lowest, on the thick strings.

**Scales** (`scale/Scale`, `scale/ScaleFinder`). `ScaleType`, twelve since Phase 47 and fourteen since Phase 49: the seven modes of the major scale, Ionian (`MAJOR`), Dorian, Phrygian, Lydian, Mixolydian, Aeolian (`NATURAL_MINOR`, shown as "minor") and Locrian; harmonic minor (natural minor with a raised 7th) and melodic minor (the ascending form, natural minor with a raised 6th and 7th, as jazz plays it both ways); the major and minor pentatonic, the blues (the minor pentatonic with the flat fifth) and, since Phase 49, major blues (its brighter twin, the major pentatonic with the minor 3rd: 1 2 ♭3 3 5 6, country and major-key blues) and Phrygian dominant (Phrygian with a major 3rd, 1 ♭2 3 4 5 ♭6 ♭7, the 5th mode of harmonic minor: flamenco, the Middle-Eastern sound, metal). In one list, the panel's order, the most used first (the owner's follow-up of 2026-10-26, which also dropped the three groups, `ScaleGroup`): major, minor, the two pentatonics, blues, major blues, Dorian, Mixolydian, harmonic minor, Lydian, Phrygian, Phrygian dominant, melodic minor, Locrian (Phase 49 put the two new ones beside their kin, which moved Lydian ahead of Phrygian). Saved by name, never by place, so the order may change: the five of before Phase 47 kept theirs (major and natural minor are the Ionian and Aeolian entries) and a saved choice still opens. `Scale(root, type)` gives its `notes` from the root up, `degreeOf` (1 = root), `colourNote` and `nameOf(pitchClass, notation)`, whatever the notation's own sign:
- **Spelling:** each type names the key it is written in (`ScaleType.key`, a `SpellingKey`, in place of the old `minorKey` flag), and its sign comes from `music/KeySpelling` as chord symbols use. A mode is written in its parent major key (`SpellingKey.major(tonicStep)`, `KeySpelling.ofMode`): D Dorian in C, F♯ Dorian with E major's sharps, B♭ Mixolydian with E♭ major's flats, E Locrian with F major's B♭. The one parent with six signs (F♯ = G♭ major) writes each mode as its root's own key instead, major or minor by the mode's third: G♯ Dorian, E♭ Aeolian, F Locrian (F G♭ …, never F F♯ …), D♭ Mixolydian; so major and natural minor come out exactly as `ofMajorKey` and `ofMinorKey` wrote them before. The major pentatonic and major blues are written in the major key, the minor pentatonic, the blues and the two minors in the minor key (`SpellingKey.MINOR`), and Phrygian dominant in the minor key a fourth above (`SpellingKey.minor(5)`), as the harmonic minor it is the 5th mode of: E Phrygian dominant in A minor, A in D minor. A step with a sign of its own (`ScaleType.signs`) keeps it where it falls on a black key: the blues' blue note always flat (E blues E G A B♭ B D), and major blues' too (G major blues G A B♭ B D E; A major blues A B C C♯ E F♯), the minors' raised 6th and 7th sharp (D harmonic minor D E F G A B♭ C♯, A melodic minor A B C D E F♯ G♯; in a flat key such as C minor they fall on white keys, B natural), and Phrygian dominant's major 3rd, its harmonic minor's raised 7th, sharp (E F G♯ A B C D, A B♭ C♯ D E F G, D E♭ F♯ G A B♭ C). As for chords, a note the key would write with a sign there is no name for (E♯, B♯, C♭) takes the name of the same note (F, C, B).
- **Colour note** (`ScaleType.colourStep`, `Scale.colourNote`; the owner's pick F of Phase 47): the note that makes a mode sound as it does, ringed on the neck: Dorian's 6th, Phrygian's ♭2, Lydian's ♯4, Mixolydian's ♭7, Locrian's ♭5, harmonic minor's raised 7th, its leading note, as standard as those, and Phrygian dominant's major 3rd (Phase 49), the note it differs from Phrygian in. None for Ionian and Aeolian, the major and natural minor the others are heard against, nor for melodic minor, which differs from natural minor in two notes rather than one, nor for the pentatonics and the two blues scales.
- **On the neck:** `ScaleFinder.notesOf(scale, tuning, highestFret = 12)` → `List<FretNote(string, fret, note, degree)>`, string by string in instrument order, each from the nut up, `isRoot` for the dots filled in the accent: the whole neck from the open strings to the 12th fret, as the owner picked (*Scale*, I).
- **Tests** (`ScaleTest`): the spelling of every type on a few roots (each mode, harmonic and melodic minor, the six-sign key's modes), every mode on every root holding its parent major scale's notes and names, major and natural minor spelled as before, the colour notes (in the scale, never the root, none where there is none), the panel's order with every saved name still meaning its scale, the notation, E minor pentatonic on standard guitar (roots and count), and that on every preset and a tuning of the player's own exactly the scale's notes are found.

## Capo
Pure `:core:dsp` maths for the Practice tab's Capo helper (Phase 33; its screen: [Metronome, widget and watch](ui-other-screens.md), *Capo helper*): "the song is in E♭, where does the capo go to play G shapes?", and which chord each shape sounds with the capo on. It reuses the chord model (`Chord`, `ChordSpelling`) and the key signs (`KeySpelling`); nothing is stored.
- **`capo/SongKey(tonic, minor)`**: a key. Its `tonicChord`, its `scale` (major or natural minor), its sign (`accidental`, from `KeySpelling`) and `symbolOf(notation)` ("E♭", "Cm", "Hm", "Mi♭"), so keys read like chord symbols: E♭ not D♯, F♯ and E♭m with six signs.
- **The fret:** a capo raises every string by the same number of frets, one equal-tempered semitone each, so `Capo.fretFor(songKey, shapeTonic) = (song tonic − shape tonic) mod 12`, 0 to `HIGHEST_FRET` = 11 (12 sounds an octave up, the same chords as no capo). Shapes only move up, so a song below the shapes wraps to the next fret round: E♭ is D shapes at 1, C at 3, A at 6, G at 8, E at 11. Minor songs keep minor shapes (Cm with Am shapes at 3).
- **`Capo.optionsFor(songKey)`**: one `CapoPlan` per common shape key, lowest fret first, so the first is the one to suggest (no capo for a song in an open key, else the capo nearest the nut, at most fret 2 for a major song and 4 for a minor one). Major songs get the CAGED keys (`COMMON_MAJOR_SHAPES`: C, A, G, E, D), minor songs the open minor keys (`COMMON_MINOR_SHAPES`: Am, Em, Dm). `Capo.planFor(songKey, shapeTonic)` is the plan for shapes the player picks.
- **`CapoPlan(songKey, fret)`**: `shapeKey` (the song moved down by the fret), `soundingOf(shape)` and `shapeOf(sounding)` (`Chord.transposed(±fret)`; `PitchClass.transposed` wraps round the octave), and the names: `soundingSymbolOf(shape, notation, shapeBass)` in the song's key, `shapeSymbolOf(…)` in the shape key, the bass moving up with the capo (a C/E shape at 3 sounds E♭/G). `Capo.soundingSymbolOf(shape, fret, notation)` names a sounding chord when the key isn't known, in the chord's usual spelling (`ChordSpelling`).
- **Spelling in a key** (`SongKey.symbolOf(chord, bass, notation)`): a root or bass that belongs to the key takes the key's sign, one from outside the chord's usual spelling. So F♯ major's vi is A♯m and D♭ major's IV G♭ (where `ChordSpelling` alone writes B♭m and F♯), while D major's borrowed chord stays B♭. A song in F with the capo at 1 reads shapes E A B C♯m (E's spelling) sounding F B♭ C Dm.
- **Tests** (`CapoTest`): the frets and options for major and minor songs, every key with every shape key, spelling in the key and outside it, the bass, every notation (Mi♭, German B and H), and that `shapeOf` undoes `soundingOf` for every chord at every fret.

## Intonation check
Phase 29 (live in v1.0 since Phase 34). The 12th fret halves the string, so it should sound exactly one octave (1200 cents) above the open string. If it is sharp, the sounding length from the fret to the saddle is too short and the saddle moves back, away from the neck; if flat, forward. `intonation/Intonation` in `:core:dsp` is the comparison, pure and JVM-tested:
- `offsetCents(openHz, fretHz) = 1200 · log2(fret / open) − 1200` (positive = sharp).
- `verdictOf(offsetCents, toleranceCents)` gives `FINE`, `MOVE_SADDLE_BACK` (sharp), `MOVE_SADDLE_FORWARD` (flat) or `NOT_THE_OCTAVE`; `check(openHz, fretHz)` returns both as an `IntonationResult`.

**Tolerance: 3 cents** (`DEFAULT_TOLERANCE_CENTS`), its own number, not the tuner's Precise/Relaxed in-tune setting. Setup practice aims for the 12th fret within a few cents of the octave. Each reading is within ±1 cent, so a difference can be 2 cents off, and 3 keeps a well set-up guitar from being told to move. With the open string retuned, moving the saddle back by `d` on a scale of length `L` changes the 12th fret by `1200 · log2((L + d) / (L + 2d)) ≈ −1731 · d / L` cents (frequency ∝ 1 / length): **≈ 2.7 cents per mm** on a 648 mm (25.5″) scale, so 3 cents is about a millimetre of saddle.

**Not the octave:** more than **50 cents** (half a semitone) from the octave, the fretted note is nearer another note: another fret, another string, the open string again, or a misread. Real intonation errors stay far below that (50 cents is a saddle ≈ 20 mm off). A light touch at the 12th fret (the harmonic) sounds the exact octave and reads `FINE`; no reading can tell it from a fretted note, so the mode asks for a pressed note.

**Relative.** Only the two readings are compared, so it works at any A4 and on any instrument's strings, and an open string a little out of tune still gets the right verdict. Players tune first anyway (the open string's tension sets the intonation); the mode checks the open reading is near the chosen string before taking it.

**Readings.** One settled frequency per step, from the tuner's smoothed reading; collecting it is the mode's job: wait until the note is steady, then take the median of what the tuner shows for about a second (`MedianFilter`), so the attack and the dying end don't count.

**Collecting a reading** (`SettledPitch`, `:core:dsp`, JVM-tested): fed the tuner's smoothed pitch each frame (a `PitchSmoother`, as the tuner's), it waits until the note is steady (0.25 s of readings each within 20 cents of the one before: the pluck's glide is over), then keeps 1 s of readings within 20 cents of the first kept one and takes their median; a jump further, or more than 0.15 s without a reading, starts over. Its arrays are allocated once (hard rule 2). `IntonationInterpreter` (`:feature:tuner`) goes through the steps with it:
- **Open:** a reading an octave off the chosen string (a weak fundamental read high) is folded onto it. Taken within **10 cents** of the string (`OPEN_TOLERANCE_CENTS`: 10 cents is 1.2 % of tension, and the comparison is relative anyway); further off, the line asks to tune the string first, with how far off it is, and the next steady note is taken instead.
- **12th fret:** readings within 50 cents of the open note are the open string still ringing (or played again) and are ignored; so is the open string where it was heard an octave up, until a new pluck (a dropout, or a frame 6 dB louder than the one before). A reading two octaves up is folded onto the octave. Not the octave: the line says so and waits for the fret again; otherwise the verdict, kept per string (`IntonationResults`, held by the ViewModel across listening sessions, cleared by a new tuning).

**Millimetres** (`SaddleMove`, `:core:dsp`, JVM-tested): a 12th fret `c` cents sharp needs the saddle back by `d` with `(L + d) / (L + 2d) = 2^(−c / 1200)`, so `d = L · (1 − r) / (2r − 1)` with `r = 2^(−c / 1200)`; for small `c` that is `d ≈ c · L · ln 2 / 1200`, 2.7 cents per mm on 648 mm. Twangle doesn't know the scale length, so millimetres show only for the guitar family (`IntonationInstruments.scaleLengthMm`: 648 mm, 25.5″), whose scales lie within a few percent of it (24″ 610 mm to 27″ 686 mm move a 3 mm answer by under 0.2 mm), rounded to the nearest 0.5 mm, and below 0.5 mm as "less than 0.5 mm". Basses (760–890 mm), ukuleles (330–480 mm) and mandolins (a bridge moved whole) get the direction only. The check is offered only on fretted instruments with a saddle (`IntonationInstruments.offers`: guitars, basses, ukulele, mandolin; not the violin, viola or cello, which have no frets). The tuner's view: [Tuner screen and tour](ui-tuner.md), *12th-fret check view*.

**Measured** (`IntonationTest`, synthetic plucks, 48 kHz): every string of every instrument's standard tuning (B0 to E5, the low window where the instrument needs it), the 12th fret ±4, ±8 and ±25 cents off and within ±1.5: every verdict right, the offset within **0.38 cents** from one frame each (0.19 for the guitar's strings from the median of 1.5 s through the tuner chain). The 11th and 13th frets, the 7th fret, the open string again, a fifth above the octave and an octave plus 55 cents: all `NOT_THE_OCTAVE`. *Known limit (modelled 2026-10-24, Phase 46 in ROADMAP.md):* those plucks have exact harmonics. A real string is stiff, and the fretted note, half as long, is four times as stiff, so YIN's reading of it is pulled sharp more than the open string's ([Signal pipeline](signal-pipeline.md), *Pitch detection*): with the strum tests' stiffness and a pick attack the check would report a well set-up low E as +4.3 cents ("move the saddle back 1.5 mm"), the A +3.2 and the G +3.1, past the 3-cent tolerance; the D, B and high E stay within it, and a finger pluck keeps every string under 1 cent. The owner's live check passed (2026-10-17); the fix is the tuner's, in Phase 46.

## Strobe model
`StrobeMotion` (`strobe/`) is the drift of a strobe pattern, one animation frame at a time; every wave look runs one (via `WaveMotion`). A mechanical strobe drifts at the beat rate in Hz, so high strings move faster; here the drift comes from cents, so every string looks the same. `driftSpeed(cents)` in stripe periods per second: **0 within ±0.5 cent** (finer than the ±1 cent target, so an in-tune string stands perfectly still), then **0.4/s per cent beyond**, up to **4/s** from 10.5 cents; positive when sharp. It ignores the in-tune flag (2–5 cents wide) because the strobe exists to show the last cent. `advance(seconds, cents)` eases the shown cents towards the reading (rate 20/s) and moves `phase` (0..1, wrapping); a step is capped at 0.1 s. Without a reading the view stops calling it and the pattern freezes. Tests feed sines 0, ±1, ±3, ±5 and ±30 cents off every string through `TunerChain`.

## Pitch history
The pitch trail (Phase 31, behind its switch `ToolSwitches.pitchTrail`) draws the note over the last few seconds. Its data is `PitchHistory` (`trail/`): a ring of fixed arrays, allocated once, so the tuner's ~47 readings a second make no garbage. Its length is a parameter, **5 s by default** (`DEFAULT_SECONDS`); the look the owner picked (2026-10-17, [Tuner screen](ui-tuner.md), *Pitch trail view*) shows the last 4 s.
- **What an entry holds:** a time stamp (seconds), the note aimed at (a MIDI number), the cents off it and whether the tuner showed it in tune. A gap (silence, the app's own reference tone and its echo, or a tool such as strum check using the microphone) has target `NO_NOTE` and NaN cents, so the line breaks. The pitch is `target + cents / 100` semitones, so a look can draw cents around the note or the pitch on a scale of notes; a change of target shows where the tuner moved to another note or string.
- **Fed on the DSP thread:** `TunerViewModel` passes every tuner reading to `PitchTrailFeed` (in `:feature:tuner`) right after `TunerInterpreter`, before `looksLike` drops readings that look the same, so the trail gets every frame, not only visible changes. Time is the tuner's audio clock, one hop (≈ 21.3 ms) per reading, kept across listening sessions so it never goes back; each session clears the history (`clear` only moves the start, so it allocates nothing either). While the switch is off nothing is written. `targetMidiOf` takes the note from the reading: 12 · log2(f / A4) + 69 less its cents.
- **Read in the draw phase, like the strobe:** the view will walk the history in `drawBehind` on each animation frame, so a new reading never recomposes the screen. One writer, one reader on another thread, no lock: the writer fills a slot and then moves `end` on (a volatile write), so everything below the `end` a reader saw is complete. Entries have lasting numbers (`Long`), and a reader walks `firstWithin(seconds, end) until end` (or the inline `forEachWithin`, which allocates nothing). The ring holds 32 entries (≈ 0.7 s, `SLACK_ENTRIES`) more than a reader may walk, so nothing it walks is overwritten during a frame; a reader stalled longer than that asks `overwritten(first)` and skips the frame.
- **Tests:** `PitchHistoryTest` (entries back in order, only the last seconds walked, wrapping, clear, no allocation per push or walk, a reader on another thread never sees a torn entry) and `PitchTrailFeedTest` (the target note, gaps, the clock, a played A2 filling the history through the ViewModel, nothing while the switch is off).

## Audio source selection
1. `MediaRecorder.AudioSource.UNPROCESSED` if `PROPERTY_SUPPORT_AUDIO_SOURCE_UNPROCESSED` is `"true"`.
2. Otherwise `VOICE_RECOGNITION`, which must have AGC and noise suppression off.
3. Never `MIC` or `VOICE_COMMUNICATION`: their AGC and noise suppression distort pitch and level.

`AudioSourceSelector.candidates()` returns these in order; `AndroidAudioRecorder` opens the first whose `AudioRecord` initializes, so a failed `UNPROCESSED` still falls back.

**Bluetooth headsets.** A headset mic only records over SCO/HFP or LE Audio: 8 or 16 kHz, band-limited, often noise-suppressed. Twangle never asks for that route, and in case something else opened it, `InputDeviceSelector` pins the built-in mic with `setPreferredDevice` whenever a Bluetooth input is present. Wired headsets and USB interfaces keep normal routing. Without a built-in mic routing is left to Android; low-rate audio is still handled. *Not yet verified with a real headset* (owner check).

## Audio capture
`AudioRecorder.chunks()` is a cold `Flow<FloatArray>`: the mic opens when collection starts and is released when it stops. `AndroidAudioRecorder` reads one-hop chunks with `READ_BLOCKING` on its own single-thread dispatcher (buffer ≥ 4 chunks). To avoid ~47 arrays a second it fills a **pool of 4 arrays** behind a channel of capacity 2, so **a chunk is only valid until the collector takes the next one**: downstream code copies samples out inside `collect` and must not `buffer()` the chunks.

`PitchPipeline` (`:feature:tuner`, since it joins audio and DSP) collects the chunks into a `FrameAssembler` and runs gate, detector, `NoiseFloorTracker` and `Clarity` on `Dispatchers.Default`, emitting one `PitchReading(frequencyHz, confidence, levelDbfs, gateOpen, noiseFloorDbfs, snrDb, clarity)` (plus peak) per hop. The window follows the tuning (`readings(range)`, see [Signal pipeline](signal-pipeline.md), *Low notes*). DSP objects are created once per collection; only the small reading is new each frame.

`TunerViewModel` creates a `TunerInterpreter` per listening session, which feeds `PitchSmoother` and turns the smoothed MIDI into a `TunerUiState`, on the same coroutine as the detector. Mode and saved settings are combined into one immutable `TunerOptions` (mode, tuning, A4, locked string, notation, precision), rebuilt only on change. Listening waits until settings have loaded. Changes take effect on the next frame without reopening the mic; only a new A4 restarts the smoother.
- **Chromatic:** the smoother's note and cents.
- **Auto:** the closest string and `centsFromString`. The target is sticky: it changes only once another string is closer by more than 15 cents. A reading within 50 cents of an octave above or below the string being tuned, with no string within 50 cents of it, stays on that string, moved by the octave (same cents). As a G3 fades, its faint odd harmonics sink into the room's noise and no single frame can tell it from G4 any more; nearest to no string, it would jump to high E at +300 cents. A 12th-fret harmonic reads as its string this way too. Only the string already tracked is kept (from scratch an octave reads as its own note), and only one octave away, so in open D plucking D3 after D2 is the D3 string. A locked string takes an octave of itself the same way.
- **Auto, string locked:** tapping a string locks the target to it (`toggleString`, `TunerOptions.lockedString`); tapping again unlocks. Kept in memory only, survives a mode switch, released when the tuning changes.
- The frequency shown is the smoothed one (`NoteMapper.frequencyOf(smoother.midi)`).
- `InTuneDetector` (`:core:dsp`, `smoothing`, shared with the watch) decides "in tune" from smoothed cents and frame duration, and restarts when the note stops or the target changes. The phone's precision setting picks its window through `InTuneDetector.forPrecision` (an extension in `:feature:tuner`, since `:core:dsp` knows no settings).
- **Steady hint line** (the owner saw "Tune up/down" flicker near the note). Two pure layers, counted in frames:
  - `TuningCue` (`TunerHint.kt`) picks the *wanted* hint: `IN_TUNE` while `InTuneDetector` says so, else up or down from the rounded cents. Inside the in-tune window the previous direction is kept, so a wobbling reading doesn't swap; a note starting inside it shows no direction.
  - `SteadyHint<T : SteadyMessage>` adds timing: a message shows once wanted for its `settleSeconds` without a break and stays at least its `minShowSeconds`; an empty line after a message also stays 0.5 s. After a reset the first message shows at once. `IN_TUNE` settles at once (the detector already held 300 ms), `TUNE_UP` / `TUNE_DOWN` after 0.2 s; each stays ≥ 0.5 s.
  - `Detected.inTune = hint == IN_TUNE`, so colour, check mark, haptic tick, glow, TalkBack and hint always agree. Tips use a second `SteadyHint` (see [Signal pipeline](signal-pipeline.md), *Tips*).

Failures end the flow with a typed `AudioRecorderException`, mapped by `toTunerError()` to a `TunerError` that the UI shows with a "Try again" button:
- `PermissionMissing` → `MIC_PERMISSION_MISSING`.
- `InitializationFailed` (no input opens at the rate) → `MIC_UNAVAILABLE`.
- `MicrophoneBusy` → `MIC_BUSY`. Before Android 10 `startRecording()` fails. From 10 the mic is shared and an app with priority (a call, an assistant, another recorder in front) silences us with zeros instead, at the start or mid-session. `ChunkReader` (pure, behind a `MicInput`, so JVM-tested with a fake mic) ends the flow with `MicrophoneBusy` when (a) the recording is silenced: `isClientSilenced` right after `startRecording()`, then an `AudioRecordingCallback` (Android 10+, registered only while recording) that sets a volatile flag the read loop checks per chunk, or (b) **2 s of exact zeros** arrive: a muted or blocked mic (the privacy switch, another app muting it) delivers digital silence, while a working mic always has some noise, even in a quiet room. Without this the tuner said "Play a note" forever.
- `ReadFailed(code)` (e.g. `ERROR_DEAD_OBJECT` when the audio server restarts) → `MIC_STOPPED`; collecting again reopens the mic. Reads that keep returning nothing (50 in a row, 5 ms apart: ≈ 0.25 s) are `ReadFailed(0)` instead of a loop that spins forever. The players' `AudioTrack` writes use the same loop (`transferFully`): 50 empty writes in a row end their session quietly.

**Measured on the Galaxy S26 (Android 16):** no `UNPROCESSED`, so `VOICE_RECOGNITION` at 48 kHz with no effects attached; a 440 Hz tone read 439.98 Hz (−0.1 cents) at −31 dBFS; quiet room noise −52 to −56 dBFS, right at the gate.

## Reference tone
The app can play a string's note so it can be tuned by ear, in the voice of the picked instrument: a plucked guitar, bass, ukulele or mandolin string, a bowed violin, viola or cello string. `TonePlayer` (`:core:audio`) has `play(frequencyHz, voice)`, `stop()` and `isPlaying: StateFlow<Boolean>`; the caller turns the note into Hz with its `NoteMapper`, so the tone follows the A4 calibration, and passes the instrument's `StringVoice` (`Instrument.voice`).
- **Sound:** `StringSynth` (`:core:dsp`, pure) plays a note in a `StringVoice`: the presets in `StringVoices`, one per kind of instrument (steel-string guitar for the 6-, 7- and 8-string, electric bass for both basses, ukulele, mandolin, violin, viola, cello). Additive synthesis, one decaying phasor per partial turned by a fixed complex step a sample (exact pitch, no tables, no allocation), but what the partials are comes from the voice, after Fletcher & Rossing (*The Physics of Musical Instruments*, ch. 2) rather than a keyboard's 1/k:
  - *the string:* plucked at fraction β of its length from the bridge, partial k starts at `sin(kπβ) / k^tilt` (the pluck point's comb, kept with its sign, so a note starts as a pluck's pulse on the bridge and not as a sawtooth; tilt 1 for a pick, 1.3–1.6 for fingers), the bass's pickup adds its own comb, a two-pole roll-off above the voice's *brightness* stands for the width of a pick, finger or bow, and a bowed string has no comb (Helmholtz motion puts a sawtooth on the bridge whatever the bow's position);
  - *stiffness:* steel strings put partial k at `k·f·√(1 + B·k²)` (Fletcher 1964), B growing toward the low strings as `(f_ref / f)^1.45` and capped at 8·10⁻⁵, so a reference tone stays nearer harmonic than a real 8-string or 5-string bass string (which YIN reads several cents sharp early in the note, Phase 46); the fundamental is exact. Nylon (the ukulele) and the bowed voices have none;
  - *body:* each voice's main resonances (`BodyMode`: hz, q, dB; the air resonance A0 and the top's modes, the violin family's A0, B1−, B1+ and bridge hill, from the literature, rounded) multiply into the partials' levels, and as two-pole resonators they ring at a pluck (the knock of wood at the start of a note) and colour the bow's noise;
  - *decay:* a partial's time constant falls with its frequency (the voice's *sustain* at 100 Hz and at 2 kHz, 1/τ linear between), and every partial decays in two planes (Weinreich 1977): a share dies about three times faster, then the rest sings on, instead of one straight exponential;
  - *the start:* a plucked note begins a few cents sharp (4 on steel, 7 on nylon) and settles over tens of ms (the phasors' steps are turned every ms while any of the glide is left), under the pick's or finger's highpassed noise burst and the body's knock; a bowed note swells in over 90–120 ms with the bow's lowpassed noise under it, louder as the bow catches, and its level sways by up to ±6 % a few times a second, so it never stands still like an organ. No vibrato: an open string is tuned without it;
  - *courses:* the mandolin's pair of strings sit 2.4 cents apart, ±1.2 around the pitch (their mean exact), and beat as a mandolin's do.

  Partials stop below 8 kHz and 0.45 × the rate, at most 60 per course (a bass B0 keeps 60, up to 1.85 kHz, so a phone speaker that can't play 31 Hz still gives the pitch). The noise is the same every note (a fixed seed), so a tone is reproducible and its loudest sample known. No recordings: nothing to download (the voices are a few numbers each, the whole change a few KB of code), any pitch at any A4 exactly, and no licence questions.

- **Level and envelope:** 4 s from start to silence. The partials' levels (unsigned) add up to what the noise path leaves under 0.98 (the loudest the noise gets is found once, in the constructor), so even all aligned a tone can't clip; a note peaks near half of that, since a pluck's partials alternate in sign. A plucked note fades in over 4 ms and the noise ramps in over 2 ms, so the first sample is 0 and nothing clicks; a raised-cosine fade over the last 0.6 s ends on exactly 0, and in a bowed voice the bow lifts as it begins (the partials switch to the string's free decay). `release()` fades out over 30 ms from the current level; a new note releases the old one first.
- **Playback:** `AndroidTonePlayer` streams `ENCODING_PCM_FLOAT` mono at the phone's native output rate through an `AudioTrack` with `USAGE_MEDIA` / `CONTENT_TYPE_MUSIC`, so the media volume sets its loudness. The track exists only while a tone sounds. `AudioTrack.write` blocks, so a session runs on its own single-thread dispatcher and renders 10 ms chunks into one buffer (buffer ≥ 4 chunks) from a `StringSynth` per voice (made the first time that voice is asked for, on the writer thread, and kept); `play` and `stop` only set a request field under a lock and return. At the end the session pads one buffer of silence (a very short tone otherwise might never start), waits for the playback head to pass the last sample, releases the track, and only then `isPlaying` turns false. It turns true on `play`. A track that fails ends the session quietly (logged).
- **Playing a string** (`TunerViewModel.playString`): a long-press on a string button plays that string of the current tuning at the current A4 (`NoteMapper(a4Hz).frequencyOf(note)`) in the picked instrument's voice (`TunerOptions.voice`, from the saved instrument); a long-press on another string while one sounds switches to it. `soundingString` is the played string while `isPlaying`, else null. A tap on *any* string while a tone sounds only stops it (`toggleString` → `stopTone`): the lock stays as it was, so stopping the sound never changes what the tuner listens for, and the next tap locks as before. A new tuning, a switch to chromatic mode (no strings to show), the tuner leaving the screen (`ON_STOP`, or another screen taking its place) and `onCleared` all stop the tone too. A long-press gives Android's own long-press haptic (`combinedClickable`).
- **The mic ignores the tone:** the microphone stays open (hard rule 4 is unchanged: no reopening, no privacy-indicator flicker), but while `isPlaying` and for `TONE_TAIL_SECONDS` = 0.3 s after (counted in frames, for the room's echo) `listen()` sets the readings aside and emits the listening state; when the tail ends it starts a fresh `TunerInterpreter`, so no smoothing, hint or reception of the tone carries over.
- **Sounding string:** rings in the style's accent (`primary`) and the button's own shape grow out of it and fade, two at a time like sound waves, every 1.2 s (`soundingRings`, `StringSelector.kt`); drawn outside the clip in the draw phase, so nothing moves (`aSoundingStringMovesNothing`). With Android's animations off: one still ring. TalkBack: the long-press is the named action "Play A 2" (double-tap and hold, or the actions menu); the sounding string's state is "Playing" ("Locked, playing"), and every string's tap action reads "Stop the tone" while one sounds. In Dutch: "a 2 laten horen", "Klinkt", "Toon stoppen" (`dutchTalkBackCanPlayAStringAndHearsItSound`). `SoundingRingsTest` pictures the row on each style's background, light and dark, with and without a sounding string: the rings clearly show round that button in all six styles and change nothing elsewhere.
- **"Playing D3":** while a string's tone sounds, the tuner's hint line (where "Play a string" stands; the state is `Listening` then, since the mic is set aside) says "Playing D3" (`tuner_hint_playing`, NL "D3 klinkt"), the string named in the player's notation like the locked-string hint, and TalkBack hears "Playing D 3" through the note block's polite live region. It takes a reception tip's place for as long as the tone sounds, and goes back to "Play a string" (or "Play the A2 string") when the tone ends or is stopped (a few seconds: no flicker). The tour's strings step says how: "Tap to lock, hold to hear" ([Tuner screen and tour](ui-tuner.md), *Tour*). Tests: `aSoundingToneIsNamedInTheHintUntilItStops`, `aSoundingToneIsNamedInThePlayersNotation`, `aSoundingToneTakesATipsPlace` (`TunerContentTest`).
- **Audio focus:** each session asks for `AUDIOFOCUS_GAIN_TRANSIENT` (not `…_MAY_DUCK`: tuning by ear needs the tone alone, so another app's music pauses, and resumes when the session gives focus back as it ends). A loss (`LOSS`, `LOSS_TRANSIENT`: a call, another app that won't share) stops the tone like `stop()`; a loss that may duck (a notification) is left to Android, which lowers the tone by itself. Refused focus (during a call) means no tone: the session ends at once, quietly.
- **Volume:** the tone plays at the media volume. While the tuner is shown (`MainActivity`, a `DisposableEffect` in the tuner's branch) `volumeControlStream = STREAM_MUSIC`, so the volume keys set the tone's loudness even between tones; on Settings, About and the tuning editor the keys do what they do elsewhere. Only on the tuner, because only it makes sound. At media volume 0 a session shows the phone's own volume panel (`adjustStreamVolume(ADJUST_SAME, FLAG_SHOW_UI)`, the level unchanged), so a silent tone explains itself without a message of ours.
- **Measured on the Xiaomi 12X:** a long-press starts a `USAGE_MEDIA` float `AudioTrack` at 48 kHz (`dumpsys audio`), the tuner showed "Play a string" throughout (a run from before the "Playing A2" line), the track is gone about 4.3 s later, within 0.5 s of a stopping tap, and within a second of pressing Home.
- **Measured on the Xiaomi 12X (focus and volume):** a long-press holds `GAIN_TRANSIENT` focus with `USAGE_MEDIA` (`dumpsys audio`, focus stack) and gives it back when the tone ends; the volume keys reach `STREAM_MUSIC` on the tuner and the phone's default stream in Settings (`adjustSuggestedStreamVolume`); at media volume 0 MIUI's volume panel opens on the long-press. `VolumeKeysTest` checks the tuner's stream on the phone; Settings isn't, since the ever-moving tuner keeps a Compose test rule from going idle.

## Drone
Phase 48: a steady low note under the Scales tool, the scale's root alone or with its fifth and octave, that keeps sounding while the player plays over it. **The owner's pick (2026-10-26):** all four sounds of round 2 (`docs/design/drone-sounds-preview/`) are offered, with every voicing and *Lower*. `DroneSynth` (`:core:dsp`, package `drone`) makes it, `DronePlayer` (`:core:audio`) plays it, and a split ▶ / ■ button at the end of Scales' name row starts and stops it, with a panel for the sound, the notes and *Lower* ([Other screens](ui-other-screens.md), *Practice tab*, *Drone*).
- **API:** `DroneSynth(sampleRate, seed = DEFAULT_SEED)`; `play(rootHz, sound = TANPURA, voicing = FIFTH, lower = false)`, `release(seconds = 0.25)`, `render(out, offset, count): Int` (samples written: fewer than asked when a release ends, 0 once silent), `isSounding`, and `rootHz`, `sound`, `voicing`, `lower` (what was last asked). `DroneSound`: `TANPURA`, `HARMONIUM`, `FLUTE_ORGAN`, `WARM_PAD`, in the drone panel's order; `DroneVoicing`: `ROOT`, `FIFTH`, `FIFTH_OCTAVE`. Both are saved by name (`droneSound`, `droneVoicing`), so their constants are never renamed. Not thread-safe: the player's writer thread calls all of it, as `TonePlayer` does with `StringSynth`.
- **Pitch:** in Hz, so the drone follows A4: the caller turns the scale's root into Hz with its `NoteMapper`. `DroneSynth.rootNote(pitchClass)` places it in the drone's octave, **G♯2 (MIDI 44) up to G3 (55)**, D3 in its middle (as the candidates were heard), so C is C3, E is E3 and A is A2; `lower` puts the whole drone an octave below that (G♯1 to G2). Every partial is a phasor at its exact frequency, the fifth a pure 3:2 and the octave 2:1 (the drone itself never beats); where a note has detuned voices (the harmonium's reeds, the pad's five saws) they sit around it so their level-weighted average is exact. Roots 40–1000 Hz are accepted.
- **The sounds** are round 2's candidates (`DroneCandidates`, test sources), ported number for number (`DroneEngines.kt`), so they sound as the owner heard them: `DroneSynthReport` measures each against its candidate at D3, centroid 975 / 718 / 180 / 315 Hz against 961 / 717 / 180 / 303, energy above 400 Hz 62 / 52 / 10 / 23 % against 63 / 52 / 10 / 21 % (the tanpura's and the pad's random draws differ; the default seed is the one whose first seconds came closest).
  - *Tanpura:* four strings plucked one after the other every 1.5 s (±60 ms), on and on: Pa (0.75 × root; a Sa for `ROOT`), Sa, Sa (the Sa above for `FIFTH_OCTAVE`), low Sa (0.5 × root). Each pluck is made afresh from the seeded random (strength ±1.5 dB, jawari buzz 22 ±15 %, where the buzz starts ±0.2 octave, the phases turned), additive, partial k at 1/k rolled off above 5 kHz, decaying 5 s at 100 Hz to 0.8 s at 2 kHz, with the jawari's bloom moving down from the 24th partial to the 8th. A pluck rings 8 s and fades over its last 1.5 s (the string has been plucked again by then), so seven voices are always enough: they are reused, never made; partials past the bloom that have died below −100 dB are no longer turned, so a pluck gets cheaper as it rings. The first pluck comes 0.1 s after `play`.
  - *Harmonium:* two reeds a note, −3 and +3.75 cents (the upper at 0.8), partial k at 1/k^0.75 with the reed chamber's hills at 1.25 kHz (+9 dB) and 2.6 kHz (+5 dB), the bellows pressing in over 0.3 s, then ±12 % every 2.4 s (the notes of a chord 2.1 rad apart), the high partials brightening with them; air noise (a 2 kHz band, RMS held at the candidate's 0.126 at any rate, its own seeded generator per note).
  - *Flute organ:* a stopped pipe, odd partials mostly (1, 0.05, 0.32, 0.02, 0.1, 0.01, 0.03), speaking over 0.15 s, then still.
  - *Warm pad:* five saws at −13, −6, 0, +6, +13 cents (levels 0.25, 0.4, 1, 0.4, 0.25, random phases), through a two-pole low-pass (Q 1) whose corner sweeps 350 Hz → 2.4 kHz → 350 Hz every 7 s, swelling in over 1.5 s.

  The steady sounds' notes are the root (1), the fifth (0.7) and the octave (0.5).
- **Never a loop, never a seam:** nothing is a recorded or precomputed piece. `PartialBank` turns each partial's phasor by its exact complex step a sample and puts it back on the unit circle after every block, so pitch and level hold for hours (`DroneSynthTest`: ten minutes at the same level within 0.1 % and the same pitch); levels are set once a block of 64 samples (1.3 ms at 48 kHz) and ramped linearly across it. What moves (plucks, bellows, the filter) is computed as it goes, and the tanpura draws every pluck anew, so no two of its 6 s cycles are alike. The same seed and the same calls give the same samples, whatever the chunk sizes.
- **Fades and changes:** `play` from silence fades in over 0.1 s (raised cosine; the steady sounds' own attacks start from 0 too, and the tanpura is silent until its first pluck). `play` while it sounds with anything changed (root, sound, voicing, lower) **crossfades** over 0.4 s: two layers, each holding one of every sound, so nothing is made; the outgoing one fades to 0 along half a cosine while the incoming one rises (a steady sound takes up the drone's clock, so it doesn't swell in again and its sweep or bellows go on where they were; an incoming tanpura needs no fade, it plucks afresh). A change asked during a crossfade waits for it to end, and only the last one asked is kept, so a finger running across the roots ends on the last root within a crossfade or two. `release(seconds)` fades out from the current level; its last sample is exactly +0 and `render` then returns 0. `play` during a release brings the drone back up from where it is. The click check (`DroneSynthTest`): four Butterworth high-passes at 14 kHz, above every partial (8 kHz at most), leave a steady drone almost nothing, and every start, crossfade, quick run of changes and release stays within twice what the steady sounds themselves leave (a tanpura's pluck, the harmonium's air) plus 3·10⁻⁵, while a step of 0.001 is caught.
- **Level:** each sound and voicing has its own gain (`DroneSynth.LEVELS`), set with `DroneSynthReport` so all twelve are equally loud, K-weighted as LUFS are (−22.5 dB at D3), the loudest the tanpura's plucks allow: across every root of the octave at A4 = 466 Hz, lower or not, and several seeds, the highest peak is 0.63 (tanpura, fifth and octave), under the clicks' limit of 0.8; harmonium 0.37, pad 0.33, flute 0.19. So the flute organ and the pad have 10 dB or more of headroom if they ever need to be louder. **Known limit:** the flute organ and the pad (and *Lower*, for every sound) are very quiet on a phone's speaker, which plays little below 400 Hz: 10 % and 23 % of their energy is above it, against the tanpura's 62 % and the harmonium's 52 %.
- **Pitch measured** (`DroneSynthTest`, the app's own YIN, folded onto the root's or the fifth's pitch class): every sound and voicing, lower or not, at D3, G♯2 (A4 = 415), G3 (466) and B (432), within 1 cent on average; the harmonium reads −0.4 cents and the pad with fifth and octave −0.3 (their detuned voices), the rest within 0.03. Where a chord's common fundamental is below the detector's 28 Hz (the tanpura with its Pa, or any fifth, an octave down) nothing is read; `lower` there only halves the root, which its own test checks.
- **Playback** (`DronePlayer`, `AndroidDronePlayer` in `:core:audio`, playback only, never the microphone): `play(rootHz, sound = TANPURA, voicing = FIFTH, lower = false)`, `stop()`, `isPlaying: StateFlow<Boolean>`; `play` and `stop` return at once and may come from the main thread (they only leave the latest request in a field under a lock). `isPlaying` follows what was asked, for a ▶ / ■ button: true from `play`, false at once on `stop` (while the drone still fades out), or when it stopped by itself (a call, another app taking the sound, headphones unplugged, a failure). Like the reference tone: `ENCODING_PCM_FLOAT` mono at the phone's native output rate, `USAGE_MEDIA` / `CONTENT_TYPE_MUSIC` (the media volume sets its loudness), the track made by `play` and released after `stop` once the 0.25 s fade and one buffer of silence have been heard (`holdsSpeaker`, a `StateFlow` of the Android class, says while), so nothing holds the speaker while the drone is off; a `play` during the fade brings it back in the same track. A session runs on the player's own writer thread at `THREAD_PRIORITY_AUDIO` (made for it, gone 5 s after), since `DroneSynth` is not thread-safe: the session takes the latest request before every 10 ms chunk and hands it to its one `DroneSynth` (made on the first drone and kept), so a change is heard from the next chunk written, about 0.12 s later: the track keeps 12 chunks queued, as the metronome, because a gap would click under a steady sound. One buffer per session, nothing allocated while it plays (hard rule 2). **Focus:** each session holds `AUDIOFOCUS_GAIN_TRANSIENT`, as the tone (a drone is for playing against on its own, so another app's music pauses, and resumes when it ends); a loss (a call, another app's sound that won't share) stops it, a loss that may duck is left to Android, refused focus (during a call) means no drone. Headphones coming unplugged (`ACTION_AUDIO_BECOMING_NOISY`) stop it, as the metronome, so it never carries on out of the speaker. At media volume 0 a session shows the phone's volume panel. A failing track ends it quietly (logged, with any underruns). **Measured on the Xiaomi 12X** (`AndroidDronePlayerTest`, 2026-10-26): `play` and `stop` return at once, the track is let go after the fade (no sooner than 0.25 s, within 2 s), no underrun through a tanpura → pad (fifth and octave, lower) → harmonium → flute run of crossfades.
- **Cost** per 10 ms chunk (480 samples at 48 kHz), in each sound's dearest case (root, fifth and octave, an octave down: the most partials), on the Xiaomi 12X ([Threading and performance](performance.md), *Drone on the Xiaomi 12X*): pad 1.1 ms (11 % of a big core; 3.3 ms on a little one), harmonium 0.45, tanpura 0.32, flute 0.04; twice that during a 0.4 s crossfade. `DroneSynthBenchmark` (JVM, with the benchmarks): every sound under 1 ms a chunk, the pad 0.57 ms.
